So let me take you back to Tottenham Hotspur Stadium. The Eagles are down sixteen to fourteen late in the fourth quarter. Jalen Hurts moves the offense into range, and with eight seconds left on the clock, Jake Elliott jogs out for a forty-eight-yard field goal. And he drills it. Seventeen to sixteen, Eagles. Eight seconds left, and Jacksonville has no timeouts. At that moment, every single Eagles fan in London and every one of us watching back home thought this game was over. The hard part was done. Jake Elliott had done his job.

And this is where the story turns. The Eagles now have to kick off. And there were really two simple ways to handle it. Option one, Elliott boots the ball through the end zone for a touchback. Under the current kickoff rules, that puts Jacksonville at their own thirty-five-yard line with only a few seconds left. They would need a miracle. Option two, a line-drive squib kick that bounces around and eats up time. Either way, the Jaguars would be looking at a very long field with almost no clock.

But the Eagles did neither of those. Nick Sirianni called for Elliott to kick the ball deep into the landing zone and keep it in play, giving Jacksonville a chance to return it. And Ameer Abdullah caught the ball at the eight-yard line. He cut back inside around the twenty, some of the coverage players overpursued, a diving tackle attempt missed at his legs, and he kept going. Jake Elliott himself was one of the guys who slowed him down, and Jonathan Jones finally brought him to the ground at the Eagles thirty-nine-yard line. A fifty-three-yard return.

And then Cam Little stepped up. Fifty-seven yards. Final play. Good. Jaguars nineteen, Eagles seventeen. A third straight loss, and the Eagles fall to two and three.

Now Eagles Nation, here is the part of the story that I really want you to hear, because this is where the title of this video comes from. Jake Elliott did not make that final decision. He was told what to do, and he did exactly that.

After the game, Elliott faced the media, and he was honest. He said, quote, Honestly, kind of like he said, there was so little time for me to kind of gather myself between those two things. I just went out and did what I was told, and I wish it obviously turned out better. End quote.

Think about what that tells you. This is a kicker who just hit a go-ahead forty-eight-yarder under massive pressure, on the other side of the Atlantic Ocean, in a game his team needed. He barely had time to catch his breath before he had to run back out and kick off. And he did not throw anyone under the bus. He did not complain. He did not say, I wanted to kick it out of the end zone. He just said, I did what I was told. That is a professional. Eliot Shorr-Parks also reported that Elliott confirmed the plan was to kick it in bounds, and that this is something the team practices.

Now let us talk about how Elliott has actually played this season, because this is where the leaving-the-Eagles talk really falls apart. Through five regular-season games, according to CBS Sports, Jake Elliott has not missed a single kick. Not one. Every field goal, every extra point. In London, he was one for one on field goals, that clutch forty-eight-yarder, plus two for two on extra points. That is a perfect start to the season.

Now, to be fair and complete, CBS also pointed out that he has made either one field goal or zero field goals in four of those five games. In other words, he has not been asked to do a lot. And Eagles Nation, that matters even more when you remember where Elliott was coming from. Because last year was not his best. So what did the front office do? This is the real final decision in the Jake Elliott story, and it happened back in March. The Eagles reworked his contract. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Elliott took a one-million-dollar pay cut for 2026, going from six million dollars down to five million. But in exchange, the Eagles made that full five million guaranteed. And here is the key detail. Jake Elliott is under contract with the Philadelphia Eagles through the 2028 season.

Now think about what that means. A team that wanted to get rid of its kicker does not guarantee his money in March. NBC Sports Philadelphia literally described the move as a signal that he is their kicker. And with that money fully guaranteed, there is no real cap reason to cut him this season.
