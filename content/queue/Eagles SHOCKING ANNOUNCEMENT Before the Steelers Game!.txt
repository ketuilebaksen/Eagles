Eagles Nation, the trade deadline is over, Jordan Davis is gone, and the Eagles are sitting at two and seven on their bye week. But while everybody was talking about draft picks, a few quieter moves and updates came out of Philadelphia that could make this team look very different when the Pittsburgh Steelers come to town. Let me be honest with you right away. There was no single shocking announcement from the Eagles this week. Nobody was fired, nobody new was hired, and no superstar was added. But there were real developments. The Eagles waived running back Jaydon Blue and left Saquon Barkley as the only running back on the active roster. Will Shipley’s first chance to come off injured reserve lines up exactly with the Steelers game. DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks are getting extra recovery time. And Howie Roseman will not hold a press conference after the deadline. Put all of that together, and the Steelers game suddenly becomes one of the most interesting games of this lost season.

This is James with Second Floor, and here is what we are covering today. The roster move that tells you who might be coming back, the running backs, the receivers, the quarterback situation, the players who stayed at the deadline and why, Howie Roseman’s silence, the young players who should get a real chance, and what all of it means for the Steelers game. Before we start, if you are still riding with this team through all of this, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor.

Let me start with the move that was buried under all the trade deadline news.

According to the team, the Eagles waived running back Jaydon Blue on Tuesday. Normally, cutting a backup running back would not make anybody think twice. But look at what it does to the roster. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Saquon Barkley is now the only running back on the active fifty-three-man roster. That is not a setup any team wants to leave untouched for long, especially with Barkley dealing with his own physical issues. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he was listed with hamstring and back issues before the Giants game. He missed the first practice of that week, and then he was a limited participant on Thursday and Friday before playing on Sunday.

So why would the Eagles leave themselves that thin? This is just my opinion, not a report, but I do not see the Blue move as an isolated decision. With a bye week before the Steelers game, the Eagles do not have to rush anything. And to me, it looks like they are making room for players they expect to have available again. The deadline may be over, but the roster work clearly is not.

So let me tell you who those players could be.

The first name is Tank Bigsby. According to Fantasy Footballers reporting from early October, Bigsby picked up an abdominal injury in the Week 4 loss to the Rams, needed core muscle surgery, and was placed on injured reserve. He had to miss at least four games, which means he became eligible to return starting with the Giants game. He was not activated for that game. But the extra rest during the bye week can only help. Now, let me be careful. Eligible does not mean cleared. The Eagles have not announced that he is coming back. But the timing makes the Steelers game a possibility.

The second name is Will Shipley, and his timeline is even more interesting. According to Fantasy Footballers, the Eagles placed Shipley on injured reserve on October sixteenth with a partial tear of the plantar fascia in his foot. He had to miss at least four games, and his first chance to play again is, quote, Sunday, Nov. 22, against the Steelers. End quote. That is the exact game we are talking about. Again, being eligible does not mean he is ready. A foot injury like that is something you do not rush, and I am not going to guess when he will be back. That is up to the team and the doctors. But the window opens right on time.

And honestly, even getting one of those two backs back would give Saquon some real support. He should not have to carry this entire rushing attack by himself, not on a team that is two and seven and trying to protect its most important offensive player.

Now, it is not just about the running backs. Two important receivers have been missing from this offense.

According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, DeVonta Smith was ruled out for the Giants game with a hamstring injury, and Dontayvion Wicks was ruled out with an ankle injury. It was the third straight game both of them missed. And according to that same report, the Eagles would not have Smith back, quote, until at least Week 11 after the bye week. End quote. Week 11 is the Steelers game.

And according to PhillyVoice, the offense should get both Smith and Wicks back, which would give Joe Milton a lot more to work with than he had against the Giants. Now, that is not a promise that they will play. Hamstrings can be tricky, and the team will make that call. But it is a much better situation than trying to push them through another short week.

Think about who Milton had to throw to in his first start. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, his receivers against the Giants were Makai Lemon, Darius Cooper, Hollywood Brown and Elijah Moore. Lemon did his part, with six catches for sixty-one yards, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia. But that is not the receiver room this offense was built around.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. If Smith is ready, I would not throw him straight into a full workload. Give him a limited number of snaps, get the ball into his hands on shorter routes, and see how he responds. He can help move the chains without carrying the offense right away. And if Wicks comes back too, Lemon suddenly does not have to be the number one option every single snap.

The injury list was longer than just the receivers. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the Eagles also ruled out linebacker Jihaad Campbell with hamstring and quad injuries, receiver Britain Covey with a knee injury, defensive back Michael Carter the second with an abdomen injury, and cornerback Jonathan Jones with a hamstring injury. Jeremiah Trotter Junior started next to Zack Baun with Campbell out, and Cooper DeJean has been handling punt returns with Covey out. So the bye week matters for a lot of players, not just the offense.

Now, the quarterback situation.

According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Tanner McKee was listed as questionable for the Giants game with a rib injury, after he was benched earlier that week in favor of Joe Milton. And that left the Eagles with only Milton and rookie Cole Payton as healthy options if McKee was inactive. Milton made the start. He completed eighteen of thirty-four passes for one hundred sixty-nine yards, ran for sixty-four more, and was sacked seven times in the nineteen to eleven loss.

And remember why this is happening in the first place. Jalen Hurts’s season is over because of a health issue, and the team has said his prognosis is very good. We all wish him a full recovery, and I am not going to speculate beyond what the team has said. But it means the Eagles have to figure this out with Milton, McKee and Payton.

According to PhillyVoice, Milton deserves at least one more start. That is the writer’s opinion. But I agree with it. One start, behind an offensive line that gave up seven sacks, with his top two receivers out, is not a fair evaluation. If Smith and Wicks come back, the Steelers game becomes a much better test of what Milton can actually do.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you want Joe Milton to start against the Steelers. Comment number two if you want Tanner McKee back under center.

And this is also a big test for offensive coordinator Sean Mannion. He has not given Eagles fans much reason to feel comfortable this season. The plays have not produced enough. The rhythm has been missing. And too often, the offense looks like it is working very hard for very little. But to be fair to him, Hurts is out, key receivers have missed time, and the backfield has been patched together. That does not erase the struggles, but it does make it harder to evaluate him. If these players return, the Steelers game becomes an early chance to see whether Mannion can build something more functional. And this is just my opinion, not a report, but those final games could help decide whether he gets another season.

Now, let me move to the trade deadline, because the players who stayed matter just as much as the one who left.

According to the Inquirer, the Eagles made one trade before the Tuesday deadline. Jordan Davis went to Jacksonville for a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick. A second deal, sending Arnold Ebiketie to Cincinnati for a sixth-round pick, fell through when the Bengals backed out before it became official. According to the Inquirer, Nick Korte of Over The Cap pointed out why. Because Ebiketie was a compensatory free agent signing, the real cost for Cincinnati would have included that sixth-round pick plus a projected third-round compensatory pick. So Ebiketie stays.

And according to PhillyVoice, Ebiketie is one of the losers from the deadline. He would have had a chance to chase a Super Bowl in Cincinnati, and instead, he returns to the team that tried to trade him. That is an awkward spot for any player. But this is also a chance for him to prove the Eagles wrong over the final eight games.

Then there is Riq Woolen. According to the Inquirer, Woolen’s biggest surprise is that he stayed. Because he is on a one-year deal, his eight point six million dollar dead cap hit in 2027 would not have changed with a trade. So the money was not the reason to keep him. He has not been the shutdown corner fans hoped for, and the penalties have been frustrating. But he can still be a useful second corner next to Quinyon Mitchell. And according to PhillyVoice, a trade to a contender would have helped his resume before free agency next March. Instead, he stays on a defense that, in the writer’s words, quote, hasn’t played well. End quote.

And Dallas Goedert stayed too. According to the Inquirer, trading him before the deadline would have roughly doubled his dead cap hit to eighteen point two million dollars. So keeping him made sense financially. But it creates an interesting story. According to PhillyVoice, Zach Ertz now likely plays second fiddle, and he may not get the eight catches he needs to become the franchise’s all-time leader in receptions. And rookie tight end Eli Stowers, who was activated from injured reserve in October along with Landon Dickerson and Grant Calcaterra, according to the team, may have a hard time getting a bigger role with Goedert still here.

Hollywood Brown also stayed. His role has been limited, and some fans expected a move. Maybe the front office believes it can still get more from this group, or maybe the offers simply were not worth taking. Either way, standing still was a choice.

And when you compare all of that with the Jordan Davis trade, Howie Roseman’s approach starts to make sense. He was not trying to trade everybody. According to the Inquirer, quote, there was no fire sale, but the Eagles trade deadline was impactful all the same. End quote. He was looking for a return big enough to justify losing someone important. And Jacksonville gave it to him.

Now, the money side. According to the Inquirer, the Eagles could lead the league in dead cap money next season. A lot of that is money tied to players who are no longer here. And remember, the roster has already changed a lot this season, with Lane Johnson retiring in October and Cam Jurgens traded to Baltimore. So a trade does not erase every contract. But it does give the team flexibility on future commitments.

And here is the part that has a lot of Eagles fans frustrated.

According to Jeff McLane of the Inquirer, as reported by Heavy, quote, Howie Roseman won’t have a post-Jordan Davis trade and NFL trade deadline news conference with reporters, per usual. End quote. According to Heavy, Roseman did speak to reporters last month after the Cam Jurgens trade. But he has not spoken publicly since the news that Jalen Hurts’s season was over.

And it is not just Roseman. According to 97.5 The Fanatic, owner Jeffrey Lurie also skipped a press conference before his Hall of Fame enshrinement during the Cowboys game. So right now, Nick Sirianni is the main team official facing the tough questions.

The 97.5 The Fanatic columnist was very clear about how he feels. He wrote, quote, there is no excuse for him ducking the media like this. End quote. And he still called Roseman one of the best general managers in the league. That is an opinion, but a lot of fans feel the same way.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I understand that nobody enjoys facing tough questions after seven straight losses. But fans deserve to hear how the people running this team see the situation. And to be fair, according to Heavy, skipping a post-deadline press conference has been his usual practice. But this was not a usual deadline. Skipping a press conference does not explain anything. It just means the questions do not go away. And whenever Roseman does speak again, the questions about the quarterback, the coaches and the long-term roster will all be waiting for him.

Now, let me talk about the defense without Jordan Davis, because there is an opportunity here.

According to PhillyVoice, Moro Ojomo is one of the big winners from the deadline. Davis was set to earn twenty-six million dollars per year over the next few years, and the writer expects Ojomo to receive a, quote, very lucrative extension. End quote. In the writer’s words, quote, he’s about to get paid. End quote. Ojomo and Jalen Carter are now the future of the interior defensive line.

And that puts a little more responsibility on Carter too. Davis was not just another teammate. They knew each other from Georgia, and they won a Super Bowl together in Philadelphia. I understand why people worry about losing that relationship. But I will not assume Carter needs Davis next to him. He is an established professional now. The Eagles chose to keep him because they believe he can be the centerpiece of this defense. So instead of treating this like a disaster before anyone plays a snap, I want to see how he responds to a bigger leadership role.

Then there is Ty Robinson. According to PhillyVoice, the 2025 fifth-round pick has played about ten percent of the defensive snaps this season, but more than thirteen percent in each of the last two weeks. He should now get more rotational time to show whether he can handle a bigger role next year. And Byron Young, according to PhillyVoice, is the logical replacement for Davis in the five-two run front. He has played about twenty-seven percent of the defensive snaps so far. The former Raiders third-round pick could improve his free agency case with a strong second half.

And this is what a losing season should be about. Rookie edge rusher Keyshawn James-Newby is on the practice squad, and the coaching staff could take a look at its younger pass rushers over the final eight games. Rookie tight end Eli Stowers needs snaps. Young offensive linemen need snaps. This is just my opinion, not a report, but a season like this should tell you which young players belong in the next version of the Eagles.

Now, let me zoom out, because that is where this gets bigger than one game.

According to the Inquirer, the Eagles now hold ten picks in the first two rounds of the next three drafts, and they are projected to have twenty-seven total picks in that span. The Davis trade added a first and a second. The Cam Jurgens trade with Baltimore, according to the Inquirer, brought back a package that included a 2028 second-round pick. And the A.J. Brown deal with New England in June added a 2028 first-round pick. Put all of that next to the Jacksonville trade, and you can see the plan taking shape.

According to PhillyVoice, the extra first-round pick, combined with a likely top-ten pick of their own, could leave the team with, quote, no choice but take a QB in the draft. End quote. That is the writer’s opinion, and it adds another layer to the Jalen Hurts conversation. I am not saying that will happen. I am saying the draft capital gives Howie choices he did not have a month ago.

The Eagles are not guaranteed to hit on every pick. Nobody is. But more picks mean more chances for affordable talent. And they mean Roseman can move up, trade down, or trade for veterans when he needs to.

And that brings me back to the Steelers.

According to the Inquirer, the game on Sunday, November twenty-second, has been moved to one in the afternoon Eastern on CBS. Then the Eagles go to Dallas on Thanksgiving, November twenty-sixth.

Here is what I will be watching. First, whether Will Shipley or Tank Bigsby is activated, and whether the Eagles add another running back. Second, whether DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks are back, and how big their roles are. Third, who starts at quarterback, Joe Milton or Tanner McKee. Fourth, how the defensive line looks without Jordan Davis, and whether Ty Robinson and Byron Young get a bigger share of the snaps. And fifth, whether the offensive line can protect the quarterback after giving up seven sacks to the Giants.

None of those things are guaranteed outcomes. They are possibilities the Eagles created by making room and changing their priorities. But at least there is something worth watching.

They will say the Eagles did not have a fire sale, but still added a first-round pick. And they will say ten picks in the first two rounds over three drafts can make a rebuild a lot faster than people expect.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I do not think the Eagles made a shocking announcement this week. I think they made a series of quiet decisions that tell you what they expect to happen next. The Blue move tells me they expect help at running back. The extra time for Smith and Wicks tells me they want them back for the final stretch. And the Davis trade tells me the defense is about to look different. The Steelers game is not going to fix a two and seven season. But it can tell us whether this group is moving in a better direction, and which players deserve to be part of the next version of the Eagles. As for Howie, I think he should talk. Not because he did anything wrong at the deadline, but because this fan base deserves to hear the plan.
