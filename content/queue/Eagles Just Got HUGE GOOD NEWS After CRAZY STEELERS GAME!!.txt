Let me start with the obvious good news. The Eagles beat the Pittsburgh Steelers nineteen to sixteen on Sunday. According to Field Level Media, it was their first win since September twentieth, and it snapped a seven-game losing streak. The Steelers came in at six and three, fighting for the playoffs. And according to Field Level Media, the Steelers still have not won in Philadelphia since 1965.

Now, let me be clear. This win does not magically fix the season. The Eagles are three and seven, and the playoff race is very difficult. But after seven straight losses, seeing this team finally finish a game mattered. And the way they won it matters even more.

Let me start with the biggest reason for optimism. The defense.

According to one Eagles analyst, the Eagles have eleven sacks in their last two games, including the game against the Giants before the bye and the game against the Steelers after it. And against Pittsburgh, according to the official game data, the Eagles sacked Aaron Rodgers five times, hit him six times, forced four three-and-outs, and made a fourth-down stop. The Steelers finished with just two hundred fifty-five total yards.

Think about how different that sounds from the pass rush we were watching earlier this season. And remember, the Eagles did this without linebacker Zack Baun, who was ruled out with a back injury, and without Jordan Davis, who was traded to Jacksonville at the deadline.

So how did the Eagles do it? Let me start inside.

According to that Eagles analyst, Jalen Carter and Moro Ojomo set the tone up front. He said they did not need big sack numbers to change the game. They pushed blockers backward, closed running lanes, and made Aaron Rodgers move before he wanted to. And the question everybody had after the Jordan Davis trade was simple. Could this front hold up without that size in the middle? On Sunday, the answer was yes.

Look at the Steelers' running game. According to the official game data, Pittsburgh ran the ball twenty times for just seventy-three yards, and their longest run was twelve yards. According to that analyst, Jaylen Warren, one of Pittsburgh's biggest offensive threats, finished with only twenty-eight yards on nine carries. I could not confirm his exact numbers myself, but the team totals tell you the story. When you take away the run, an offense becomes predictable. And that is when the pass rush can take over.

Now, let me talk about the edge rushers.

According to Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice, Jonathan Greenard was part of the sack party again, and Nolan Smith recorded his sixth sack of the season in the fourth quarter, which pushed the Steelers out of field goal range. And according to that Eagles analyst, Jalyx Hunt brought real energy off the edge too. I love seeing pressure come from different players. That is how this defense becomes dangerous again. The quarterback never knows which matchup is going to break down first.

And the secondary did its part. According to that analyst, Quinyon Mitchell and Cooper DeJean made DK Metcalf's day miserable. He said Metcalf finished with one catch for two yards before he was ejected. And according to Field Level Media, Metcalf was ejected after his second unsportsmanlike conduct penalty. The analyst also gave credit to Marcus Epps for making important tackles coming forward, and to Andrew Mukuba for protecting the deep part of the field.

Now, I am not calling this a flawless performance. The Steelers did score a late touchdown, a twenty-yard pass from Rodgers to rookie Max Hurleman with a minute and twenty-one seconds left. But the defense kept Pittsburgh out of the end zone for nearly the entire afternoon. And according to Kempski, seventy-two of the Steelers' yards came on that one late drive.

Now, the best surprise might have been the players nobody expected to headline this win.

According to Kempski, Byron Young had the first two sacks of his season. That is a huge response from a player who had struggled to make a consistent impact. Gabe Hall added the first sack of his NFL career, and according to Kempski, Hall had been waived or released four times in three seasons. According to that Eagles analyst, Ty Robinson also got opportunities in the rotation, and I want to see whether he can build on those snaps. You cannot expect the stars to do everything. When role players step up, a defense gets deeper and tougher.

And then there are the young linebackers. According to Kempski, Jeremiah Trotter Junior led all Eagles with thirteen tackles. And according to the Eagles' official live blog, he stopped Jaylen Warren on fourth and one on the Steelers' first drive. Over his last few games, Trotter has been everywhere around the football. He is making a real case for the future.

And according to that Eagles analyst, Jihaad Campbell injured his left pinky during the game, briefly left the field, and then came back to make important tackles. That says a lot about his toughness. With Zack Baun out, these young linebackers had to carry real responsibility, and they answered.

After the game, Nick Sirianni said it would be easy to look at the stats and say Trotter and Campbell played a great game, and they did. But he said, quote, it takes great block destruction from the front, end quote, to let those linebackers run and make plays. He called it good team defense.

And the cornerbacks had to adjust on the fly. According to Kempski, Jonathan Jones came in for Riq Woolen at one point and broke up a third-down pass to Germie Bernard in the end zone. According to that analyst, Jones also gave up a big completion, so it was not perfect. Sirianni said they took Woolen out to reset a little bit, and when Woolen came back in, he was right there in coverage on a long pass. Sirianni said, quote, I got a lot of faith in him. End quote. And according to that analyst, Kelee Ringo also stepped in after Quinyon Mitchell went down with a hamstring injury.

Now, let me get to the offense, because there were some real sparks.

Watching Saquon Barkley on that thirty-eight-yard touchdown run brought back memories of 2024. According to the Eagles' official live blog, he escaped T.J. Watt in the backfield. And according to that Eagles analyst, Darius Cooper deserves credit for his block on that run. Barkley saw a small opening, made his cut, and suddenly nobody could catch him. According to Bleacher Report, he finished with seventeen carries for seventy-five yards and a touchdown. That burst is still there.

And getting DeVonta Smith back reminded everybody what this offense has been missing. According to that analyst, Smith finished with five catches for forty-one yards. Those numbers might not jump off the page. But look at when the biggest one happened. With a minute and twenty-one seconds left, a three-point lead and the Steelers pushing to get the ball back, Smith got open on third and six and caught a seven-yard pass from Joe Milton that sealed the game. According to NBC Sports, Milton targeted Smith a team-high twelve times.

Sirianni explained the call after the game. He said, quote, you just want to be aggressive in four-minute situations. End quote. And he said, quote, we wanted to end it with the ball in our hands. End quote.

Darius Cooper made plays too. According to Field Level Media, Milton hit Cooper for twenty-nine yards in the first half, and according to that analyst, Milton escaped pressure on that play. There is still offensive talent on this roster.

Now, let me give special teams their credit, because this game does not get won without them.

According to the official game data, Jake Elliott went four for four on field goals, including a fifty-three-yarder and a fifty-five-yarder. Those kicks mattered because the offense kept stalling. And Braden Mann had a seventy-yard punt, according to Inquirer columnist Mike Sielski. Field position and reliable kicking can decide a close game. This was a perfect example.

And I want to recognize rookie right tackle Markeel Bell. Going against T.J. Watt is a tough job for any tackle, especially a young one replacing future Hall of Famer Lane Johnson. According to that Eagles analyst, Bell held up well for much of the game with help from his teammates and limited Watt's impact early. The offensive line was not perfect, but young linemen competing instead of folding under pressure gives the front office something real to evaluate.

Now, here is where I have to slow down the celebration. The offense still has serious problems.

According to Field Level Media, Joe Milton finished eleven of twenty-one for one hundred forty-one yards. And according to the official game data, the Eagles had only eleven first downs in the entire game and just two hundred fourteen total yards. According to that analyst, Milton overthrew DeVonta Smith on deep balls and sometimes put too much speed on shorter throws when the receiver needed touch. And the protection struggled at times. According to the official game data, Milton was sacked three times.

You cannot expect to keep beating good teams with an offense playing like that. But I also have to give Milton credit for the ending. When the game got tight, he made the throw that sealed it. In an uneven game, he still found one big moment. And Sirianni said after the game, quote, he's confident and he should be. He's a talented guy. End quote.

Here is the downside of this win. Before the game, according to Kempski, the Eagles were in the top five of projected draft positioning. And according to that Eagles analyst, after the win and the rest of Sunday's results, the Eagles' own projected pick moved to eighth. A loss might have put them closer to the top three, depending on other results and tiebreakers.

And according to the official standings, there are six teams with worse records than the Eagles right now. The Chargers, the Dolphins and the Titans are one and nine. The Cardinals, the Jets and the Colts are three and eight. And the Eagles are in a group of three and seven teams with the Saints, the Buccaneers and the Falcons.

That analyst named some of the top prospects the Eagles could miss out on with a lower pick, like edge rushers Colin Simmons and Dylan Stewart, receiver Jeremiah Smith, cornerback Leonard Moore, and receiver Charlie Becker. A higher pick means more control over who you get.

But here is the other side. That analyst made a great point. If the Eagles end up picking somewhere between seventh and twelfth, that is still a valuable position. It might even make more sense to take an offensive tackle or another player who fills an immediate hole. And remember, the Eagles also have the Jaguars' first-round pick in 2027 from the Jordan Davis trade. And according to Larry Rupp of Eagles On SI, the Eagles have ten picks in the first two rounds of the next three drafts. One win has not ruined the draft. If Howie Roseman gets those picks right, the Eagles can still land players who change this team.

Now, let me talk about the big picture, because this is where the real good news is.

I do not believe the Eagles need to tear everything apart to become respectable again in 2027. Look at this defensive core. Jalen Carter, Nolan Smith, Jihaad Campbell, Jeremiah Trotter Junior, Cooper DeJean, Quinyon Mitchell, Andrew Mukuba. Look at Saquon Barkley still breaking explosive runs, DeVonta Smith still getting open, and young players like Markeel Bell proving they belong.

And then think about the players who are not on the field right now. Jalen Hurts' return will depend on medical clearance, and I will only report what the team officially says. But if Zack Baun, Dontayvion Wicks and the other injured contributors get healthy, a healthier version of this roster should be capable of competing in the NFC East again.

But competing for the division and winning another Super Bowl are two different standards. According to that Eagles analyst, Howie Roseman still has plenty to address. The offensive line needs help at tackle and guard. The tight end room needs more depth. Safety remains a question. And there are questions at quarterback, defensive tackle and edge rusher too.

And that analyst was honest about the coaching too. He said he still has serious questions about Nick Sirianni and offensive coordinator Sean Mannion, the play calling, the way possessions stall, and the lack of steady protection. He said those problems cannot just be blamed on injuries. That is his opinion, but it is a fair point. You can celebrate a win and still believe the Eagles need real changes in how they build and run this offense.
