Eagles Nation, one quiet line on the NFL’s transaction wire just dropped the two biggest rivals in the NFC East into the same story. On Tuesday, the Dallas Cowboys let go of a young offensive tackle. By Wednesday, before the news had even cooled off, the Philadelphia Eagles had grabbed him. Now, let me be honest about the headline you might have seen. Howie Roseman did not steal anybody. There was no trade, no secret deal and no rule broken. The Cowboys waived a player, and the Eagles claimed him off waivers, which any team in the league could have done. But the speed of the move, the size of the player and the fact that he came straight from Dallas, just one week before the Eagles go to Dallas on Thanksgiving, make this one of the more interesting depth moves of the season. So let me tell you who he is, why the Cowboys let him go, why the Eagles wanted him, and what else is happening before the Steelers game.

The player the Eagles just claimed from Dallas, why the Cowboys had to make room on their roster, what he can and cannot bring to the Eagles, how he fits on a rebuilt offensive line, and the latest injury news before Sunday, including a starting linebacker who has now missed two straight practices. Let me start with the name. Ajani Cornelius.

According to the team’s official website, the Eagles claimed offensive tackle Ajani Cornelius off waivers from the Cowboys on Wednesday, November eighteenth, one day after Dallas waived him. And he went straight onto the active roster, effective immediately. According to the team, he is six foot five and twenty-four years old.

His college path is a good one. According to the team, he started his career at Rhode Island, where he earned first-team All-CAA honors in 2022. Then he transferred to Oregon. According to Heavy, he started twenty-eight games at right tackle over two seasons there, against some of the best competition in college football. And according to the team, he was named a second-team All-American in his final season.

The Cowboys then drafted him in the sixth round of the 2025 NFL Draft. According to Heavy, he was the two hundred fourth overall pick. And according to the team, he appeared in four NFL games over his first two seasons, three of them this year.

Now, why did Dallas let him go? This part is important, because it tells you this was not really about Cornelius being a bad player. It was about timing, roster math and an injury to a starter in the middle of the season, when a team needs answers right away and cannot wait for a young player to develop.

According to the Cowboys’ official website, Dallas lost a wild game to the San Francisco 49ers, forty-six to forty, on Sunday, November fifteenth. And in that game, starting right guard Tyler Booker hurt his ankle and did not return for most of the second half. According to the Cowboys, by Wednesday the team was treating Booker as out, and on Thursday he was not practicing while he rehabbed.

So Dallas had an emergency. According to the Cowboys’ website, T.J. Bass, who had been starting at left guard, had to slide over to right guard. And the Cowboys brought back veteran lineman Nick Leverett, who can play center and guard, to give them more versatility. To make room for Leverett, somebody had to go. And that somebody was Ajani Cornelius.

Think about it from Dallas’s side. They needed a veteran interior lineman who could help right now. Cornelius is a young tackle still developing. So they made the move that fit their immediate need. That does not mean the Cowboys made a mistake. And it does not mean the Eagles found some hidden star. It just means two teams looked at the same player from two very different situations.

And for the Eagles, the situation was simple. According to Ed Kracz of Eagles On SI, the Eagles had four open roster spots after trading Jordan Davis and releasing Jaydon Blue, Hollywood Brown and Fred Johnson. Zach Ertz, John Ojukwu and Jaydon Blue filled three of them earlier this week. And now Cornelius fills the fourth. The Eagles had a spot, they had a need for young tackle depth, and Dallas put a young tackle on the waiver wire. Howie Roseman did not waste any time, and that is exactly what you want from your general manager.

And here is a fun part. According to the Cowboys’ website, Cornelius and the Eagles will likely face Dallas in Arlington on Thanksgiving Day. So less than ten days after the Cowboys let him go, he could be standing on the opposite sideline. You know that is going to be a storyline.

Now, let me be fair about what Cornelius is and what he is not, because this is where a lot of the online coverage goes too far.

According to Heavy, in four regular-season games with Dallas, Cornelius did not play a single offensive snap. He logged twenty-one special teams snaps. So there is almost no NFL tape of him as an offensive lineman.

And the scouting report coming out of college was honest. According to Heavy, NFL.com’s Lance Zierlein described him as a durable lineman with NFL traits, but said he, quote, lacks protection talent to stay outside as a pro. End quote. Zierlein projected him as a possible Day 3 pick whose ceiling was likely a backup.

So let me be clear. The Eagles did not just find their next starting tackle. What they found is a young, big, durable lineman with a lot of college experience, at no cost in draft picks, who now gets a chance to develop in a new building. That is a smart, low-risk bet. Nothing more, and nothing less.

And remember, the Eagles’ coaching staff has developed young linemen before. Jordan Mailata came into the league as a seventh-round pick who had never played football, and he became one of the best left tackles in the NFL. I am not saying Cornelius is the next Mailata. That would not be fair to anyone. But it shows that this organization knows how to be patient with big, raw offensive linemen.

And claiming a player off waivers is very different from making a trade. There was no draft pick sent to Dallas. The Eagles just took on his contract. For a team that is trying to balance the rest of this season with building for 2027, that kind of move makes a lot of sense.

And the news broke fast. According to ClutchPoints, as published on Yahoo Sports, NFL Network’s Mike Garafolo reported it simply. Quote, the Eagles claimed OL Ajani Cornelius off waivers from the Cowboys. End quote. ClutchPoints listed him at six foot five and three hundred eighteen pounds, and pointed out that he has never started an NFL game. The writer’s view was that Cornelius is unlikely to change the offense’s fortunes, but looks like a useful depth addition. And it is not the first time this year the Eagles have picked up a young player Dallas drafted. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, running back Jaydon Blue was a 2025 fifth-round pick by the Cowboys too, and he is now on the Eagles’ active roster as their main kickoff returner.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I like this move. I do not expect Cornelius to start on Sunday, and I do not think he will save this offensive line. But the Eagles are in evaluation mode, and young players with size and experience are exactly the kind of players you want to evaluate. If the coaching staff can clean up his technique, he might become a useful swing tackle. And if he does not, it cost the Eagles nothing but a roster spot.

So what does Cornelius need to do to stick? This is just my opinion, not a report, but it comes down to the little things. An offensive tackle in the NFL has to recognize what the defense is doing before the snap, understand every blocking combination in the playbook, and keep his balance against edge rushers who are faster than anyone he saw in college. One bad step with his feet, and the quarterback is under pressure. One wrong angle on a run play, and the whole play falls apart. His size gives him a head start. But technique, discipline and a lot of practice reps are what will decide whether he becomes a real NFL player. And he has to learn a brand new playbook, new terminology and new teammates in the middle of a season. That is not easy for anybody.

Now, let me show you how he fits on this offensive line, because it has changed a lot this year.

According to Heavy, the Eagles now have five offensive tackles on the active roster. Jordan Mailata and rookie Markel Bell are expected to start at left and right tackle. Cornelius and John Ojukwu are the depth options. And the fifth tackle is Lane Johnson, who, according to Heavy and the Inquirer, is retired but is still on the roster for salary cap reasons. According to Heavy, the Eagles also have three offensive linemen on the practice squad, but none of them are listed as tackles. So the Eagles really did need another young tackle.

And remember why the depth matters. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Markel Bell, a rookie third-round pick from Miami, took over at right tackle after Lane Johnson suddenly retired in October. According to Pro Football Talk, the Eagles released veteran Fred Johnson last week, the swing tackle who had filled in for both Mailata and Lane Johnson in past seasons. According to PhillyVoice, John Ojukwu was an undrafted free agent out of Boise State in 2023 who played sixteen games with nine starts for the Titans. And according to the Inquirer, he was promoted to the active roster on Monday.

So the Eagles went from having two veteran tackles behind the starters to having two young players, Ojukwu and Cornelius, competing for that swing tackle role. And with the offensive line already giving up seven sacks against the Giants, the coaches need to know who they can trust if somebody goes down.

And there is one more name connected to the running game. According to ESPN’s Adam Schefter, the Eagles brought back TJ Paganetti as a consultant this week. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he spent ten seasons with the Eagles, including both Super Bowl staffs, and he worked as an assistant offensive line coach. According to Athlon Sports, the 2024 Eagles set a franchise record with three thousand eight hundred sixty-six rushing yards when he was on staff. So with Paganetti in the building and offensive line coach Chris Kuper running the room, Cornelius will have plenty of people helping him develop.
