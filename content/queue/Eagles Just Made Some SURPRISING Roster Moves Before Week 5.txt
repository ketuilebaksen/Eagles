The Eagles just made some surprising roster moves before Week 5, Eagles Nation. A two-time Pro Bowler traded away. A veteran running back promoted to the 53-man roster. A first-team All-American lineman snapped up off the street. A former Dolphins receiver added to the practice squad. And when you put all of it together, you might expect the guy at the center of it all, Jalen Hurts, to be frustrated. Worried. Maybe even a little rattled. Instead, he walked up to the podium this week and said something that should fire up every single Eagles fan on the planet. Quote, “This is when you find out what you have.” So today on Second Floor, we’re going to break down every roster move, explain why some of them are actually smart, and show you why Jalen Hurts might be the biggest reason to feel good about this team heading into London.

Welcome in, everybody. This is James, and you’re on Second Floor. Today is a positive one, so get comfortable. If you love this team and you want to hear the other side of the story, the side that isn’t all doom and gloom, hit that like button, subscribe, and turn on notifications. Let’s go.

Let’s start with the moves, because there’s been a lot of them. And I want to walk through them one at a time, so you understand exactly what Howie Roseman is doing.

Move number one, and the biggest one. On Wednesday, the Eagles traded center Cam Jurgens and a 2027 seventh-round pick to the Baltimore Ravens for a 2027 fifth-round pick and a 2028 second-round pick. Yes, it was surprising. Yes, it came a day after Lane Johnson’s retirement. But look at the return. Jurgens had been struggling at left guard, he was in the concussion protocol, and he’d battled back issues for two years. And Baltimore, desperate after losing three centers to injured reserve, handed over a future second-rounder. Howie said, quote, “you don’t get many opportunities to get second-round picks in trades.” And he made it clear this isn’t a white flag. Quote, “We’re trying to win this year.” The Eagles now have 16 picks in the 2027 and 2028 drafts, according to Dave Spadaro. That’s ammunition. And Howie knows how to use ammunition. Don’t be surprised if some of it gets used before the November 10th trade deadline.

Move number two. The running back room. With Saquon Barkley dealing with a hamstring injury and Tank Bigsby headed for core muscle surgery, the Eagles placed Bigsby on injured reserve on Tuesday. Then on Wednesday, they promoted Dameon Pierce from the practice squad to the 53-man roster. And here’s why that’s a smart move. Pierce isn’t some random name. In three seasons with Houston, he ran for 1,674 yards and eight touchdowns. As a rookie in 2022, he was a starter who ran for 939 yards. He’s a tough, downhill runner who knows how to finish. And he’s not alone. Will Shipley, a fourth-round pick in 2024 out of Clemson, gets his biggest opportunity yet. And the good news on Barkley? NFL Network reported the hamstring is not considered a long-term injury. He’s week-to-week. This is a bridge, not a crisis.

Move number three. The Eagles signed offensive lineman James Brockermeyer to the practice squad. And this guy has a résumé. He was a first-team All-American at Miami last season and helped the Hurricanes reach the national championship game. Before that, he played at Alabama and TCU. His dad, Blake Brockermeyer, played 14 seasons in the NFL. He went undrafted this year, spent time with Atlanta, and now he’s in Philadelphia, learning from one of the best offensive line rooms in football. Teams don’t find All-Americans on the street very often. That’s a nice depth pickup.

Move number four. The Eagles signed wide receiver Erik Ezukanma to the practice squad. With DeVonta Smith and Hollywood Brown banged up, and Dontayvion Wicks popping up on the injury report with a hamstring on Thursday, this is just smart insurance. You can never have too many bodies at receiver during a stretch like this.

And there’s one more name that isn’t technically a roster move yet, but could be the biggest surprise of all. Jake Majors. With center Drew Kendall missing practice on Wednesday and Thursday with a knee injury, Majors was lining up with the first team at center. He’s a 24-year-old out of Texas, where he played 58 games over five seasons. He went undrafted in 2025 and has been on the Eagles’ practice squad. If Kendall can’t go, Majors could make his NFL debut as the starting center, in London. And he’s ready for it. He said, quote, “The foundation we have in this O-line room is special.” Jason Kelce even worked with him this summer and told him to be confident in who he is. That’s a pretty good mentor to have.

So that’s the roster. A lot of change, a lot of new faces. Now let’s talk about the most important person in the building. The quarterback.

Jalen Hurts.

Here’s what I love about Jalen this week. He had every reason to point fingers. His Pro Bowl right tackle retired. His starting center from last year got traded. His top two receivers are hurt. His star running back is out. And how did he respond? Here’s what he said on Wednesday, quote, “I feel good about the guys that we have.” He said, “We’ve got a lot of opportunity in front of us.” And then the line that really stuck with me. Quote, “This is when you find out what you have. You find out who you’re going out there to battle with. You see how you respond to adversity, how you show up to work every day.”

He called it a next-man-up mentality, and he said, quote, “That’s truly my mentality.” He also said, “It’s definitely a challenge that nobody is going to wait on us to overcome. We have to be a persistent group, continue to jell, continue to come together.” And the one that sums it all up. Quote, “It’s going to take all of us.”

And look at how he handled the hardest part of the week. When Lane Johnson retired and Cam Jurgens was traded, Hurts didn’t talk about blocking schemes or what it meant for his stats. He talked about people. He said those were guys he’d “gone to bat with,” and he said about Lane, he just wants to see him “smiling again” and “at peace.” That’s the kind of thing that doesn’t show up on a stat sheet, but it shows up in a locker room. Players run through walls for a quarterback like that.

Eagles Nation, that’s a leader. That’s not a guy hiding from the moment. That’s a guy who’s been here before, and who knows exactly what this locker room needs to hear.

And let’s be real about who Jalen Hurts is, because after two rough weeks, people forget fast. This is a Super Bowl MVP. This is a quarterback who has won at the highest level. He’s been to multiple Super Bowls, he’s been through slumps before, and he’s always come out the other side. Every time people started to doubt him, he answered on the field. The same people saying “what’s wrong with Hurts” right now were saying it before the Super Bowl run too. And the numbers this week tell a really encouraging story.

According to the Eagles’ official “By the Numbers” preview, Hurts has 32 passing touchdowns since the start of 2025. That’s tied for sixth in the entire NFL. And here’s the stat I want every Eagles fan to remember before Sunday. In his career, including the playoffs, against top-five scoring defenses, the Eagles are 15 and 5 with Hurts at quarterback. Fifteen and five. They’ve averaged 24.6 points per game in those matchups, with 42 touchdowns against just 17 giveaways. Why does that matter? Because Jacksonville has one of the best defenses in football right now. The Jaguars are second in the league in points allowed. And Jalen Hurts has a long history of rising up against exactly that kind of opponent.

And don’t forget what we saw just three weeks ago in Tennessee. Down 20 to 17. One minute and 56 seconds left. No timeouts. In 150-degree heat on the field. And Hurts went 65 yards in nine plays and threw the game-winning touchdown to Darius Cooper with nine seconds left. That was his eighth fourth-quarter comeback since 2023. When the game is on the line, this guy delivers. Every Eagles fan has seen it.

And think about how a quarterback like Hurts can protect a young offensive line. This isn’t a statue in the pocket. When the pocket breaks down, he can escape and make plays with his legs. On the goal line and in short yardage, he’s still one of the most dangerous quarterbacks in football. Remember, in that Titans game he converted on fourth-and-1 with a quarterback sneak, and again on third down in the fourth quarter. With a rookie at right tackle and possibly an undrafted center making his first start, that mobility is a huge safety net. Quick throws, bootlegs, designed runs, getting the ball to Goedert underneath. That’s how you keep a young line out of trouble, and Hurts is built for exactly that.

Now, I’m not going to pretend the last two weeks were good. They weren’t. But Hurts owned it right away. After the Rams game, he called it “poor execution.” No excuses. And this week, instead of dwelling on it, he’s talking about building, jelling and attacking. Quote, “We’ve got a lot of room to improve and we’re attacking that every day.” That’s the mindset you want from your franchise quarterback.

And here’s the thing. Hurts isn’t walking into London alone. Let me give you some reasons for optimism around him, because there are a lot more than you might think.

Start with the young receivers. Dontayvion Wicks is averaging 16.4 yards per catch through four games with the Eagles. According to the team, that’s the most by an Eagles receiver through four games since Kevin Curtis back in 2007. Darius Cooper, at 24 years old, is the youngest undrafted receiver with at least three touchdowns in his team’s first four games since Allen Hurns in 2014. Cooper keeps showing up in the biggest moments. And Makai Lemon, the first-round rookie, is getting more and more reps. Landon Dickerson said he’s doing a tremendous job.

And there’s even better news. Dallas Goedert. He was back at practice this week for the first time since his MCL sprain, and he said he feels, quote, “really good.” He appears set to return on Sunday. Do you know what that means for Hurts? Since the start of 2025, Goedert has 13 receiving touchdowns, tied with Trey McBride for the most by any tight end in the NFL. Hurts gets his favorite safety valve back. And the young offensive line gets an extra blocker who knows exactly what he’s doing.

And when DeVonta Smith does come back, and he could be back as soon as Week 6, remember what kind of weapon he is. According to the team, Smith is averaging 6.3 catches and 78.3 yards per game this season, both top-10 in the NFL. And since the start of 2025, he has 511 receiving yards on third down, the most in the entire league. Third down has been the Eagles’ biggest problem the last two weeks. The best third-down receiver in football is on his way back. That’s a big deal.

Now look at the defense, because it’s been quietly solid. Quinyon Mitchell, who hurt his knee against the Rams, was a full participant on Thursday and is expected to play. He’s allowing just 7.9 yards per catch, tied for seventh-fewest among cornerbacks. Riq Woolen is allowing a 50 percent completion rate, also top-10. Zack Baun has four forced incompletions, the most among all NFL linebackers. Jihaad Campbell and Fred Warner are the only two linebackers in the league with at least 35 tackles, a sack and a forced fumble this season. Jordan Davis has a 77.8 run defense grade from PFF, fourth among interior linemen. And Jeremiah Trotter Jr. had 15 tackles against the Rams.

And special teams? Britain Covey has 135 punt return yards, second in the NFL. Braden Mann has 10 punts inside the 20, second in the league. And Jake Elliott is one of only seven kickers this season with multiple field goals of 55-plus yards without a miss. Field position wins close games, and the Eagles have some of the best in the business.

And now, the history. Eagles Nation, you’re going to like this. The Eagles have won five straight games against the Jacksonville Jaguars. Five in a row. The Eagles are 2 and 0 in international regular-season games, beating the Jaguars in London in 2018 and the Packers in Brazil in 2024. According to the team, the Eagles and Panthers are the only two franchises still undefeated outside the United States. And guess who was under center for that win in Brazil? Jalen Hurts. So when it comes to playing football outside the United States, Hurts is undefeated too. He knows what the travel feels like, the different routine, the strange kickoff time, the unfamiliar stadium. That experience matters when you’re leading a roster full of young players who have never done this before. In a game like this, the quarterback sets the tone for everybody. Calm quarterback, calm huddle. And Jalen Hurts might be the calmest quarterback in the league when the lights are brightest.

And Nick Sirianni? Since 2021, the Eagles have the best road record in the entire NFL, 31 and 14. And Sirianni is 6 and 0 against teams from the AFC South. Undefeated.

Now, I promised you a positive video, but I also promised you an honest one. So let’s respect the opponent. Jacksonville is good. Really good. They’re 3 and 1. Since Week 11 of last season, they’re 11 and 1, with the league’s best scoring offense and scoring defense in that span. They’re the only team this season that hasn’t allowed more than 20 points in any game. Trevor Lawrence ties Tom Brady for the most international wins by a quarterback and has the most international passing yards in NFL history. Travis Hunter played all 71 defensive snaps last week. And the Jaguars have 41 pressures without blitzing, tied for fifth in the league. They’re going to test this young offensive line.

But here’s the thing about tough tests. That’s exactly when you find out who you are. And that’s exactly what Hurts said.

So here’s my take, and you can fight me in the comments.

I think these roster moves make sense. Every single one of them. The Jurgens trade was good business. Pierce gives the backfield a tough, experienced runner. Brockermeyer and Ezukanma add depth at positions where the Eagles need bodies. And none of it changes the most important fact about this team. The Eagles have Jalen Hurts. And as long as they have Jalen Hurts, they have a chance in every single game.

I’ll go even further. I think London could be a turning point. Not because everything’s fixed. It’s not. But because this team needs a win that proves it can survive adversity. A young line holding up. A veteran running back stepping in. Goedert back as the safety valve. Hurts making the big throws when it matters. If the Eagles walk out of Tottenham Hotspur Stadium with a win, they’re 3 and 2, Dickerson is eligible to come back next week, Smith and Barkley are getting healthier, and the whole conversation in Philadelphia changes overnight.

And honestly? Every great Eagles season has a moment like this. A week where everybody counts them out. Where the injury report is a mile long. Where the experts pick against them. And then they go out and remind everybody what this franchise is all about. Hurts said it best. Quote, “We have to be committed to overcoming anything and everything.” I believe that. And I think this team believes it too.

Here’s what I’m watching on Sunday. Number one, Jalen Hurts’ first quarter. Can he get the ball out quick, find a rhythm early, and settle that young line down? Number two, Dallas Goedert. If he’s back, watch how often Hurts looks his way on third down. Number three, Dameon Pierce. Does the veteran give this run game some juice? Number four, the young offensive line. Majors or Kendall at center, Lampkin at left guard, Bell at right tackle. Just survive and advance. And number five, Darius Cooper. The kid keeps making plays. Can he do it again on an international stage?

Let’s zoom out one last time. Yes, it’s been a crazy week. A legend retired. A Pro Bowler was traded. New faces are stepping in all over the field. But this team still has a franchise quarterback who’s won a Super Bowl, a coach with the best road record in football, a defense full of rising young talent, and a front office with a war chest of picks and a clear plan. That’s not a team falling apart. That’s a team reloading on the fly.
