EAGLES OWNER Bombshell Decision Shocks The NFL Before COMMANDERS GAME!

Eagles Nation, it is game day. Tonight, the Eagles visit the Washington Commanders on Sunday Night Football, and the pressure on this franchise has never been higher in the Nick Sirianni era. Five straight losses. A two and five record. And this week, a lot of the conversation was not just about the players or the coaches. It was about the owner. Jeffrey Lurie.

So let me be honest with you right from the start, because that is how we do it on this channel. Jeffrey Lurie has not made any bombshell decision this week. He has not fired Nick Sirianni, he has not fired Howie Roseman, and he has not announced any change. What did happen is that Lurie was inducted into the Eagles Hall of Fame on Monday night, he released a message to the fans, and that message drew real criticism from a fan base that wants answers about Jalen Hurts. And on top of that, CBS Sports analysts had a lot to say about this team and about Sirianni’s job heading into tonight.

This is James with Second Floor, and today we are going to cover all of it. What Lurie said, why fans were upset, what CBS Sports said about the Eagles and Sirianni, the final roster moves for tonight, and how the Eagles can snap this losing streak in Washington. Before we start, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor, because we are covering every snap of this season, good or bad.

Let me start with Jeffrey Lurie.

On Monday night, during the loss to Dallas, Lurie was inducted into the Eagles Hall of Fame at halftime. That same morning, the team posted a special message from him to the fans. In it, Lurie said, quote, my induction into the Eagles Hall of Fame will always be a moment I look back on with immense pride. End quote. He told fans, quote, you are the most passionate and deserving sports fans on the planet. End quote. And he said, quote, it has been a privilege of a lifetime to lead this organization. End quote. He also talked about the goal of bringing world championships to Philadelphia and celebrating with parades down Broad Street.

Now, here is why a lot of fans were not happy. According to ClutchPoints, many fans criticized the timing of that message. Because while the owner was celebrating his Hall of Fame induction, Jalen Hurts was out for the season with blood clots in both lungs, and neither Lurie nor Howie Roseman had publicly addressed how the situation was handled. One fan wrote, quote, we deserve answers. End quote. Others called the timing the worst possible, even while acknowledging that Lurie has been a good owner overall.

Eagles Nation, I want to be fair here. Jeffrey Lurie has been one of the best owners in sports. Two Super Bowl titles. Constant investment in this team. A commitment to winning that most fan bases would love to have. He absolutely deserves to be in the Eagles Hall of Fame. Under his ownership, this franchise has been to four Super Bowls and won two of them, and it has become one of the most respected organizations in the league. But I also understand the frustration. The NFL and the players union are reviewing how Jalen’s blood clot situation was handled. An Inquirer column reported that, according to league sources, the NFL is unhappy with the Eagles’ communication. And so far, Nick Sirianni has been the only team representative speaking publicly about it. When fans see the owner celebrating while those questions are unanswered, it stings.

And the most important thing remains the same. The team and Jalen’s agent have said his prognosis is very good and that they expect a full recovery. That is what matters most. Whatever you think about the communication, nobody in that building wants anything other than a healthy Jalen Hurts back on the field in 2027.

There is one more owner-related story that has been bubbling in Philadelphia. According to the Philadelphia Inquirer, the Eagles are still in what Lurie called an exploratory research phase about the team’s future home. They could renovate Lincoln Financial Field, which is twenty-three years old, or build a new stadium. The lease at the Linc runs through 2032. So no decision has been made there either. But it is another big question that sits on the owner’s desk.

Now, let us talk about what CBS Sports said heading into tonight, because it was brutal.

On CBS Sports, Ryan Wilson and John Breech previewed tonight’s game, and they started with a stat that tells you everything. According to the CBS segment, these two teams have combined for the worst winning percentage by any two teams entering a Sunday night game in Week 8 or later in the past twenty years. And the CBS crew noted that a loss tonight would drop the Eagles to two and six, which they said would be the franchise’s worst start since 1999.

And Breech made a great point. Just twenty-one months ago, these two teams played in the NFC Championship Game. The Eagles crushed Washington that day and went on to win the Super Bowl. Now, less than two years later, people are joking about how unwatchable this game is. Breech said, in his words, maybe it would have made more sense to show videos of players trick-or-treating with their kids. That is how far both teams have fallen. Two teams that were once among the best in the conference are now fighting just to stay relevant in November.

But he also said something important. Both teams are still alive in the NFC East. The Giants lead the division at four, two and one, the Cowboys are three and four, and the Eagles and Commanders are right behind. The winner of this game keeps its head above water. The loser heads into the trade deadline in a very bad spot.

Then the CBS crew turned to the Eagles, and they did not hold back. They said that during this five-game losing streak, the Eagles have averaged the fewest yards per game over that stretch since 1976, and the fewest points since 1998. Ryan Wilson said the only good news he had for Eagles fans was that the season will be over in three months. Ouch. That is the kind of line that stings, especially for a fan base that has been spoiled by success over the last few years. But it also tells you how the national media sees this team right now. As a team that is done. The only way to change that narrative is to win.

But here is the part I want you to hear, because Wilson also defended this team. He said, and I am paraphrasing, that the players who are available are playing hard. They just do not have the firepower to compete with healthier teams right now. He pointed to all the injuries, to Jalen Hurts not playing his best before the blood clot diagnosis, and to Sean Mannion’s offense not working out the way the Eagles hoped. And he said the Eagles also lost games they had no business losing, even when they were healthy.

And think about how much this franchise has changed in just twenty-one months. In that NFC Championship Game in January of 2025, the Eagles beat Washington fifty-five to twenty-three. Saquon Barkley ran wild. Jalen Hurts was in command. The defense was dominant. Two weeks later, the Eagles won the Super Bowl. Now look at the injury list. Hurts is out for the season. DeVonta Smith, Wicks and Covey are out. Saquon is playing hurt. Lane Johnson has retired, Cam Jurgens was traded and A.J. Brown is in New England. It is a completely different team, and that is why it is so hard to compare this season to the ones before it.

And the injuries are not just bad luck in one spot. They have hit almost every position. Four running backs have gone on injured reserve this season, according to the team, including Dameon Pierce this week. The receiver room has been decimated. The edge rushers have been in and out. And now the secondary is thin. No coach in the league could keep a team on track with that many key players missing. That does not excuse every loss, but it is real.

Now, the coaching hot seat. The CBS host asked whose seat is hotter, Nick Sirianni or Washington’s Dan Quinn. And both analysts said Dan Quinn. Breech said that if a coach has been to two Super Bowls in the past four seasons, that should buy him a longer leash. He said if that does not buy you security, then every coach in the NFL is on the hot seat. And Wilson had a message for frustrated Eagles fans. He pointed out that fans and media do not always know what they want. He reminded everyone that Ravens fans ran John Harbaugh out of town, and now Harbaugh is in first place with the New York Giants. His point was simple. Sirianni has gotten this team to the Super Bowl consistently. Take a deep breath, let the team get healthy, and then evaluate.

Eagles Nation, I agree with that. And it lines up with what Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice wrote this week, that Jeffrey Lurie likes Sirianni and that an in-season firing feels unlikely.

Now, what about Washington? Jayden Daniels has had a tough stretch. According to the CBS crew, he is winless in his past eight starts, and he has not won a game as a starter in more than a year. He has been playing with a brace on his left arm after an elbow injury. But they also said he is not the problem. They said he has five touchdowns and no interceptions in four games this season, and that Washington has lost close games because of mistakes, including a tie with the Giants and a fourth-down mistake against the 49ers. The CBS crew said they feel more optimistic about Washington’s long-term outlook than the Eagles’, because of the Eagles’ injuries at quarterback.

And their picks? Both CBS analysts picked Washington. Wilson called this a game the Commanders have to win, and said Washington is playing a JV version of the Eagles because of injuries. Breech picked Washington twenty-three to sixteen.

Now, let us look at what the Eagles have for tonight, because the final roster moves came in on Saturday.

According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the Eagles elevated running back Elijah Mitchell and outside linebacker Mason Reiger from the practice squad. Mitchell, a former 49ers sixth-round pick, made his Eagles debut last week against Dallas with three carries for eleven yards and three catches for twenty-three yards. Reiger is an undrafted rookie from Wisconsin, and tonight could be his NFL debut. So the Eagles’ running backs tonight will be Saquon Barkley, Jaydon Blue and Elijah Mitchell. And Saquon is playing through hamstring and back injuries, so expect the Eagles to spread out the work.

There was also a downgrade. According to Jeff McLane of the Inquirer, cornerback Jakorian Bennett was ruled out with a knee injury. With Jonathan Jones also out with a hamstring, the secondary is thin behind Quinyon Mitchell, Cooper DeJean and Riq Woolen.

On the good side, NBC Sports Philadelphia reported that Nolan Smith is expected back from his concussion, and that Jalyx Hunt and Jonathan Greenard will be available as they play through their injuries. After zero sacks against Dallas, that pass rush help could not come at a better time.

And the offense is still without DeVonta Smith, Dontayvion Wicks and Britain Covey. That means Makai Lemon, Darius Cooper, Elijah Moore and Hollywood Brown at receiver, with Lemon expected to handle punt returns.

And there is one more thing to keep in mind about tonight. Washington is coming off a bye week. They had extra time to rest, extra time to study the Eagles’ tape from Monday night, and extra time to put together a game plan. Dallas showed the blueprint. Run the ball, keep the Eagles’ defense on the field, and pick your spots through the air. The Eagles know that, too. The question is whether they can stop it.

On the Eagles’ side, there is a short week to deal with. They played on Monday night, so they had less time to recover and prepare. Sirianni said last week that the staff has been adjusting schedules and practices, and that everything is on the table. Tonight is a chance to see whether those changes show up on the field.

So how do the Eagles prove CBS wrong tonight? Here are my keys.

First, run the football smartly. Saquon, Blue and Mitchell can share the load. Saquon showed on Monday night that even banged up, he can rip off big runs. Keep him fresh for the big moments.

Second, protect Tanner McKee and get him into a rhythm early. Quick throws to Lemon and Dallas Goedert. Let McKee build confidence before you take shots to Hollywood Brown.

Third, stop the run. After Javonte Williams ran for one hundred fifty-five yards on Monday night, Washington will absolutely try the same plan. Jalen Carter, Jordan Davis and Moro Ojomo have to control the line of scrimmage.

And fourth, make Jayden Daniels uncomfortable. With Nolan Smith, Greenard and Hunt back, Vic Fangio has the pass rushers to get after him. Daniels has been careful with the football. Force him into mistakes.

And there is a new name in the trade rumors this week too. Tight end Dallas Goedert. According to NFL.com, the Chicago Bears placed tight end Cole Kmet on injured reserve with a season-ending injury. And that has led some analysts, including Christopher Knox of Bleacher Report, to call Chicago an ideal landing spot for Goedert in a proposed trade. Let me be clear, that is a trade idea, not a report of talks. But Goedert is on a one-year deal, and Breer listed him as one of the contract-year players who could be available. Goedert has been one of the most important players of this era, a second-round pick who became a big part of a Super Bowl run. If tonight is one of his last games in an Eagles uniform, it would be the end of a great chapter.

And remember what is riding on this beyond tonight. The trade deadline is November tenth. Albert Breer of Sports Illustrated reported that Hollywood Brown, Riq Woolen and Dallas Goedert could be available, especially if the Eagles lose tonight. And Breer reported that the Eagles told teams Moro Ojomo is not available. A win tonight could change how Howie Roseman approaches the next ten days.

On one side, the pessimists. They will say CBS is right. This team has the fewest yards per game over a five-game stretch since 1976 and the fewest points since 1998. They will say a backup quarterback, no DeVonta Smith, a banged-up Saquon and a thin secondary are a recipe for a sixth straight loss. They will say the owner celebrating his Hall of Fame induction while staying quiet about Jalen Hurts shows an organization that is out of touch. And they will say two and six would make selling at the deadline unavoidable.

On the other side, the believers. They will say Washington is one, four and one and has not won in more than a month. They will say Jayden Daniels has not won as a starter in more than a year. They will say the Eagles are getting Nolan Smith and Greenard back, and that Saquon is playing. They will say CBS’s Ryan Wilson admitted the Eagles’ healthy players are playing hard, and that both analysts said Sirianni has earned a longer leash. And they will say one win is all it takes to change the mood of this season.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I think Jeffrey Lurie deserves his place in the Eagles Hall of Fame, and I also think the organization owes fans more communication about Jalen Hurts. Both things can be true. As for tonight, everybody is picking Washington. And honestly, that is exactly the kind of game a proud team can win. I am picking the Eagles. Not because they are healthy, but because they are desperate. Run the ball, win the turnover battle, rush the passer, and snap this streak. Eagles twenty, Commanders seventeen. Prove the doubters wrong, Eagles. This is the night to do it.
