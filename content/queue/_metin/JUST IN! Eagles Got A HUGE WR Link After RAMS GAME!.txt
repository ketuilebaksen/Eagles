Let me take you through how this started, because the timeline matters. Hill has been sitting at home all season. No team. Now, to be fair, Podell himself pointed out that the timing looked more like a signal toward Green Bay, because Hill posted it the same day the Packers announced Jayden Reed was out for the season with a neck injury. Now let's talk about why Tyreek Hill's name still carries so much weight, even at 32. This guy was a fifth-round pick in the 2016 draft by the Kansas City Chiefs, out of West Alabama. A fifth-rounder from a small school. And he turned into a weapon of mass destruction next to Patrick Mahomes. Six seasons in Kansas City, and every single one of them ended with a Pro Bowl. In 2022, the Chiefs traded him to Miami, and he didn't slow down one bit. All in all, that's eight Pro Bowls, five first-team All-Pro selections, a spot on the NFL's All-Decade Team of the 2010s, and six seasons with more than 1,000 receiving yards.

The peak came in 2023. That year, Hill led the entire NFL with 1,799 receiving yards and 13 touchdowns. One thousand seven hundred and ninety-nine yards. That's one of the greatest receiving seasons we've ever seen. And there's one number that explains the kind of threat he is better than anything. According to CBS Sports, his 28 career touchdowns of 50 yards or more rank fourth in NFL history. Fourth. Ever. When Hill touches the ball, the whole stadium holds its breath.

When he hit free agency, Hill had 819 catches, 11,363 yards and 83 touchdowns. That's a Hall of Fame conversation.

But here's the thing, Eagles Nation. The fall came as fast as his sprints. In 2024, his production dropped to 959 yards. Still solid, but not the Tyreek we knew. And in 2025, it all came crashing down. He had 21 catches for 265 yards and one touchdown when, in Week 4 against the New York Jets, he suffered a dislocated left knee, a torn ACL, and other torn ligaments. Season over. In February, the Dolphins released him. Hill was set to carry a cap hit of around 51 million dollars in 2026, and the release freed up about 22.8 million in space, according to Adam Schefter.

For the first time in his career, Tyreek Hill was an unrestricted free agent. And the market did not come running. In July, ESPN's Jeremy Fowler reported there wasn't much interest at that point. In August, his agent, Drew Rosenhaus, said on The Pat McAfee Show that he didn't expect Hill to sign before the season started, and that the priority wasn't when he came back, but how he came back. Rosenhaus even said they hadn't talked about money yet.
