I’ll be honest with you, Eagles Nation. When I first saw everything that happened this week, my first reaction was, what in the world is Philadelphia doing? A future Hall of Fame right tackle retires. A two-time Pro Bowl center gets traded. And now, two days before London, the young center who was supposed to replace him has missed back-to-back practices. But the more I look at it, the more I think there’s a bigger plan hiding underneath all of this. Because whether the Eagles wanted it or not, Sunday in London just became the biggest audition of the season. And the offensive line that walks onto that field might include a guy who has never played a single regular-season snap in the NFL. So today on Second Floor, we’re breaking down the youth movement that just got forced on this team, the new names you need to know, and the one message from Jason Kelce that sums it all up.

Welcome in, everybody. This is James, and you’re on Second Floor. Real Eagles talk, real facts, real passion. If that’s what you’re here for, hit that like button, subscribe, and turn on notifications, because this London game is going to tell us a lot about the future of this franchise, and we’re going to cover every piece of it. Let’s go.

Here’s the biggest thing that stands out to me. This isn’t the offseason. This isn’t an experiment where the Eagles have months to figure things out. This is game week, on the road, in another country, and the offensive line looks almost nothing like the group that opened the season. Cam Jurgens is in Baltimore. Lane Johnson has retired. Landon Dickerson is on injured reserve. And younger players are being asked to carry responsibilities that weren’t supposed to land on them this early.

We covered the Jurgens trade in detail already, so here’s the quick version. Philadelphia sent Jurgens and a 2027 seventh-round pick to Baltimore for a 2027 fifth-rounder and a 2028 second-rounder. After his back problems, his play had declined, and the move from center to left guard just didn’t work. And the big reason the Eagles felt comfortable making that trade was Drew Kendall. He’d shown he could handle center. Howie Roseman said it himself. Kendall “is doing a nice job.” The Eagles had an internal option they could trust.

And then, this happened.

Drew Kendall missed practice on Wednesday with a knee injury. Then he missed practice again on Thursday. Two straight days. According to the reporting out of Philadelphia, that’s a strong sign he might not play against Jacksonville. The guy the Eagles trusted enough to trade Jurgens might not be on the field in London.

So who’s snapping the ball? On Thursday, ESPN’s Tim McManus noticed something. Jake Majors was lining up at center with the first team, with Willie Lampkin at left guard. And if you’re asking, “who is Jake Majors?” you’re not alone.

Here’s his story. Majors is 24 years old. He played center at Texas for five seasons, appearing in 58 games. He went undrafted in 2025, signed with the Tampa Bay Buccaneers after the draft, and eventually landed on the Eagles’ practice squad, where he’s been ever since. He’s admitted his transition to the NFL was rocky last year. And if Kendall can’t go, according to the Inquirer, Majors would likely make his regular-season debut as the starting center. His first NFL game. In London. Against a Jaguars team that’s 3 and 1.

And I love what he said about it. Quote, “Everybody just reminded me, ‘Dude, you’re going against one of the best defenses in the NFL.’” He also said, quote, “The foundation we have in this O-line room is special.” And here’s a cool detail. Jason Kelce spent time with Majors back in August, and his advice was simple. Be confident in who you are. If you’re going to snap the ball in your first NFL game, that’s not bad advice to have in your back pocket.

So let’s look at the line that might take the field on Sunday. Jordan Mailata at left tackle. Willie Lampkin at left guard. Jake Majors at center. Tyler Steen at right guard. And Markel Bell at right tackle. Think about that. That would be the first career start for Lampkin, the first career start and NFL debut for Majors, and only the second career start for Bell. Three of the five spots filled by guys with one combined start. Mailata and Steen are the only two who’ve been through the wars. The Eagles could also turn to rookie Micah Morris, who has never started either. And there’s a brand-new guy on the practice squad, James Brockermeyer, an undersized but athletic center who was a first-team All-American at Miami. Nobody’s asking him to replace anyone right now, but he’s one more young piece the Eagles can develop.

And here’s a detail I find fascinating. If that lineup holds, the two guys right in the middle of the Eagles’ offensive line, at left guard and center, would both be undrafted players. Lampkin went undrafted out of North Carolina. Majors went undrafted out of Texas. For years, the middle of this line was Jason Kelce, a sixth-round pick who became a Hall of Fame-caliber center, next to Pro Bowlers. Philadelphia has a long history of finding gems in places nobody else looks. Maybe Sunday is the start of the next chapter of that story.

And this is where I think the bigger picture comes into focus. The Eagles didn’t plan it this way. But they’re about to find out, in real games, who belongs in their future.

Let’s start with the toughest job of all. Replacing Lane Johnson.

There is no simple replacement for Lane. And Pro Football Focus put some numbers on just how special he was. Over 14 seasons, Johnson played 11,917 offensive snaps. On 6,886 pass-blocking snaps, he allowed just 33 sacks. At one point, from Week 11 of 2020 to Week 9 of 2023, he went 1,405 straight pass-blocking snaps without giving up a single sack. And here’s the crazy part. He was still elite when he walked away. Through the first weeks of this season, he had a 97.2 percent pass-block win rate, the best in the entire NFL. He allowed just two hurries on 112 pass-blocking snaps, with no sacks and no quarterback hits. That’s not a guy who was slowing down. That’s a guy who was still on top of the world. PFF had him as a top-10 tackle in six different seasons. His best overall grade came in 2024, an 88.9, the year the Eagles won the Super Bowl. And in 2022, his 90.1 pass-blocking grade ranked second among all tackles in the league. Even last year, when a Lisfranc foot injury cost him seven games, he didn’t allow a single sack or quarterback hit on 262 pass-blocking snaps. That’s the standard Markel Bell is being measured against.

And Lane told us why he stepped away. In his statement, he said it is critical that he focus on his mental health, and that he owes it to his teammates, coaches, the organization and the fans to have his head and heart 100 percent in the game. That’s all we need to know. We respect it completely, and we wish him nothing but peace.

From a football perspective, it leaves a massive hole. But it also gives the Eagles something they normally wouldn’t have. An entire season to evaluate the player they hoped would eventually succeed him. And that player is Markel Bell.

Bell’s opportunity is enormous. The Eagles now get real evidence instead of guessing what he might become. He’s 22 years old, a third-round pick out of Miami, and he’s a mountain. 6 foot 9, 346 pounds. And his first start against the Rams showed both sides of the coin. When he got his hands on defenders, his size and length gave them real problems, especially in the run game. But he also gave up a team-high six pressures and three sacks. There were communication issues with Tyler Steen next to him, he struggled with some twists and stunts, and speed rushers gave him trouble.

That’s exactly why this season matters for him. The Eagles don’t need Bell to be Lane Johnson tomorrow. They need to know whether he can grow into a dependable starting tackle. If he does, that answers a huge question for the next decade. If he doesn’t, the Eagles will know they need to address the position, and they’ll have the draft picks to do it.

And speaking of Lane, there’s a money side to this that Eagles fans need to understand. According to Mike Florio of ProFootballTalk, Johnson’s contract is structured in a way that, if his retirement is processed right now, could put around 54.8 million dollars of dead money on the Eagles’ 2027 salary cap. That’s a huge number. Florio pointed out that the Eagles have ways to manage it, like how and when the retirement is officially processed, and that the team hadn’t made a roster move yet, which suggests they’re working on a strategy. I’m not a cap expert, and I won’t pretend to know exactly how Howie will handle it. But it’s one more reason why the extra draft picks from the Jurgens trade matter. Young players on rookie contracts are how you survive cap hits like that.

And the Eagles now have plenty of those picks. Including two first-rounders and two second-rounders in 2028. Sixteen combined picks in 2027 and 2028, according to Dave Spadaro. But here’s an important point. Collecting picks doesn’t automatically fix a roster. The young players already in the building have to develop. And this season, a lot of them are getting their shot. Markel Bell. Drew Kendall, when he’s healthy. Willie Lampkin. Jake Majors. Andrew Mukuba at safety. Jihaad Campbell at linebacker. Jeremiah Trotter Jr., who had 15 tackles against the Rams. These are the guys who will decide what this roster looks like for the next few years.

And now, here’s Jason Kelce’s message. On New Heights, while talking about Lane Johnson’s retirement with his brother Travis, Kelce had a direct message for Markel Bell. Quote, “It’s time to go, Bubba.” He added, “You’re going to get put in there. You’ve been able to learn from Lane a little bit.” And he said, “It’s going to be a bummer for me not being able to watch Lane do what he does and dominate the way he is.”

Eagles Nation, that sums it up perfectly. Bell was supposed to have time. Time to learn, time to develop, time to watch Lane handle the position. That time is over. Now he has to take what he learned from Lane, and from offensive line coach Chris Kuper, and use it with games on the line. I hear that message as a challenge. Take ownership. One assignment, one adjustment, one game at a time.

But the offensive line isn’t the only place where things are shifting. The injury report gave us more news on Thursday, some good, some not so good.

The bad first. Dontayvion Wicks showed up on the injury report with a hamstring injury. He was limited on Thursday, and it looks like he got hurt in practice. That one hurts, because with DeVonta Smith out, Wicks had become the number one receiver. Smith didn’t practice again on Thursday and looks on track to miss a second straight game, though he could be back in Week 6. Hollywood Brown didn’t practice either. Saquon Barkley is still week-to-week and on track to miss Sunday. Marcus Epps and Jonathan Greenard didn’t practice.

Now the good news. Quinyon Mitchell was upgraded to a full practice and is expected to play. That’s huge for the secondary. Dallas Goedert was limited, but he told reporters he feels, quote, “really good,” and he appears set to return. Zack Baun was limited again as he works back from his concussion, and Jihaad Campbell was limited too. Fred Johnson was also limited.

So think about the offense Jalen Hurts might have on Sunday. A brand-new offensive line. Will Shipley and Dameon Pierce in the backfield, with Carson Steele a possible practice squad call-up. No DeVonta Smith. A banged-up Dontayvion Wicks. But hopefully Dallas Goedert, back as that safety valve. Darius Cooper, who has three touchdowns in his last two games. And Makai Lemon, the rookie who’s getting more snaps every week.

And here’s something that might matter even more than the offensive line. The pass rush. The Eagles have struggled to get to the quarterback. Through four games, they have just four sacks, compared to 12 for their opponents. And there’s a big reason why. Jalen Carter has been playing with a hairline fracture in his wrist. Vic Fangio admitted the injury affected him more against the Rams than it had before, and he said that even playing with two hands is hard enough. Fangio also said it’s possible they’ve been playing Carter too much, and the team is considering a reduced role for him. Carter has just one sack this season. Nolan Smith leads the team with two.

But Fangio isn’t panicking. He pointed out that penalties have wiped out two sacks over the last two games, and he said, quote, “I just believe in the guys.” Still, until Carter’s wrist heals, somebody else has to step up and win one-on-one. Nolan Smith, Jalyx Hunt, Moro Ojomo, Jonathan Greenard when he’s healthy. Pick one, Eagles Nation. Somebody has to get home.

And now, let’s put it all together against Jacksonville. The Jaguars are fast, physical and disruptive. Last season they went 13 and 4 under Liam Coen, and Trevor Lawrence set a franchise record with 38 total touchdowns. This year, they’re 3 and 1. And they’re not fully healthy either. On Thursday, edge rusher Josh Hines-Allen was limited with a groin injury, and they had several cornerbacks limited, including Jourdan Lewis and Jarrian Jones. So there may be some opportunities for Hurts in the passing game.

But Jacksonville doesn’t care that Philadelphia is experimenting. They’re going to attack whatever weakness they find. Twists and stunts against a rookie right tackle. Overloads against a center making his first start. Bull rushes against a 5-foot-11 guard. That’s what’s coming.

So here’s what I’m watching. Number one, the center position. Is it Kendall or Majors? And whoever it is, how does he handle the protection calls? Number two, Willie Lampkin. Can he hold up against a physical defensive front in his first start? Number three, Markel Bell. Can he clean up the communication with Tyler Steen and handle the games up front? Number four, Mailata and Steen. Can the two veterans keep everyone around them steady? And number five, the pass rush. With Carter limited, who wins one-on-one?

And there’s one more thing that makes this test different. London. The schedule is unusual. A long flight, a new time zone, a morning kickoff. Young players have to handle all of that while learning on the fly. So this isn’t just a test of whether the Eagles can block Jacksonville for one afternoon. It’s an early evaluation of whether the young pieces of this roster can handle bigger roles while the veterans around them are hurt, gone, or in Baltimore.

So here’s my take, and you can fight me in the comments.

I think the Eagles stumbled into something that might help them long term. Nobody wanted Lane Johnson to retire. Nobody wanted Kendall to hurt his knee. But the Jurgens trade showed us what this front office is thinking. They’re not afraid to find out what they have. And now, over the next few weeks, they’re going to get real answers about Bell, Lampkin, Majors, Kendall, Mukuba, Campbell and Trotter. That’s information you can’t get in practice. You can only get it on Sundays.

But let me be clear. I’m not saying this is the year to rebuild. Howie said, “We’re trying to win this year,” and I believe him. The Eagles are 2 and 2, not 0 and 4. The NFC is wide open. Smith, Barkley, Dickerson and Kendall will come back. And if these young guys can survive the next couple of weeks, the Eagles could come out of this stretch with a deeper, younger and hungrier roster than the one they started with.

The plan only works if the young players deliver. And against Jacksonville, I’m looking for three things. Cleaner communication. Fewer breakdowns. And an offensive line that gives this offense a chance to function. On defense, somebody has to create pressure while Jalen Carter is limited. If the Eagles get those things, they can win this game. Even with all the missing pieces.

And if they don’t, well, that’s information too. Better to learn it in October than in January.

I understand why the Eagles want to see these guys play. I also understand why the fans are nervous. On Sunday, we find out how much trust this group has earned.
