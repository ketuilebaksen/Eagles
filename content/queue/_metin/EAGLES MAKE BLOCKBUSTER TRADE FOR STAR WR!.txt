Eagles Nation, the trade deadline is less than two weeks away, and the rumor mill around this team is spinning faster than ever. This week, one of ESPN’s top analysts floated a blockbuster idea. DeVonta Smith to the Kansas City Chiefs for a first-round pick and more. At the same time, a new report says the Eagles are carrying more than eighty-two million dollars in dead money this season, and that Howie Roseman may need another draft-driven reset to fix it. And one of the best reporters covering this team says one name on this roster is not going anywhere.

Now, let me be clear right away, because this channel is built on honesty. The Eagles have not made a blockbuster trade for a star wide receiver. They have not traded DeVonta Smith, and they have not acquired anyone. What we have is a trade proposal from an analyst, a report on the salary cap, and some real reporting about what Howie Roseman is actually looking for. And that is what we are going to break down today. Because a headline is not a transaction, and you deserve to know the difference.

Let me start with the trade idea that has everybody talking.

On Tuesday, October twenty-seventh, ESPN’s Ben Solak looked at which teams could make a move for a wide receiver before the deadline. And for the Kansas City Chiefs, he named DeVonta Smith. According to Heavy, Solak wrote, quote, if I were the Chiefs, I’d be putting a first and then some on the table for DeVonta Smith. End quote.

Think about that for a second. A first-round pick and more for a receiver who has missed time this season with a hamstring injury. That tells you how highly people around the league still think of DeVonta Smith. Analysts do not throw around first-round picks for players they think are declining. They do it for players they believe can change an offense. And it is easy to see why Kansas City would want him. Patrick Mahomes would love a receiver who wins with precise route running, finds soft spots in zone coverage, and makes contested catches look easy.

But here is the important part, and Solak said it himself. He does not expect the deal to happen. According to Heavy, Solak said the idea likely would not work because Howie Roseman would not trade both A.J. Brown and DeVonta Smith in the same season. Remember, the Eagles already traded A.J. Brown to the New England Patriots earlier this year. Moving their other top receiver in the same year would be a massive change in direction.

And Solak had a backup idea. If Smith is not available, he suggested the Chiefs offer a 2027 fourth-round pick for Dontayvion Wicks. He described Wicks as a receiver with three-level traits, meaning he can win short, intermediate and deep, but he said Wicks has been inconsistent with his route running and his hands. And he said a Day 3 pick could get it done.

So let me be clear. These are proposals from one analyst. They are not reports of trade talks. Nobody has reported that the Chiefs have called the Eagles about Smith or Wicks.

Now, here is the reporting that matters most. On Wednesday, Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice put together a list of Eagles players he considers untouchable or untradeable at the deadline. And at the top of his building blocks list was DeVonta Smith. His words were simple. Quote, DeVonta Smith isn’t going anywhere. End quote. Kempski described Smith as a top wide receiver with a competitive attitude, while acknowledging that he has struggled to stay healthy this season.

And Smith was not alone on that list. Kempski also listed Jordan Mailata, Quinyon Mitchell, Cooper DeJean and Jalyx Hunt as building blocks. He listed young developing starters like Makai Lemon, Drew Kendall, Markel Bell, Jihaad Campbell, Jeremiah Trotter and Andrew Mukuba. And he said players like Jalen Carter, Jordan Davis and Jonathan Greenard are essentially untradeable because of their contracts. As for Jalen Hurts, Kempski noted that his season is over because of the blood clots, so he is not a trade candidate right now.

Eagles Nation, that list tells you a lot about how the Eagles are likely thinking. They are not looking to tear down the core. They are looking to protect it while they work through a very tough season. The young defense, the left tackle, the young receivers and DeVonta Smith are the foundation. If anything moves at the deadline, it is more likely to be veterans on expiring deals.

And let us remember who DeVonta Smith is. He won the Heisman Trophy at Alabama in 2020, the first wide receiver to win it in nearly three decades. The Eagles traded up with, of all teams, the Dallas Cowboys to draft him tenth overall in 2021. He put together back-to-back thousand-yard seasons in 2022 and 2023, and he was a big part of the Super Bowl run in the 2024 season. He has never been the loudest player in the building, but he has been one of the most dependable. Players like that are the ones you build around, not the ones you sell when times get tough.

Now, let me give you my take on DeVonta Smith, because I feel strongly about this. Smith is one of the most polished route runners in the league. He creates separation, he understands coverage, and he gives his quarterback a reliable target. Those qualities do not grow on trees. Yes, a first-round pick has real value. But a first-round pick gives you a chance to find a receiver like DeVonta Smith. The Eagles already have one. And think about the receiver room. A.J. Brown is gone. Makai Lemon is a rookie who just scored his first touchdown on Monday night. Dontayvion Wicks is still developing. If you move Smith too, you could be left with a young, unproven group, no matter who is at quarterback next season. That does not sound like progress to me.

Now, what about the Wicks idea? That is a completely different conversation. Dontayvion Wicks has real physical tools, and he has made some big plays this season, including a seventeen-yard catch on third down against Carolina. But he is also dealing with an ankle injury that kept him out of the Dallas game, and Solak himself pointed to inconsistency in his routes and his hands. If a team offered a fourth-round pick for a depth receiver, the Eagles would at least have to think about it. Moving a depth piece for a mid-round pick is the kind of move that adds future value without hurting the core. Moving DeVonta Smith is not.

And think about the rest of the receiver room right now. Hollywood Brown is a veteran on a one-year deal, and he has been mentioned on some lists as a possible trade candidate himself. Darius Cooper is still learning. Elijah Moore and Britain Covey are role players, and Covey just hurt his knee. Makai Lemon looks like the real deal, but he is a rookie. If the Eagles moved both Smith and Wicks, the next quarterback would be throwing to a group with very little proven production. That is not how you set up a young quarterback, or a returning Jalen Hurts, for success.

And there is a contract piece to this. According to Heavy, Smith has two seasons left on the three-year extension he signed in April of 2024. Trading him would not wipe away all of his salary cap charges. Previously paid bonuses can still create dead money, and that brings us to the second big story this week.

On Monday, Glenn Erby of Eagles Wire reported that the Eagles are facing more than eighty-two million dollars in dead money this season. Dead money is cap space being charged for players who are no longer on the roster, or whose contracts were restructured in a way that pushed money into the future. Erby wrote, quote, the Eagles are not trapped, but their margin for error has narrowed. End quote.

For years, Howie Roseman has been one of the most creative cap managers in the NFL. Restructures, signing bonuses, voids and post-June first designations helped the Eagles keep championship rosters together. And it worked. Two Super Bowl titles since the 2017 season. But eventually, those future charges become today’s problem. Erby pointed out that teams are limited to two post-June first designations each year, which, in his words, delays the reckoning without eliminating it.

So what is the solution? According to Erby, it is the same one Philadelphia used after the Carson Wentz era. Draft well, get younger, rely on cheap contributors on rookie deals, and absorb the dead money. And remember how that worked last time. After the Wentz trade, the Eagles built around young players like Jalen Hurts, DeVonta Smith, Landon Dickerson and Jordan Mailata. Once the foundation was set, Roseman went out and traded for A.J. Brown and signed big-name veterans, and within two years the Eagles were in the Super Bowl.

In a second article on Tuesday, Erby wrote that the Eagles will need another draft-driven reset. But he also wrote, quote, the Eagles do not need a complete teardown. End quote. He listed the offensive line as the top priority, because Lane Johnson has retired and Landon Dickerson’s durability has become a concern. And he listed tight end, edge rusher and the secondary as other significant needs. On the bright side, he pointed to Jalen Carter, Quinyon Mitchell, Cooper DeJean, Nolan Smith and Jalyx Hunt as a young defensive foundation. The question is whether the Eagles can build something just as strong on offense.

And that lines up with what the Eagles are actually doing behind the scenes. According to the Philadelphia Inquirer’s trade deadline tracker, Jeff McLane reported on his podcast that Howie Roseman has been shopping for a starting-caliber offensive lineman. That is a real report, and it tells you the Eagles are not only thinking about selling. They are also trying to fix the offensive line right now. And remember, the Eagles have extra draft capital to work with after trading Cam Jurgens to Baltimore, including a 2028 second-round pick.

And it is not just DeVonta Smith whose name is floating around. Before Monday night’s game, Conor Orr of Sports Illustrated said on ESPN’s Bill Barnwell Show that the Eagles should unquestionably trade Saquon Barkley, and ESPN’s Ben Solak also floated a Saquon deal with the Houston Texans. But according to Yahoo Sports, a Saquon trade would leave about twenty-six point four million dollars in dead cap space next year, and now he is dealing with hamstring and back problems. That makes a Saquon trade even less likely. On the other side, Adam Schefter said on ESPN that it is not in Howie Roseman’s DNA to be a seller, and that Roseman can be a buyer and a seller at the same time. That fits with the McLane report about Roseman looking for an offensive lineman. The Eagles may sell some veterans and buy a piece for the future at the same time.

And the pressure on this front office is real. The Eagles lost to the Dallas Cowboys thirty-eight to twenty-one on Monday night, their fifth straight loss, and they are now two and five. The defense gave up two hundred four rushing yards, and Javonte Williams ran for one hundred fifty-five, the most a running back has ever gained against a Vic Fangio defense in Philadelphia, according to the Inquirer. Nick Sirianni’s job security has become a national conversation, although Kempski wrote that an in-season firing feels unlikely. All of that is why every trade idea, real or not, gets so much attention right now.

So when you put it all together, here is the honest picture of the trade deadline. The Eagles have not traded for a star wide receiver. DeVonta Smith is very unlikely to be traded, according to Kempski. The Eagles may look for offensive line help. And the players most likely to be moved, according to the Inquirer, are veterans like Dallas Goedert, Moro Ojomo, Tyler Steen and Riq Woolen, with names like Landon Dickerson and Nolan Smith also mentioned. The deadline is Tuesday, November tenth, at four in the afternoon Eastern.

Now, a quick look at Smith’s health, because that matters for Sunday. DeVonta Smith did not practice on Wednesday because of his hamstring. According to the Delaware News Journal, this is his third hamstring absence since training camp, and he has missed the last two games. Saquon Barkley also did not practice because of hamstring and back issues, though he says he expects to play. Wicks and Britain Covey also sat out. On Sunday night, the Eagles play at Washington, and the Commanders come in at one, four and one, rested after their bye week.

And Smith’s injury history is exactly why some people bring up trade ideas in the first place. When a receiver keeps missing time, fans start to wonder about the long-term plan. But I see it differently. A hamstring injury is frustrating, but it is not a reason to give away one of the best route runners in the league. Get him healthy, let him finish the season strong, and build the next version of this offense around him and Makai Lemon.

Sunday night is a big one, too. A win in Washington would snap the losing streak, move the Eagles to three and five, and set up a home game against the Giants with the deadline right around the corner. A loss would push the Eagles closer to selling. Tanner McKee and Makai Lemon showed real chemistry on Monday night, with Lemon catching seven passes for sixty-seven yards and his first NFL touchdown. If Smith can get back on the field soon, a Smith and Lemon combination could be the foundation of this receiver room for years, and that is exactly why I would not trade him.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides on the DeVonta Smith question.

On one side, the people who would listen to offers. They will say the Eagles are two and five, the season is slipping away, and a first-round pick is a lot of value for a receiver who has missed three games with hamstring problems. They will say the Eagles have more than eighty-two million dollars in dead money, and the smartest way out is to get younger and cheaper with more draft picks. They will say Roseman already traded A.J. Brown, so the team is clearly in a transition. And they will say no player should be untouchable when a team is in this position.

On the other side, the people who say keep him. They will say DeVonta Smith is a proven, polished receiver in his prime, and you do not trade players like that for a chance at finding someone like him. They will say the Eagles already moved A.J. Brown, and trading Smith too would leave the next quarterback with almost nothing proven to throw to. They will say trading him would not solve the dead money problem, because his past bonuses would still count. And they will say Kempski is right, Smith is a building block, not a trade chip.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I keep DeVonta Smith. Not close. Not even for a first-round pick. The Eagles do need to get younger and cheaper, and Roseman should absolutely sell some veterans on expiring deals if the next two games go badly. But you rebuild around players like Smith, not by trading them. He is the kind of receiver who makes a young quarterback better, and he is the perfect mentor for Makai Lemon. If the Chiefs want a receiver, they can call about someone else. And if I am Howie Roseman, my focus before November tenth is the offensive line, not shipping away my best receiver.

So let me wrap it up. The Eagles have not made a trade for a star wide receiver, and they have not traded DeVonta Smith.
