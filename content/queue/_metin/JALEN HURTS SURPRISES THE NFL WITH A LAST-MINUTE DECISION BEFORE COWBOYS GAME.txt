Let us start with Jalen, because he matters more than any football game.

Here is the timeline, and I want you to have it right. A superficial blood clot in his calf was detected the day before the London game against Jacksonville on October eleventh. Jalen was cleared to play, and he did not carry an injury designation. After the team flew home, the clot had moved, and he was diagnosed with blood clots in both lungs. He was placed on injured reserve, and the Eagles and his agent, Nicole Lynn, said in a joint statement that his prognosis is very good and that a full recovery is expected. He will stay around the team and keep conditioning while he is on blood thinners, and he is expected to miss the rest of the season.

Now, here is where the story got bigger this week. On Tuesday, the NFL Players Association called for a full investigation into the medical care Jalen received. According to theScore, the union said it was not aware of any NFL investigation and that the league had told it no review was planned. The league, on the other hand, has publicly backed the way the Eagles handled it. NFL executive Jeff Miller said Monday’s reporting contained considerable and uninformed speculation, and chief medical officer Allen Sills described the handling as very conservative, very patient-focused and appropriately guarded in every step. The league also confirmed that the Eagles will not face discipline.

So you have the league on one side, the union on the other, and a lot of national voices piling on. Mike Florio of Pro Football Talk wrote that criticism has come even from outlets with business ties to the league, including ESPN’s First Take, where Chris Russo said the Eagles did a horrendous job. And Mike Silver of The Athletic wrote that league medical officials knew about the first clot the night before the game.

Eagles Nation, I am not a doctor, and neither is anybody yelling about this on television. I am not going to tell you who made the right call and who made the wrong one, because we do not have all the facts, and Jalen’s medical details are his business. What I will tell you is this. When the union and the league disagree this publicly, the questions are not going away. They are going to follow this franchise for a while.

And what did the team say this week? On Thursday, Nick Sirianni was asked if he agrees with the union pushing for an investigation. According to Pro Football Talk, he said, quote, I’m not going to go into any of that, end quote. But he also said, quote, I care deeply about Jalen, end quote, and that Jalen’s wellbeing is first and foremost. And offensive coordinator Sean Mannion said on Wednesday that Jalen was around the team last week, and that the Eagles want him to keep being around and to be a positive impact while he gets himself healthy.

So when you see a headline about a last-minute Jalen Hurts decision, here is the honest version. The only thing Jalen is focused on right now is getting healthy. And the most powerful thing he can do for this team is exactly what Mannion described. Be in the building when he can, help Tanner McKee when he can, and take care of himself first. That is not a shocking decision. That is a leader doing what leaders do.

Now, let us talk about football, because there are real reasons to feel a little more hopeful going into Monday night.

The biggest one, without question, is Saquon Barkley. Saquon practiced on Thursday for the first time since he hurt his hamstring against the Rams in Week 4. He was limited, but he was out there, and Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice wrote that he is expected to play on Monday night. I want to be careful here. Limited is not full, and hamstrings can be tricky. But this is the best sign we have seen in weeks.

And you can see why the Eagles have been building up the backfield behind him. They brought Elijah Mitchell back to the practice squad this week. Some people looked at that and wondered if it meant the team expects Saquon back, or if it meant they were worried. My honest take is that a move like that does not confirm anything by itself. It is depth. What confirms Saquon’s status is how he practices on Friday and Saturday and what the team says over the weekend.

Now, let me also be honest about this. Getting Saquon back does not automatically fix the running game. The Eagles ran for only thirty-five yards against Carolina, and even when Saquon was healthy earlier this season, this offense was not moving the ball consistently. But having your best running back on the field gives you options you just do not have without him. He takes pressure off Tanner McKee. He forces linebackers to step up and respect the run. And he can turn a small crease into a forty-yard play in a heartbeat. This offense needs that kind of threat right now, because too many drives have needed everything to go perfectly just to get a first down.

Now, the offensive line. And this is the part I keep coming back to.

Against Carolina, the protection was not good enough. According to the Philadelphia Inquirer’s grades, Tanner McKee was sacked twice when blitzers came through completely unblocked, and he was hit or hurried on three straight drops during one bad drive early in the game. The Inquirer pointed out that it was not all on the linemen, because blitz pickup involves backs and tight ends too. But the result was the same. A quarterback making his first start had defenders in his face way too often.

There were changes up front, too. Jake Majors started at center in place of the injured Drew Kendall, and Majors had a bad snap in the fourth quarter. Rookie right tackle Markel Bell had some ups and downs. Willie Lampkin held his ground at left guard but got pushed back at times, and he missed practice on Thursday with an illness. And Landon Dickerson was limited on Thursday with knee and pectoral issues. The Eagles even signed Evan Neal to the practice squad for more depth.

Here is my take. Getting healthier helps, but it does not fix missed assignments by itself. Every lineman needs to know exactly who he is blocking before the ball is snapped. Dallas is not going to take it easy on this group because somebody is coming off an injury. If the Eagles leave a defender untouched or fail to recognize pressure, Tanner McKee is the one who pays for it. I want to see a clear protection plan that this group can actually execute, especially on third downs when the whole stadium knows Philadelphia has to throw.

Now let us flip to the defense, because the injury news there is encouraging too.

Jalyx Hunt is back on the practice field. Remember, Hunt hurt his knee and ankle on the last play of the first half in London. He said he heard and felt a pop, and he told 94WIP, quote, I thought I was done for, man, end quote. Vic Fangio called it scary looking. But Hunt also said the injury was not as bad as they first feared, and he credited his flexibility for possibly saving him from something much worse. On Thursday, he was a limited participant. And before he got hurt, Hunt led the Eagles with sixteen pressures. If he is ready, he gives Fangio another way to get after Dak Prescott without asking the secondary to cover forever.

Then there is Jonathan Greenard, who has been working back from an ankle injury and was limited on Thursday. With Greenard, the question is not just whether he plays. It is how effective he can be. Being available and playing your best football are not always the same thing. I want to see him moving comfortably, finishing his rushes, and giving this defense the disruption it has been missing. If Greenard keeps improving and Hunt can go, Fangio suddenly has real options to rotate his pass rushers and keep them fresh.

And that matters, Eagles Nation, because this defense has spent far too much time on the field. When the offense keeps stalling, the defenders barely get a breather before they are back out there. Eventually that shows up in the pass rush, in the tackling, and in what Fangio can call. Yes, the defense has to force its own stops. But the offense has to help. Even one long drive that flips the field and gives those guys a few minutes on the sideline can change the fourth quarter.

Which brings me to a concern we cannot ignore. Cooper DeJean’s tackling.

According to the Philadelphia Inquirer, DeJean leads the entire NFL with twelve missed tackles through six games. In his first two seasons, he averaged eleven per season, so he is already past that pace. He had three misses against Carolina alone. Vic Fangio said DeJean suffered a stinger when he left the Rams game in Week 4, and that it has, quote, affected him a little bit, end quote. Fangio admitted there have been, quote, a few more than usual, end quote, but he also said, quote, Cooper’s playing good overall, end quote.

And I love how DeJean handled it himself. He did not hide behind the injury, and he said the position change is not really an excuse to use. That is the mindset you want.

Let me be clear. I do not think Cooper DeJean has suddenly become a bad player. We know exactly how good he can be. That is why the standard is so high. And with Quinyon Mitchell playing like one of the best corners in the league, expectations for this young secondary are sky high. Against Dallas, the Cowboys are going to try to get the ball out quickly and make defenders tackle in space. A short completion cannot keep turning into a big play because the first tackle does not get made. Take good angles, wrap up, finish. That is the whole ballgame on the back end.

Now let us go back to the offense, and to Sean Mannion.

We keep hearing about fundamentals, communication and execution. And all of that is true. But those improvements have to show up on the field. When the line is not working together, or a pressure look creates confusion, the quarterback barely gets a chance. Then you are taking sacks, losing yards, and facing third and long, which is exactly where a defense wants you.

What worries me just as much is how hard it has been to create explosive plays. Against Carolina, the biggest gains were a nineteen-yard touchdown to DeVonta Smith, an eighteen-yard catch by Dallas Goedert, a seventeen-yard catch by Dontayvion Wicks on third down, and a sixteen-yard play-action throw to Smith. That is it. When the running game is not producing and the passing game is not stretching the field, you are asking the offense to string together twelve clean plays to score. That is a tough way to live, especially with protection issues.

And now the health of the receivers and tight ends matters too. DeVonta Smith did not practice on Thursday because of his hamstring, and Dallas Goedert did not practice with a knee issue. Neither has been labeled a setback, so I am not calling it one. But if either of them is limited on Monday night, the offense gets even thinner.

So here is what I want from Mannion. I am not saying throw out the offense in the middle of the season. I am saying if certain formations or protection calls keep putting your quarterback in trouble, adjust them. Use more shotgun if it helps McKee see the field. Give him quick answers when the pressure comes. Better execution matters, but the coaches have a responsibility to make those assignments manageable, too. And as Sirianni said this week, it is always players over plays.

Now, the quarterback conversation.

I do not believe Tanner McKee is the biggest reason this offense has struggled. In his first start, he went twenty-six of forty-five for two hundred fifteen yards, a touchdown and no interceptions. The Inquirer gave him a C, and said he did better than expected given everything around him. McKee still has to make good decisions and deliver the ball on time. Nobody gets a free pass. But you cannot fairly judge a quarterback who is constantly dealing with defenders in his face. Give him a cleaner pocket and a real running game, and then let us see what this offense looks like.

Of course, when the offense struggles, the backup becomes the most popular guy in town. Now that Joe Milton the third is in Philadelphia, I completely understand why fans are curious about that big arm. But I am not assuming a change is coming before Dallas, and nothing the team has said points to one. Sirianni said this week that McKee is highly intelligent, tough, and has a tendency to reach his ceiling. My preference is to give McKee a fair shot with better protection and healthier weapons. If things keep going badly, the staff will have to evaluate its options. But switching quarterbacks without fixing the problems around them could just mean watching the same mistakes with somebody else taking the snaps.

Now let us talk about the trade deadline, because that conversation is heating up, and I want to separate what is real from what is just talk.

One name a lot of people keep bringing up is Hollywood Brown. Against Carolina, he was the Eagles’ only healthy inactive receiver, with Elijah Moore active ahead of him, according to the Inquirer. Philadelphia desperately needs explosive plays, so I want to understand how Brown fits into the plan. If the Eagles believe he can help, find ways to get him involved. If they do not see a role for him, then listening to offers makes sense.

A to Z Sports put together a list of veteran trade candidates, including Brown, Dallas Goedert, Moro Ojomo, Riq Woolen and Marcus Epps, and the outlet listed the trade deadline as November tenth. But here is the important part. That list is opinion and analysis. There are no reports of actual trade talks for any of those players. There is a huge difference between a move that sounds reasonable on paper and a trade the Eagles are actually preparing to make.

And the same caution applies to names like Nolan Smith or, even more so, Saquon Barkley. I would not start selling important pieces just because the first six games have been frustrating. The Eagles are two and four, but so are the Cowboys, and the entire NFC East is bunched together. There is still time to turn this around. Howie Roseman needs an honest read on where this team stands before making decisions that affect the next few seasons, but I do not want anybody treating trade speculation as if Roseman has already decided to break up the roster.

Now, quickly, the Cowboys. Dallas is two and four and has lost two straight. Dak Prescott has thrown four interceptions in his last two games. Returner KaVontae Turpin did not practice Thursday and is not expected to play, according to Kempski. Linebacker Dee Winters did not practice. George Pickens, Tyler Guyton and Cobie Durant were limited, DeMarvion Overshown was a full participant, and safety Jalen Thompson is on injured reserve. So Dallas is banged up too. And with Dak throwing picks, Vic Fangio’s disguised coverages could be a real problem for him on Monday night.

On one side, the worried fans. McKee gets a full week of preparation as the starter. And they will say nobody in the NFC East is running away with anything.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I think Monday night is the most important game of the Eagles’ season so far. Not because it decides the playoffs in October, but because it decides the direction. If Saquon plays, the line gives McKee a clean pocket, and the pass rush with Hunt and Greenard makes Dak uncomfortable, I believe the Eagles win this game. And if they win, all of that trade deadline talk starts to quiet down. As for Jalen, I just want him healthy. Everything else can wait.
