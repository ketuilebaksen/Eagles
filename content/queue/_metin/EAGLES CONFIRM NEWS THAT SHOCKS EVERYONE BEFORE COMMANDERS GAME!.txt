Eagles Nation, one of the Eagles’ starters just broke his silence on the trade rumors swirling around Philadelphia, and what he said is not what a lot of people expected. On Monday night, after the loss to Dallas, he would not say a word. He declined to answer questions in the locker room. But on Wednesday, he stood in front of the cameras and made his position very clear. And at the same time, ESPN put a number on his chances of being traded that nobody in Philadelphia wanted to see.

That player is cornerback Riq Woolen. And today, we are going to break down everything. What Woolen said, what ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported, why his contract makes him one of the Eagles’ most tradable players, and what it all means with the trade deadline less than two weeks away. And yes, another Eagles starter showed up on that same ESPN list, with a comment from Fowler that should get your attention too.

This is James with Second Floor, and before we start, hit that like button and subscribe, because we are tracking every trade rumor and every real move all the way to the November tenth deadline.

Let me start with who Riq Woolen is, because his story is important here.

Woolen came to Philadelphia in March on a one-year deal, just a few weeks after winning the Super Bowl with the Seattle Seahawks. According to Ed Kracz of Eagles On SI, he was hoping to do what LeGarrette Blount and Chris Long did. Those two won the Super Bowl with the Patriots in the 2016 season, came to Philadelphia, and won it all again with the Eagles the very next year. That was the dream. Back-to-back rings with two different teams. And honestly, it was not a crazy dream. The Eagles were coming off a championship in the 2024 season and expected to contend again.

But this season has not gone that way. The Eagles are two and five, and according to Kracz, Woolen has been on the field for ninety-nine percent of the defensive snaps. He is a ball hawk by reputation. He had at least one interception in each of his first four seasons in the league, including six as a rookie, which Kracz noted is a Seahawks franchise record. This year, he has zero. And on Monday night against Dallas, he had one of his toughest games as an Eagle. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia’s grades, he gave up a career-high one hundred eleven yards. That was the game he declined to talk about.

And Woolen’s path to the NFL makes his story even more interesting. He was a fifth-round pick out of UTSA in 2022, and he was not supposed to be a star right away. Then he ran a four point two six forty-yard dash at the combine, showed up in Seattle, and made the Pro Bowl as a rookie with those six interceptions. Few players his size can move like that. That is why teams still talk about him. Even in a down year, the physical tools are rare, and contenders looking for a corner who can match up with big receivers will always pick up the phone.

And think about the Blount and Long comparison for a second, because it shows you how much this season has changed. In 2017, LeGarrette Blount ran for over seven hundred yards and scored a huge touchdown in Super Bowl fifty-two. Chris Long was a key piece of the pass rush and the locker room, and he donated his game checks to charity. Those two players came to Philadelphia for one reason, to win again, and they did. Woolen came here with the same goal. Instead, he is sitting on a two and five team, with his name on trade lists. That is a tough place for any player to be.

Now, let us get to the number. On Wednesday, ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler and Matt Bowen released their ranking of the top twenty-five trade candidates in the league. And Riq Woolen was number eight on that list, with a predicted thirty-five percent chance of being traded. That is not a majority. But it is roughly one chance in three that he is wearing a different jersey after November tenth.

And Fowler added a detail that tells you this is not just media speculation. He wrote that when rival teams discuss which Eagles could move, Woolen’s name, quote, comes up fairly often. End quote. He noted that Woolen is a 2027 free agent and that his one point two six million dollar base salary could help make a trade work. ESPN listed the Lions, Panthers and Steelers as possible fits. And Matt Bowen described Woolen as a, quote, disruptor on the perimeter, end quote, with six-foot-four length, twelve interceptions and fifty-seven pass breakups over five seasons, while noting his tackling and eye discipline could improve.

So, on Wednesday, Woolen finally spoke. And according to the Philadelphia Inquirer and NJ.com, here is what he said.

On the rumors, his first answer was a message. Quote, you hear things, but nobody knows the truth. End quote. He said people on social media and in the news can say a million things, but nobody really knows until you talk to the general manager and the head coach. Think about that. He did not talk about any other team. He pointed straight at the two people who make the decision. Howie Roseman and Nick Sirianni.

Then he said, quote, you can see me going to any team or you can hear anything about me going to a team, but to me, that doesn’t matter to me. End quote. He said what matters is that he is here every day working with his teammates.

And he explained that he has been through this before. When his name came up in trade reports in Seattle, he said he kept showing up and working because his teammates needed him. And Seattle never traded him. He finished the season and won the Super Bowl.

If you are new to Second Floor, welcome. Comment one if you want Riq Woolen in Philadelphia through the last game of the season. Comment two if you think the Eagles should trade him right now for a draft pick. I am reading every single one.

And then came the strongest part. Woolen said, quote, I’m right here in Philly, and it’s a place I want to be. End quote. He talked about working hard with his teammates all offseason and riding with them through the season no matter what the record is. And when asked about players on losing teams who might want out, he said, quote, that’s not the way I roll. I gotta be where I’m at. End quote.

Eagles Nation, you have to respect that. A player in the final year of his deal, on a two and five team, with his name all over trade lists, and he looks into the camera and says Philadelphia is where he wants to be. That is a professional. In a season where so much has gone wrong, hearing a veteran commit to his teammates like that means something.

But here is the hard truth. That decision is not his. As Woolen himself said, it belongs to Howie Roseman and Nick Sirianni.

Now, let us talk about the money, because that explains why his name keeps showing up. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, citing Over The Cap, Woolen signed a one-year, twelve million dollar deal, fully guaranteed, with a six million dollar signing bonus. The Eagles added four void years to keep his 2026 cap hit low, at about three point four million dollars. But that means there is about eight point six million dollars of dead money waiting in 2027. And here is the key detail. That charge is coming whether he stays or whether he is traded, because he is set to become a free agent after this season.

So think about it from Howie Roseman’s perspective. If Woolen is likely to leave in free agency anyway, and the dead money is coming either way, then a trade gives the Eagles a chance to get something back, like a draft pick, instead of watching him walk for nothing. That is exactly why Woolen is considered one of the Eagles’ most realistic trade chips.

But there is also a reason his trade value might not be huge. Zero interceptions this season, a rough game against Dallas, and only a few months left on his contract. Any team trading for him would only get him for ten games, plus a possible playoff run. In my view, the realistic return for a player in that situation is probably somewhere in the middle or late rounds of the draft, not a first or second-round pick.

So who could actually call? ESPN named three fits. The Detroit Lions, the Carolina Panthers and the Pittsburgh Steelers. Think about why those teams make sense. Contenders and teams pushing for the playoffs always look for help in the secondary at the deadline, especially a long, fast corner who can match up with bigger receivers. For a team chasing a playoff spot, Woolen’s low base salary of about one point two six million dollars makes him easy to fit under the cap. For the Eagles, the question is simple. Does any of those teams offer enough to make it worth losing a starter in the middle of a season where the NFC East is still wide open?

And Woolen made it clear how he plans to handle the next two weeks. He said he is going to focus on being with his team, being a great player, getting better every game, and being someone his teammates can rely on while he is here. In other words, he is going to stay where his feet are and let the chips fall where they may. That is the right mindset. If he plays well on Sunday night, he helps the Eagles win, and he raises his own value at the same time. Either way, the Eagles benefit.

Now, Woolen was not the only Eagle on ESPN’s list, and this next one is a bigger surprise. Landon Dickerson was number twelve, with a twenty-five percent chance of being traded. And Fowler wrote, quote, something feels off between the Eagles and Dickerson. End quote. According to ESPN, Fowler said Dickerson recently made a passive-aggressive remark about how the front office sees his long-term future, and noted that his guarantees run out next year. ESPN listed the Chargers and Packers as possible fits. Bowen pointed out that Dickerson has a ninety-three point nine percent pass block win rate over five seasons with the Eagles.

Eagles Nation, that one is interesting. Dickerson just returned from injured reserve against Dallas, and he said after the game, quote, nobody’s quitting, and that’s what you want to see. End quote. But if Fowler is right that something feels off, that could become a real story before November tenth. Dallas Goedert and Hollywood Brown were also listed as honorable mentions on ESPN’s list. And according to the Inquirer’s deadline tracker, Jeff McLane reported that Howie Roseman has been shopping for a starting-caliber offensive lineman, which would make even more sense if Dickerson is moved. And remember, Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice wrote this week that DeVonta Smith isn’t going anywhere.

And it is worth remembering how Howie Roseman has handled the trade deadline before. Roseman is never afraid to make a move. In 2023, he traded for safety Kevin Byard in the middle of the season. This year, he already traded Cam Jurgens to Baltimore for a 2028 second-round pick after Lane Johnson’s retirement. Adam Schefter said on ESPN that it is not in Roseman’s DNA to be a seller, and that he can be a buyer and a seller at the same time. So a Woolen trade would not necessarily mean the Eagles are giving up. It could simply mean Roseman is turning a player on an expiring deal into future value, while looking to add an offensive lineman for the long term.

And there is more going on around this team than just trade rumors. Jalen Hurts is out for the season while he recovers from blood clots, and NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport has said a decision on his contract will likely be one of the stories of the offseason. Jalen Carter called his own season trash this week. And Nick Sirianni’s job security has become a national conversation, although Kempski wrote that an in-season firing feels unlikely. In the middle of all that, a veteran saying he wants to be here and wants to ride with his teammates is exactly the kind of voice this locker room needs.

Now, if Woolen does go, what does the cornerback room look like? Quinyon Mitchell, who is one of the best young corners in football, and Cooper DeJean, who can play all over the secondary. Jonathan Jones and Jakorian Bennett add depth, though Jones missed practice on Wednesday and Thursday with a hamstring injury. Pulling a six-foot-four starter out of the lineup in the middle of the season is never simple. But the Eagles have built their future in the secondary around Mitchell and DeJean, not around a player on a one-year deal.

And this all ties into Sunday night. The Eagles play the Commanders in Washington on Sunday Night Football. Washington is one, four and one and coming off a bye. Quarterback Jayden Daniels was a full participant in practice on Thursday, and so was Stefon Diggs. Every snap Woolen plays on Sunday will be watched closely, by Eagles fans and by every team thinking about calling Howie Roseman. Scouts will be watching how he tackles, how he tracks the ball and how he handles Washington’s receivers.

On the Eagles’ side, Saquon Barkley, DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks all missed practice on Wednesday and Thursday. Dameon Pierce went on injured reserve, and Jaydon Blue was promoted to the active roster. So this offense is still banged up. The defense has to step up after giving up two hundred four rushing yards to Dallas. And a big game from Woolen on Sunday night would help the Eagles and help his trade value at the same time.

Let me also say this. The defense needs to rediscover its identity. Vic Fangio’s units have been the backbone of this team, but against Dallas, the Eagles had zero sacks and zero quarterback hits, and Javonte Williams ran for one hundred fifty-five yards. Jalen Carter called his own season trash this week. If the defense plays with the kind of pride Woolen talked about, Sunday night can look very different.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides on Riq Woolen.

On one side, the people who say trade him. They will say he is on a one-year deal, he is likely to leave in free agency, and the dead money is coming in 2027 no matter what. They will say the Eagles are two and five, the season is slipping away, and getting a draft pick back is better than getting nothing. They will say his zero interceptions and his rough night against Dallas show he has not been the same player in Philadelphia. And they will say that with Quinyon Mitchell and Cooper DeJean, the future of the secondary is already set.

On the other side, the people who say keep him. They will say Woolen just told everyone he wants to be in Philadelphia and is riding with this team no matter what. They will say he is a Super Bowl champion and a leader in that locker room, and trading him would send the wrong message to the players who are still fighting. They will say the return would probably only be a mid or late-round pick, which is not worth weakening the defense in the middle of a season where the division is still wide open. And they will say if he plays well the rest of the way, the Eagles could even try to re-sign him.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I respect the heck out of what Riq Woolen said. That is exactly the attitude you want. But I also think business is business. If Howie Roseman gets a solid offer, something in the fourth round or better, I think he has to take it, especially if the Eagles lose on Sunday night. Woolen is on a one-year deal, the dead money is coming either way, and the Eagles’ long-term secondary is Quinyon Mitchell and Cooper DeJean. But if the offers are only late-round picks, I keep him, let him finish the season with his teammates, and see if a re-signing makes sense. The next two games will decide a lot. Washington on Sunday night, then the Giants. Win both, and maybe the Eagles keep everyone. Lose both, and the phones in Howie Roseman’s office start ringing a lot louder.

So let me wrap it up. Riq Woolen broke his silence on Wednesday. He said, nobody knows the truth, that it does not matter to him what people say, and that Philly is a place he wants to be. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler gave him a thirty-five percent chance of being traded and said his name comes up fairly often with rival teams. His contract leaves about eight point six million dollars of dead money in 2027 either way. Fowler also gave Landon Dickerson a twenty-five percent chance, writing that something feels off between him and the Eagles.
