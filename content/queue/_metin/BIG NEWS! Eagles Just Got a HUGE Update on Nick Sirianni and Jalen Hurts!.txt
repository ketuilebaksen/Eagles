Let me start with the Sirianni update.

According to Heavy and The Spun, NFL Network insider Mike Garafolo went on The Anthony Gargano Show and said, quote, everything I’m hearing, and you’re hearing, is that he should be safe. End quote. According to Heavy, that was published on November fourteenth.

Now, let me be careful about what that means. Garafolo is reporting what he is hearing. It is not an official statement from the Eagles, and nothing has been finalized. Sirianni still has eight games left, and a lot can change. According to The Spun, a finish of four and thirteen or worse could increase the pressure on him. But right now, the strongest signal from inside the league is that Sirianni is not in immediate danger.

And Garafolo made another point that I think a lot of fans are missing. According to The Spun, he said, quote, some of these hotter offensive minds right now are not play-callers. End quote. That is a big deal. A lot of fans have been talking about young offensive coordinators like Grant Udinski as possible replacements. But according to PhillyVoice, the main concern with Udinski is exactly that. Liam Coen calls the plays in Jacksonville, not Udinski. So if the Eagles ever did make a change, the hot names might not be the plug-and-play answer fans think they are.

And here is the case for Sirianni. According to Heavy, he has a winning percentage of sixty-four point nine percent, two NFC Championship wins and a Super Bowl title within five years. And Jason Kelce said on 94 WIP, according to The Spun, quote, okay, this guy can clearly get it done. End quote.

But there is the other side too. According to Heavy, since last year’s bye week, including the one playoff game, the Eagles are seven and twelve. And Kelce also said, quote, this season, though, is not going well. End quote. According to Heavy, the Eagles changed offensive coordinators, but the system has not improved.

And the Heavy writer brought up an interesting comparison. Andy Reid struggled in his final days in Philadelphia, and he went on to become one of the best head coaches in NFL history in Kansas City. The point is that a bad stretch does not always mean a coach has lost it. Sometimes the situation around him is the problem. Sometimes the fit is the problem. And sometimes, a team that moves on too fast ends up regretting it for years. According to Heavy, the writer’s view is that Sirianni will likely finish the season as the head coach, though the final stretch could still change things.

And remember how much this locker room has been through. When Hurts went down, according to NFL.com, receiver Britain Covey said, quote, I feel bad for him. End quote. Losing your quarterback, your right tackle, your center and one of your captains in the same season would test any team. How these players respond over the final eight games will say a lot about the culture Sirianni has built, and that is exactly the kind of thing Jason Kelce said should decide his future.

Now, here is the debate that is splitting Eagles fans. Some people want to move on from Jalen Hurts because of his health and his contract. Others look at what has happened since he went down and say the coach should be the one under pressure.

Let me tell you where I stand, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I am closer to the second group. Not because Sirianni has done everything wrong, but because of what the offense has looked like without Hurts. It was already struggling before he went out. And since he went out, it has only gotten harder to watch. According to Athlon Sports, the Eagles’ running game ranks in the bottom five in the NFL, with seven hundred ninety-seven yards and three point seven yards per carry. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the offense ranks thirty-second in yards per game and thirtieth in points per game.

And here is the point that I keep coming back to. I am not expecting Tanner McKee or Joe Milton to walk in and give the Eagles everything Hurts gives them. But when your starting quarterback is out, the coaching staff has to adjust. Simplify the reads. Protect the passer. Find easy throws. Build a plan that gives the backups a chance. Every team deals with injuries. Good coaching staffs do not just throw up their hands when an important player goes down. And when a team looks functional with its franchise quarterback and completely lost without him, maybe the quarterback was covering up more problems than people gave him credit for.

And look at how this started. According to Glenn Erby of Eagles Wire, in the first game without Hurts back in October, Tanner McKee needed forty-five pass attempts to throw for two hundred fifteen yards. The Eagles ran for just thirty-five yards on thirteen carries, and they committed eleven penalties for eighty-four yards. According to NFL.com, Sirianni said afterward, quote, we’re looking for answers, and we’re looking for solutions. End quote. And he said the play-calling responsibilities would stay the same for now. Weeks later, the Eagles are still looking for those answers.

That is why I am not ready to say moving on from Hurts would fix anything. If the problem is the system, a new quarterback walks into the same system.

Now, let me talk about Hurts himself, and I want to do it the right way.

Back in October, according to The Athletic, as published by NFL.com, the Eagles and Hurts’s agent Nicole Lynn released a joint statement after tests in Philadelphia found deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary emboli. According to that statement, Lynn said, quote, we expect a full recovery. End quote. The statement also said, quote, we anticipate that he will have no long-term effects. End quote. And it said they anticipate he will be able to resume contact football activities when he is off blood thinners. According to Eagles Wire, the statement said his prognosis is very good, and that he has no significant functional limitations.

That is all I am going to say about his health, because that is what the team and his agent have said. There is no return date, and nobody should be guessing at one. His recovery comes first. I care a lot more about Jalen Hurts being healthy than about winning an argument on the internet.

And Nick Sirianni has made it clear how he feels. According to NFL.com, he said, quote, I miss him. End quote. And, quote, I just deeply care about him and hope that he continues to improve with what he’s going through. End quote. And owner Jeffrey Lurie said, quote, I love everything about him. End quote.

Now, the contract. According to NFL.com, citing Over The Cap, Hurts is in the third season of a five-year, two hundred fifty-five million dollar extension. His guarantees drop to twenty-two million dollars after this season, and he has no guaranteed money after 2027. He also has a no-trade clause, which means any trade would need his approval. And according to NFL.com, moving on from him would carry big salary-cap consequences.

So when people say the Eagles should just move on from Hurts, it is not that simple. He controls where he could go. The cap hit would be significant. And he is still a proven winner who, if healthy, gives this team its best chance.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. If Hurts recovers and wants to stay, I do not understand why the Eagles would move on from a proven winner. Starting over sounds easy until you are asking a rookie or a new veteran to rescue an offense that still has not solved its own problems. Fix the system first. Then decide on the quarterback.

Now, let me get to the moves the Eagles made on Monday, because some of them are about fixing exactly what we just talked about.

First, a coaching move. According to Athlon Sports, citing ESPN’s Adam Schefter, the Eagles announced they have, quote, brought back assistant coach TJ Paganetti in a consulting role. End quote. According to Athlon, Paganetti spent ten seasons with the Eagles, including both Super Bowl wins. In 2024, he was the run game specialist and assistant offensive line coach, and that season the Eagles set a franchise record with three thousand eight hundred sixty-six rushing yards. He left after 2024 to become the run game coordinator for the New Orleans Saints, and according to Athlon, the Saints fired him earlier in November.

Now, the exact details of his role have not been made public. But think about why this matters. The running game is in the bottom five in the league right now. And the Eagles just brought back a coach who was a big part of the best rushing season in franchise history. That is not a coincidence. That is the self-scout in action.

And this is just my opinion, not a report. I love this move. It is not a firing, and it is not a big shake-up. But it is a real attempt to fix the most broken part of this offense, with someone who already knows the players, the building and the system that worked in 2024.

Second, Zach Ertz. According to ClutchPoints, the Eagles signed Ertz from the practice squad to the active roster on Monday. He is thirty-six years old and second in franchise history with five hundred eighty-two catches. And according to Ed Kracz of Eagles On SI, he is just six catches short of Harold Carmichael’s franchise record of five hundred eighty-nine. Now that he is on the fifty-three-man roster, he has a real chance to chase it over the final eight games.

Third, Will Shipley. According to Heavy, citing NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport, Shipley’s twenty-one-day practice window has opened. According to Fantasy Footballers, he was placed on injured reserve on October sixteenth with a partial tear of the plantar fascia in his foot. According to Heavy, before the injury, he had moved up to second string in Week 4 and started by Week 6, and he scored his only touchdown of the season against Jacksonville. Opening the window does not mean he will play on Sunday. There is no timeline. But it is a step, and Saquon Barkley could use the help.

And there were more moves. According to ClutchPoints, the Eagles also signed offensive tackle John Ojukwu from the practice squad. They added running back Jaydon Blue, linebacker Chance Campbell and tight end Hayden Rucci to the practice squad. And they released defensive back Shaun Wade. So Jaydon Blue, who was waived last week, is back in the building on the practice squad.

And there is one more name to watch. According to Yahoo Sports, citing Zach Berman of The Athletic, the Eagles brought in defensive tackle Leki Fotu for a tryout on Monday. According to that report, Fotu is twenty-eight years old and has played sixty-six games with twenty-six starts for five teams. Berman described him as an experienced nose tackle, which is exactly the position the Eagles need after trading Jordan Davis to Jacksonville.

And Fotu has been productive when he has played. According to Yahoo Sports, he has recorded at least one sack in four NFL seasons since 2020, and his last full season was in 2022 with the Arizona Cardinals, when he had thirty-one combined tackles. He most recently played for the Houston Texans and the Las Vegas Raiders. He is not Jordan Davis. But an experienced run-stopper could help Moro Ojomo, Jalen Carter, Ty Robinson and Byron Young hold up against a physical Steelers team.

And back to Shipley for a second. According to Heavy, before he got hurt, he had twenty carries for fifty-seven yards. With Saquon healthy, Shipley is not expected to take over the starting job. But he can give Barkley a breather, and he can help on special teams and in the passing game. And remember, right now Saquon is the only running back on the active roster. Getting even one more healthy back would make a big difference.

Now, let me connect the roster moves to the bigger picture, because there is a clear plan here.

Last week, the Eagles moved on from Hollywood Brown. According to ESPN sources, as reported by Yardbarker, Brown asked for his release, and the Eagles pointed to the expected returns of DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks and the improved play of Darius Cooper and Makai Lemon. Brown signed a one-year deal in March, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, but he played in only four of nine games and caught five passes for sixty-three yards. Sometimes moving on makes sense for both sides, even if it means admitting the signing did not work.

The Eagles also released veteran tackle Fred Johnson, according to Pro Football Talk. Some fans worried about depth, and that is fair. Offensive line injuries happen. But according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, rookie third-round pick Markel Bell has taken over at right tackle since Lane Johnson retired. And now John Ojukwu has been promoted to the active roster. The Eagles need to find out which young linemen can handle a role going forward.

And then there is the Jordan Davis trade. According to the team, the Jaguars sent a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, The Athletic gave the Eagles an A plus, and ESPN, CBS Sports and NFL.com all gave them an A. And according to ClutchPoints, citing ESPN’s Rapoport and Schefter, the 49ers were among the teams that asked about Dallas Goedert, but the Eagles kept him, and he could even re-sign for 2027.

So put it all together. Moving on from veterans who are not part of the future. Keeping veterans who are. Collecting draft picks. Giving young players snaps. And now bringing back a coach to fix the running game. That is not a team giving up. That is a team trying to figure out what the next version of the Eagles looks like.

And here is where the Eagles stand in the division. Going into the weekend, the Giants led the NFC East at five, three and one, which put the Eagles three and a half games back. So the division is basically gone, but the Eagles still play the Cowboys on Thanksgiving and the Giants to close the season. Those games still matter, if only for pride.

Now, the Steelers. According to the Inquirer, the Eagles host Pittsburgh on Sunday, November twenty-second, at one in the afternoon Eastern on CBS. The Steelers are coming off a thirty-eight to eighteen loss to the Bengals on Sunday Night Football, and they are six and three. According to Ed Kracz, Nick Sirianni is five and zero coming out of bye weeks in his career, including a thirty-five to thirteen win over the Steelers in 2022. And according to Heavy, after the Steelers, the Eagles face the Cowboys on Thanksgiving on a short week.

Here is what I will be watching. First, whether the running game looks different with TJ Paganetti back in the building. Second, whether DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks are active. Third, whether Will Shipley practices this week and gets activated. Fourth, whether Zach Ertz gets closer to the franchise record. And fifth, whether Sirianni can extend his perfect record after the bye, because a win would make Garafolo’s report look even stronger.
