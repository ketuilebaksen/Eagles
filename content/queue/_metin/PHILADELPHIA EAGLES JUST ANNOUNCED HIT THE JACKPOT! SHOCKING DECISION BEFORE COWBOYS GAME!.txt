Eagles Nation, it is game day. Tonight the Eagles host the Dallas Cowboys on Monday Night Football, but there is a conversation happening on national platforms that every Eagles fan needs to hear before kickoff. On ESPN’s Bill Barnwell Show, Barnwell and Conor Orr of Sports Illustrated were asked a simple question. Should the Eagles trade Saquon Barkley? And Orr’s answer was immediate. Quote, I think the answer is unquestionably yes. End quote. And Barnwell said, quote, okay, I’m in. End quote.

Now, before we go any further, let me be clear about something. The Eagles have not made any decision to trade Saquon Barkley. There is no report that the Eagles are shopping him, and no report of actual trade talks. What we have is two respected national analysts saying the Eagles should consider it, and a front office sitting on a real jackpot of future draft picks. And that combination is exactly why this conversation is not going away.

This is James with Second Floor, and today we are going to break it all down. What Barnwell and Orr said, why they think the Eagles should lean into the future, what the Eagles’ draft pick jackpot actually looks like, why Saquon’s contract makes this so complicated, and what all of it means for tonight’s game against Dallas. Before we start, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor, because we are covering this team from every angle, the good and the hard.

Let me set the scene first, because this conversation does not happen in a vacuum.

The Eagles are two and four, and they have lost four straight. Jalen Hurts is out for the rest of the season after being diagnosed with blood clots in his leg and both lungs. The team and his agent have said his prognosis is very good and that a full recovery is expected. But a lot has changed in a short amount of time. On October sixth, Lane Johnson announced his retirement after fourteen seasons. Johnson is one of the greatest right tackles of his generation, a cornerstone of two Super Bowl championship teams. And the very next day, the Eagles traded two-time Pro Bowl center and guard Cam Jurgens to the Baltimore Ravens.

Here are the details of that trade, according to FOX 29. The Eagles sent Jurgens and a 2027 seventh-round pick to Baltimore. In return, they got a 2027 fifth-round pick and a 2028 second-round pick. And FOX 29 reported that after that deal, the Eagles have sixteen draft picks over the next two years, before compensatory picks. That includes two first-round picks and two second-round picks in 2028.

Eagles Nation, that is the jackpot. Howie Roseman is sitting on one of the biggest piles of future draft capital in the league. And that is exactly what Barnwell and Orr were talking about.

So let us get into what they said.

Barnwell opened by pointing out that Howie Roseman is usually the general manager on the phone at this time of year, trying to add every veteran he can for a playoff push. But Barnwell said that is a place he does not project the Eagles to get to this year. And then he asked Orr directly if the Eagles should be trading away veterans who are not likely to be on the roster in the years to come.

Orr said yes, and he explained why. Not just because of this season, but because of the near future. He said, quote, the bill’s coming due for the championship years. End quote. He talked about the cap situation, and said it is not truly a mess if you have a good front office that can manage it. But he said what the Eagles need now are cost-controlled contracts. Young players on rookie deals. And he said the Eagles were, quote, telling on themselves a little bit, end quote, with the Jurgens trade, by breaking in younger players on the offensive line. He predicted that will be the theme at other positions for the rest of the season. And he said, quote, I think Howie’s going to be obsessed with accumulating draft capital. End quote.

And Orr also said something to Eagles fans that I want to repeat, because it is a fair point. He reminded everyone that the Eagles have been to three Super Bowls since the 2017 season. That is an incredible run. And he said fans may need to accept, in his words, a headache year or two while the team retools.

Barnwell agreed and added more context. He said the Eagles have young stars who will need new contracts soon. Quinyon Mitchell will be eligible for an extension after this season. So will Cooper DeJean. The Eagles have already extended Jalen Carter and Jordan Davis. Barnwell pointed out that during the championship run, the Eagles spent heavily on offense. He said they had one of the most expensive offenses in league history in terms of cash spending in 2023 and 2024, while spending very little on defense, because so many defensive players were young and on cheap deals. Now, he said, the Eagles will have to flip that, and move more of their spending to the defense.

If you are new to Second Floor, welcome. Drop a comment right now. Should the Eagles trade Saquon Barkley before the deadline, yes or no? I want to see where Eagles Nation stands.

And remember why this matters so much to Philadelphia. Saquon came here in 2024 and had one of the greatest seasons a running back has ever had, running for more than two thousand yards and helping lead the Eagles to a Super Bowl title. He is not just a running back on this team. He is one of the faces of a championship. That is why the idea of trading him hits fans so hard. It is not just a football decision. It is an emotional one. And Howie Roseman knows that better than anybody.

Now, let me give you the real complication, because this is where it gets tricky. According to Yahoo Sports, if the Eagles traded Saquon, they would be left with about twenty-six point four million dollars in dead cap space next year. That is a huge number. And that is why Zach Berman of The Athletic said trading Saquon could be a tougher debate than people think. According to Yahoo, Berman also said the Eagles have not decided whether they will even listen to offers for him.

And there is another layer. Saquon has missed the last two games with a hamstring injury. He was a full participant in practice on Saturday and is listed as questionable for tonight, and he is expected to play. But teams are not going to give up big value for a running back unless they see him healthy and producing. So tonight is not just about beating Dallas. It is about Saquon showing the league he is still Saquon.

And yes, some teams have already been connected to him in speculation. ESPN’s Ben Solak floated a hypothetical trade that would send Saquon to the Houston Texans for running back Woody Marks, a 2027 third-round pick, a 2027 sixth-round pick and a 2028 second-round pick. Solak also named the Seahawks and Vikings as possible fits. But I want to be very clear. That is one analyst’s idea. It is not a report of talks. Nobody has reported that Houston has called.

Now, here is the part of the Barnwell and Orr conversation that I actually found encouraging. Even in what they called a disastrous year, they said the Eagles will come out the other side with real cornerstone players. A shutdown corner in Quinyon Mitchell. A movable chess piece in the secondary in Cooper DeJean. An elite defensive tackle in Jalen Carter, which they called a unicorn in today’s NFL. And at least one wide receiver to help whoever is at quarterback. Orr said, quote, it might take a year, but it’s not going to be a complete and total wasteland for half a decade. End quote.

And Barnwell brought up history. He went back to the 2020 season. The Eagles had won a Super Bowl with Carson Wentz as their franchise quarterback. Then they drafted Jalen Hurts, the season fell apart, the offensive line was banged up, Wentz struggled and eventually got benched for Hurts. The Eagles won only four games. But instead of launching into a full rebuild, Howie Roseman traded Wentz to the Colts for draft capital, traded up to draft DeVonta Smith, got the offensive line healthy, and a year later, the Eagles were back in the playoffs. Two years after that, they were in the Super Bowl.

So Barnwell asked Orr, can they make that kind of quick turnaround again? And Orr was honest. He said it will be hard, because that turnaround was built on one of the best offensive lines in modern NFL history. He called Jason Kelce one of the two or three best centers of all time, he called Lane Johnson one of the greatest right tackles of all time, and he said the Eagles hit on Jordan Mailata out of nowhere. He also credited hires like Shane Steichen and Kellen Moore as coordinators. And he said there will probably be some trial and error before the next golden era of Eagles football.

Eagles Nation, that is a fair point. Kelce is retired. Lane Johnson just retired. Jurgens is in Baltimore. The foundation that carried this offense for years is changing right in front of us. And that is why the pressure on Howie Roseman this deadline is so big.

Now, let me give you the other side of this, because not everyone agrees with Barnwell and Orr. Adam Schefter said on ESPN last week that it is not in Howie Roseman’s DNA to be a seller. He said Roseman could be a buyer and a seller at the same time, and that the record would not decide how he approaches the deadline. And the timing matters. According to the Philadelphia Inquirer, the trade deadline is November tenth, and the Eagles play three division games before then. Dallas tonight, then Washington, then the Giants. The entire NFC East is struggling. Win those three, and the Eagles are five and four and right in the middle of the race.

So which way does Roseman go? I think the honest answer is that tonight’s game, and the two after it, will decide that. Roseman has never been afraid of a bold move in either direction. He traded for A.J. Brown on draft night. He traded up for DeVonta Smith. He signed Saquon away from the Giants. And this season, he already moved Jurgens for a future second-round pick. Whatever he decides, it will not be timid.

Now, let us talk about tonight, because the Saquon conversation is directly tied to what happens at the Linc.

The Eagles will be without DeVonta Smith, Dontayvion Wicks, Nolan Smith and Michael Carter the second. Saquon, Jonathan Greenard, Jalyx Hunt and four players returning from injured reserve are questionable, including Landon Dickerson and rookie tight end Eli Stowers. Dallas Goedert is available after a full practice on Saturday. Drew Kendall is expected back as the starting center. And the Eagles have until four in the afternoon Eastern today to activate their injured reserve players.

On the other side, Dallas is also two and four. The Cowboys have ruled out linebacker Dee Winters, returner KaVontae Turpin and tackle Drew Shelton, but they get linebacker DeMarvion Overshown and cornerback Cobie Durant back. Dak Prescott has thrown four interceptions in his last two games.

And in his final press conference before the game, Nick Sirianni said everything is on the table, and that the Eagles adjusted schemes, schedules and practice this week. So we should see something different tonight. Sirianni also called Hollywood Brown a great pro who made one of the best catches he has seen in practice this week, so do not be surprised if Brown gets a bigger role with Smith and Wicks out.

Here is my thought. If Saquon plays and runs well tonight, two things happen at once. First, the Eagles have a much better chance to beat Dallas and snap this losing streak. And second, his trade value goes up, if Roseman ever decides to listen. That is the strange position this team is in. The best thing for the present and the best thing for the future could both run through the same player tonight.

And if Roseman does decide to sell some pieces, Saquon is not the only name people are talking about. The Philadelphia Inquirer’s deadline analysis pointed to players on expiring contracts, like Dallas Goedert, Moro Ojomo, Tyler Steen and Riq Woolen. Hollywood Brown has also come up in a lot of conversations. These are the kinds of moves that add picks without tearing the core apart. And honestly, that is the type of selling that makes the most sense to me if the next three games go badly. You move a veteran on a one-year deal for a mid-round pick. You do not move your captain.

Now, the Barnwell and Orr conversation also touched on the biggest question of all. The quarterback position. Barnwell said the Eagles are paying DeVonta Smith, and he assumed they would keep paying Jalen Hurts, but he said that could become a conversation as well. And the panel said the Eagles will probably need a bridge quarterback at some point. Eagles Nation, I want to be careful here. Jalen is dealing with a serious health situation, and the team and his agent have said they expect a full recovery. Right now, the only thing that matters is Jalen getting healthy. The long-term quarterback conversation can wait. But it is clear that national analysts are already thinking about what this roster looks like in 2027 and beyond, and that includes Tanner McKee and Joe Milton auditioning for the future every time they take the field.

And here is what Saquon means for tonight, specifically. Against Carolina, the Eagles ran for only thirty-five yards as a team, and Tanner McKee had to throw forty-five passes. That is not a recipe for winning with a backup quarterback. With DeVonta Smith and Wicks out, the Eagles need Saquon to carry this offense. Run the ball early. Make the Dallas linebackers commit. Open up play action for McKee and get Goedert free over the middle. If Saquon gets twenty touches tonight and looks like himself, the Eagles have a real chance. And with Kendall back at center and Dickerson possibly back at left guard, the offensive line in front of him could be the healthiest it has looked in weeks.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the sellers. They will say Barnwell and Orr are right. The Eagles are two and four, the franchise quarterback is out for the year, Lane Johnson retired, Jurgens is gone, and the cap bill from the championship years is coming due. They will say Saquon is the most valuable veteran trade chip on the roster, that running backs do not age well, and that the smartest move is to cash in now, add more picks to the jackpot, and build around Mitchell, DeJean, Carter and Davis. They will say a headache year now is better than a mediocre team for three years.

On the other side, the believers. They will say trading Saquon leaves more than twenty-six million dollars in dead money next year and sends a terrible message to the locker room. They will say Saquon is a captain and a leader, and that you do not trade a player like that in the middle of a season where the division is still wide open. They will say Schefter is right that Roseman never waves the white flag. And they will say that with three straight division games before the deadline, the Eagles should play them out before making any decision that affects the next few seasons.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I do not trade Saquon Barkley. Not now. The dead money is too big, the division is too open, and he is too important to this locker room. I understand the case Barnwell and Orr are making, and I agree with them on one thing. The Eagles do need to get younger and cheaper, and Roseman should keep stacking draft picks. But you do that with veterans on expiring deals, not with your best offensive player and team captain. Keep the core together, keep adding young talent around it, and give this coaching staff a fair chance to fix the offense. Let Saquon play tonight. Let him help this team beat Dallas. And then let the next three weeks tell you who this team really is.
