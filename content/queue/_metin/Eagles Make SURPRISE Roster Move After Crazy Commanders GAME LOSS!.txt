Eagles Nation, it happened again. Six straight losses. On Sunday Night Football, the Washington Commanders came back from a ten-point deficit and beat the Eagles twenty-seven to ten. The Eagles are now two and six. According to Pro Football Talk, this is the franchise’s first six-game losing streak since 2012. And before the game even started, the Eagles made a set of roster moves that tell you exactly how thin this team has become, including one quarterback move that surprised a lot of people.

This is James with Second Floor, and today we are going to break it all down. The roster moves the Eagles made before kickoff, what happened in the game, how Washington took over, what Nick Sirianni said afterward, and what this means with the trade deadline just eight days away. Before we start, if you are still riding with this team, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor. This is when real fans show up.

Let me start with the roster moves, because that is where this story begins.

On Saturday, the Eagles elevated two players from the practice squad. The first was running back Elijah Mitchell. And the reason was simple. Saquon Barkley had missed two practices this week with hamstring and back problems before returning on Friday. Dameon Pierce was placed on injured reserve on Wednesday. That left Jaydon Blue as the only other healthy running back on the active roster. One star with a sore hamstring, one young backup, and nobody else. So the Eagles called on Mitchell for the second straight week.

Mitchell’s story is a fascinating one. The San Francisco 49ers drafted him in the sixth round out of Louisiana in 2021. As a rookie, he ran for nine hundred sixty-three yards and five touchdowns, one of the best rookie seasons of any running back that year. But then injuries piled up. And in 2024, a hamstring injury put him on injured reserve for the entire season. He had a short stop in Kansas City, signed with the Eagles in June, went on injured reserve in August with an injury settlement, and then came back to the practice squad on October twentieth. Against Dallas, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he had three carries for eleven yards and three catches for twenty-three yards.

The second elevation was outside linebacker Mason Reiger, an undrafted rookie out of Wisconsin. The Dolphins signed him in May, and the Eagles added him to the practice squad in late August. With Jihaad Campbell out with hamstring and quad injuries, the Eagles needed help at linebacker, and Reiger was in line to make his NFL debut on Sunday night. Cornerback Jakorian Bennett, who was working his way back from injured reserve, was also downgraded to out on Saturday, according to Jeff McLane of the Inquirer.

And then came the surprise. According to Heavy, citing McLane, the Eagles made rookie quarterback Cole Payton active for the game. And here is the key detail. He was not listed as the emergency third quarterback. According to Zach Berman of The Athletic, Payton was active over center Jake Majors, which suggested a possible role on special teams, and McLane even floated the idea of a possible role on offense. When you are activating a rookie quarterback for reasons other than playing quarterback, it tells you just how creative the Eagles have had to get with this roster. Injuries have stretched this team so thin that every single spot on the game-day roster has to be used in the smartest possible way.

And by the way, tight end Zach Ertz remains on the practice squad. According to Yahoo Sports, he is no longer eligible for practice squad elevations, so he would have to be signed to the active roster to play. With Dallas Goedert healthy and rookie Eli Stowers back, the Eagles did not make that move.

Now, let us talk about the game, because for one quarter, it looked like the Eagles had finally found something.

The Eagles led ten to nothing after the first quarter. According to Pro Football Talk, Saquon Barkley punched in a three-yard touchdown run midway through the first quarter, and Jake Elliott added a field goal. Zack Baun recovered a fumble. The defense was flying around. For the first time in weeks, the Eagles looked like a team that was in control.

And then everything changed. Washington kicker Drew Stevens hit a fifty-six-yard field goal. Stefon Diggs caught a touchdown pass to tie the game in the second quarter. And Stevens hit a thirty-nine-yarder right before halftime. Washington led thirteen to ten at the break. Stevens added two more field goals in the second half, from twenty-five and forty-two yards, and Rachaad White ran for a touchdown. The Eagles did not score again after the first quarter.

There were also some painful moments in the kicking game for the Eagles. According to Pro Football Talk, Jake Elliott missed a forty-one-yard field goal that hit the left upright in the fourth quarter. An earlier thirty-six-yard attempt was wiped out by an illegal formation penalty. When your offense is struggling to score, those are the kinds of plays that end games.

Let us look at the numbers. According to Yahoo Sports, Tanner McKee completed twenty-three of thirty-four passes for two hundred twelve yards, with no touchdowns and one interception. And according to Pro Football Talk, he was sacked four times, and Washington recorded nine official quarterback hits. That is a quarterback who was under pressure all night. No young quarterback can thrive when he is getting hit that often.

Saquon Barkley ran for thirty yards and a touchdown on thirteen carries while playing through his hamstring and back injuries. Elijah Mitchell actually outgained him, with thirty-four yards on eleven carries. So the elevation mattered. Mitchell got real work. He ran hard and gave the Eagles a reliable option when Saquon needed a breather. For a player who was released with an injury settlement in August, that is a great sign.

The bright spots in the passing game were the rookies. Makai Lemon caught six passes for fifty-seven yards, and rookie tight end Eli Stowers caught three passes for fifty-three yards, according to Yahoo Sports. Those two keep showing they can be part of the future.

On defense, there were real positives too. According to Yahoo Sports, the Eagles held Washington out of the end zone on several drives, forcing five field goal attempts. Jalen Carter and Nolan Smith each recorded a sack, the Eagles’ first sacks since the Carolina game. Jayden Daniels threw for two hundred seventeen yards and one touchdown with no interceptions. Treylon Burks caught three passes for eighty-one yards, and Rachaad White ran for fifty-five yards and a touchdown. But this was not the defense that got run over by Dallas. This defense kept the Eagles in the game for a long time. The offense just could not finish.

And think about what this game meant for Washington. Before kickoff, CBS Sports pointed out that Jayden Daniels had not won a game as a starter in more than a year, and that he was winless in his past eight starts. The Eagles let him snap that streak. Washington improved to two, four and one, and now the Commanders are right there with the Eagles in the NFC East. A week ago, people were calling this game unwatchable. Now Washington has life, and the Eagles are the team sinking.

And let me say something about the defense, because it deserves credit. After the disaster against Dallas, where Javonte Williams ran for one hundred fifty-five yards and the Eagles did not record a single sack, this unit responded. Two sacks. A fumble recovery. Five Washington drives that stalled and ended in field goal attempts. Vic Fangio’s group bent, but it did not completely break, at least not until late. Nolan Smith’s return from his concussion made a real difference. And Jalen Carter, who called his own season trash this week, answered with a sack. That is the kind of response you want to see. The problem is that you cannot ask a defense to hold an opponent to field goals all night when your offense goes silent after the first quarter.

Now, what did Nick Sirianni say? According to Jeff McLane of the Inquirer, Sirianni was asked about the late fourth-down sequence, and he said, quote, I didn’t feel like I was close to calling a timeout there. End quote. On the field goal sequence, he said, quote, not a good sequence there. End quote. When he was asked about possible staff changes, he said, quote, I know what good coaches look like, and I know I got good coaches in there. End quote. He said, quote, if my delivery needs to be even harsher, it needs to be harsher. End quote. And he finished with, quote, but I believe in those guys, and I believe that we can get it turned around. End quote.

Eagles Nation, here is the honest truth. The pressure on Nick Sirianni just went up a lot. McLane’s article on Monday was headlined, Nick Sirianni’s Eagles are burning, and it suggested that Jeffrey Lurie may be looking beyond his tenure. Now, let me be careful here. That is McLane’s analysis. He did not cite a source saying Lurie has decided anything. He pointed to Lurie’s history of firing coaches after the 2012, 2015 and 2020 seasons, and he wrote that Sirianni has nine games left to salvage this season. But when one of the most respected reporters covering this team writes that, you know the temperature has changed.

And earlier this week, Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice wrote that an in-season firing feels unlikely, and CBS Sports analysts said Sirianni’s seat was not as hot as Washington’s Dan Quinn. That was before Washington beat the Eagles by seventeen points. Funny how fast things can turn in this league. One bad night can change the entire conversation.

Now, let us talk about the trade deadline, because this loss changes the conversation. The deadline is Tuesday, November tenth, and the Eagles have one more game before it, at home against the New York Giants, who lead the NFC East at four, two and one. Albert Breer of Sports Illustrated reported that contract-year players like Hollywood Brown, Riq Woolen and Dallas Goedert could be available, especially if the Eagles lost on Sunday night. Well, they lost. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler gave Woolen a thirty-five percent chance of being traded and Landon Dickerson a twenty-five percent chance. Breer also reported that the Eagles told teams Moro Ojomo is not available. And Jimmy Kempski wrote that DeVonta Smith isn’t going anywhere.

Now, let us look ahead to the Giants, because this is the last game before the deadline. According to Giants.com, the Giants went into their bye week after their game against Houston, so they will come to Philadelphia rested. J.J. McCarthy has taken over at quarterback, and John Harbaugh, who came over from Baltimore, has the Giants leading the division. For the Eagles, the math is simple. A win keeps a tiny bit of hope alive and could make Roseman think twice about selling. A loss, and the Eagles would be two and seven with a seven-game losing streak heading into their own bye week, and the deadline would almost certainly turn into a selling event.

And there is something else to keep in mind. After the Giants game, the Eagles have their bye week. That gives this team time to get healthier. DeVonta Smith, Dontayvion Wicks, Britain Covey and Jonathan Jones could all use that time. Saquon could too. If the Eagles can find a way to win one game before the bye, the second half of the season could at least look more competitive, with more players back and the young guys gaining experience every week.

So here is what I expect. I think the Eagles will listen very seriously on their veterans with expiring contracts over the next eight days. Woolen, Goedert and Brown are the most likely names. The young core, Smith, Ojomo, Lemon, Stowers, Mitchell, DeJean and the rest, stays. And there is still a chance Howie Roseman adds an offensive lineman for the future, after Jeff McLane reported he was shopping for one.

And what about the running back room? This was the second straight week Mitchell was elevated from the practice squad. Under NFL rules, a team can elevate the same practice squad player only three times in a season. That means the Eagles have one elevation left for Mitchell. After the way he played on Sunday, outgaining Saquon, I would not be surprised if the Eagles sign him to the active roster soon. That is my speculation, not a report. But with Saquon still dealing with hamstring and back problems, Mitchell has earned a real look.

And speaking of Saquon, there is a fair question to ask. Should he keep playing through these injuries? Thirteen carries for thirty yards is not the Saquon we know. He is clearly not healthy. With the season slipping away, I think the Eagles need to consider giving him time to heal, for his long-term health and for the team’s future. That is my opinion, not a report.

Let me also talk about Tanner McKee for a second. Three starts, three losses. But he has not been the main problem. He has been sacked over and over, he is throwing to rookie receivers, and his running game has been inconsistent. He made some good throws on Sunday night, especially to Lemon and Stowers. But nine quarterback hits in one game is not a quarterback problem. That is a protection problem. And with Jalen Hurts out for the season while he recovers from blood clots, the Eagles have to keep developing McKee and protecting him better. Remember, the team and Jalen’s agent have said they expect a full recovery for Jalen, and that is still the most important thing in all of this.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the people who say it is time for big changes. They will say six straight losses is the longest streak since 2012, the season that ended with Andy Reid being fired. They will say the offense scored ten points against a team that came in at one, four and one. They will say the Eagles gave up a ten-point lead and did not score after the first quarter. They will say McLane is right, the Eagles are burning, and that Jeffrey Lurie should start thinking about the future. And they will say Howie Roseman should sell every veteran on an expiring contract before November tenth.

On the other side, the people who say be patient. They will say the defense actually played well, holding Washington to five field goal attempts and getting two sacks. They will say the Eagles are playing without Jalen Hurts, DeVonta Smith, Dontayvion Wicks, Britain Covey and a fully healthy Saquon. They will say the rookies, Lemon and Stowers, keep showing up, and that Mitchell gave the run game a lift. They will say Sirianni has two Super Bowl appearances in four years and has earned the chance to finish the season. And they will say the right move at the deadline is careful selling, not a fire sale.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. This loss hurt more than Dallas, because the Eagles had it in their hands. Ten to nothing, the defense flying around, and then the offense disappeared. I still do not think Sirianni will be fired during the season, but McLane’s article tells me the conversation inside the building is getting serious. At the deadline, I would sell the veterans on one-year deals, keep the young core, and let these last nine games be about development. Get Lemon, Stowers, Cooper, Bell and Kendall as many snaps as possible. Give Mitchell a real role. And protect Saquon’s long-term health. The goal now is to make sure the 2027 Eagles are a lot better than the 2026 Eagles. That does not mean giving up on Sunday’s game against the Giants. It means being honest about where this team is.

So let me wrap it up. Before the game, the Eagles elevated Elijah Mitchell and Mason Reiger, ruled out Jakorian Bennett, and surprisingly activated rookie quarterback Cole Payton over Jake Majors. Then the Commanders came back from ten to nothing and won twenty-seven to ten. It is the Eagles’ sixth straight loss, their first six-game skid since 2012. McKee threw for two hundred twelve yards and an interception and was sacked four times. Mitchell outran Saquon. Lemon and Stowers stood out. Jalen Carter and Nolan Smith got sacks. Sirianni said he believes the team can get it turned around. And next up, the Giants come to Philadelphia, with the trade deadline right behind.
