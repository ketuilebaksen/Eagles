Eagles Nation, the trade deadline has come and gone, and Howie Roseman made one of the biggest moves of his career. On Monday evening, the Eagles traded captain Jordan Davis to the Jacksonville Jaguars for a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick. And then, on deadline day, the story kept going. A second trade was agreed to and then fell apart. Several veterans everyone expected to be moved are still here. The Eagles now have a pile of draft picks unlike anything we have seen in years. And one young player suddenly looks like the centerpiece of the defensive line. So today, we are going through the whole wave of updates, before the Eagles come back from the bye against the Pittsburgh Steelers.

This is James with Second Floor, and here is the plan. First, the full story behind the Jordan Davis trade, including the detail about Moro Ojomo that changes how you see it. Then, why Jacksonville wanted Davis so badly. Then, everything Davis meant to this franchise. Then, the trade that fell through on deadline day, the players who were not traded, the draft capital Howie now has, and what all of it means for the Steelers game. Before we start, if you are still riding with this team, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor.

Let me start with the blockbuster.

On Monday evening, a little less than twenty-four hours before the deadline, the Eagles announced the trade. According to the team’s official website, the Eagles sent defensive tackle Jordan Davis to the Jaguars for a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick. According to Reuben Frank of NBC Sports Philadelphia, it is the first time Howie Roseman has traded an Eagles first-round pick since Carson Wentz in 2020. And heading into deadline week, most people expected the Eagles to sell small pieces. Role players. Spare parts. Nobody expected a twenty-six-year-old captain to be the one who left.

Here is the part that got a lot of attention. According to NFL insider Jordan Schultz, as reported by Heavy, the Jaguars, quote, initially checked on DT Moro Ojomo, but were told Philadelphia was not trading him. End quote. So Jacksonville turned its attention to Davis, a player they did not think was even available. And according to Heavy, the Eagles’ asking price for Davis was sky high. The Jaguars paid it.

Think about that, Eagles Nation. Jacksonville knocked on the door for Ojomo, and the Eagles said no. Then they asked about the bigger name, the former first-round pick with the big contract, and the Eagles said yes. Why would Philadelphia say no to one and yes to the other? That is the question that explains this entire trade, and we will get to it in a moment.

If you are new to Second Floor, welcome. Drop a comment right now. Comment number one if you think Davis was the heart of this defense. Comment number two if you think the Eagles can replace him just fine.

Now, why did the Jaguars want Davis so badly? According to Sports Illustrated, Jacksonville already needed help at defensive tackle, and a season-ending injury to DaVon Hamilton made it urgent. And the Jaguars were not done. According to the New York Post, Davis was the third trade Jacksonville made on Monday. They also acquired safety Jessie Bates from the Falcons and defensive tackle Davon Godchaux from the Saints. According to the Post, the Jaguars are six and two and have allowed the second-fewest points per game in the NFL, at fifteen point one. That is a team going all in.

And Jaguars general manager James Gladstone made it clear how much he values Davis. According to the New York Post, he said, quote, Jordan’s impact on the field can be felt on every down. End quote. And there is a fun connection here too. Davis will be reunited in Jacksonville with Travon Walker, his former Georgia teammate, who went first overall in the 2022 draft, twelve picks before Davis.

Now, let me tell you what the Eagles are losing, because Jordan Davis was more than a big body in the middle.

According to the team’s official website, Davis was named to the All-Rookie Team after being drafted in 2022. He helped the Eagles win three NFC East titles, two NFC Championships and Super Bowl fifty-nine. In the 2024 playoffs, he played four games with six tackles and two sacks. And in 2025, he had seventy-two tackles, four and a half sacks, twenty-two quarterback pressures and six passes defensed.

And do you remember the Rams game in 2025? According to the team, Davis authored the play of the year, a game-sealing field goal block that he scooped up and returned for a touchdown. He was named NFC Special Teams Player of the Week for that game. Later that season, he was named NFC Defensive Player of the Week for the game against Detroit. According to the team, he is the first Eagle to win both awards in the same year. And this season, he was named a captain for the first time.

According to Reuben Frank, Davis was the only Eagle to start all sixty regular-season games since 2022. Over his career in Philadelphia, he had nine and a half sacks, seventeen tackles for loss and one hundred ninety-three tackles.

And the Eagles thanked him in their statement. According to the team’s website, the Eagles said, quote, we are extremely grateful to Jordan for his contributions to our team on the field. End quote. They said, quote, he has been a key contributor to our team culture and on-field success. End quote. They said, quote, his growth and development as both a player and a leader are a testament to his hard work and character. End quote. And they closed with, quote, we wish Jordan nothing but continued success. End quote.

And think about what this means for Jalen Carter. According to Frank, Carter and Davis were college teammates and close friends. They won a national championship together at Georgia, they came to Philadelphia together, and they won a Super Bowl together. Now Carter is the anchor of this defensive line without his partner next to him. That is a big responsibility, and it is also a big opportunity. Carter is the player the Eagles paid to be the centerpiece, and now there is no doubt about who leads that room.

Now, the money side. According to the New York Post, Davis signed a three-year, seventy-eight million dollar extension this offseason, with sixty-four point four million dollars guaranteed. He was under contract through 2029. So this was not a rental. The Eagles traded a player they had locked up for years.

So why did they do it? Let me give you three reasons.

First, his production dropped. According to Geoff Mosher of PhillyVoice, Davis played fifty-two percent of the defensive snaps this season, down from a career-best sixty-one percent last year. And Moro Ojomo has played sixty-one percent. According to Jeff Neiburg of the Inquirer, citing NFL Next Gen Stats, Davis had fifty-eight run stops last season and twenty-five through nine games this year. And according to the New York Post, he dealt with a hand issue and an ankle injury earlier this year.

Second, the money. Jalen Carter signed a four-year, one hundred fifty-two million dollar deal in July, according to Frank. Paying two defensive tackles that much, and then trying to pay a third one in Ojomo, was going to be very hard.

And third, Ojomo. According to the New York Post, the Eagles plan to prioritize a new deal for Ojomo, who is in the final year of his rookie contract. According to Sports Illustrated, the trade creates cap space that would help extend him. And according to Mosher, the trade will likely help the Eagles re-sign Ojomo, who in his opinion has been the better player this season.

So that is the answer to the question. The Eagles said no on Ojomo and yes on Davis because they believe Ojomo is the better fit for the future, at a better price.

But let me be honest with you. Nothing has been announced about an Ojomo extension. No deal, no agreement. ESPN reported that the Eagles are inclined to keep and potentially extend him. That is a strong signal. But until he signs, it is not done. And if Ojomo leaves in free agency, this trade will look very different.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you would rather have Jordan Davis on the field. Comment number two if you love the picks and the Ojomo plan.

And look at the bigger pattern. This is the third major trade Howie Roseman has made in about five months. On June first, according to FOX 29, he sent A.J. Brown to the Patriots for a 2028 first-round pick and a 2027 fifth-round pick. On October seventh, according to NFL dot com, he sent Cam Jurgens and a 2027 seventh-round pick to the Ravens for a 2027 fifth-round pick and a 2028 second-round pick. And now Jordan Davis. According to Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice, the speed of the Davis trade could be a sign that Roseman is planning a larger, multi-year overhaul. That is Kempski’s read, not a report. But when you put these three trades together, it is hard to argue with him.

Now, how are people grading this trade? According to Sports Illustrated, Eva Geitheim gave the Eagles an A and the Jaguars a C plus. She called it a risky bet for Jacksonville, because Davis has been inconsistent this season. But she also noted that he ranked sixth among defensive tackles in run stop win rate last season, so he could make an already strong Jaguars defense even better. For the Eagles, she said the trade brings in significant draft capital and creates cap space.

And according to Anthony Miller of Inside The Iggles, the Jurgens and Davis trades were smart and not forced, because other teams made offers and Roseman accepted them.

Now, the wave of deadline-day updates. And this is where it gets interesting.

According to Dave Zangaro of NBC Sports Philadelphia, the Eagles agreed to trade edge rusher Arnold Ebiketie to the Cincinnati Bengals for a 2027 sixth-round pick. And then the deal fell through. A league source confirmed it to NBC Sports Philadelphia. Why? According to Zangaro, the trade would have canceled out a 2027 third-round compensatory pick the Bengals were set to receive after losing Trey Hendrickson to Baltimore. So the Bengals would have gained a sixth-round pick and lost a third-round pick. They backed out.

That is one of the strangest deadline stories you will ever see. A trade agreed to, and then undone because of a compensatory pick formula. So Ebiketie stays in Philadelphia. According to Zangaro, he is twenty-seven, on a one-year deal, and he has played in all nine games, with three hundred snaps, third among Eagles edge rushers behind Nolan Smith and Jalyx Hunt. He has twenty-five tackles and two quarterback hits, but no sacks. And the failed deal also affects a young player. According to Zangaro, rookie Keyshawn James-Newby, who has been on the practice squad, likely would have seen more playing time if the trade had gone through. Now the edge rotation stays Nolan Smith, Jalyx Hunt, Jonathan Greenard, Ebiketie and A.J. Epenesa, with Greenard expected to play more now that he is healthier, and James-Newby waiting for his chance. And the Eagles still do not have a 2027 sixth-round pick. For a franchise that loves to collect picks, that is a small but frustrating detail.

And then, the players who were not traded. According to Inside The Iggles, Roseman did not trade several players on one-year or expiring deals, including Riq Woolen, Hollywood Brown and Dallas Goedert. Anthony Miller argued that Woolen and Brown could have brought back Day 3 picks, and called it a missed opportunity. That is his opinion. But it means the Eagles will head into the offseason with several players who can sign elsewhere.

Remember what those players said. Woolen told PennLive, quote, my heart is here. End quote. Goedert said, according to SB Nation, quote, I love this city. I always dreamt of retiring here. End quote. Saquon Barkley said, according to the Inquirer, quote, I would love to be here. I plan on being here. End quote. And multiple reports had already said Saquon would not be traded. So the core of this team, the players who want to be here, is still here.

And Landon Dickerson, who drew interest from several teams according to NBC Sports Philadelphia and ESPN, also stayed. So did Tanner McKee.

Now, the draft capital. And this is the part that should make every Eagles fan pay attention.

According to the team’s official website, the Eagles now hold two first-round picks in 2027, their own and Jacksonville’s. In 2028, they hold two first-round picks, their own and New England’s from the A.J. Brown trade, plus two second-round picks, including Baltimore’s from the Cam Jurgens trade. And in 2029, they hold multiple second-round picks, including the one from Jacksonville. According to Frank, that is seven first or second-round picks over the next two drafts.

That is a lot of ammunition. Very few teams in the league have that kind of flexibility heading into the next two drafts, and Howie Roseman has always been at his best when he has picks to move around. And according to Athlon Sports, the Eagles are slotted at number five in Tankathon’s 2027 draft order heading into Week 10. But the Inquirer points out that, because the Jaguars are a contender, the pick from Jacksonville will likely land late in the first round. So the value of that pick is not set in stone.

So what will Howie do with all of it? Move up for a star? Rebuild the offensive line after losing Lane Johnson and Cam Jurgens? Add weapons for Jalen Hurts when he returns? Remember, Jalen is out for the season after doctors found blood clots in his lungs, and the team expects a full recovery. With this many picks, Howie can do almost anything.

And let me remind you how we got here, because the timing matters. The Davis trade came the day after a nineteen to eleven loss to the Giants at Lincoln Financial Field, the Giants’ first win in Philadelphia since 2013, according to Athlon Sports. It was the Eagles’ seventh straight loss. Joe Milton was sacked seven times in his first start. The offense gained just two hundred twenty yards. According to CBS Sports, it was the Eagles’ first two and seven start since 1999. And according to the Inquirer, The Athletic put their playoff odds below one percent. When a season reaches that point, a general manager has to think about the future. That is exactly what Howie did.

Now, let us talk about what this means on the field.

According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the defensive tackle group is now Jalen Carter, Moro Ojomo, Byron Young and Ty Robinson. That means more snaps for young players, and a chance for the coaches to see what they have. But there is a concern. According to Mosher of PhillyVoice, losing Davis raises questions about Vic Fangio’s defense, because his five-two front needs a run-stuffing nose tackle. That was Davis’s job. Now someone else has to do it.

And the defense was the best part of this team in the Giants game. According to PHLY Sports, the Eagles had six sacks, their most in a game this season, and Davis had one of them. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, they held the Giants to eighty rushing yards, the first time all season an opponent was held under one hundred. The question now is whether that run defense can hold up without Davis in the middle.

And then there is Nick Sirianni. On Monday, before the trade, he said he had not made any staff changes, according to the Inquirer, and that the team was going through a self-scout process. He also denied flipping off fans after the Giants loss, saying, according to CBS Sports, quote, absolutely not. End quote. So the coaching staff remains the same for now, and the bye week is when any changes would likely happen.

And here is something that gets lost in all the trade talk. The players left in that locker room still have eight games to play. A captain just left. The coaching staff is under pressure. The quarterback is a player who was on another team’s practice squad a month ago. That is a lot for any group to handle. How the veterans respond during this bye, players like Saquon, Goedert, Mailata, Carter and Woolen, will say a lot about the culture that is left in that building.

Here is what I will be watching before the Steelers game. First, whether the Eagles start extension talks with Moro Ojomo. That would confirm the plan behind the Davis trade. Second, any coaching changes coming out of the self-scout. Third, who starts at quarterback against Pittsburgh. And fourth, the injury report, especially Zack Baun’s back, DeVonta Smith’s hamstring and Saquon’s hamstring and back. The bye week should help all of them, but I am not going to guess on timelines.

And remember, the Steelers game on Sunday, November twenty-second, has been moved to one in the afternoon Eastern on CBS, according to the Inquirer. Then the Eagles go to Dallas on Thanksgiving.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the people who think the Eagles got it wrong. They will say Jordan Davis was a captain, a Super Bowl champion and the anchor of the run defense, and that trading him sends a terrible message. They will say the Eagles just gave him a seventy-eight million dollar extension, so trading him months later shows a lack of planning. They will say the Jacksonville first-round pick will likely be late in the round. They will say Howie should have also sold Woolen, Brown and Goedert instead of letting them potentially walk. And they will say the Ebiketie mess shows the deadline did not go smoothly.

On the other side, the people who think Howie nailed it. They will say Davis’s snaps and run stops dropped this season, and that getting a first and a second for him is a great return. They will say Sports Illustrated gave the Eagles an A. They will say keeping Ojomo, who has played more snaps and is younger and cheaper, is the smart long-term move. They will say the Eagles now have two first-round picks in each of the next two drafts. And they will say keeping Woolen, Goedert and Saquon, players who want to be here, is good for the locker room.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I think this was a smart trade, even though it hurts to watch Jordan Davis leave. Howie sold a big contract at the right time, kept the younger defensive tackle and loaded up for the future. But the trade is only half done. The other half is signing Moro Ojomo. If the Eagles extend him, this deal looks like a home run. If they let him walk, it looks like a mistake. And I would have liked to see Howie get something for Hollywood Brown, because he is not part of the future. But overall, the Eagles came out of this deadline in a much better position for 2027 and beyond.

So let me wrap it up. The Eagles traded captain Jordan Davis to the Jaguars for a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick, the first time Howie Roseman has traded an Eagles first-round pick since Carson Wentz. The Jaguars first asked about Moro Ojomo and were told no. Jacksonville also added Jessie Bates and Davon Godchaux. Sports Illustrated gave the Eagles an A. On deadline day, an Arnold Ebiketie trade to the Bengals was agreed to and then canceled because of a compensatory pick formula. Woolen, Brown, Goedert, Dickerson and McKee all stayed. The Eagles now hold two first-round picks in both 2027 and 2028. And the next test is the Steelers on November twenty-second at one in the afternoon Eastern on CBS.
