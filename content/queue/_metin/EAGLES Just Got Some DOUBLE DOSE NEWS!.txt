Let's start with dose number one, and we'll keep it short, because we gave Lane Johnson the tribute he deserves in our last video. On Tuesday, October 6th, in his 14th season, Lane Johnson announced his retirement, saying, quote, "Right now, it is critical that I focus on my mental health." Six Pro Bowls. Two first-team All-Pro selections. Two Super Bowl championships. 171 games, every one of them a start. Jeffrey Lurie called him "an all-time Eagle." And on Wednesday, his teammates spoke up. And then, the very next day, came dose number two.

On Wednesday, October 7th, the Eagles and Ravens announced the trade. According to Adam Schefter and confirmed by both teams, here's the deal. The Eagles send Cam Jurgens and a 2027 seventh-round pick to Baltimore. In return, the Eagles get a 2027 fifth-round pick and a 2028 second-round pick. And according to Howie Roseman, there's money coming back in the deal too, which means real salary cap flexibility for Philadelphia.

Let that sink in. A 2028 second-round pick, plus a pick swap that moves the Eagles up from the seventh round to the fifth, for a player who was ranked 73rd out of 74 guards by Pro Football Focus this season, and who was sitting in the concussion protocol on the day he was traded. Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice called it a high price for an underperforming player with significant injury concerns. In other words, Baltimore paid up.

Now, I want to be fair to Cam Jurgens here, because he deserves it. This is a guy the Eagles drafted in the second round in 2022 to be Jason Kelce's successor. Kelce himself was a big believer in him. He started at right guard in 2023, took over at center in 2024, and made the Pro Bowl in both 2024 and 2025. He played through a back injury during the Super Bowl run. He had a procedure for nerve pain. He admitted he wasn't fully recovered last year and didn't play to his own standard. He went to Colombia for stem cell treatment this offseason to get his body right. And when Landon Dickerson went down in Week 1, he did the selfless thing and moved to a position he'd never played. It didn't go well. But he never complained. The Eagles' official account posted it simply. "Thank you for everything, Cam." I'll say the same. Thank you, Cam. Get healthy, and good luck in Baltimore.

So why did Howie do it? Why trade a starter in October, the day after losing your right tackle?

Here's what Roseman said on Wednesday. Quote, "We weren't looking to move Cam." He said the deal came together fast. Quote, "Over the last 24 hours, it got to the point where we felt like it could be a win-win." And then he explained the thinking. Quote, "My job is to look at how we can have sustained success for a long period of time." And, "you don't get many opportunities to get second-round picks in trades." He also said that with so many players on long-term deals, quote, "being able to create some flexibility is a good situation for us."

And for everybody screaming that the Eagles just waved the white flag, Howie addressed that directly. Quote, "We're trying to win this year." He also made it clear that the Lane Johnson retirement and the Jurgens trade were two separate situations. One didn't cause the other. And honestly, that tracks. Roseman said the Ravens call turned into a real deal within a single day. That's not a long-planned teardown. That's a GM reacting to a market that suddenly opened up.

Now, let's look at it from Baltimore's side, because this is why the price was so high. The Ravens' center room has been wiped out. According to NFL.com, Danny Pinter suffered a season-ending injury before the season. Jovaughn Gwyn fractured his fibula and is out for months. Ethan Pocic didn't stick after a knee injury. All three are on injured reserve. On top of that, Lamar Jackson hurt his ankle in Week 4, and NFL.com noted that Tyler Huntley could end up starting at quarterback soon. Think about that from Baltimore's point of view. If you might be playing a backup quarterback, the last thing you can afford is chaos at center, the guy who touches the ball on every single snap. So Baltimore needed an experienced center immediately, and they had nowhere else to turn. Ravens general manager Eric DeCosta called Jurgens a young foundational piece, and he's under contract through 2029. And remember, Jurgens is still in the concussion protocol. Baltimore plays Atlanta this week, and there's no word yet on when he'll be cleared to make his Ravens debut. So the Ravens paid a second-round pick for a player they can't even put on the field yet. That's how badly they needed a center. When a contender is desperate, the price goes up. And Howie Roseman has made a career out of being on the right side of desperate.

John McMullen of SI's Eagles Today put it well. He argued that a lot of general managers undervalue future draft picks because they're worried about their own job security. They need to win now. Roseman, who's been in that building for over two decades, can think long term. And that's how you end up turning a struggling, injured guard into a future second-rounder.

And here's a number that blew my mind. According to Dave Spadaro of the Eagles' official site, the Eagles now hold 16 combined picks in the 2027 and 2028 drafts, not even counting compensatory picks. That includes two first-rounders and two second-rounders in 2028. Sixteen picks. That's a war chest. And the trade deadline is November 10th.

Look at that group for a second. Mailata, at 29, is suddenly the veteran of the line. Steen is in his second year as a starter. And then you've got three young guys. And there's a part of this that doesn't get talked about enough. The center isn't just a blocker. He's the communicator. Before every snap, the center is identifying the middle linebacker, setting the protection, calling out the slide, and making sure all five guys are on the same page. With Jurgens, the Eagles had a two-time Pro Bowler who'd been doing that for years in this building. Now that job belongs to Drew Kendall, a second-year player, working next to an undrafted guard making his first start, with a rookie at right tackle. That means Jalen Hurts has to carry more of that load himself. He's going to have to see the blitz, adjust the protection, and get the ball out fast. The good news? Hurts has always been one of the smartest pre-snap quarterbacks in the league. This week, he's going to need every bit of that.

So let's put this double dose in perspective. Here's the case that Howie got this right. First, value. A 2028 second-rounder and a pick upgrade for a struggling, injured guard is excellent business. Second, the position. Jurgens wasn't going to play left guard long term, and when Dickerson comes back, Kendall had already shown he could handle center. Jurgens could have ended up as a very expensive backup. Third, the health. Back surgery, nerve pain, stem cell treatment, two concussions in less than a year. There were real questions about whether his body would ever be the same. And fourth, the flexibility. Sixteen picks over two drafts and more cap room gives Howie the ammunition to make a big move before the deadline.

Now here's the case against. Timing. You just lost Lane Johnson. Your line is already young and fragile. And now your most experienced interior lineman is gone too, days before an international trip, while your new starting center is sitting out practice with a knee injury. Leadership. Jurgens was a smart, experienced voice who knew every protection call in this offense. You can't trade for that overnight. And the message. When fans see a team at 2 and 2, on a two-game skid, trading a starter for future picks, it's natural to wonder if the front office is thinking more about 2028 than 2026, no matter what Howie says.

So here's my take, and you can fight me in the comments.

I like the trade. I really do. And I know that might be an unpopular opinion this week. But you have to separate the emotion from the business. If Jurgens was healthy and playing at his Pro Bowl level at center, I'd be furious. But that's not the player the Eagles had in October of 2026.
