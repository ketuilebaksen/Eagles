Eagles Nation, you have probably seen headlines this week saying the Eagles just fired somebody, or that Jeffrey Lurie made some shocking decision. So let me start with the truth, because that is what this channel is about. Nobody has been fired. Jeffrey Lurie has not fired Nick Sirianni, he has not fired any coordinator, and he has not cut any player in some surprise move. If you heard otherwise, it is not true. Headlines like that get clicks, but they do not help anyone understand what is really happening with this team.

What is true is a story that is actually more interesting. With the trade deadline ten days away, a young Eagles defensive tackle has become one of the hottest names on the market. An analyst from The Athletic proposed sending him to the Buffalo Bills. Other teams have reportedly called the Eagles about him. And according to Albert Breer of Sports Illustrated, the Eagles told those teams something you might not expect. He is not available.

That player is Moro Ojomo. And today, we are going to break down the trade idea, the real reporting, why the Eagles are holding on to him, and what it tells us about how Howie Roseman is approaching this deadline. Before we start, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor, because we are tracking every rumor and every real move until the November tenth deadline.

Let me set the scene first. The Eagles are two and five after a thirty-eight to twenty-one loss to the Dallas Cowboys on Monday night. That was their fifth straight loss. They are third in the NFC East, and just one game ahead of the Washington Commanders, who they play on Sunday night. The Commanders are one, four and one. A win could change the mood around this team. More losses could make selling at the deadline feel unavoidable.

And that is where Moro Ojomo comes in.

On Thursday, Ted Nguyen of The Athletic laid out ten hypothetical trades that could help Super Bowl contenders before the deadline. One of them sent Ojomo to the Buffalo Bills. According to Heavy, Nguyen wrote, quote, after missing out on Buckner, the Bills could make a splash for a young defensive tackle with loads of potential. End quote. He argued that Ojomo could fit in Buffalo’s three-four scheme under defensive coordinator Jim Leonhard, next to their young defensive lineman Deone Walker, and that a system giving him more freedom to attack upfield could make him even more effective.

Now, let me be clear. That is a hypothetical trade idea from an analyst. It is not a report that Buffalo made an offer. But it put Ojomo right in the spotlight.

So who is Moro Ojomo? He was a seventh-round pick out of Texas in 2023. A seventh-rounder. And the Eagles developed him into a real contributor. Last season, according to Nguyen, he started nine games and finished with six sacks. That is excellent production for a player drafted that late. He is twenty-five years old, and this is the final year of his rookie contract, with a base salary of about three point seven million dollars. For a player with six sacks last season, that is an incredible bargain, and it is a big reason why contending teams are interested.

This season, the numbers are more modest. According to Heavy, in seven games, Ojomo has eighteen tackles, sixteen pressures, thirteen quarterback hurries, three quarterback hits and two tackles for loss. And Pro Football Focus has not been kind to him. According to Heavy, PFF gives him an overall grade of fifty-three point nine, which ranks eighty-third out of one hundred twenty-six interior defensive linemen. His pass rush grade ranks one hundred tenth, and his run defense grade ranks sixty-first.

So any team trading for Ojomo would be betting partly on what he could become, not only on what he has done this year. And honestly, that is the whole appeal. He is young, he is cheap, he has already shown he can get to the quarterback, and a new team could see him as more than a short-term rental.

And think about the Eagles’ history with defensive linemen, because it explains a lot. This franchise has always believed that games are won in the trenches. For years, the Eagles kept investing in the defensive line, even when it seemed like they already had enough. Fletcher Cox, Brandon Graham, Javon Hargrave, Jalen Carter, Jordan Davis. The philosophy in Philadelphia has long been that you can never have too many good defensive linemen, because injuries happen and the pass rush has to stay fresh over a long season. Keeping a young, cheap, productive interior player like Ojomo fits that philosophy perfectly.

And look at his journey. As a seventh-round pick, he was not guaranteed anything. Most seventh-rounders never become regular contributors. Ojomo earned his snaps, worked his way into the rotation, and then put up six sacks last season. That is the kind of development the Eagles’ coaching staff takes pride in. Trading him the moment he becomes valuable would send a message to every young player in that building that development does not get rewarded. Keeping him sends the opposite message.

Now, here is the real reporting, and this is the part that matters most.

On Friday, according to NFL Trade Rumors, citing Albert Breer of Sports Illustrated, the Eagles have fielded trade calls about Ojomo, but they have told teams he is not available. And Breer said the Eagles have, quote, given off the vibe, end quote, that they still intend to re-sign him.

Eagles Nation, that is a big statement. Think about why it is surprising. The Eagles have already paid Jalen Carter and Jordan Davis at defensive tackle. Breer himself noted that a trade would make sense for exactly that reason. A lot of people assumed the Eagles could not afford to keep a third defensive tackle on a big deal, and that trading Ojomo now would be the smart way to avoid losing him for nothing in free agency. And yet the Eagles are signaling they want to keep him.

And Ojomo was not the only name in that reporting. According to Yardbarker, Brooks Kubena and Daniel Popper of The Athletic estimated what the Eagles could get for Ojomo if Howie Roseman changed his mind. Their number was a second-round pick plus a fifth-rounder. That is a lot more than the third-round pick that some people suggested. And for Saquon Barkley, they estimated a third-round pick plus a late Day 3 sweetener. But Breer said a Saquon trade, quote, seems less likely, especially with Barkley banged up. End quote.

So why would the Eagles hold on to Ojomo? Let me give you my read.

First, he is exactly the kind of player Howie Roseman loves. A late-round pick who outplayed his draft slot, developed in the Eagles’ system, and is still only twenty-five. That is a homegrown player, and those are the players championship rosters are built around.

Second, he fits the long-term plan on defense. Eagles Wire wrote this week that the Eagles need another draft-driven reset, but not a complete teardown, and that the young defense is the foundation. Jalen Carter, Jordan Davis, Quinyon Mitchell, Cooper DeJean, Nolan Smith and Jalyx Hunt. Keeping Ojomo adds another young piece to that group.

Third, interior defensive linemen who can rush the passer are hard to find. Six sacks from the inside is a real number. If the Eagles believe last season’s production is closer to who he is than this season’s grades, then letting him go for a mid-round pick would be selling low.

And fourth, the run defense. The Eagles just gave up two hundred four rushing yards to Dallas. Javonte Williams ran for one hundred fifty-five yards, the most a running back has ever gained against a Vic Fangio defense in Philadelphia, according to the Inquirer. Trading away a defensive tackle right now would make that problem even harder to fix. Ojomo was dealing with an abdomen injury last week and a neck issue this week, but he has no game designation for Sunday night.

Now, let me give you the other side. Breer also listed the Eagles’ contract-year players who could be available. Hollywood Brown, Riq Woolen and Dallas Goedert. And he said those moves become more likely if the Eagles lose on Sunday night. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler gave Woolen a thirty-five percent chance of being traded, and Landon Dickerson a twenty-five percent chance, writing that something feels off between the Eagles and Dickerson. So this is not a team that is refusing to listen to anything. It is a team that is choosing carefully who it might move.

And that, to me, is the most important takeaway. The Eagles seem to be drawing a line. Young players they developed and want to keep, like Ojomo and DeVonta Smith, who Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice says isn’t going anywhere, are off the table. Veterans on one-year deals, like Woolen, Brown and Goedert, are the ones who could be moved. That is not a fire sale. That is a plan. And honestly, after five straight losses, it is good to see a plan. Fans want to know that the front office is thinking clearly, not panicking. Holding on to Ojomo while listening on the veterans is the kind of balanced approach that can set this team up to bounce back quickly.

And it lines up with what Adam Schefter said on ESPN. It is not in Howie Roseman’s DNA to be a seller, and he can be a buyer and a seller at the same time. Jeff McLane also reported that Roseman has been shopping for a starting-caliber offensive lineman. So the Eagles could sell a veteran or two, keep their young core, and even add a lineman for the future, all before November tenth.

And there is a financial angle too. According to Eagles Wire, the Eagles are dealing with more than eighty-two million dollars in dead money this season, and their margin for error has narrowed. That is exactly why Breer’s report is so interesting. Re-signing Ojomo would cost real money. But compared to what the Eagles would have to pay a veteran defensive tackle in free agency, keeping a young player they already know could be the more efficient choice. Roseman has always been creative with contracts, and if the Eagles want Ojomo back, they will find a way to structure it.

And let us not forget what is going on at quarterback. Jalen Hurts is out for the season while he recovers from blood clots, and the team and his agent expect a full recovery. NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport said his contract will be one of the big decisions of the offseason. Every dollar the Eagles commit this offseason, to Ojomo, to Hurts, to anyone, is connected. That is why these deadline decisions are about much more than just the next ten games.

Now, about those firing rumors. Let me address them directly, because you deserve to know where things really stand. There has been a lot of talk about Nick Sirianni’s job security after five straight losses. But Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice wrote that Jeffrey Lurie likes Sirianni, and that an in-season firing feels unlikely. Lurie has historically waited until after the season to make coaching changes, with Chip Kelly in 2015 being the exception. So when you see a headline saying the owner just fired somebody, check the facts. As of right now, nothing like that has happened.

Now, let us look at Sunday night. The Eagles play at Washington on Sunday, November first, at eight twenty Eastern, on Sunday Night Football. According to Yardbarker, DraftKings had the Eagles as six-point underdogs as of Friday morning. Think about that. A team that won the Super Bowl less than two years ago is a six-point underdog against a one, four and one team. That tells you how far things have fallen. But it also means nobody expects much from this team right now, and sometimes that is exactly when a wounded team plays its best football.

But there is some good news. Saquon Barkley returned to practice on Friday and has no game designation, so he is on track to play. Nolan Smith and Jonathan Greenard practiced fully on Friday. On the other hand, DeVonta Smith, Dontayvion Wicks, Britain Covey, Jihaad Campbell and Jonathan Jones have been ruled out. And Dameon Pierce went on injured reserve this week, with Jaydon Blue promoted to the active roster.

For Ojomo specifically, this is a big game. If the Eagles really plan to re-sign him, Sunday is a chance for him to show why. Washington’s offensive line has been banged up, with Nick Allegretti missing practice on Thursday. If Ojomo can collapse the pocket and help shut down the run, it will remind everyone why the Eagles are not picking up the phone.

So what do the Eagles need to do on Sunday night? Let me give you my keys.

On offense, lean on Saquon early, but do not overload him. He is coming off hamstring and back problems, so mix in Jaydon Blue and Elijah Mitchell. Get the ball to Makai Lemon, who led the team with seven catches and his first touchdown on Monday night, and use Dallas Goedert as Tanner McKee’s safety blanket. And give McKee quick throws early, so he can get into a rhythm against the Washington pass rush.

On defense, stop the run first. Washington watched what Dallas did, and they will try to do the same thing. That means Jalen Carter, Jordan Davis and Moro Ojomo have to control the line of scrimmage. Jalen Carter called his own season trash this week, and said the fans’ opinion does not really matter. The best way for him to answer the critics is to dominate on Sunday night. And then, get after Jayden Daniels. Zero sacks against Dallas cannot happen again. With Nolan Smith and Jonathan Greenard practicing fully on Friday, the Eagles finally have a chance to get their pass rush back.

And on special teams, protect the football. The two muffed kickoffs against Dallas cannot happen again either. With Britain Covey out, Makai Lemon is expected to handle punt returns, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides on Moro Ojomo.

On one side, the people who say trade him. They will say he is in the final year of his rookie deal, and the Eagles have already paid Jalen Carter and Jordan Davis at the same position. They will say his PFF grades this season are below average, and that a second-round pick, if Kubena and Popper are right, would be a great return for a former seventh-round pick. They will say the Eagles are two and five, the season is slipping, and this is the moment to cash in. And they will say keeping three defensive tackles on big contracts is not realistic with more than eighty-two million dollars in dead money already on the books this season.

On the other side, the people who say keep him. They will say he is twenty-five years old, homegrown, and had six sacks just last season. They will say interior pass rushers are rare and expensive, and if he hits free agency, someone else will pay him. They will say trading a defensive tackle right after giving up two hundred four rushing yards would make the defense worse. And they will say Breer’s reporting tells you the Eagles see Ojomo as part of the future, not a trade chip.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I agree with the Eagles. Keep Moro Ojomo. If a team offers a second-round pick, Howie Roseman should at least listen, because that is serious value. But I love that the Eagles are telling teams he is not available. You do not rebuild by trading away your young, homegrown players. You rebuild by keeping them and moving the veterans on one-year deals. And if the Eagles can find a way to re-sign him, they will have one of the deepest young defensive lines in the league for years.
