He ran practice on Wednesday, he is preparing the team for Sunday night in Washington, and there is no report that Jeffrey Lurie is planning to fire him during the season. What is real is that the conversation about his future is now very loud, and the people who cover this team every day are asking serious questions. So today, we are going to do this the right way. Let me start with where this conversation comes from. On Tuesday, Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice published a detailed breakdown of Sirianni’s job security. And his key point was simple. Sirianni’s resume is outstanding, but the 2026 season has fans calling for a change, and the offense under him has been struggling for almost two years.

And to understand why the pressure is so intense right now, remember what happened on Monday night. The Dallas Cowboys came into Lincoln Financial Field and beat the Eagles thirty-eight to twenty-one. Javonte Williams ran for one hundred fifty-five yards, the most ever by a running back against a Vic Fangio defense in Philadelphia, according to the Inquirer. Dak Prescott threw three touchdowns and was never sacked. And the Eagles, who led twenty-one to twenty in the third quarter, gave up the final eighteen points. It was Dallas’s first win in Philadelphia since 2021. Losses like that are the ones that turn a quiet conversation about a head coach into a loud one.

And all of this is happening while Jalen Hurts is out for the season with blood clots, with the team and his agent saying his prognosis is very good and a full recovery is expected. The NFL and the players union are also reviewing how his situation was handled. So this is a franchise dealing with a lot at once, on and off the field.

Let us start with the case for keeping him, because it is not a small one.

In the regular season, Nick Sirianni is sixty-one and thirty-one. That is a winning percentage of six sixty-three. In the playoffs, he is six and four. He has made the playoffs in every full season he has coached, five out of five. He has taken the Eagles to two Super Bowls. And he won the Lombardi Trophy a little more than twenty months ago. Those are not the numbers of a coach who got lucky once.

And here is the stat that surprises a lot of people. According to Kempski, among active head coaches with at least three full seasons of experience, Sirianni has the best winning percentage in the league. Kevin O’Connell is right behind him at six sixty-two. Then Matt LaFleur at six forty-nine. Andy Reid, one of the greatest coaches of all time, is at six forty-two. Sean McVay is at six oh nine. Kyle Shanahan is at five sixty-four. Eagles Nation, think about that for a second. On paper, Nick Sirianni has a better winning percentage than Andy Reid and Sean McVay. That is why the case for keeping him cannot be dismissed as blind loyalty.

The second reason he could stay is his relationship with Howie Roseman. According to Kempski, Roseman has full control of roster decisions, and Sirianni is comfortable with that setup. He focuses on coaching and lets Roseman build the roster. The two seem to get along, and after the disappointing finish to the 2023 season, Roseman supported Sirianni keeping his job. What happened next? A Super Bowl championship. That history matters, and it will make the organization think very carefully before moving on. Continuity between the head coach and the general manager has been one of the foundations of the Eagles’ success over the last five years.

The third reason is the locker room. Players have generally spoken well of Sirianni, and they buy into the fundamentals his staff teaches. And after Monday night, the players were still fighting for him. Landon Dickerson said, quote, nobody’s quitting, and that’s what you want to see. End quote. And Saquon Barkley addressed the team after the game, which he said was unusual for him, and told his teammates they have to keep fighting. When a team is losing, the head coach needs the players to keep listening. Right now, there is no sign that they have stopped. And that is the single most important thing a struggling coach can have.

And the fourth reason is practical. Who would replace him? Kempski points out that firing Sirianni could leave the Eagles with less attractive options. Philadelphia is one of the most demanding markets in sports. The job reportedly requires the coach to defer to Roseman on the roster, and some top candidates want more control than that. And candidates would remember that the Eagles moved on from Doug Pederson only three seasons after he won a Super Bowl. If they also moved on from Sirianni after a championship, some coaches might wonder how much time they would actually get.

Now, let us look at the case against him, because this is where it gets hard to defend.

Sirianni is an offensive head coach. He has given Vic Fangio a lot of room to run the defense, so the offense is the part of the team most closely tied to Sirianni’s leadership and philosophy. And the offense has been disappointing for two straight seasons.

In 2025, the warning signs were already there. According to PhillyVoice, the Eagles averaged twenty-two point one points per game, which ranked nineteenth. They averaged three hundred eleven yards per game, which ranked twenty-fourth. They gained five point two yards per play, twentieth in the league. And they averaged just four point one yards per carry, twenty-fifth in the league. That is with Jalen Hurts, Saquon Barkley, A.J. Brown, DeVonta Smith, Dallas Goedert, Lane Johnson, Jordan Mailata, Landon Dickerson and Cam Jurgens. That is a roster full of Pro Bowlers and All-Pros. And the offense ranked in the bottom half in almost every major category.

And in 2026, it has gotten worse. Through seven games, according to PhillyVoice, the Eagles are averaging eighteen point one points per game, which ranks twenty-ninth. They are averaging two hundred sixty-four point nine yards per game, thirty-first. And four point five yards per play, which ranks dead last in the league. Thirty-second out of thirty-two. Their success rate ranks thirtieth. They average five point eight yards per pass attempt, which ranks twenty-ninth. And their time of possession ranks thirty-first. Kempski described the structure of this offense as nearly nonfunctional.

Now, injuries are a real part of this. Jalen Hurts is out for the season. Saquon has been in and out. DeVonta Smith keeps fighting his hamstring. Lane Johnson retired. Cam Jurgens was traded. No offense can operate at full strength with that many important players missing. But the problem is that the offense was already struggling before the injuries piled up, and it has not shown a clear identity even when the lineup was healthier.

The next issue is the coordinator hires. After the Super Bowl season in 2024, Kellen Moore left to become head coach of the New Orleans Saints. Sirianni promoted Kevin Patullo, who had been with him since 2021, and spoke publicly about the value of continuity. But the offense did not live up to that trust, and Patullo was fired after the 2025 season. Then the Eagles hired Sean Mannion to bring in a new scheme and a fresh start. And through seven games, the offense has taken a step backward. Kempski argues that both of those hires reflect poorly on Sirianni, or on Roseman if Sirianni did not have a major role in them.

And then there is game management, which falls directly on the head coach. Sirianni does not call the plays, and the scheme is not his. But the big decisions are. In 2025, the Eagles became ultra-conservative in the second half of several games after building leads. This season, against the Bears, the Eagles passed on a fourth and one, took a delay of game penalty, and punted. And just three minutes later, in a similar spot, they went for it on fourth and five. And then there was London. According to PhillyVoice, the Eagles left eight seconds on the clock at the end of the game against Jacksonville. Ameer Abdullah returned the kick fifty-three yards, and Cam Little hit a fifty-seven-yard field goal to win it. Sirianni said afterward, quote, I put that fully on me. End quote. To his credit, he owned it. But those are exactly the moments that make fans lose patience.

Now, let us talk about the most important person in all of this. Jeffrey Lurie.

Kempski points out that Lurie has a history of acting quickly when he believes a coach has run his course. He fired Andy Reid after a four and twelve season in 2012, after fourteen years together. He fired Chip Kelly in the middle of the 2015 season. And he moved on from Doug Pederson after a four, eleven and one season in 2020, just three seasons after Pederson delivered the franchise’s first Super Bowl title.

But here is the key point. According to Kempski, Lurie likes Sirianni, and an in-season firing feels unlikely. That is the honest read from one of the most plugged-in reporters covering this team. So when you see a headline saying Sirianni has been fired, or that Lurie has already made his decision, understand that it is not what the reporting says. What the reporting says is that Sirianni likely needs a dramatic turnaround, and probably some postseason success, to feel secure about his job beyond this season.

And how is Sirianni handling the pressure? On Wednesday, he was asked if the Eagles had an effort problem against Dallas. And he was firm. According to the Delaware News Journal, he said the team grades every play on effort, in games and in practice, and he said, quote, I did not see an effort issue on Monday night. End quote. He admitted the team does not play perfect games. And when he was asked about the lack of pass rush, he explained that blitzing is hard against Dallas because it leaves fewer players to cover CeeDee Lamb and George Pickens. He also praised rookie Makai Lemon, calling him tough and hard-working after Lemon’s seven catches and his first career touchdown.

Now, the injury report for Sunday. On Wednesday, Saquon Barkley did not practice with hamstring and back injuries. DeVonta Smith did not practice with his hamstring. Dontayvion Wicks, Britain Covey, rookie linebacker Jihaad Campbell and Jonathan Jones also did not practice. Nolan Smith was limited as he works through the concussion protocol, along with Jonathan Greenard, Jalyx Hunt, Moro Ojomo and Michael Carter the second. On the good side, Dallas Goedert, Landon Dickerson, Drew Kendall, Tyler Steen, Zack Baun and Jakorian Bennett were all full participants. And according to the Delaware News Journal, Saquon says he expects to play against Washington. We will see how the rest of the week goes.

And remember, Sirianni has been in tough spots before and found his way out. In 2021, his first season, the Eagles started two and five, exactly where they are right now, and they made the playoffs. In 2023, the Eagles started ten and one and then collapsed, losing six of their last seven games including the playoffs. A lot of people wanted him gone then too. Lurie and Roseman kept him, he made changes to his staff, and the next season the Eagles won the Super Bowl. That does not guarantee anything this time. But it is a reminder that writing off Nick Sirianni has not worked out well for people in the past.

So what does Sirianni need to do to turn this around? In my opinion, four things. First, give this offense an identity. Whether it is the quick passing game with Tanner McKee and Makai Lemon, or a commitment to the run when Saquon is healthy, the Eagles need to know who they are on offense. Second, be aggressive in the big moments. No more passing on short fourth downs and no more leaving time on the clock. Third, fix the run defense with Vic Fangio, because two hundred four rushing yards against Dallas cannot happen again. And fourth, keep the locker room together. As long as the players keep fighting for him, he has a chance.

Now, the next test comes Sunday night in Washington. The Commanders are one, four and one, coming off a loss in San Francisco and a bye week. They are rested, and they are desperate too. For the Eagles, this is about as close to a must-win game as you can get in early November. A win, and the Eagles are three and five with the Giants up next and the trade deadline on November tenth. A loss, and the questions about Sirianni, the deadline and the rest of this season get even louder.
