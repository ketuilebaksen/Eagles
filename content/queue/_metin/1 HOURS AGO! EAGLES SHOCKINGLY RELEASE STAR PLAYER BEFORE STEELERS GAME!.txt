Eagles Nation, you may have seen a headline saying the Eagles just shockingly released a star player before the Steelers game. Let me clear that up right away, because it is not true. The Eagles have not released a star this week. The only player the Eagles released in the last few days was defensive back Shaun Wade, a depth player who was let go from the practice squad. And the big-name releases you might be thinking of, Hollywood Brown and veteran tackle Fred Johnson, happened last week, before the bye ended. But that does not mean nothing is happening. In fact, the Eagles have been one of the busiest teams in the league since Monday. They brought back a coach from both Super Bowl staffs, they promoted a franchise legend who is now chasing history, they brought back a running back for the third time this season, and they added a nose tackle to help replace Jordan Davis. So let me give you the real story.

Let me start with the releases, so we are all on the same page.

According to Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice, the Eagles released defensive back Shaun Wade from the practice squad on Monday, November sixteenth, as part of a group of roster moves. That is the only release from this week. And according to the Inquirer, wide receiver and returner Xavier Gipson left the Eagles’ practice squad when the New York Giants signed him to their roster. Practice squad players can be signed by any team at any time, so that was the Giants’ move, not the Eagles’.

Last week was the bigger week for departures. According to ESPN sources, as reported by Yardbarker, Hollywood Brown asked for his release, and the Eagles let him go. And according to Ian Rapoport of NFL Media, as reported by Pro Football Talk, the Eagles released veteran offensive tackle Fred Johnson. According to Jeff Neiburg of the Inquirer, those two moves, plus the Jordan Davis trade, were the biggest changes coming out of the trade deadline and the bye week.

So if you saw a headline about a star being released this week, you can relax. Nobody important is gone. In fact, a lot more is coming in than going out, and that is a very different story from the one those headlines are telling.

Now, let me get to the real news, starting with the coaching move.

According to ESPN’s Adam Schefter, the Eagles brought back TJ Paganetti as a consultant. And according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, this is not a brand new idea for the Eagles. Under Nick Sirianni, the team has used consultants before. Jim Bob Cooter had a similar role in 2021, and Marcus Brady had one in 2022 and 2023. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Paganetti will give advice to Howie Roseman and Sirianni, but the exact details of his job have not been made public.

And Paganetti knows this building as well as anyone. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he first worked with Chip Kelly as a student at Oregon. He joined the Eagles in 2013 as an offensive and quarterback analyst. He went back to Oregon in 2015 and 2016, then returned to Philadelphia under Doug Pederson in 2017. From 2017 to 2020, he worked as a quality control coach and assistant offensive line coach under Jeff Stoutland, then as assistant running backs coach in 2019 and assistant run game coordinator in 2020. Under Sirianni, he was a quality control coach in 2021, a run game specialist and assistant tight ends coach in 2023, and a run game specialist and assistant offensive line coach in 2024. That is ten seasons and two Super Bowl staffs.

According to Athlon Sports, citing Schefter, the 2024 Eagles set a franchise record with three thousand eight hundred sixty-six rushing yards, with Paganetti on the staff. Then he left to become the run game coordinator for the New Orleans Saints under Kellen Moore. And according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the Saints let him go earlier this month, when they were three and six.

And here is a quote I really like. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Jason Kelce said, quote, he brought a ton of value for us in Philadelphia. End quote. When the center from the best offensive lines in franchise history says that about you, it means something.

Now, let me be careful here, because a lot of the online coverage is getting this wrong. Paganetti is not the new offensive coordinator. Sean Mannion is still the offensive coordinator. And Paganetti is not taking over the offensive line. Jeff Stoutland is no longer the Eagles’ offensive line coach. The line is coached by Chris Kuper now. So this is not a coaching overhaul. It is a consultant, someone who can look at the tape, give ideas and help a running game that, according to Athlon Sports, ranks in the bottom five in the league with seven hundred ninety-seven yards and three point seven yards per carry.

And there is one more thing to keep in mind. The Saints’ rushing attack did not take off during his time there. So this is not a magic button. The offense in Philadelphia today is not the same one Paganetti knew in 2024. The real test is whether his experience can turn into adjustments that fit this roster.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I like this move. It is not a desperate firing, and it is not a panic hire. It is a smart, low-risk way to bring in someone who understands what worked here and can help fix the most broken part of this offense. If the running game looks even a little better against the Steelers, it will be worth it.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you like bringing back Paganetti. Comment number two if you would rather see brand new voices.

Now, the second big story. Zach Ertz.

According to the Inquirer, the Eagles signed Ertz to the active roster on November sixteenth. He is thirty-six years old. He signed with the practice squad in late September and was elevated for three games, from Week 3 through Week 5. According to PhillyVoice, he caught three passes for twenty-two yards on eight targets in those games. And according to the Inquirer, he had used up his practice squad elevations, which is why the Eagles had to sign him to the fifty-three-man roster to keep using him.

And here is the history. According to the Inquirer, Ertz has five hundred eighty-two catches with the Eagles. Harold Carmichael holds the franchise record with five hundred eighty-nine, from 1971 to 1983. So Ertz needs seven catches to tie the record, and eight to break it. That distinction matters. Seven ties him with a legend. Eight makes him the all-time leader in Eagles history.

And according to Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice, quote, Ertz’s function on the roster for the rest of this season is basically just to break that record. End quote. That is Kempski’s opinion. But it tells you how much this milestone means.

Now, I want to be fair here. There is no official plan from the Eagles to force-feed Ertz the ball. Nobody has promised he will break the record. And the tight end room is crowded. According to the Inquirer, the Eagles now have Ertz, Dallas Goedert, rookie Eli Stowers, Johnny Mundt and Grant Calcaterra on the roster, and the question is how the playing time will be split. According to NJ.com, Goedert has thirty-one catches for three hundred eight yards and two touchdowns. And according to PhillyVoice, Stowers has played just thirty-two snaps in the last three games, and a lot of analysts want him to get more.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I would love to see Ertz break that record. He was a huge part of the team that won the franchise’s first Super Bowl, and he caught the go-ahead touchdown in that game. He has earned this moment. But the offense cannot become a record-chasing exercise. If Ertz finds space on third down, throw him the ball. If he helps convert a tough possession, great. But Stowers still needs real snaps, because he is the future. You can do both. Ertz on a few key downs, Stowers in a bigger rotation, and Goedert still leading the room.

Now, let me walk you through every other move from this week, because there were a lot of them.

First, offensive tackle John Ojukwu. According to the Inquirer, he was promoted from the practice squad to the active roster. According to PhillyVoice, he was an undrafted free agent out of Boise State in 2023. He played sixteen games with nine starts for the Tennessee Titans. And in two starts in 2025, according to PhillyVoice, Pro Football Focus charged him with four sacks. So he is a depth piece, not a starter. But with Fred Johnson gone, he is a swing tackle option behind Jordan Mailata and rookie Markel Bell.

Second, running back Will Shipley. According to Heavy, citing NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport, his twenty-one-day practice window opened on Monday. According to the Inquirer, he has been on injured reserve since Week 5 with a foot injury. Before that, according to Heavy, he had twenty carries for fifty-seven yards and one touchdown. According to Kempski, he is probably the best pass-protecting running back on the roster. Opening the window does not mean he will play on Sunday, and there is no timeline. But it is a step. And according to the Inquirer, Tank Bigsby, who had core muscle surgery after Week 4, is also eligible to come off injured reserve, but his practice window has not been opened yet.

Third, Jaydon Blue. And this one is a wild story. According to the team, Blue was promoted to the active roster on Tuesday, November seventeenth. That is his third time on the active roster this season. He was waived on November tenth, signed to the practice squad on November sixteenth, and then promoted again the very next day. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he was a 2025 fifth-round pick by the Dallas Cowboys, and as a rookie he had thirty-four carries for one hundred twenty-nine yards and a touchdown. And he has been the Eagles’ main kickoff returner. So now Saquon Barkley is no longer the only running back on the active roster.

Fourth, defensive tackle Leki Fotu. According to the team, he signed to the practice squad on November seventeenth after his tryout. According to the team, he is twenty-eight years old, six foot five and three hundred thirty pounds. He was a 2020 fourth-round pick by Arizona out of Utah, and he has played for Arizona, the Jets, the Raiders, the Texans and the Giants. He has sixty-six games with twenty-six starts, one hundred three tackles, four and a half sacks, fifteen tackles for loss, a forced fumble and a fumble recovery. And according to the team, he grew up in Herriman, Utah, and played rugby before football. Since the Davis trade, the Eagles have not had a true classic nose tackle, and Fotu could fill that role.

And finally, according to the team, wide receiver Quentin Skinner was brought back to the practice squad. Linebacker Chance Campbell and tight end Hayden Rucci were also added to the practice squad. According to the team, Rucci played six games for the Chargers, catching one pass for seven yards and a touchdown.

So think about this week. Ed Kracz of Eagles On SI pointed out that the Eagles had four open roster spots after the Davis trade and the releases of Blue, Brown and Johnson. Ertz, Ojukwu and Blue have now filled three of them. That is a lot of movement for a two and seven team.

Now, according to Jeff Neiburg of the Inquirer, in an article published on November seventeenth, there are still some big questions the Eagles have to answer coming out of the bye.

The first is the coaching staff. According to Neiburg, Sirianni initially did not rule out staff changes, then said none had been made. Paganetti is the only addition so far, and Neiburg asked whether he will be the only change.

And Neiburg also laid out the current offensive line. Jordan Mailata is the left tackle and rookie Markel Bell is the right tackle. And here is a detail a lot of fans did not know. According to Neiburg, Lane Johnson is retired, but he is still on the roster for salary cap reasons. So when you look at the roster and see his name, that is why. It does not mean he is coming back.

The second is the receivers. According to Neiburg, DeVonta Smith has played in four of nine games with a hamstring issue, and Dontayvion Wicks has played in six games with an ankle issue. Both have missed the last three games, but neither was placed on injured reserve. And Neiburg suggested that releasing Hollywood Brown may be a sign that they are close to coming back.

The third is linebacker. According to Neiburg, Jihaad Campbell may return from hamstring and quad injuries. Before he got hurt, he was rotating with Jeremiah Trotter Junior next to Zack Baun.

And the fourth is quarterback. According to Neiburg, Joe Milton started against the Giants, but the offense did not improve. So the question is whether Tanner McKee gets the job back this week, or whether rookie fifth-round pick Cole Payton gets a look. According to the team, Hurts is on injured reserve and is not expected to play again this season, and the team and his agent have said they expect a full recovery.

And there is a bigger conversation happening too. According to Kempski, in an article published on November seventeenth, the Eagles should shift their focus to the long term over the final eight games. He said they should protect injured players like Saquon Barkley, who had thirteen carries for twenty-two yards against the Giants while playing through pain. He wants more snaps for young players like Eli Stowers, guards Willie Lampkin and Micah Morris, defensive tackle Ty Robinson, edge rusher Keyshawn James-Newby and quarterback Cole Payton. And he even suggested that Payton could start in Week 18 if Milton struggles. That is Kempski’s opinion. And he also said he sees a stronger case for moving on from Sirianni, while Mike Garafolo of NFL Network said, according to The Spun, quote, everything I’m hearing, and you’re hearing, is that he should be safe. End quote.

Kempski also made two more points worth mentioning. First, he wants the Eagles to scout practice squads around the league and watch the waiver wire closely, because a two and seven record gives them a high waiver priority. If a good young player gets cut somewhere else, the Eagles will be near the front of the line to claim him. And second, he pointed back to the end of the 2020 season, saying the Eagles are still feeling the benefits of that year. Remember, that was the season that led to the draft where the Eagles moved up and took DeVonta Smith. So there is some history behind the idea that a bad season can turn into a great pick.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I agree with a lot of what Kempski said about young players. They need snaps. But I will never root for this team to lose. These players are competing for jobs and for their careers. The best thing that can happen is for the young players to play well and for the Eagles to win a few games along the way.

Now, the Steelers. According to the Inquirer, the Eagles host Pittsburgh on Sunday, November twenty-second, at one in the afternoon Eastern on CBS. The Steelers lost thirty-eight to eighteen to the Bengals on Sunday Night Football, dropping them to six and three. According to Ed Kracz of Eagles On SI, Nick Sirianni is five and zero coming out of bye weeks in his career, including a thirty-five to thirteen win over the Steelers in 2022. And the Steelers have not won a game in Philadelphia since 1965. That is zero wins in eleven tries. So history is on the Eagles’ side, even in a season like this one. A win on Sunday would snap the seven-game losing streak, extend Sirianni’s perfect record after byes to six and zero, and give this young roster something to build on before the short week leading into Thanksgiving in Dallas.

Here is what I will be watching. First, whether DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks are on the field. Second, who starts at quarterback. Third, whether the running game looks different with Paganetti in the building and Blue and maybe Shipley in the backfield. Fourth, whether Ertz gets closer to the record. And fifth, whether Leki Fotu is elevated to help the run defense without Jordan Davis.
