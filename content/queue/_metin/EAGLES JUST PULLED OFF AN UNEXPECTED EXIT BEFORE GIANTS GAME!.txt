Eagles Nation, before the Giants come to Lincoln Financial Field on Sunday, the Eagles have already made some exits that a lot of fans did not see coming. Let me be clear right away about what this is and what it is not. There is no blockbuster trade, and no star has been shipped out of Philadelphia. But this week, the Eagles moved on from a player they traded for just last year. They pushed Tanner McKee out of the starting lineup, only two days after calling him their guy. And with the trade deadline on Tuesday, more names are being linked to possible exits. So let us go through all of it.

This is James with Second Floor, and today we are breaking down every exit and every rumor. The cornerback the Eagles waived, why Tanner McKee is out and Joe Milton the third is in, the players other teams are calling about, what ESPN is saying about a possible rebuild, and the offense that some say is too complicated for this roster. Before we start, if you are still riding with this team, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor.

Let me start with the exit that surprised a lot of people.

On Tuesday, the Eagles announced that they waived cornerback Jakorian Bennett from injured reserve. According to the team’s official website, the Eagles acquired Bennett in a trade with the Las Vegas Raiders before the 2025 season. He was a fourth-round pick out of Maryland in 2023. And according to Brandon Lee Gowton of Bleeding Green Nation, the Eagles gave up defensive tackle Thomas Booker the fourth in that trade.

Bennett’s time in Philadelphia never really took off. According to the team, he dealt with a pectoral injury in 2025 and recorded seven tackles over eleven games in his first season. Then he suffered a knee injury in training camp and was placed on injured reserve on August thirtieth. His practice window opened ahead of Week 6, and he returned to practice, but the Eagles never activated him. And now he is gone. As Gowton put it, quote, instead, Bennett is gone. End quote. And according to Gowton, Booker has continued to play a significant role for the Raiders. That is the part of this trade that will sting for some fans.

And that was not the only move. The Eagles also released wide receiver Quentin Skinner from the practice squad, just a week after signing him. And the team signed cornerback Christian Braswell to the practice squad. These are small moves, but they tell you the Eagles are reshaping the bottom of the roster before the deadline. And think about what a roster spot means right now. With so many injuries at cornerback, receiver and on the offensive line, every spot on the active roster and the practice squad matters. Keeping a player on injured reserve who is not close to helping does not make sense for a team that needs healthy bodies every week. So while the Bennett move is surprising, because the Eagles gave up a real player to get him, it is also a sign that Howie Roseman is not afraid to admit when something did not work and move on.

Now, the bigger exit. Tanner McKee is out as the starting quarterback.

On Monday, Nick Sirianni said, quote, Tanner’s our guy. We’re not making any changes there. End quote. Two days later, according to ESPN’s Adam Schefter and NFL Network’s Mike Garafolo, the Eagles decided to start Joe Milton the third against the Giants. According to NFL dot com, McKee started three games after Jalen Hurts went down, and the Eagles lost all three. He completed seventy-two of one hundred fourteen passes for six hundred thirty-eight yards, with two touchdowns and two interceptions.

And this is McKee’s second exit from a job this year. According to NFL dot com, he lost the preseason backup battle to Andy Dalton and started the season as the third quarterback. Then, according to PennLive, Dalton was cut after an interception in Week 3 against the Bears. That opened the door for McKee, and when Jalen was placed on injured reserve with blood clots, McKee became the starter. Now, after three losses, that door has closed again.

But I have to give McKee credit for the way he handled it. On the team’s official website, he said, quote, I’m here to help us win, no matter what that means. End quote. He said, quote, I am going to stay ready and learn from the experience and be at my best when my number is called again. End quote. And he said, quote, Joe is doing a great job, and I think he’s going to go out there and play a good game on Sunday. End quote. That is a professional, Eagles Nation.

So who is Joe Milton? He is twenty-six years old and known for his big arm. He played at Michigan and Tennessee, and according to the New York Post, he went thirteen and eight as a college starter. The Patriots drafted him in the sixth round in 2024. In Week 18 of his rookie season, he played most of the game and completed twenty-two of twenty-nine passes for two hundred forty-one yards and a touchdown, and he ran for another. Then he was traded to Dallas, where he appeared in four games last season, completing fifteen of twenty-four passes for one hundred eighty-three yards, with a touchdown and two interceptions. He did not make the Cowboys’ fifty-three-man roster this summer, and the Eagles signed him off their practice squad on October sixteenth.

Milton has never started an NFL game. But listen to how he talked this week. Quote, we all know that you have to be ready to be on the field at all times. So, I’m going to be ready on Sunday. End quote. And, quote, I’m going to play my game and stay within the offense, and that’s the job. End quote.

Now, let me talk about Saquon Barkley, because he is a big part of what Milton needs on Sunday. Saquon did not practice on Wednesday, but on Thursday, according to Eagles Wire, he was upgraded to a limited participant with his hamstring and back injuries. With Dameon Pierce on injured reserve, this team needs Saquon in the run game. And here is a report every Eagles fan should hear. According to PhillyVoice, multiple reports from the ESPN and NFL Network media family said the Eagles will not trade Saquon Barkley before the deadline. So that is one exit that is not happening.

And one more important update. The injury report is still long. According to Eagles Wire, DeVonta Smith missed his second straight practice on Thursday with a hamstring injury, and Dontayvion Wicks did not practice with an ankle injury. Jihaad Campbell, Michael Carter the second, Britain Covey and Jonathan Jones also sat out. Jake Elliott was limited with a right ankle issue. And Tanner McKee was limited on Thursday with a rib injury. So Milton might not have his top two receivers on Sunday. That makes Dallas Goedert, Makai Lemon and Eli Stowers even more important.

And there was one entrance this week that every Eagles fan loved. According to ESPN’s Tim McManus, Jalen Hurts made an appearance at the team’s practice facility on Wednesday and sat in on meetings. He did not address the team, but according to a source cited by McManus, his presence, quote, did provide a morale boost for some. End quote. And Sirianni said, quote, I miss him like crazy. End quote. Jalen is still out for the season, and the team expects a full recovery. But seeing him back in the building is the best news this team has had in weeks.

Now, let us talk about the exits that could still happen.

According to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, his sense is that the Eagles, quote, could be trading some players but likely won’t engage in a rebuild. End quote. So do not expect a fire sale. But do expect calls. And here are the names that keep coming up.

First, Moro Ojomo. According to Pro Football Rumors, citing Fowler, the Jacksonville Jaguars are, quote, prepared to be aggressive, end quote, in adding to their defensive line, and Ojomo is on their radar. The Jaguars are five and two, and they lost DaVon Hamilton for the season. According to PhillyVoice, other teams are interested in Ojomo too. But here is the key. Albert Breer of Sports Illustrated reported that the Eagles told teams Ojomo is not available, and according to PhillyVoice, the ESPN report said Philadelphia is, quote, inclined to keep and potentially extend him. End quote.

So why is his name out there at all? Money. Ojomo is twenty-five, a former seventh-round pick, and he is in a contract year. According to Pro Football Rumors, he had six sacks last season, none so far this year, but ESPN’s pass rush win rate ranks him fifteenth among interior defensive linemen. And the Eagles have already extended Jordan Davis and Jalen Carter. Paying a third defensive tackle is expensive. Pro Football Rumors also pointed out that if Ojomo leaves in free agency, the Eagles could get a compensatory pick in 2028. And remember, the Eagles have shown they will sell for the right price.

Second, Hollywood Brown. Back on October twentieth, Jordan Schultz reported that Brown, quote, is a name that teams seeking WR help believe could be traded in the coming weeks. End quote. According to PhillyVoice, Brown’s agent worked with Schultz on that post. And Jimmy Kempski called the report plausible, noting that Brown was a healthy scratch and is barely playing. Last season with the Chiefs, according to Newsweek, Brown caught forty-nine passes for five hundred eighty-seven yards and five touchdowns. He has gone over five hundred receiving yards in six of his previous seven seasons. In Philadelphia, he simply has not found a role. If a team needs a veteran receiver, this could be a move that helps both sides.

And there are other names out there too. Fowler has put the odds of Riq Woolen being traded at thirty-five percent and Landon Dickerson at twenty-five percent. The Athletic’s Daniel Popper named Denver as the best trade fit for guard Tyler Steen. A Boston columnist pitched the Patriots calling about Dallas Goedert. And Jimmy Kempski wrote that DeVonta Smith isn’t going anywhere.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you would sell veterans like Hollywood Brown before the deadline. Comment number two if you would keep everybody and fight for the season.

Now, there is one more story this week that matters a lot, because it might explain why the Eagles made the quarterback change. The offense itself.

According to John McMullen of Eagles On SI, the Eagles’ offense may simply be too complicated for the players on the roster right now. Let us go back to the play everybody is still talking about. The Eagles trailed sixteen to ten with just over nine minutes left, at the Washington thirteen-yard line. Sirianni decided to go for it on fourth and four. Then rookie right tackle Markel Bell jumped before the snap. Jake Elliott’s field goal was wiped out by an illegal formation penalty on Jordan Mailata. And Elliott missed the longer try. Washington went on to win twenty-seven to ten.

When Sean Mannion was asked about that sequence, he said, quote, I think any quarterback’s going to want to gather some degree of information at the line of scrimmage. End quote. He said, quote, sometimes it’s because you may have a kill on the play; sometimes it’s not. End quote. And he said, quote, obviously, that was unfortunate with us jumping offsides there. End quote.

McMullen’s point is that the false start was the visible mistake, but the real problem is how much this offense asks of everyone before the snap. The system is influenced by Kyle Shanahan and Sean McVay. It uses a lot of pre-snap motion, different formations and play action. When it works, it confuses defenses. But with a backup quarterback, a rebuilt offensive line and missing receivers, it can confuse your own players. McMullen even suggested that starting Joe Milton might be a way for Sirianni to push his coordinator to simplify things.

And this is not the first time we have heard this. Back in October, after the loss to Carolina, Zach Berman of The Athletic wrote that the offense was the constant, with Jalen Hurts and with Tanner McKee, and that it needed more flexibility. And remember, Sirianni said on Monday that he is not changing his offensive coordinator. Berman also pointed out some ugly numbers from that Carolina game. The Eagles punted on their first five possessions, and at that point they led the NFL in punts and in three-and-out percentage. And their efficiency against the blitz was negative for the first time in the Sirianni era. That is a sign that defenses have figured out how to attack this offense. And according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the Eagles have now allowed a free rusher on eighteen point two percent of pass plays, the most in the league. So the fix has to come from Mannion himself.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. Simplify. Joe Milton has been in this building for three weeks. Do not ask him to run the full playbook against a Giants defense that will be rested after its bye. Give him easy reads. Let him use his legs. Throw in some deep shots for that big arm. Lean on Saquon when he can go. And above all, cut down on the pre-snap penalties. You do not need to fool the defense if you cannot get out of the huddle cleanly.

And let me tell you what Milton is walking into on Sunday. The Giants come to Lincoln Financial Field at one in the afternoon Eastern, on FOX. They are four, two and one, and according to Sports Illustrated, they are three-and-a-half-point favorites on the road. But they have their own problems. According to PhillyVoice, Jaxson Dart is out for the season, so J.J. McCarthy is their quarterback. Brian Burns is also done for the year. And running back Cam Skattebo missed practice on Wednesday with a hamstring injury. Giants coach John Harbaugh said, according to the New York Post, quote, we’ll try to anticipate what exactly they’re gonna run with him, but it’s still gonna be their offense. End quote.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the people who say these exits are a sign of a team falling apart. They will say waiving Bennett proves the trade for him did not work. They will say benching McKee two days after calling him the guy shows the coaching staff does not have a plan. They will say Hollywood Brown never fit, that Ojomo is going to leave anyway, and that the Eagles should sell now and get something before it is too late. And they will say the offense is broken, and that keeping Mannion is a mistake.

On the other side, the people who say these are smart moves. They will say cutting ties with an injured player who was not going to help this year is just good roster management. They will say Sirianni deserves credit for making a change at quarterback instead of standing still. They will say keeping Saquon and Ojomo shows the Eagles are not giving up on the future. They will say Fowler is right, and that there will not be a rebuild. And they will say that if Mannion simplifies the offense for Milton, this team still has the talent to win games.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I am fine with the Bennett move. It was not working, and the Eagles needed the roster spot to mean something. I like the Joe Milton change, because doing the same thing over and over was not working either. On the trade front, I would listen on Hollywood Brown, because he is not part of the plan here and a contender could use him. But I keep Moro Ojomo unless someone blows Howie Roseman away. Young, cheap defensive linemen who can win their matchups are hard to find, and he could be part of this defense for years. And I absolutely keep Saquon.

So let me wrap it up. The Eagles made some exits before the Giants game. They waived cornerback Jakorian Bennett from injured reserve, a player they traded Thomas Booker for last year. They released Quentin Skinner from the practice squad and signed Christian Braswell. Tanner McKee is out as the starter, and Joe Milton the third will make his first NFL start on Sunday. Saquon is not being traded. Ojomo is drawing interest from the Jaguars, but the Eagles are inclined to keep him. Hollywood Brown could be moved. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler says the Eagles likely won’t rebuild. And Sean Mannion’s offense is under the microscope. The Giants are next on Sunday at one in the afternoon Eastern, and the trade deadline is Tuesday, November tenth, at four in the afternoon Eastern.
