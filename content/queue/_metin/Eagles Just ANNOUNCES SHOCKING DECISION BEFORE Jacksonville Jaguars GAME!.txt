He had missed Sunday's game against the Rams for what the team called personal reasons, after taking first-team reps all week. And in his statement, he explained why. He said, quote, "Right now, it is critical that I focus on my mental health. I owe it to my teammates, coaches, the organization and the fans to have my head and heart 100 percent in the game."

Eagles Nation, I want to say this clearly. That takes courage. A lot of it. And whatever this means for the football team, the only thing that matters first is Lane Johnson the person. We wish him and his family nothing but the best. Respect. Full stop.

And what a career it was. Fourteen seasons, all in Philadelphia. 171 games played, every single one of them a start. Fifteen playoff starts. Six Pro Bowls. Two first-team All-Pro selections. Three Super Bowl appearances. And two Super Bowl championships. For more than a decade, the right side of this offensive line was simply closed. Edge rushers knew it. Defensive coordinators knew it. And Eagles chairman Jeffrey Lurie said it best, calling Johnson "an all-time Eagle," and saying he's confident Lane will one day be in the Pro Football Hall of Fame. I agree with every word.

Markel Bell made his first career start against the Rams. Fred Johnson, the veteran swing tackle, has missed two straight games with a knee issue. So the right side of the line, which for years was the safest place on the field, is now a question mark. And that's why the next part of this story matters so much.

So when I say there's real good news this week, I mean it. Landon Dickerson.

Dickerson went on 94WIP this week and gave an update on his knee, and honestly, it was the most calming thing I've heard from this team in two weeks. Here's what he said, quote, "I'm feeling good, feeling better. Everything is going on track, on schedule." He added that it's now up to the people upstairs, and that he has a minimum amount of time he has to meet before he can come back.

Now, let's remember what this injury is, because that part is important. Back in training camp, a few weeks before the opener, Dickerson got rolled up on in practice. It turned into a bone bruise in his right knee. And instead of getting better, it got worse. He played through it in Week 1 against Washington, and he was brutally honest about how that went. He admitted he was a complete liability out there, and that he shouldn't have been on the field until he was healthy. Pro Football Focus gave him a 37.6 grade in that game, last among starting guards. For a guy who's usually graded like a Pro Bowler, that tells you how hurt he really was.

But here's the part that gave me hope. Back in September, Dickerson explained that this isn't the kind of injury that follows you around. In his words, "Once it gets better, you don't really have to worry about it moving forward." And this week on WIP, he made the same point again, comparing it to bigger problems, like meniscus issues that can leave lasting effects, and basically saying, this is not that. After everything Dickerson's body has been through in his career, that's exactly what Eagles fans needed to hear.

So where is he in the process? He went on injured reserve after Week 1, which means at least four games. According to Dickerson, the first couple of weeks were pure rest, because rest was the main fix for a bone bruise. After that, he got back into football work and conditioning. And now it's about getting his body back into that game feeling. He's eligible to return for the Week 6 game against the Carolina Panthers on October 18th. But I want to be really clear here. There is no official return date yet. The team still has to clear him. So we can be hopeful. We just can't start counting the days.

And I really liked what he said about how he's handled being hurt. Dickerson hasn't disappeared from the building. He treats it like a normal week. He comes in early for treatment and rehab, then goes to meetings and lifts with his teammates. He said he doesn't believe in sending a guy home for a month and bringing him back cold. He also admitted that watching from the sideline is torture for him, because he wants to be out there fixing things. So he helps however he can. Scouting reports. How a certain defender likes to play. Small details like hand placement and footwork. And that matters a lot for a young guy like Drew Kendall, who's been holding down center. But Dickerson is careful about it too. He said he doesn't want to over-coach during a game or get inside a teammate's head and make things worse. That's a veteran who gets it.

And here's why his return could help in more than one spot. Think about Cam Jurgens. He's been the starting center since 2024. He started at right guard in 2023. But when Dickerson went down, the team slid Jurgens over to left guard for the first time in his career and put Drew Kendall at center starting in Week 2 against the Titans.

Dickerson said he actually feels bad for Jurgens, because that's not the job he was brought in to do. He also said he hates the idea of fixing one hole by shaking up two spots. But he understands why it happened. The team didn't have the depth you'd ideally want, so the goal was to get the best five linemen on the field.

And Eagles Nation, let me put this in context, because Cam Jurgens has taken a ton of heat this week. We talked about it in our last video. Changing positions in the middle of a season is incredibly hard. It usually only happens in an emergency, or in the offseason when you have time to prepare. Your stance changes. Your footwork changes. Your pass sets change. Dickerson said he has a lot of respect for how Jurgens has handled it. Practicing, working on the basics, trying to be the best he can at a spot that was never supposed to be his. Jurgens did the selfless thing for the team. And now he's in the concussion protocol after self-reporting symptoms. So when Dickerson comes back, the hope is that everybody goes back to where they belong.

Now, the goal-line play. You know the one. Third-and-goal from the 2 against the Rams, up 20 to 10. The handoff got messed up, Hurts bobbled it, and it looked like Jurgens ran right into the ball. Some people, including film analyst Brett Kollmann, said Jurgens moved early and it just wasn't called. Dickerson saw it differently. He explained that near the goal line, linemen tighten their splits because the defense stacks the box and fills every gap. So Jurgens cut down his split and played it like a goal-line play, and the ball and the body ended up crashing together. Dickerson said it was a new play in this offense, it worked in practice, and it didn't work in the game. Jalen Hurts kept using the word execution after the game, and that fits. A small mistake, at the worst possible moment. Something to learn from and fix.

Now here's the part that gave me the most hope. The running game. Dickerson went back through the film and says he sees it starting to click. Players are finding a rhythm, and that only comes from reps. When you keep calling the same runs, the line starts to feel the flow. And we've seen flashes of it. Dickerson pointed to the first half in Chicago as the best the run game has looked all year, with some explosive plays, and there were flashes against the Rams too. Saquon had 82 yards on 15 carries in Chicago. But flashes have to become a full game. Then a full month.

And Dickerson was honest about what still needs fixing. Third down has been a problem, and that's putting it nicely. Two for twenty-one over the last two games. He also pointed to blitz recognition and blitz pickups. And he made a point that a lot of fans miss. That isn't only on the line. The quarterback, the linemen, the running backs and the tight ends all share that job. He said the Rams showed some looks the Eagles hadn't seen in a long time, so the lesson is simple. Practice what you see on film, and also practice what the other team hasn't shown you yet.

Which brings us to the biggest argument in Philadelphia right now. Coaching versus execution. Where are the adjustments? Dickerson explained that adjustments do happen, even in the middle of a drive. Players talk in the huddle about what the defense is showing and try something different on the next snap. And they happen at halftime too. But the other team is adjusting as well, so it almost feels like a brand new game after the break. After watching the film from above, Dickerson believes good plays were being called. The players just have to run them better.

Offensive coordinator Sean Mannion said something similar this week. He said, quote, "We believe in the things that we've installed," and that they're going to "keep leaning into that." He said it's never any one thing, it's just cleaner execution in general.

There's one more name Dickerson brought up, and it's a young one. Makai Lemon, the first-round rookie receiver. So what's the big picture? Dickerson kept coming back to one word. Process. This team has had rough starts before and found its way. Nothing flips overnight.
