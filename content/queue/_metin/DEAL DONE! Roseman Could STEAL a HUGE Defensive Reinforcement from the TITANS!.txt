Let me start with where this all came from.

According to Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice, in an article published on November eighteenth, the Eagles should look at players sitting on other teams’ practice squads. With a two and seven record and eight games left, Kempski’s point is that the Eagles should be looking for young players who could help them in the future. And he named three of them. One of them is a defensive back from the Tennessee Titans’ practice squad named Bishop Fitzgerald.

So let me be very clear before we go any further. This is a writer’s suggestion. It is not a report that the Eagles are interested, and it is definitely not a signing. Nobody has said the Eagles have reached out to Fitzgerald. But it is a smart idea, so let me explain why.

According to Kempski, Fitzgerald is a twenty-three-year-old safety. He is five foot eleven and two hundred one pounds. And his college resume is hard to ignore.

According to Justin Grasso of Heavy, Fitzgerald started his college career at NC State, where he had ninety-seven tackles, eleven pass deflections and five interceptions. Then he transferred to USC. And in his senior season at USC in 2025, he played ten games and had fifty-one tackles, two and a half sacks, three pass deflections and five more interceptions. That is ten interceptions in his college career.

And according to Kempski, quote, he was thought of as an instinctive safety against the pass and the run. End quote. That is exactly what you want from a safety. He is not just a guy who covers deep. He is a guy who finds the football and also helps against the run.

And here is the part that makes his story so interesting. According to Kempski, NFL Network’s Daniel Jeremiah ranked Fitzgerald as the one hundred thirtieth best player in the 2026 draft. That is roughly a fourth-round grade. And yet, all seven rounds went by, and nobody drafted him.

Now, let me tell you about his rookie year, because it has been a wild ride.

According to Kempski, Fitzgerald signed with the Titans as an undrafted free agent. But he was waived at the final cuts to fifty-three players. Then the Pittsburgh Steelers signed him to their practice squad. Then, according to Heavy, the Detroit Lions signed him to their active roster in September when they were dealing with injuries at safety. But he never got into a game, and he was waived in early November once their safeties got healthy. And now, according to Kempski, he has re-signed with the Titans’ practice squad.

So think about that. Tennessee, Pittsburgh, Detroit and then Tennessee again, all in his first year in the NFL. Four stops, and he still has not played in a regular-season game. That tells you two things. First, teams clearly see something in him, because three different organizations wanted him on their roster. And second, nobody has given him a real chance to play yet.

And there is a little twist for this week. He spent time on the Steelers’ practice squad earlier this season, and the Eagles play the Steelers on Sunday. So if the Eagles ever did bring him in, he would know a little bit about the team they are facing. That is just a fun connection, not a reason to sign him, but it is the kind of detail Eagles fans will enjoy.

Now, how does practice squad poaching actually work? This is important, because a lot of people think it works like a trade. It does not.

In the NFL, a team can sign a player off another team’s practice squad, as long as it signs him to its own active roster. There is no trade, and no draft pick changes hands. The team just has to open a roster spot and offer him a contract on the fifty-three-man roster. That is why it is called poaching.

And there is one more detail. Under league rules, a player who is signed off another team’s practice squad generally has to stay on his new team’s active roster for a few weeks. So this is not a move where you sign a guy, look at him for one practice and cut him the next day. If the Eagles do it, they have to be serious about it.

And the Eagles have done this before. According to Kempski, the Eagles have a long history of finding players this way. They added kicker Jake Elliott and running back Boston Scott off other teams’ practice squads in the past. And this season, according to Kempski, they signed quarterback Joe Milton off the Cowboys’ practice squad. And he is starting for them on Sunday.

So let me be fair about the cost. It is not free. According to Ed Kracz of Eagles On SI, the Eagles had four open roster spots after the trade deadline and the releases of Jaydon Blue, Hollywood Brown and Fred Johnson. Zach Ertz, John Ojukwu, Jaydon Blue and offensive tackle Ajani Cornelius, who the Eagles claimed from Dallas this week, have now filled all four of those spots. So if the Eagles wanted to sign Fitzgerald, they would have to release somebody else to make room.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you would cut a veteran to make room for Fitzgerald. Comment number two if you think the Eagles should keep the roster the way it is.

And this connects to something Kempski wrote earlier in the week. In his soft tank roadmap for the rest of the season, published on November seventeenth, Kempski said the Eagles should scout practice squads around the league and watch the waiver wire closely. Because of their two and seven record, the Eagles have a high waiver priority. That means if a promising young player gets released somewhere else, the Eagles will be near the front of the line to claim him. And that is exactly how they got Ajani Cornelius from Dallas this week. So the Fitzgerald idea is not random. It is part of a bigger plan that Kempski has been laying out for weeks.

Now, let me show you why a young safety makes sense for this team.

According to Heavy, the Eagles’ safeties right now are Michael Carter the second, Andrew Mukuba, Marcus Epps and Kenny Moore. And according to Heavy, Mukuba is the only younger, homegrown option in that group. Mukuba was a second-round pick in 2025. The rest are veterans.

And then there is the rookie. According to Ed Kracz, the Eagles drafted safety Cole Wisniewski in the seventh round of the 2026 draft, and Kracz wants him to get more snaps over the final eight games. In fact, Kracz wrote that more playing time for Wisniewski could put Marcus Epps in jeopardy. But here is what Kempski wrote about Fitzgerald. Quote, Fitzgerald feels closer to contributing in the NFL for a team than, say, Cole Wisniewski. End quote. That is his opinion, but it is a strong statement about a player who went undrafted.

And if I am the Eagles’ scouting department, here is what I would be looking at on Fitzgerald’s tape. First, his eyes. Does he read the quarterback, or does he guess? Second, his range. Can he get from the middle of the field to the sideline in time at the NFL level? Third, his tackling. A safety who makes interceptions but misses tackles is a problem. And fourth, his special teams ability, because that is where a young safety usually earns his first real snaps. Those answers are what would decide whether he is worth a roster spot. Ten interceptions get you noticed. The details get you signed. And the Eagles’ scouts have shown again and again that they know how to find those details in players other teams overlooked, from undrafted free agents to practice squad pickups.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I like this idea. The Eagles are two and seven, and the final eight games should be about finding young players for the future. A twenty-three-year-old safety with ten college interceptions is exactly the kind of low-cost bet that a rebuilding team should take. If he becomes a real NFL safety, great. If he does not, it costs a roster spot for a few weeks. But I would not throw him into the starting lineup right away. Let him learn the system, play special teams, and earn his snaps.

And there is a real argument against rushing it. A safety who made plays in college can struggle against NFL quarterbacks who move defenders with their eyes, receivers who change direction without slowing down, and coordinators who spot weaknesses in a hurry. Throwing him in as a starter just because fans want something new could expose him and hurt the defense. The smart path is to bring him in, watch how he learns, and give him a role that matches where he is.

But waiting forever has a cost too. Detroit already grabbed him once this season. If the Eagles like him, another team could reach the same conclusion. Spotting talent and acting at the right moment are two different skills.

Now, Fitzgerald was not the only name on Kempski’s list. Let me tell you about the other two.

The first is tight end Michael Trigg of the Dallas Cowboys’ practice squad. According to Kempski, he is six foot four and two hundred forty pounds. At Baylor in 2025, he caught fifty passes for six hundred ninety-four yards and six touchdowns, which was third in the nation among tight ends in receiving yards. Kempski thought he could have gone as high as Day 2 of the draft, but he went undrafted because of character concerns. And according to Kempski, Cowboys beat writer Clarence Hill called him a developmental player, quote, on and off the field. End quote. Kempski wrote that if his off-field issues are workable, Trigg makes sense as a guy you can add to the roster. But with Dallas Goedert, Eli Stowers and Zach Ertz already in the room, tight end might not be the biggest need.

The second is linebacker Red Murdock of the Denver Broncos’ practice squad. According to Kempski, he is six foot one, two hundred thirty-two pounds and twenty-three years old. At Buffalo, over his final two seasons, he had two hundred ninety-eight combined tackles, thirty tackles for loss, seven sacks, a pick-six and thirteen forced fumbles. And according to Kempski, he holds the all-time FBS career record with seventeen forced fumbles. He was the two hundred fifty-seventh and final pick of the 2026 draft, which means he was Mister Irrelevant. Kempski called him, quote, a bit of a throwback linebacker, end quote, who lacks ideal size and athleticism and was a liability in coverage. But he compared him to Eagles linebackers like T.J. Edwards, Nakobe Dean and Jeremiah Trotter Junior, who were also considered small and slow coming out of college. And Kempski said he likes Murdock’s potential to make plays on special teams.

Shipley has been on injured reserve since October sixteenth with a foot injury. If the Eagles activate him, Saquon Barkley would finally have a little more help in the backfield.
