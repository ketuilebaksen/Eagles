Let me start with the trade that everyone is talking about this week. Jordan Davis.

According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the Eagles traded Jordan Davis to the Jacksonville Jaguars right before the trade deadline. In return, the Eagles got a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick. A first and a second for a defensive tackle who, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, was averaging about thirty-six snaps per game and was third among the Eagles' defensive tackles in playing time for most of the season.

And this was not an easy goodbye. According to theScore, Davis said he tried not to cry as he left the Eagles for the Jaguars. He was a fan favorite, a Super Bowl champion and a big part of this defense for years. So let me be clear. This is not about knocking Jordan Davis as a person. This is about the value of the trade.

Now, here is what happened this weekend. Davis made his debut for Jacksonville on Sunday against the New York Giants. And according to one Eagles analyst, Davis had zero pressures and one tackle in thirty-four snaps in that game. The Jaguars lost to the Giants twenty-three to eighteen, according to the official score data. And that analyst even showed a post from a frustrated Jaguars fan on social media who was not happy with Davis's effort on a big play. I am not going to read you the exact words, because they were not exactly family friendly. But you get the point. Jaguars fans did not love what they saw.

And on the same day, in Philadelphia, the Eagles' defensive line without Jordan Davis sacked Aaron Rodgers five times. According to Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice, defensive tackle Byron Young had the first two sacks of his season. And Gabe Hall, who according to Kempski had been waived or released four times in three seasons, got the first sack of his NFL career. The Steelers ran the ball twenty times for just seventy-three yards, according to the official game data, and their longest run was twelve yards.

Think about that. The run defense was supposed to fall apart without Jordan Davis. Instead, it might have played its best game of the year.

Now, let me be fair. One game does not decide a trade. Davis just arrived in Jacksonville, he is learning a new defense, and he will have plenty of chances to prove himself. And the Eagles' run defense still has to show it can do this every week. But on the first weekend where you could compare both sides, the Eagles came out looking very good.

Now, let me talk about the second trade. A.J. Brown.

Earlier this year, the Eagles traded A.J. Brown to the New England Patriots. According to reports at the time of the deal, the Eagles got a 2028 first-round pick and a 2027 fifth-round pick in that deal. And at the time, a lot of fans were angry. A.J. Brown was one of the best receivers in football, and he was a huge part of the Eagles' Super Bowl run.

But here is where things stand now. According to Boston.com, Brown has played in just four of a possible ten games for the Patriots this season. In those four games, he has eleven catches for one hundred twenty-eight yards and no touchdowns. According to Boston.com, he suffered a high ankle sprain in Week One that sent him to injured reserve, he has dealt with a knee issue, and most recently he was pulled from the Patriots' loss to the Lions in Munich with a hamstring injury. ESPN's Jeremy Fowler reported that the hamstring injury will most likely keep him out for a little while.

Now, I want to be careful here, because nobody should celebrate a player getting hurt. A.J. Brown gave everything to this city, and I hope he gets healthy. I am not going to speculate about his injuries beyond what has been reported. But from a pure trade standpoint, the Eagles moved on from a receiver at the right time, and they got a first-round pick for him. And with DeVonta Smith back and young players like Makai Lemon and Darius Cooper getting snaps, the Eagles have something to build on at receiver.

Now, let me talk about the third trade. Cam Jurgens.

According to reports at the time of the deal, the Eagles traded center Cam Jurgens to the Baltimore Ravens, getting a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 fifth-round pick, while sending a 2027 seventh-round pick. And according to one Eagles analyst, the Ravens have not won a single game with Jurgens as their starting center. I checked the official score data, and the Ravens have lost their last five games. They lost to the Bengals thirty-five to thirteen, to the Bills thirty-six to ten, to the Jaguars thirty-three to ten, to the Chargers twenty-three to twenty, and to the Panthers thirty-five to thirty-three on Sunday. The Ravens are now four and seven.

Now, let me be fair again. A center does not win or lose games by himself. The Ravens have a lot of problems that have nothing to do with Cam Jurgens. But once again, from a pure value standpoint, Howie got a second-round pick for a player whose new team has struggled badly.

Now, let me get to the good news from the Steelers game itself, because this is where the future starts to show up.

The Eagles beat the Steelers nineteen to sixteen on Sunday and snapped a seven-game losing streak. According to Field Level Media, it was their first win since September twentieth. And according to Field Level Media, the Steelers have not won in Philadelphia since 1965.

And in a lost season, what you really want to see is your young players growing. On Sunday, three of them stood out.

The first is linebacker Jihaad Campbell. According to one Eagles analyst, Campbell had his best game of the year and one of his best games in an Eagles uniform. He said Campbell made good plays against the run, was really good in coverage, and finished with seven tackles and a tackle for loss. And the analyst said Campbell was reading and reacting much quicker, after a season where there had been a lot of hesitation and second-guessing. And according to the Eagles' official live blog, Campbell dropped Jaylen Warren for a loss in the first quarter to force a three-and-out.

The second is linebacker Jeremiah Trotter Junior. According to Kempski, Trotter led all Eagles with thirteen tackles. And according to the Eagles' official live blog, he stopped Jaylen Warren on fourth and one on the Steelers' first drive, which set the tone for the whole game. According to that same Eagles analyst, Trotter was wearing the green dot on his helmet, which means he was the one getting the defensive calls in the huddle. And the analyst said that in his three starts this year, Trotter has thirty-six tackles, three tackles for loss and three quarterback pressures. He called him, quote, a player. End quote.

That analyst even went a step further. He said it would not surprise him if the Eagles built around Trotter and Campbell and moved on from Zack Baun after the season because of his contract. Now, let me be clear. That is one analyst's opinion, not a report. Zack Baun is still a leader of this defense, and he missed this game with a back injury. But it does show you how much Trotter and Campbell have raised their stock.

The third young player is offensive tackle Markeel Bell. The Eagles took him in the third round, and he has the toughest job on the team, replacing future Hall of Famer Lane Johnson at right tackle. According to that Eagles analyst, Bell's pressure rate allowed has improved a lot over the last few weeks. He said it was ten and a half percent in Week Seven and eleven point six percent in Week Eight, then it dropped to four point two percent in Week Nine, and four point two percent again against the Steelers and T.J. Watt. And he said Bell has not allowed a sack in the last two games, against two very good defensive lines in the Giants and the Steelers.

That is exactly what you want to see in a year like this. A rookie tackle getting better week by week against some of the best pass rushers in football.

Now, let me talk about the defense as a whole, because this group might be turning a corner.

According to that Eagles analyst, the Eagles have ten sacks in their last two games, five against the Giants and five against the Steelers. And he gave a lot of credit to defensive coordinator Vic Fangio and to Nick Sirianni. In his words, Sirianni's team is not quitting on him.

And Sirianni himself said after the game that it takes great block destruction from the front to let the linebackers run and make plays. He said even when Rodgers had to get rid of the ball quickly on short throws, that was a product of how Fangio called the game and how the defensive line was taking on blocks. According to the official game data, Rodgers needed forty-four attempts to throw for two hundred twenty-seven yards.

But there is a concern. Quinyon Mitchell left the game with a hamstring injury, and the Eagles' official account posted, quote, CB Quinyon Mitchell, hamstring, is out. End quote. That is all the team has said, and I am not going to speculate. We will wait for the official update this week.

Now, let me talk about the offense, because there were some adjustments worth noticing.

According to that Eagles analyst, offensive coordinator Sean Mannion put Joe Milton in the shotgun more. And Sirianni said everybody was better on offense, coaches, players, everybody. But let me be honest. According to the official game data, the Eagles finished with just two hundred fourteen total yards. The offense is still a work in progress.

And the analyst pointed out that Milton's accuracy comes and goes. He missed DeVonta Smith on a deep ball. And according to that analyst, Milton admitted he overthrew it, and Smith joked after the game that he did not think he could get overthrown, but Milton's arm was so strong that he still overthrew him. The analyst's advice was simple. Milton does not have to throw every ball at full speed. Sometimes he needs to take a little off and throw with more touch on out routes and over the middle.

But then there was the throw that won the game. On third and six, with a minute and twenty-one seconds left and a three-point lead, Sirianni called a pass, and Milton threw a seven-yard out to DeVonta Smith for the first down. Sirianni said after the game, quote, you just want to be aggressive in four-minute situations. End quote. And he said, quote, we wanted to end it with the ball in our hands. End quote. That analyst loved it too. He called it a gutsy throw and credited Sirianni and Mannion for having a go-to play ready and making the decision to throw it instead of run it.

And let me give some love to Jake Elliott. According to the official game data, Elliott went four for four on field goals, including two from more than fifty yards. There were a lot of questions about Elliott coming into this season. On Sunday, he was perfect.
