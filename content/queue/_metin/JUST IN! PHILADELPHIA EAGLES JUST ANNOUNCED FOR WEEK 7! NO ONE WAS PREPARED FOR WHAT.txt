Eagles Nation, the final days before Monday Night Football against the Dallas Cowboys are here, and the news coming out of the NovaCare Complex is a real mix of good and bad. The Eagles released their Friday practice report, and it tells us a lot about what this team is going to look like on Monday night. At the same time, Adam Schefter went on ESPN and talked about exactly how Howie Roseman is thinking about the trade deadline. And over in New York, the Giants made a quarterback decision that could shake up the whole NFC East race.

This is James with Second Floor, and today we are going to go through all of it. The Week 7 practice report, who is trending up and who is trending down, what Schefter said about the Eagles being buyers or sellers, what the ESPN crew thinks about Monday night, and what the Giants’ quarterback change means for this division. Before we jump in, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor, because we are covering this entire Cowboys week from start to finish.

Let me start with the practice report, because this is the news that matters most for Monday night.

First, the good news. Saquon Barkley practiced again on Friday. He was limited for the second straight day, but he was out there, and NBC Sports Philadelphia wrote that he appears to be on track to play against Dallas. PhillyVoice also listed him as anticipated to play. Remember, Saquon hurt his hamstring early in Week 4 against the Rams, and he has missed a couple of games since. This team ran for just thirty-five yards against Carolina. Getting Saquon back does not fix everything, but it changes how every defense has to line up against this offense.

And there is more good news up front. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Drew Kendall and Landon Dickerson practiced on Friday and lined up next to each other with the first-team offensive line. Kendall has missed the last couple of games with a knee injury, and PhillyVoice listed him as a full participant on Friday. Dickerson has missed the last five games after playing in the season opener. Now, one important detail. Dickerson is still on injured reserve, with his practice window open. So for him to play Monday night, the Eagles would have to activate him to the fifty-three-man roster. Keep an eye on that move this weekend. Willie Lampkin also returned on Friday after missing Thursday with an illness, and Tyler Steen is dealing with a shoulder issue but, according to PhillyVoice, will likely play.

Eagles Nation, think about what that means. Against Carolina, Tanner McKee was sacked twice by blitzers who came through completely unblocked, and Jake Majors had to start at center. If Kendall is back in the middle and Dickerson is back at left guard, this is a much more experienced, much more physical offensive line than the one McKee played behind last Sunday. That might be the most underrated news of the week. Because no matter who is catching the ball or who is lining up at quarterback, football games are still won and lost in the trenches, and a healthier line is the fastest way to help a young quarterback.

Now, the bad news. And there is a lot of it.

DeVonta Smith missed practice again on Friday with his hamstring. That is two straight days. Dallas Goedert missed practice again with his knee. Dontayvion Wicks missed practice again with his ankle. Nolan Smith, still in the concussion protocol after the Carolina game, missed again, and NBC Sports Philadelphia described him as likely out. Defensive back Michael Carter the second missed practice again with an abdomen injury and was working on a side field. And Jonathan Greenard, who was limited on Thursday with his ankle, did not practice on Friday. NBC Sports Philadelphia put it simply. Quote, it’s looking like the Eagles will be short-handed on Monday night, especially with their skill players. End quote.

So let us talk about what happens if DeVonta Smith and Wicks cannot go. According to the Philadelphia Inquirer, the receiver room would be Makai Lemon, Darius Cooper, Hollywood Brown, Elijah Moore and Britain Covey. That is a very different group. Smith was coming off a game where he caught seven passes for seventy-five yards and a touchdown. He is McKee’s most trusted target. Without him, this offense needs somebody to step up.

And this is where Hollywood Brown gets interesting. Against Carolina, he was the only healthy receiver on the inactive list. Now, he could be thrown right into the fire on Monday night. Brown was a full participant all week. If the Eagles need speed and somebody who can take the top off a defense, he might finally get his chance.

At tight end, if Goedert is out, the Inquirer reported that the Eagles could promote Zach Ertz from the practice squad, and that they might make that move either way. Yes, Eagles Nation, that Zach Ertz. The man who caught the touchdown in Super Bowl fifty-two. Grant Calcaterra and Eli Stowers were full participants, so there is some depth there too. But if Ertz ends up back on the field at the Linc on Monday night, that is going to be a moment.

On the edge, it is getting thin. Nolan Smith is likely out. Greenard missed Friday. Jalyx Hunt practiced on a limited basis for the second straight day as he comes back from the knee and ankle injury he suffered in London. A.J. Epenesa was limited with a back injury. And Arnold Ebiketie, who played seventy-two snaps against Carolina, was a full participant despite a shoulder issue. Vic Fangio may have to get very creative to get pressure on Dak Prescott.

Now, one more important thing. Because the game is on Monday night, the final injury report with official game designations comes out on Saturday. So everything we just went through is practice participation, not final status. If you see headlines saying somebody is officially out, check the date. The real answers come on Saturday.

Now let us move to the trade deadline, because Adam Schefter had something to say about the Eagles on ESPN, and it was not what a lot of people expected.

The question was simple. The trade deadline is approaching, and the Eagles have lost four straight. So what is Philadelphia thinking? And Schefter’s answer was this. With the division packed together and up for grabs, he said that for Howie Roseman, it is not in his DNA to be a seller. He said Roseman might sell off some parts if he gets an offer that is good for the team, but he would be just as likely to buy a player as to sell one. Schefter called the Eagles one of those rare teams that can be both buyers and sellers. He said it would not be a surprise if they traded away a player or two, and it would not be a surprise if they added a player or two. And then he said the record and the current state of the team have nothing to do with how they will approach the deadline.

Eagles Nation, that is a big statement. A lot of fans have already written this season off. A lot of analysts have been making lists of Eagles who could be traded. And Schefter is basically saying, slow down. Howie Roseman does not think like that.

And the timing matters here. According to the Philadelphia Inquirer, the trade deadline is November tenth, and the Eagles play three games before then. All three are against NFC East opponents. Dallas on Monday night, then Washington, then the Giants. Think about that. If the Eagles win those three games, they are five and four at the deadline and right in the middle of the division race. If they lose two or three of them, the conversation changes completely.

Now, to be fair, the Inquirer also laid out the case for selling. The paper’s analysis said selling is the likelier path, and it pointed to players on expiring deals, like Dallas Goedert, Moro Ojomo, Tyler Steen and Riq Woolen. It also mentioned that the Eagles hold two first-round picks and two second-round picks in 2028, which gives Roseman a lot of flexibility. And it had a line I really liked. Quote, News flash: You can’t trade for an offensive coordinator or scheme. End quote. Meanwhile, Sean Mannion said this week that the problems on offense are, quote, totally fixable, end quote.

So you have Schefter saying Roseman can be a buyer and a seller at the same time, and the Inquirer saying selling is more likely. And honestly, both can be true. Roseman has always been aggressive. He traded Cam Jurgens to Baltimore for a second-round pick, according to the Inquirer. He has extended Jordan Davis and Jalen Carter. He is never afraid to make a move. The question is just which direction he moves.

Now let us talk about Monday night itself, because the ESPN crew talked about it too.

One analyst made the Eagles beating the Cowboys his favorite pick of the week. He pointed to the Eagles’ history against Dallas at home, and the fact that it is a primetime game. And then he said something that made me smile. Tanner McKee whispers. He said he is liking this guy, and he does not think Dallas is going to blow it up against a beat-up offensive line.

Then the panel was asked a great question. If the Eagles win on Monday night, do we all get back on the train? And the answer was basically yes. If the Eagles win, they snap a four-game losing streak, and they are right back in the middle of the division. One panelist said that at that point, the Eagles would not have a choice but to keep pushing forward, because the division is so down. And then he flipped it. He said if the Cowboys lose this game, Dallas should absolutely become sellers, because they have not faced the kind of adversity the Eagles have. They are just not performing. And he asked, how many layups can you throw Dallas’s way before you realize they just do not have it this year?

Eagles Nation, I love that framing. Think about what the Eagles have been through. A franchise quarterback out for the season with blood clots. Saquon hurt. DeVonta hurt. Goedert hurt. Half the offensive line in and out of the lineup. And the Eagles are still two and four, the same record as Dallas. The Cowboys do not have those excuses. That is why this game feels so big. It is not just about the standings. It is about which team actually has the fight to get out of this hole.

Now let us talk about the Giants, because what happened in New York this week matters for the Eagles.

The Giants announced that J.J. McCarthy will start at quarterback on Sunday against the Houston Texans. Jameis Winston, who had started the last four games since Jaxson Dart suffered a season-ending knee injury in Week 2, goes back to the bench. According to Giants.com, the Giants traded a 2027 fifth-round pick to the Vikings for McCarthy, the tenth overall pick in the 2024 draft. Head coach John Harbaugh said, quote, this is going to be our direction. End quote.

The ESPN crew talked about why McCarthy and why now. Schefter pointed to the fourth quarter of the Giants’ loss to New Orleans, when McCarthy came in and threw for two hundred forty-three yards in one quarter. The panel liked his deep ball and his ability to run, and they pointed out that the Giants’ running game has been struggling. But they also warned that he can get frantic under pressure, and that he is still very early in his career as a starter.

Here is why Eagles fans should care. The Giants are three, two and one, sitting near the top of a very weak NFC East, and they face the Eagles before the trade deadline. If McCarthy gives that offense a spark, the Giants could start to separate. If he struggles against that Houston defense, the division stays wide open. Either way, the Eagles are going to see this new version of the Giants very soon.

And according to that ESPN segment, the NFC East went two straight weeks without a single win. Not one team in the division won a game, even in a week when two of them played each other. Schefter said the division is redefining what we can see from a division. And that is exactly why the Eagles cannot give up on this season. Nobody is running away with it.

Now, quickly, let us look at the other sideline. Dallas is two and four and has lost two straight, and Dak Prescott has thrown four interceptions in his last two games. On Thursday’s report, returner KaVontae Turpin did not practice with a ribs injury, and Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice wrote that he is not expected to play. That matters, because Turpin might be the best return man in the league, and the Eagles’ special teams will not have to deal with him. Starting linebacker Dee Winters and tackle Drew Shelton also did not practice on Thursday. George Pickens was limited with a shoulder, and left tackle Tyler Guyton and cornerback Cobie Durant were limited too. So Dallas has its own problems.

And there is a fun chess match on the other side of the ball. The Cowboys’ defensive coordinator, Christian Parker, worked under Vic Fangio in 2024 and 2025. The student knows the teacher’s system. But Fangio also knows exactly how Parker thinks. And with Dak throwing picks lately, Fangio’s disguised coverages could bait him into another mistake on Monday night.

And do not forget about Tanner McKee. Last week he started on short notice. This week, he gets a full week of first-team reps and a game plan built around him. Nick Sirianni said on Thursday that McKee loves football, is highly intelligent and tough, and that guys like him have a tendency to reach their ceiling. In his first start against Carolina, McKee went twenty-six of forty-five for two hundred fifteen yards, a touchdown and no interceptions. He protected the football. If he does that again, with Saquon behind him and a healthier offensive line in front of him, the Eagles have a real chance.

Alright Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the pessimists. They will look at this practice report and say the Eagles are about to play the Cowboys without DeVonta Smith, without Dallas Goedert, without Dontayvion Wicks, without Nolan Smith, maybe without Jonathan Greenard, and obviously without Jalen Hurts. They will say a backup quarterback with a patched-together receiver room is not going to beat anybody in primetime. They will say the Inquirer is right, that selling is the smart path, and that Roseman should start thinking about 2027 and 2028 instead of chasing a weak division. And they will say four straight losses are not a slump, they are a pattern.

On the other side, the optimists. They will say Saquon is coming back, and the offensive line could have Kendall and Dickerson back together for the first time in weeks. They will say Schefter is right that Roseman never waves the white flag. They will say Dallas is two and four with no excuses, and that the Eagles are six games into a season where nobody in the division has separated. And they will say a win on Monday night could turn the next three weeks into a run at the top of the NFC East.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I agree with Schefter. I do not think Howie Roseman sells this team off before he knows what he has in the next three games. Three division games before the deadline is a gift. Win two of them and you are buying. Lose two of them and you start making smart, targeted sales, maybe a veteran on an expiring deal, but you do not tear it down. As for Monday night, I am worried about the receivers. Losing DeVonta Smith would hurt a lot. But if Saquon is back and that offensive line is whole again, I think the Eagles can lean on the run, protect McKee, and grind out a win. Run the ball, win the turnover battle, and let the Linc do the rest.
