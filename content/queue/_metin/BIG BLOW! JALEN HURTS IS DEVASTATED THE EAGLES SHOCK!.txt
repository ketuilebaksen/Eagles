Eagles Nation, you have probably seen the headlines saying Jalen Hurts is devastated and that the Eagles just delivered a big blow. So let me start with the truth, because you deserve it. There is no report that Jalen Hurts is devastated. In fact, the latest update on Jalen is good news. Former Eagles quarterback Donovan McNabb said this week that he has talked to Jalen, and that he is doing well and in good spirits. But there are real blows hitting this team right now. The Eagles are two and six. Nick Sirianni has decided to keep his offensive coordinator and his quarterback. Saquon Barkley played less than a third of the snaps on Sunday night. And this week, a respected reporter named one of the Eagles’ young starting offensive linemen as a possible trade candidate before the deadline.

This is James with Second Floor, and today we are going to break it all down. The real update on Jalen, what Sirianni decided, the numbers behind this offense, the rookies who are giving this fan base something to believe in, where the Eagles would pick in the draft right now, and the trade pitch for Tyler Steen that has a lot of fans worried. Before we start, if you are still riding with this team, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor.

Let me start with Jalen, because he comes first.

According to Athlon Sports, Donovan McNabb spoke about Jalen on the Eagles Collective podcast. McNabb said, quote, I had an opportunity to talk to him a little bit. End quote. And then, quote, he’s doing well. He’s in good spirits. End quote. McNabb said he wanted to focus on Jalen’s well-being, not football. And I think that is exactly the right approach. Donovan McNabb knows what it is like to be the face of this franchise, with the whole city watching every move you make. When a man like that tells you Jalen is in good spirits, that should mean something to every Eagles fan. Jalen has always been calm, steady and focused, through the Super Bowl runs and through the hard seasons. It sounds like that has not changed, even now. And for a team that has lost six straight, knowing their leader is doing well matters more than people think.

Here is what we know. Jalen is out for the rest of the season after being diagnosed with blood clots in his leg and both lungs. The Eagles and his agent have said, quote, Jalen’s prognosis is very good, and we expect a full recovery. End quote. He is under contract, and twenty-two million dollars of his 2027 pay is already fully guaranteed, according to Front Office Sports, citing Spotrac. So when you see a title telling you Jalen is devastated, or that he is leaving, or that the Eagles shocked him, understand that none of that has been reported. A man who is in good spirits while recovering from a serious health scare is the real story. And that is a story worth celebrating.

But I will tell you who might be devastated. Eagles fans. Because the team Jalen left behind is struggling badly without him.

Now, let us talk about what Sirianni decided.

After the twenty-seven to ten loss in Washington, Sirianni said on Monday that he is keeping Sean Mannion as the offensive coordinator and Tanner McKee as the starting quarterback. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he said, quote, I’m not making any changes there. End quote. And about McKee, quote, Tanner’s our guy. We’re not making any changes there. End quote.

And according to Martin Frank of the Delaware News Journal, Sirianni also said, quote, it’s our job as coaches to be problem solvers. End quote. He said, quote, with that being said, all of us have things that we have to improve on. End quote. And then he said something that a lot of fans are going to remember. Quote, to keep doing the same thing over and over again and expect a different result is crazy. End quote.

Eagles Nation, I understand wanting stability. Firing a coordinator in the middle of the season does not automatically fix the blocking. Benching McKee does not magically create open receivers. But when you are two and six, stability only matters if the results change. And look at this number from the Delaware News Journal. During this six-game losing streak, the offense has gone over three hundred yards only once. Once. That is not good enough for any team, let alone a team with this much talent.

And remember what Sirianni said right after the game on Sunday night. He said the struggles are, in his words, one hundred percent on him. He said, quote, with all the detail that’s not happening right now, I got to try a different approach. End quote. He said that if his delivery needs to be harsher, it needs to be harsher. And he said, quote, I know nobody feels sorry for us. End quote. So the head coach is saying all the right things. He is taking the blame. He is promising a new approach. But he is also keeping the same people in charge of the offense. And that is why so many fans are confused this week.

Sirianni says changes are happening behind the scenes, in the scheme, in the schedule, in the practice plays. Fine. But the fans are not watching practice schedules. We are watching what happens on Sunday. And so far, the execution is not improving.

Now, let me give McKee some perspective, because he deserves it. He has lost all three starts since Jalen went down. But look at what is around him. Lane Johnson is no longer at right tackle, and the line has been reshuffled. DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks were unavailable against Washington. McKee still has to make smarter decisions, and he knows it. But pretending the quarterback alone is responsible ignores everything that is happening around him.

And here is a sequence that shows you exactly what I mean. According to the Delaware News Journal, the Eagles had fourth and four at the Washington thirteen-yard line. Rookie right tackle Markel Bell, a third-round pick starting in place of Lane Johnson, was called for a false start. That turned fourth and four into fourth and nine. Then, according to the Inquirer, a successful Eagles field goal was wiped out by an illegal formation penalty on Jordan Mailata. And then Jake Elliott missed the next try. One drive, two penalties, zero points. In a game you are trying to win on the road, that is a killer.

Now, let us talk about Saquon Barkley, because this might be the biggest blow of all.

According to the Delaware News Journal, Saquon played just twenty-two snaps against Washington, about thirty-two percent, on a pitch count. He carried the ball thirteen times for thirty yards. He has now played two straight games with a strained hamstring and back. And when he was asked whether sitting out would be better, Saquon gave a one-word answer. No.

You have to respect that competitiveness. That is who Saquon is. He wants to be out there for his teammates, no matter what. But this is my opinion, not a report. In a season like this one, protecting his health matters more than proving a point. With the bye week coming after the Giants game, I hope the Eagles think very carefully about how much they ask of him. Saquon is not just important for the rest of this season. He is important for 2027.

And this brings us to the part of this season that Philadelphia can actually build on. The rookies.

With DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks out, first-round receiver Makai Lemon has stepped into a bigger role. According to the Delaware News Journal, Lemon led the Eagles in catches and receiving yards for the second straight game. He caught six passes for fifty-seven yards against Washington, and over his last two games, he has thirteen catches for one hundred twenty-four yards. That is real production from a rookie in an offense that is struggling to find any rhythm.

Then there is second-round tight end Eli Stowers. He spent the first six games of the season on injured reserve. Against Washington, he caught his first career pass, and he finished with three catches for fifty-three yards. Not bad for a debut in a game like that.

And then there is Markel Bell. Yes, he had the costly false start. But think about the job he has been given. A rookie third-round pick, stepping in at right tackle, the spot Lane Johnson held for more than a decade. Young players make mistakes. The question is whether this coaching staff can turn those mistakes into growth. Because if this season cannot be rescued, the Eagles need to find out which young players deserve bigger roles next year.

Now, the draft. According to the Delaware News Journal, citing Tankathon, if the season ended today, the two and six Eagles would pick fifth overall. Nobody in that building is trying to lose. Nobody should be. But a pick that high could help Howie Roseman add a game-changing player on the offensive line, in the pass rush or in the secondary. And that is not giving up on this season. It is being realistic about where this team is.

But a high draft pick will not solve bad execution. You still have to develop the talent you bring in. And that leads directly to our second story.

On Tuesday, Daniel Popper of The Athletic named the Denver Broncos as the best trade fit for Eagles guard Tyler Steen. According to Heavy, Popper asked, quote, would GM Howie Roseman consider trading Steen? End quote.

Let me make something clear right away. This is a proposed trade fit, not a deal. Nobody has reported any talks between Philadelphia and Denver. But the idea raises a big question. Should the Eagles really consider moving a young starting offensive lineman while their protection is already struggling?

Here is who Tyler Steen is. He is six foot six, three hundred twenty-one pounds, and he was a third-round pick in 2023. He developed into the Eagles’ starting right guard. In 2025, according to Heavy, he started all seventeen games and played ninety-six percent of the offensive snaps. Pro Football Focus ranked him seventeenth overall among guards last season, and eleventh in pass protection. And Bleacher Report’s Kristopher Knox called him the Eagles’ most promising building block. That is not an unproven backup. That is a starter who has shown he belongs.

Why would Denver want him? According to Heavy, guard is a weak spot on the Broncos’ line, next to starter Ben Powers. A team with playoff hopes could use a player like Steen to strengthen its protection down the stretch.

And here is the part that makes some sense from the Eagles’ side. Steen is in the final year of his rookie contract, and he is set to become a free agent in 2027, because the Eagles have not extended him. Spotrac projects his next deal at about three years and fifteen million dollars. That is a projection, not an offer. If the Eagles do not plan to pay him, getting something for him now could make sense.

But here is the problem. Heavy suggested Denver might only give up a sixth-round pick in 2027 or 2028. A sixth-round pick. For a starting guard who just played almost every snap of a full season. That does not excite me at all. Starting offensive linemen are hard to find, and the Eagles know that better than anybody.

And Eagles fans will remember Steen for one more thing. In Week 16 last season, against Washington, he got into a fight with Commanders cornerback Mike Sainristil and then with several other players. According to Heavy, he was ejected and later fined, but he avoided a suspension. Fighting after the whistle is not smart football. But that moment showed this city that Steen will stand his ground. And Philadelphia has always appreciated toughness.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you would trade Tyler Steen for a late-round pick. Comment number two if you would keep him and try to re-sign him. I want to see where you land.

And Steen is not the only name out there. The trade talk around this team is getting louder every day. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler has put the odds of Riq Woolen being traded at thirty-five percent and Landon Dickerson at twenty-five percent. Think about that for a second. Dickerson and Steen are both interior offensive linemen. If both of them were moved, the Eagles would be rebuilding the middle of their line in the middle of a season. A Boston columnist, Meghan Ottolini of WEEI, also pitched the Patriots calling about Dallas Goedert as a pure rental. On the other side, Albert Breer of Sports Illustrated reported that the Eagles told teams Moro Ojomo is not available. And Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice wrote that DeVonta Smith isn’t going anywhere. So the Eagles appear to be protecting some players and listening on others. None of these are deals. But they tell you the mood around this team has changed.

Now, why does this matter so much? Because the Eagles have always been at their best when they control the trenches. When the offensive line dominates, the running game works, the quarterback has time, and the whole offense becomes dangerous. Right now, that identity is missing. Jeff Stoutland is gone. Lane Johnson is gone from the lineup. A rookie is starting at right tackle. Why make rebuilding that line even harder by trading away a young starter for almost nothing?

And before the deadline, there is one more game. The Giants. They are four, two and one, and they are coming off their bye, so they will be rested. The Eagles will be coming off a road loss, with a banged-up Saquon and a young offensive line. That is a tough spot. But it is also the last chance for this group to show Howie Roseman that it is worth keeping together. If the Eagles play clean, physical football against the Giants, the selling talk could quiet down. If they get blown out again, I think every veteran on an expiring deal becomes a real trade candidate. After the Giants, the Eagles finally get their bye week, and that could be the moment when real changes happen, whether in the playbook or in the roster.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the people who say sell. They will say the Eagles are two and six, they are projected to pick fifth, and Jalen is out for the year. They will say Steen is on an expiring deal, and that if the Eagles are not going to pay him, they should get something for him now. They will say keeping Mannion and McKee means nothing will change, so the front office should start planning for 2027 today. And they will say that a smart team sells when the season is going nowhere.

On the other side, the people who say stay the course. They will say Steen is a young, proven starter, and that a sixth-round pick is not worth opening another hole on a line that is already struggling. They will say Sirianni is right that changing the coordinator in the middle of the season rarely fixes anything. They will say the rookies like Makai Lemon, Eli Stowers and Markel Bell are growing every week, and that the team needs stability around them. And they will say Jalen is in good spirits, he is coming back, and the Eagles need to keep the core of his offense together for 2027.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I do not trade Tyler Steen for a sixth-round pick. If someone offers real value, a pick that actually helps the future, then you listen. But a late-round pick for a starting guard is not a plan. It is a panic move. And on the coaching side, I understand why Sirianni is not firing anyone right now. But he said it himself. Doing the same thing over and over and expecting a different result is crazy. So against the Giants, we need to see something different. Fewer penalties. Smarter situational football. A plan that actually helps Tanner McKee. And a careful plan for Saquon.

So let me wrap it up. Jalen Hurts is not devastated. According to Donovan McNabb, he is doing well and in good spirits, and he is expected to make a full recovery. The real blows are on the field. The Eagles are two and six, Sirianni is keeping Sean Mannion and Tanner McKee, and the offense has topped three hundred yards only once in six games. Saquon played just twenty-two snaps. The rookies are giving this fan base hope. The Eagles would pick fifth if the season ended today.
