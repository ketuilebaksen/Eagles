Let me start with Jalen, because his health matters more than any football game.

On Monday, the Eagles and Jalen’s agent, Nicole Lynn, released a joint statement. In London, specialists had first diagnosed what they called a left calf superficial vein thrombosis. But after further testing, the statement said, quote, the current diagnosis is a deep vein thrombosis, as well as bilateral pulmonary emboli. End quote. In plain words, a clot in a deeper vein of his leg, and clots that were found in both lungs.

Now, Eagles Nation, here is the most important part of that statement, and I want you to hear it clearly. It said Jalen is, quote, clinically stable and has no significant functional limitations. End quote. His treatment will involve blood thinners. And while he is on those blood thinners, he is not allowed to take part in any contact activities, including football. And then the statement said, quote, Jalen’s prognosis is very good, and we expect a full recovery. End quote. The team also said it anticipates no long-term effects, and that Jalen will remain active around the team and continue to condition.

Ian Rapoport and Adam Schefter both reported that Hurts is out for the season, and Rapoport reported that the treatment will take at least three months. The statement itself did not use the word season. But the bottom line is clear. Jalen Hurts will not be playing football for the Eagles for a long time this year.

And let me say this from the heart. When the words clots in both lungs came across my screen, football stopped mattering for a minute. That is serious. And the best news in this whole story is the line that says he is stable and expected to make a full recovery. That is what we pray for first. Get well, Jalen. The city is behind you.

And there was one more encouraging detail from the experts. Dr. William Gray of Main Line Health, who is not connected to the team, explained to Billy Penn that Jalen’s case is what doctors call provoked, meaning there was a clear cause, the injury to his leg. And he said that makes future clots less likely. Again, I am not giving you medical advice, I am just passing along what a specialist said publicly. But for Jalen’s long-term health and his career, that is genuinely good news.

I also want to be fair about one more thing. A lot of fans are asking whether Hurts should have played in London after that first superficial clot was found. That question is out there, and local outlets have reported that the team’s handling has come under scrutiny. But I am not a doctor, and I am not going to pretend to be one. The team said doctors in London concluded he could safely play at the time, and Dr. Gray also told 6abc the team did a great job picking this up early. What matters now is that Jalen gets the right care.

Okay. Now the football side. The huge quarterback decision.

With Hurts out for the year, Tanner McKee is the Eagles starting quarterback for the foreseeable future. NBC Sports Philadelphia already noted he would start at least through the bye week, and now the timeline is much longer than that. Behind him, rookie Cole Payton. And Joe Milton, the strong-armed quarterback the Eagles just signed off the Cowboys practice squad, is learning the offense as fast as he can.

And on Monday, Nick Sirianni made his decision on the offense very clear. He was asked directly whether Sean Mannion would keep calling plays, and his answer was simple. Yes. Sirianni said, according to The Athletic’s Zach Berman, that, quote, everything’s on the table, end quote, when it comes to fixing the offense. But no changes to the play-caller. No changes to the coaching staff. The Eagles are going to fix this with the people they have.

And think about what this means for the quarterback room long term. Tanner McKee is in the final year of his rookie contract, so the rest of this season is basically a long audition for him, in Philadelphia and around the league. Cole Payton is a rookie getting a crash course in what an NFL season really looks like. And Joe Milton, with that huge arm, now has weeks, not days, to learn this offense. Five NFL appearances, no starts, a lot of tools and a lot to prove. If McKee goes down, or if the offense keeps stalling, do not be surprised if the conversation about Milton gets louder.

So the big decision is this. Sirianni is riding with Tanner McKee and Sean Mannion. Together. Through the toughest stretch of his tenure.

Now let me walk you through the game against Carolina, because there are lessons in it that will decide whether this decision works.

It started about as badly as it could. The Panthers won the toss and deferred. On the very first play, McKee was sacked for an eleven-yard loss by Bobby Okereke. The Eagles went three and out, and the offense managed just forty-five total yards on its first twenty plays, according to the Philadelphia Inquirer. Carolina kicked a forty-seven-yard field goal, and in the second quarter, Bryce Young hit tight end Tommy Tremble for a twelve-yard touchdown, a drive that was helped by a roughing-the-passer penalty. Ten to nothing, Panthers.

And Bryce Young did not light up the completion column. He finished twenty of thirty-nine. But he threw two touchdown passes and no interceptions, both of the touchdowns to Tommy Tremble, and he made the plays when Carolina needed them. Credit where it is due. The Panthers came in rested off a bye and they played like it.

Then McKee found something. When he got the ball out quickly, the offense moved, and Dameon Pierce finished a drive with a two-yard touchdown run late in the second quarter. Ten to seven at halftime. And honestly, Carolina gave the Eagles chances. The Panthers were flagged a bunch in that game, including two pass interference calls on rookie cornerback Will Lee, one of them on a deep shot to Dallas Goedert. The door was open.

But the third quarter was quiet again. Carolina added a fifty-two-yard field goal, and the Eagles did not score. According to the Inquirer, the Eagles have now scored just six points in six third quarters this season. That is the fewest in the NFL. That is not bad luck, Eagles Nation. That is a pattern.

Then came the fourth quarter. On a fourth-and-one, Moro Ojomo jumped offside, giving Carolina a fresh set of downs, and Chuba Hubbard punched it in from one yard out. Nineteen to seven. But the Eagles answered. With ten minutes and forty-seven seconds left, McKee dropped a beautiful over-the-shoulder throw to DeVonta Smith for a nineteen-yard touchdown. Nineteen to fourteen. The defense got a stop. The Linc was alive. The Eagles had the ball with a chance to take the lead.

And then, the play that broke the comeback. On first down, a bad snap from center Jake Majors turned into a twelve-yard loss. The Eagles went three and out, their fifth three-and-out of the day. Then Anthony Tyus of the Panthers got a hand on Braden Mann’s punt, and it traveled just eight yards. Carolina had a short field, Cooper DeJean was flagged for unnecessary roughness, and Bryce Young found Tremble again for a nineteen-yard touchdown with six minutes and thirty-eight seconds left. Twenty-five to fourteen. Game over.

Special teams, Eagles Nation. Two weeks in a row. In London, it was the kickoff return. On Sunday, it was the partially blocked punt. You cannot keep giving away hidden yards and expect to win close games.

Alright Eagles Nation, now both sides of the big decision.

On one side, the people who say Sirianni is making a mistake by standing pat. They will say that four straight losses, a run game that cannot get going, five three-and-outs and the fewest third-quarter points in the league are proof that something has to change now. They will say that if Mannion is not the problem, then who is? And they will point out that with Hurts out for the year, the Eagles cannot afford to waste weeks waiting for the offense to figure itself out. Some will even say the Eagles should look at the trade market for a veteran quarterback before the November tenth deadline.

On the other side, the people who say this is the right call. They will say that firing or demoting a coordinator in the middle of a crisis rarely fixes anything, and it can make things worse. They will say McKee has made one midseason start, with no Hurts, no Barkley, no Dickerson, and a backfield held together with tape, and he still did not turn the ball over. They will point out that the Panthers were a tough opponent coming off a bye. And they will say the Eagles need stability right now, not panic.

And here is where I land. Again, this is just my opinion, not a report. I understand why Sirianni is sticking with Mannion. Changing the play caller during the week of a primetime game against Dallas would be chaos. But everything is on the table, and the offense has to look different against the Cowboys. Simplify it. Get McKee into rhythm early with quick throws. Feed DeVonta Smith. Commit to the run, even if the first few carries go nowhere. And clean up the special teams, because those mistakes cost this team two games in a row.

If I had to boil it down to three keys for Dallas, here they are. Number one, win the first quarter. Stop digging holes. Script the opening drive around McKee’s quick release so he completes a few easy passes and settles in. Number two, protect him against the blitz. Carolina blitzed and the Eagles struggled to pick it up, and you can bet the Cowboys watched that tape. Hot routes, chips from the tight ends, quick answers. Number three, discipline. No offside on fourth-and-one. No personal fouls that hand the other team fifteen free yards. No breakdowns in the kicking game. Do those three things, and with this defense, the Eagles can beat anybody, even without their franchise quarterback.

As for McKee, I am all in on giving him a real chance. He did not throw an interception. He owned his mistakes. And he showed on that touchdown throw to DeVonta that he can make big-time plays. One start is not enough to judge anyone. Give him the Cowboys game, with a full week of first-team reps, and then let us talk.
