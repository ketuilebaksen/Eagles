Let me start with the announcement itself, because the details matter, and because when it comes to a player’s health, I only want to give you what the team has actually said.

On Thursday, the Eagles released a statement on Jalen Hurts. The team explained that Hurts was cleated in the calf during the Week 4 loss to the Rams on October fourth. On October eighth, he went to the training room after practice asking for treatment. And then, in the team’s words, out of an abundance of caution, an ultrasound was done on Saturday in London. That ultrasound found a superficial blood clot. The medical staff concluded he could safely play, and he played without limitation against Jacksonville. After the game and the flight home, a follow-up ultrasound was recommended. Hurts received treatment Monday and Tuesday, practiced fully on Wednesday, and then on Wednesday night, the follow-up showed movement in that small clot. So the team ruled him out for Sunday while they determine the appropriate course of treatment. The Eagles also said they have been in ongoing contact with the league office.

And the statement ended with one sentence that I think says everything. Quote, His health and safety are our highest priority. End quote.

Eagles Nation, I want to be very clear on this channel. No return date has been announced. I am not going to guess one. I am not going to read a medical chart I do not have. This is a person’s health, and the team is doing exactly what it should be doing. Football comes second. And I think every Eagles fan, no matter how frustrated you have been with this offense, feels the same way today.

Now, here is the part of the announcement that tells you how serious the team is about getting this right. Ian Rapoport of NFL Network reported that Tanner McKee will start until Hurts’ medical situation is solved. Not just Sunday. Until it is solved. That means McKee needs to be ready not only for Carolina, but potentially for whatever comes after. And according to Fox Sports, the next stretch on the schedule includes the Cowboys, the Commanders and the Giants. Division games. Games that will decide the NFC East.

So let us talk about the man who is stepping in. Because I think a lot of fans see the name Tanner McKee and think, backup quarterback, sixth-round pick, that is it. But his story is way more interesting than that.

Tanner McKee grew up in Corona, California, and played at Centennial High School. He was one of the top high school quarterbacks in the country. He played in the Army All-American Bowl, and he was named MVP of the 2018 Polynesian Bowl. And then he did something that almost no elite quarterback prospect does. Before college, he spent two years as a full-time missionary in Brazil for The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. He learned Portuguese. He put football on hold for two full years.

Then he came back and went to Stanford. Over three seasons, he played in twenty-three games with twenty-one starts, and he threw for five thousand three hundred thirty-six yards, twenty-eight touchdowns and fifteen interceptions. He is listed at six foot six. A big, classic pocket passer.

The Eagles took him in the sixth round of the 2023 draft, number one hundred eighty-eight overall. And he has been in this building ever since. He was part of the team that won Super Bowl Fifty-Nine. He has been learning behind Jalen Hurts for years. He knows the offense. He knows the receivers. He knows the coaches. This is not a stranger coming in off the street.

And think about the patience that takes. Four seasons of preparing every single week like you are the starter, knowing that most Sundays you will never take a snap. Learning every protection, every check, every adjustment, just in case. That is what a professional backup does. And from everything McKee said this week, that is the word that comes through loud and clear. Prepared. He told Eagles.com that he has prepared for exactly this kind of scenario, and that being ready at any moment is simply part of the job.

And he has done this before. In Week 18 of the 2024 season, with Hurts and Kenny Pickett both inactive, McKee started against the New York Giants. He threw for two hundred sixty-nine yards and two touchdowns with no turnovers, and the Eagles won twenty to thirteen. His first NFL win. Then in Week 18 of last season, he started at Washington, went twenty-one of forty for two hundred forty-one yards, one touchdown and one interception, in a twenty-four to seventeen loss.

So his career line coming into Sunday, according to the numbers through last season, looks like this. Six games, two starts, a one and one record, fifty-four of eighty-eight passing, sixty-one point four percent, five hundred ninety-seven yards, five touchdowns, one interception, and a ninety-five point seven passer rating. That is a small sample size. But five touchdowns and one interception is not nothing. He has protected the football.

And here is what he said this week, from Eagles.com. Quote, At any moment, you have to be ready to play on a Sunday. End quote. He also said, quote, Every opportunity is a huge opportunity. End quote. And he talked about being a sixth-round pick and how much this moment means to him. He said he is very excited and ready to make the most of it.

But there is one big difference this time, and I need to be honest about it. His two previous starts both came in Week 18 finales, when the stakes were different. This will be the first start of his career that is not a season finale. This is a meaningful October game, with a three-game losing streak hanging over the team, in front of a home crowd that is hungry for a win. That is a completely different kind of pressure. And it is the biggest test of his career.

So what changes for the offense with McKee under center? Now, this is my opinion, not a report, because the Eagles are not going to tell us their game plan. But here is what I expect from Sean Mannion and this offensive staff.

First, I expect a lot of quick, rhythm throws. McKee is a pocket passer. He is not going to run the ball the way Hurts does, so the quarterback run game, the designed runs, a lot of that is going to be scaled back. That means the offense has to win with timing and accuracy.

Second, I expect them to lean on the running game, even with a thin backfield. Saquon Barkley did not practice again on Thursday and is tracking to miss a second straight game. Tank Bigsby is on injured reserve. Will Shipley is out for multiple weeks. So it will likely be Dameon Pierce and Carson Steele, with practice squad backs like Jaydon Blue and newly signed Ian Wheeler as options to be elevated. That group needs to do a lot better than the sixteen carries for twenty yards we saw in London.

And this is exactly why the front office has been so busy this week. The Eagles officially signed Ian Wheeler, the MVP of the 2026 UFL United Bowl, to the practice squad on Thursday. They were also reported by Matt Zenitz to be bringing back Ja’Quinden Jackson, although the team had not announced that move as of Thursday. When your starting quarterback is out, the run game becomes the best friend of the backup, and every body in that backfield matters.

Third, and this is the good news, DeVonta Smith practiced on Thursday for the first time since missing the last two games. He was limited, but Mike Garafolo reported there is optimism he plays on Sunday. A healthy DeVonta Smith is exactly what a backup quarterback needs. A smooth route runner who gets open and catches everything. On the other hand, Dontayvion Wicks did not practice on Thursday, so the receiver group is still not at full strength. And Landon Dickerson was limited, and the thinking is he probably needs a full practice before he returns.

Now let us talk about the opponent, because this Carolina team is not the Carolina team some of you remember.

The Panthers are two and two, and they are coming off a bye week, so they are rested and they have had extra time to prepare. And the story of their season is Bryce Young. Mike Jones of The Athletic named Young as the player who has made the biggest leap in the NFL this year. And the numbers back that up. Entering Week 6, according to Yahoo Sports, Young is averaging three hundred seventeen passing yards per game, the most in the league, with nine touchdown passes, seventeen completions of twenty-plus yards, and a QBR of seventy-four point eight. So the Eagles defense has a real challenge on Sunday. Jalyx Hunt did not practice on Thursday and is expected to miss the game. Jonathan Greenard was limited and is not a lock to play.
