Eagles Nation, after another painful Sunday at Lincoln Financial Field, the headlines are already saying Nick Sirianni’s replacement has been hired. So let me give it to you straight before anything else. Nobody has been hired. Nick Sirianni is still the head coach of the Philadelphia Eagles. There is no report that Jeffrey Lurie has made a decision, and there is no report that a new head coach is waiting in the building. But after a nineteen to eleven loss to the New York Giants, the conversation about Sirianni’s future is louder than it has ever been. The Eagles are two and seven. They have lost seven straight games for the first time since 2012. And with the bye week here and the trade deadline on Tuesday, the next few days could shape the future of this franchise.

What happened against the Giants, how Joe Milton played in his first start, what Sirianni said about possible staff changes, who is actually next in line on this coaching staff if Lurie ever makes a move, what history tells us about how Lurie handles a crisis, and what is coming before Tuesday’s deadline. Let me start with the game, because it tells you everything about where this team is.

The Giants beat the Eagles nineteen to eleven at the Linc. According to Athlon Sports, it was the Giants’ first win in Philadelphia since 2013. And remember, the Eagles came into this game as home underdogs for the first time since 2009, according to the Inquirer. So the oddsmakers were right.

The game started with some promise. The Eagles’ defense came out flying. According to Yahoo Sports, on the Giants’ very first drive, Zack Baun sacked J.J. McCarthy on third and ten after a Vic Fangio blitz, and the Giants had to settle for a fifty-three-yard field goal. And this defense kept coming. According to PHLY Sports, the Eagles recorded six sacks, their most in a game this season. Baun had two of them in the first half. Jalen Carter, Jordan Davis, Nolan Smith and Jonathan Greenard each had one, and it was Greenard’s first sack as an Eagle.

According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the defense also held the Giants to eighty rushing yards, the first time all season the Eagles held an opponent under one hundred yards on the ground. Jeremiah Trotter Junior, starting for the injured Jihaad Campbell, led the team with ten tackles, three tackles for loss and a forced fumble. Quinyon Mitchell held Malik Nabers to two catches for seven yards. That is a great day for a young corner against one of the best receivers in the league.

But there was one drive that hurt. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, when McCarthy went out of the game with an injury, veteran Jameis Winston came in and led a seventy-six-yard touchdown drive. A backup quarterback walking down the field against this defense in the middle of a close game. That is the kind of moment that has defined this season.

And then there was the offense. And this is where it gets ugly.

According to PHLY Sports, the Eagles scored on one touchdown drive in thirteen possessions, and they went three and out seven times. According to TruMedia data cited by PHLY, it was the Eagles’ worst offensive performance of the season by expected points per drive and success rate. It ranked two hundred forty-fifth out of two hundred sixty offensive performances in the entire NFL this season. And PHLY called it the fourth-worst offensive game of the Sirianni era.

According to Athlon Sports, the Eagles gained just two hundred twenty yards, and it was the first time since 2019 that they scored eleven or fewer points in back-to-back games. Saquon Barkley, still playing through hamstring and back injuries, had thirteen carries for twenty-two yards, though he did score the Eagles’ only touchdown, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia. And Joe Milton added a two-point conversion. Jake Elliott, playing through an ankle injury, hit a forty-five-yard field goal. That was all eleven points.

And here is the number that sums it all up. According to PHLY Sports, Joe Milton was sacked seven times. Seven. Giants rookie linebacker Arvell Reese had three of them, including a strip sack where he came off the edge unblocked. According to Athlon Sports, the Giants had only nine sacks in seven games coming into this one. And the Eagles have now allowed twelve sacks in the last two games and thirty through nine games, with three different quarterbacks.

Now, let me talk about Joe Milton, because there were some positives.

According to the Inquirer, Milton went eighteen of thirty-four for one hundred sixty-nine yards, with no touchdowns and no interceptions. He lost a fumble on that strip sack. But he also led the team in rushing, with nine carries for sixty-four yards, including scrambles of nineteen and twenty-four yards, according to Athlon Sports. According to Reuben Frank of NBC Sports Philadelphia, as cited by Athlon, his receivers dropped four passes. And according to PHLY Sports, Sean Mannion did call more quarterback runs, rollouts and deep shots, which is exactly what a lot of fans asked for.

After the game, Milton said, quote, for my first game here, I feel like I did all right. End quote. He said, quote, there are a lot of things that I can clean up as far as protection. End quote. And about the start, he said, quote, I felt more blessed than anything. End quote. Sirianni called it a mixed bag. He said, quote, he had some really good plays, and then he’s going to have plays that he’s going to want back. End quote.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. Milton showed you his legs. He showed you he can extend plays. But a quarterback who gets sacked seven times, with his top two receivers out and receivers dropping passes, is not going to win many games. That is not all on Milton. It is on everything around him.

And Makai Lemon kept showing up. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the rookie caught six of nine targets for sixty-one yards, one of the bright spots on a dark day. Dallas Goedert caught five passes for fifty yards, but most of that came in the fourth quarter. And rookie tight end Eli Stowers played only seven snaps, which is something a lot of fans are going to ask about.

And then there was the ending. According to Athlon Sports, with about two minutes and thirty seconds left, Elijah Mitchell caught a low pass on third down, went to the ground without being touched, and stayed down short of the marker. The Eagles had to settle for that forty-five-yard field goal. Later, with thirty-one seconds left and no timeouts, Mitchell caught a short pass and kept running upfield instead of stepping out of bounds, forcing Milton to spike the ball. Those are the kind of situational mistakes that keep showing up for this team.

There was one fun moment, though. According to PHLY Sports, punter Braden Mann blasted an eighty-six-yard punt at the end of the first half. That was the longest punt in the NFL since 2001, and the second-longest in Eagles history, behind Randall Cunningham’s ninety-one-yarder in 1989. But according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Mann later had a punt of minus two yards after a fumbled snap. That pretty much sums up this season.

And one more injury note. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Zack Baun left the game with a back injury after his two-sack day.

Now, let us get to the question everyone is asking. Is Nick Sirianni about to be replaced?

After the game, Sirianni was asked if staff changes are on the table during the bye week. According to Crossing Broad, he answered, quote, we’ll talk. End quote. And then he said, quote, everything can always be on the table, but I don’t think you change just to change. End quote. He said he would, quote, look at myself internally first. End quote. And he said, quote, you’ve got to look at what’s really real and not react to anything else. End quote. According to Athlon Sports, he also said that if there is a strong reason for a move and he is, in his words, convicted on it, that’s how we’ll go about our business.

So Sirianni is not ruling out changes. But notice what he is talking about. He is talking about his staff. Not his own job. That decision belongs to Jeffrey Lurie.

And last week, Sirianni talked about his relationship with the owner. According to Dave Zangaro of NBC Sports Philadelphia, he said, quote, I’ve always felt nothing but complete support from Mr. Lurie in everything. End quote. He said the owner has his back through good times and through bad times. But Zangaro also wrote that nobody, including Lurie, knows yet whether Sirianni will keep his job, with nine games left in the season. And according to Zangaro, The Athletic gave the Eagles a one percent chance to make the playoffs. If they miss, it would end a streak of playoff trips under Sirianni.

So let me be crystal clear. There is no replacement hired. But there are real questions about the future. And that brings us to the most interesting part of this story. If Lurie ever did make a move during the season, who would take over?

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you think Sirianni finishes the season as head coach. Comment number two if you think Lurie makes a change before the season ends.

Now, here is a detail that most Eagles fans missed this offseason. According to the Inquirer’s breakdown of the 2026 coaching staff, defensive line coach Clint Hurtt had a new title added. He is now the defensive line coach, senior defensive assistant and associate head coach. And running backs coach Jemal Singleton also carries the title of assistant head coach.

Why does that matter? Because if a team ever needs an interim head coach in the middle of a season, it usually turns to someone already in the building, someone the players know and respect. And an associate head coach title tells you the organization values that coach and wants to give him more responsibility.

Clint Hurtt has experience running a unit at this level. He was the defensive coordinator for the Seattle Seahawks for two seasons before coming to Philadelphia. And this past January, according to the Inquirer, he was a head coach at the Senior Bowl. He has worked with this defensive line every day, and that unit just had six sacks against the Giants.

Now, I want to be very careful here. Nobody has reported that Clint Hurtt is going to replace Nick Sirianni. Nobody has reported that the Eagles are even considering an interim coach. This is a question about the coaching structure, not a report about a decision. If you see a headline telling you a replacement is already hired, it is not telling you the truth.

And there are other names in the building. Vic Fangio has been a head coach before, with the Denver Broncos. But his job right now is running this defense, and I would have a hard time picturing him running the whole team in the middle of a season like this one. And on offense, according to Athlon Sports, pass game coordinator Josh Grizzard, a former Buccaneers play caller, is the in-house option if the Eagles ever made a change at offensive coordinator. That is the more likely change, if any change happens at all.

Now, let us talk about history, because Jeffrey Lurie has been in this spot before.

Lurie bought the Eagles in 1994. That season, Rich Kotite’s team started seven and two, then lost seven straight, and Kotite finished the season before he was let go. In 1998, Ray Rhodes went three and thirteen, and he was let go after the final game. In 2012, Andy Reid went four and twelve, and Lurie waited until the season was over to move on. The one exception was Chip Kelly. In 2015, Lurie released Kelly with one game left, and Pat Shurmur coached the final game as interim. According to Athlon Sports, that move came only after the Eagles were eliminated.

So Lurie’s history says he usually waits until the season ends. Athlon Sports even argued that Lurie should not fire Sirianni during the bye, because handing eight games to an interim coach would accomplish nothing a January decision could not. That is their opinion. But it matches Lurie’s track record.

And then there is Doug Pederson. In January 2021, after a four and eleven and one season, Lurie moved on from the coach who had won the franchise’s first Super Bowl. When he explained the decision, Lurie said the difference in vision was, in his words, much more about where we’re at as a franchise. He said the Eagles were at a transition point. He said they had to get younger, get more volume of draft picks, and focus on the midterm and the long term, not on how to maximize one season. And he said it was not a difference of opinion. It was a difference of where they both were.

That tells you something important. A Super Bowl ring did not save Doug Pederson. Lurie made a decision about the future of the franchise, not just the past.

But there is another side to Lurie’s history. After the 2023 season, the Eagles lost six of their last seven games, including the playoff loss. A lot of people thought Sirianni was done. But Lurie kept him. The Eagles changed coordinators, bringing in Kellen Moore and Vic Fangio. And the very next season, the Eagles won Super Bowl fifty-nine. According to Zangaro, that experience may weigh heavily on Lurie’s thinking right now. He gave Sirianni a second chance once, and it led to a championship.

So which Lurie shows up this time? The one who moved on from Pederson after a Super Bowl? Or the one who stuck with Sirianni and won a title?

Here is what makes this time different. The Eagles already changed coordinators this offseason, with Sean Mannion running the offense. They lost Lane Johnson to retirement and traded Cam Jurgens. Jalen Hurts is out for the season with blood clots in his lungs, and the team expects a full recovery. They have started three different quarterbacks. And they have lost seven straight. That is a lot more than one bad stretch.

And here is the strongest case for patience. Sirianni has made the playoffs in every full season as head coach. He has been to two Super Bowls and won one. Athlon Sports wrote that he has earned the rest of the season, and that Lurie should evaluate him in January. But Athlon also noted that seven straight losses, three quarterbacks and five coordinators in six years give Lurie grounds to hold Sirianni to the standard he set himself.

Now, let us talk about the next forty-eight hours, because this matters too. The trade deadline is Tuesday, November tenth, at four in the afternoon Eastern. According to Athlon Sports, the Eagles are slotted number five in Tankathon’s 2027 draft order heading into Week 10, and Athlon argues the Eagles should sell. Landon Dickerson has drawn interest from other teams, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia’s Ryan Fowler and ESPN’s Adam Schefter. Riq Woolen and Moro Ojomo are the other names most often mentioned. Albert Breer reported that Tanner McKee is among the players other teams see as available. And ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler said his sense is that the Eagles, quote, could be trading some players but likely won’t engage in a rebuild. End quote.

So if the Eagles start selling on Tuesday, it will tell you a lot about how the front office sees this season. And if they hold, it will tell you they still believe in this group.

After the bye, the Eagles come back home against the Pittsburgh Steelers on Sunday, November twenty-second, in the late afternoon window. Then they go to Dallas on Thanksgiving. Those two games could decide a lot about the rest of this season, and maybe about Sirianni’s future.

Here is what I will be watching during the bye. First, the coaching staff. If Sirianni makes a change at offensive coordinator or on the offensive line, that is a sign he is serious about a new approach. Second, the trade deadline. Selling veterans would tell you the front office is thinking about 2027. Third, the injury report. A healthier DeVonta Smith, Dontayvion Wicks and Saquon Barkley after two weeks of rest could change how this offense looks. And fourth, Joe Milton. If he stays the starter, two weeks of practice could make a real difference for a quarterback who has only been in this building for three weeks.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the people who say it is time for a change. They will say the Eagles are two and seven, they have lost seven straight, and the offense just had one of its worst games of the entire Sirianni era. They will say Milton was sacked seven times, and that the same mistakes keep happening week after week. They will say Lurie moved on from Doug Pederson after a Super Bowl, so a ring does not protect anybody. They will say the Eagles have an associate head coach in Clint Hurtt who could steady the team for the rest of the season. And they will say that if nothing changes during the bye, the rest of this season will look exactly the same.

On the other side, the people who say keep Sirianni. They will say he has made the playoffs in every full season and won a Super Bowl two seasons ago. They will say no coach can win with his starting quarterback out for the year, his star running back banged up, his top two receivers hurt and his offensive line rebuilt. They will say Lurie stuck with him after the 2023 collapse, and it led to a championship. They will say the defense just had six sacks and held the Giants under one hundred rushing yards, which shows the players are still fighting. And they will say Lurie’s history says he will wait until January, and that is the right call.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I do not think Lurie fires Nick Sirianni during the bye week. His history says he waits, and handing the team to an interim coach for eight games would not fix the real problems. But I do think the bye week has to bring real changes. Sirianni said, we’ll talk. He said everything is on the table. Now he has to show it. That could mean a change in play calling, a simpler offense, or a new approach to protection, because seven sacks is not acceptable. And I think the second half of this season is a full evaluation of everybody, including the head coach. If the Eagles come out of the bye and look the same against Pittsburgh and Dallas, the pressure on Lurie to act will become enormous.

As for the headlines saying Sirianni’s replacement is already hired, ignore them. The real story is bigger than one name. It is about whether Lurie believes this coaching staff can lead the Eagles back, or whether he believes it is time for a new direction.
