Eagles Nation, the Philadelphia Eagles just went shopping for a running back, and there is a little twist in this story that I absolutely love. Because the back they are bringing in once belonged to the very same team that just broke our hearts in London. A big, physical runner who scored fifteen touchdowns in the SEC, who knows this building, who knows this playbook, and who could find himself on the field a lot sooner than anyone expected because of what is happening in the Eagles backfield right now.

Let me start with the problem, because you cannot understand this move without understanding just how thin this backfield has gotten.

Saquon Barkley has been dealing with a hamstring injury. He did not play in London, and on Wednesday he did not practice. Nick Sirianni said earlier this week that he is hopeful Saquon can play on Sunday, but after Wednesday, that is still a big question mark.

Tank Bigsby is on injured reserve. And remember, the Eagles traded a fifth and a sixth-round pick to get Bigsby from Jacksonville back in September of 2025. He has been an important part of this backfield, and now he is out for at least a few more games.

Then in London, Will Shipley went down with a foot injury. ESPN reported it is a partial plantar fascia tear and that he is expected to miss multiple weeks. Shipley actually scored a touchdown in that game. So in one afternoon, the Eagles lost another back.

And it is not like this started in London. Back in training camp, the Eagles had already lost Elijah Mitchell, the former 49ers back they signed in June, when he was placed on injured reserve in August. Injuries have been chewing through this running back room since before the season even started.

So who is left? On the active roster, Dameon Pierce and Carson Steele. On the practice squad, Jaydon Blue. That is it. And Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice pointed out the number that tells you everything. In London, without Barkley and without Bigsby, Eagles running backs ran sixteen times for twenty yards. Sixteen carries, twenty yards. That is barely more than a yard per carry. Eagles Nation, you cannot win football games like that. You cannot protect a lead like that. And you cannot help your quarterback like that.

So the front office went to work. According to Matt Zenitz of CBS Sports, the Eagles are expected to sign running back Ja’Quinden Jackson to their practice squad. Jackson visited the team on Tuesday. And this is not a random name off the street. Zenitz pointed out that Jackson was with Philadelphia during the offseason. He knows the people. He knows the building. He knows the system.

And Jackson is not the only practice squad move this week. According to Zach Berman of The Athletic, confirmed by the NFL transaction wire, the Eagles also signed offensive tackle Xavier Truss and defensive tackle Zion Wilson to the practice squad, and released tight end Josiah Deguara and cornerback Shaun Wade. We did a full video on Zion Wilson’s incredible story earlier this week, so go check that out if you missed it. The point is, this front office is reshuffling the bottom of the roster to match the injuries, and the running back spot is the most urgent of all.

Now let me tell you about this guy, because his story is a fun one.

Ja’Quinden Jackson came out of Duncanville High School in Texas as a quarterback. He signed with the University of Texas, but he never got into a game there. So he transferred to Utah. In 2021, he was a backup quarterback behind Cameron Rising. And then Utah made a decision that changed his career. They moved him to running back. In 2022, his first year at the position, he ran for five hundred thirty-one yards and nine touchdowns on just seventy-eight carries. That is almost seven yards a carry. In 2023, he became the starter and ran for seven hundred ninety-seven yards.

Then he transferred one more time, to Arkansas, for his final college season in 2024. And that is where he really broke out. One hundred forty-nine carries, seven hundred ninety yards, and fifteen rushing touchdowns in ten games. Fifteen touchdowns in the SEC. He earned second-team All-SEC honors. And he is listed at six foot two and around two hundred thirty-three pounds. That is a big back. That is a downhill, short-yardage, goal-line kind of body. And remember, he played quarterback in high school, so he understands protections, reads and timing in a way a lot of young running backs do not.

And here is the twist I told you about. When he went undrafted in 2025, the team that signed him was the Jacksonville Jaguars. He spent his rookie season on their practice squad, they signed him to a futures contract in January, and then they waived him in May. And now, the week after the Jaguars beat the Eagles on a walk-off field goal in London, Philadelphia is going back and grabbing a running back who came through Jacksonville. Just like they did with Tank Bigsby. I am not saying it is revenge. But I am saying it is a fun little storyline.

Now, there is one more part of Jackson’s story that I want to handle with care. The Eagles signed him on August tenth of this year, during training camp. And in a preseason game against the New England Patriots, he went down after a collision on the opening kickoff and was taken off the field in an ambulance. That was a scary moment for everyone watching. Thankfully, he turned out to be okay, and reports say he is now past that injury. The Eagles waived him with an injury settlement on August twenty-fourth. And now, less than two months later, he is coming back to the same building. I think that is a pretty cool full-circle moment for a young man who went through something really scary.

Now, Eagles Nation, I need to be straight with you about something, because I know how these headlines get written. Is Ja’Quinden Jackson a star running back right now? No. Not yet. He has never played in an NFL regular-season game. This is a practice squad signing, not a blockbuster trade. Nobody gave up a first-round pick. If anybody tells you the Eagles just stole a Pro Bowl back from another team, that is not what happened. What happened is that the Eagles grabbed a big, productive college back who already knows their system, at a moment when they badly need bodies at running back.

But here is why I still think this is a smart move, and why it could matter more than people think.

Number one, the practice squad is not a dead end. Teams can elevate practice squad players to the game-day roster. So if Saquon cannot go on Sunday, and with Shipley and Bigsby out, Jackson could realistically be in uniform against Carolina. Yahoo Sports even mentioned that he could make his NFL debut this Sunday at the Linc. Imagine that. Just picture it for a second. A former quarterback turned running back, waived by the Jaguars, taken off the field in an ambulance in the preseason, making his NFL debut a few weeks later in front of sixty-nine thousand people in Philadelphia.

Number two, his body type fills a need. At around two hundred thirty pounds, Jackson is the kind of back you can hand the ball to in short yardage, near the goal line, or late in games when you need to grind the clock. And Eagles Nation, think about London. The whole debate about that final drive was about burning clock. If you have a big back you trust to pick up four yards and stay in bounds, those final seconds look completely different.

Number three, he already knows the offense. He was in this building in August. He sat in those meetings. He knows the terminology. That matters a lot in the middle of the season, when you do not have time to teach somebody everything from scratch.

And number four, this is a move that signals the front office is paying attention. When your running backs produce twenty yards on sixteen carries, you have to do something. Howie Roseman did not panic and trade a first-round pick, but he did not sit still either.

So what does Jackson have to do this week to earn a shot? Simple. Show up in practice, run hard on the scout team, protect the football, and show the coaches he can pick up the blitz. Pass protection is the thing that keeps young backs off the field more than anything else. If he can prove to the staff that he will keep Jalen Hurts clean on third down, his chances of getting elevated on Sunday go way up.

Now let me connect this to the bigger story around this team, because this move does not happen in a vacuum.

Nick Sirianni is under a lot of pressure right now. Three straight losses. Two and three. CBS Sports noted it is the first time the Eagles have been under five hundred since 2021, and he has been mentioned in hot seat conversations. After London, Damien Harris on NFL on CBS called the ending, quote, nothing short of coaching malpractice. End quote. Sirianni himself said, quote, I didn’t make the right decision and I lost us that game. End quote. And on Monday, he added, quote, I think right now offensively we all have to be better. End quote.

Now, I want to keep perspective here. This is a head coach who has taken the Eagles to two Super Bowls, and who won one of them with this very core of players. Nobody in Philadelphia should forget that. But the NFL is a results business, and when you lose three straight, the questions get louder every single week. The fastest way to quiet them is to win on Sunday, and the fastest way to win on Sunday is to fix the run game.

And that last quote is exactly where the running back position comes in. You want to know how this offense gets better fast? Run the football. When the run game works, everything opens up for Jalen Hurts. The play-action game comes alive. The clock is on your side. The defense gets to rest. When the run game produces twenty yards, everything falls on the quarterback, and that is exactly what we saw in the second half in London.

So in a way, Eagles Nation, this practice squad move connects directly to the biggest questions about this team. Can the offense finish games? Can it control the clock? Can it take pressure off the defense and off Hurts? None of that happens without a running game.

And there is more good news on the offensive side. Landon Dickerson returned to practice on Wednesday as a limited participant after the Eagles opened his twenty-one-day practice window. Getting a Pro Bowl-caliber guard back would be a huge boost for the run game, whoever is carrying the ball. Jalen Hurts was on the injury report with a calf issue but was a full participant. DeVonta Smith did not practice with his hamstring. And over on the Panthers side, Carolina has cornerbacks Jaycee Horn and Mike Jackson on injured reserve, so their secondary is banged up.

Sunday, the Carolina Panthers come to Lincoln Financial Field. Kickoff is one in the afternoon Eastern time, on CBS. Carolina is two and two. The Eagles are two and three. And honestly, I think this game will be decided by whether the Eagles can run the football. If Saquon plays, great. If he does not, somebody like Dameon Pierce, Carson Steele, or maybe even Ja’Quinden Jackson is going to have to step up.

Alright, Eagles Nation, now let us look at both sides of this.

On one side, the skeptics. They will say a practice squad running back is not an upgrade, it is a patch. They will say that a guy who has never played an NFL snap cannot be the answer to a run game that just produced twenty yards. They will say that if the Eagles were serious about fixing this backfield, they would make a real trade before the November tenth deadline and bring in a proven veteran. And they will say this move is just a sign of how thin the roster has gotten. That is a fair argument, and I understand it.

And to be fair to that side, the clock is ticking on a bigger move if the Eagles want one. The NFL trade deadline is November tenth at four in the afternoon Eastern time. That gives Howie Roseman a few more weeks to see how Saquon’s hamstring responds, how Shipley’s foot heals, and whether this patched-together backfield can hold up. If the injuries keep piling up, I would not be shocked to see the Eagles look for a veteran back before that deadline. But nothing has been reported on that front, so I am not going to invent a trade for you. For now, Jackson is the move.

On the other side, the optimists. They will say this is exactly the kind of smart, low-cost depth move good teams make. They will point to fifteen touchdowns in the SEC, to a big body built for short yardage, and to the fact that he already knows the system. They will say that Saquon is week-to-week, not out for the season, and the Eagles just need someone to hold things down until he comes back. And they will say that you do not need a star to run behind this offensive line, especially with Dickerson getting closer to returning. You need someone who runs hard, holds onto the football, and falls forward.

And here is where I land. Again, this is just my opinion, not a report. I like this move. I like it a lot more than I expected to. It is not a game-changer by itself, and I am not going to pretend it is. But it is the right move at the right time. You bring back a guy you already know, who fits a need you clearly have, and who costs you almost nothing. And if he gets his chance and runs with it, this could be one of those stories we look back on later in the season and say, remember when the Eagles brought back that big back from the practice squad in October? That is how a lot of great NFL stories start.

And Eagles fans, we have seen this movie before. Think about Boston Scott. The Eagles signed him off the New Orleans Saints practice squad late in the 2018 season. Nobody thought much of it at the time. He was a small, under-the-radar back. And he turned into a fan favorite, a guy who came up big in so many moments, especially against the Giants. Nobody is saying Ja’Quinden Jackson will be the next Boston Scott. They are completely different players. Jackson is a big, physical runner, Scott was a little spark plug. But the lesson is the same. Sometimes the move that looks the smallest on paper ends up mattering the most when the opportunity shows up.

And think about what the opportunity looks like right now. Dameon Pierce brings starting experience from his time in Houston. Carson Steele is a tough, hard-nosed runner who earned his spot on the active roster. Jaydon Blue brings speed. Add Jackson’s size to that group, and suddenly you have a few different flavors to work with while you wait for Saquon. Does that replace an All-Pro? Of course not. But it gives the coaching staff options, and options are exactly what you need when injuries pile up.

But let me also say this. The real upgrade to this offense is not Ja’Quinden Jackson. The real upgrade is Saquon Barkley getting healthy, DeVonta Smith getting back on the field, and Landon Dickerson returning to that offensive line. Jackson is a bridge. A smart bridge. But the Eagles need their stars back to truly turn this season around.

So let me wrap it all up. The Eagles backfield has been hit hard. Saquon is week-to-week with a hamstring. Bigsby is on injured reserve. Shipley is out for weeks with a foot injury. The running backs ran for twenty yards in London. And in response, the Eagles are expected to bring back Ja’Quinden Jackson, a six-foot-two, two-hundred-thirty-pound former quarterback who scored fifteen touchdowns in the SEC, who came through Jacksonville, and who already knows this building. It is not a blockbuster. But it is a move with a purpose. And on Sunday against Carolina, it might just matter.
