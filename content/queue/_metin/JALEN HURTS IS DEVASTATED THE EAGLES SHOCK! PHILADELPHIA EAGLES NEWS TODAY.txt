Eagles Nation, this has been one of the strangest weeks in recent memory for this franchise. Four straight losses. Jalen Hurts out for the season with blood clots in his leg and both lungs. A report that the league hit Nick Sirianni with a quarter-million-dollar fine for his conduct toward officials. And then, on Thursday, Sirianni stepped up to the podium and gave one of the most honest, most personal press conferences of his entire time in Philadelphia. He talked about missing Jalen. He talked about Saquon. He talked about Tanner McKee. And he talked about what it feels like to be a head coach when everything around you is going wrong.

This is James with Second Floor, and today we are going to go through that press conference answer by answer, and I am going to tell you what each answer really means for Monday night against the Dallas Cowboys. We will also look at the brand new practice report, because there is great news and there is worrying news, and we will look at that fine. Let me start with Jalen, because I know that is where your heart is right now.

First, let me be clear about something. A lot of headlines this week are saying Jalen Hurts is devastated. Jalen himself has not spoken publicly since his diagnosis, and I am not going to put feelings into his mouth that he has not shared. What we do know is what the Eagles and his agent, Nicole Lynn, said on Monday. Deep vein thrombosis, and clots in both lungs. He is, quote, clinically stable and has no significant functional limitations, end quote. And, quote, Jalen’s prognosis is very good, and we expect a full recovery, end quote. He will remain around the team and continue to condition while he is on blood thinners.

But on Thursday, Sirianni was asked how much he expects Jalen to be around the team for the rest of the year. And his answer told you a lot. He said, quote, we’ll see. Everybody’s a little bit different in their recoveries and with their meetings and where they are. End quote. He said it is fluid. He said he was not going to put any of that business out there. And then he said this, quote, as I’ve told you guys, I miss Jalen, miss being around him on a daily basis. End quote.

Eagles Nation, think about that for a second. Just two weeks ago, this whole city was arguing about whether Sirianni and Hurts were on the same page, after Jalen said it was not his place to question the clock management in London and Sirianni answered that the coaches call the plays. And now the head coach is standing at the podium saying, I miss him. I miss being around him every day. That is not a coach managing a relationship. That is a man who cares about his quarterback. Whatever you thought about that debate, it feels like a very long time ago now.

And Sirianni was also clear about privacy. He is not going to tell us how often Jalen is in the building or what his schedule looks like. And honestly, that is exactly how it should be. Jalen’s health is his business. When he is ready to talk, he will talk.

Now, the good news. Saquon Barkley.

On Thursday, Saquon practiced for the first time since he hurt his hamstring early in Week 4 against the Rams. He was limited. And Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice wrote that he is expected to play on Monday night. Eagles Nation, that is the best news this team has had in weeks.

And Sirianni was asked what Saquon’s return would mean, not just on the field, but for the morale of the whole team. Here is what he said. Quote, obviously Saquon’s a phenomenal football player, phenomenal leader, captain. I mean, they voted him captain, his teammates. So anytime you have an opportunity to get someone like him back on the field, that’s huge. End quote. He also said, quote, we’ll see where we are as the week progresses, end quote, and that he was looking forward to seeing him out there.

Then he was asked how much Saquon’s presence helps Tanner McKee in the play-action game. And this answer was a little window into how this staff thinks. Sirianni said, quote, your play action pass game gets better and better as you run the football better. End quote. He talked about complementary football, about converting on third downs, about staying in manageable situations. And he said, quote, anytime you add a player of Saquon’s caliber to your team, that’s obviously a huge plus. End quote.

Let me translate that into plain English. The Eagles ran for thirty-five yards against Carolina. They rank twenty-eighth in the league in rushing yards per game, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia. When you cannot run, nobody bites on play action, every third down is third and long, and a backup quarterback gets buried. Saquon fixes that overnight. Even if he is on a pitch count, defenses have to respect him. One healthy Saquon Barkley makes Tanner McKee a better quarterback without McKee changing a single thing.

Now let me be careful, because Saquon has been healthy for only two full games this season, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia. He has thirty-six carries for one hundred eighty-two yards, which is five point one yards a carry, but no touchdowns yet. Hamstrings can be tricky, and the team will be smart with him. But if he plays Monday night, the energy at the Linc is going to be electric.

Now, the worrying news from that same practice report. DeVonta Smith did not practice on Thursday because of his hamstring. Remember, Smith missed two games with that hamstring, came back against Carolina, and caught seven passes for seventy-five yards and a touchdown. Now he is back on the report. The team has not said it is a setback, and Kempski did not call it one either, so I am not going to call it one. But it is something to watch closely on Friday and Saturday. Dallas Goedert also did not practice, with a knee issue. Nolan Smith did not practice as he works through the concussion from the Carolina game. Willie Lampkin was out with an illness, and Dontayvion Wicks did not practice with an ankle.

On the other side, there were some encouraging names. Landon Dickerson was limited with knee and pectoral issues, but he was out there. Jalyx Hunt and Jonathan Greenard were both limited. And Jordan Davis, Jalen Carter, Zack Baun, Jihaad Campbell and Grant Calcaterra were all full participants. The Eagles also signed former Giants first-round pick Evan Neal to the practice squad after a tryout, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia. Offensive line depth, again. And Elijah Mitchell, the back the Eagles brought back to the practice squad on Tuesday, gives them another body in the backfield behind Saquon and Dameon Pierce.

Now, let us talk about the fine, because a lot of you have been asking. According to Albert Breer of Sports Illustrated, the NFL fined Nick Sirianni two hundred fifty thousand dollars for his conduct toward officials. Patriots coach Mike Vrabel and Jets coach Aaron Glenn were reportedly fined the same amount. But here is the important part. Breer reported that the fines are being held in abeyance. That means they are on hold, and they could go away with good behavior. So, as PhillyVoice put it, Sirianni was kind of, sort of, not really fined, at least not yet.

At the press conference, a reporter asked Sirianni whether the league specified a particular moment. His answer was firm. Quote, I won’t get into my conversations with the league. End quote. He said he keeps his conversations with players in house, his conversations with the league in house, and his conversations with the referees association in house. And he left it at that.

And this is not a brand new conversation in Philadelphia. Back in March of 2024, owner Jeffrey Lurie talked about Sirianni’s sideline behavior. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Lurie said that on the sideline, quote, you don’t want to infuriate the referees, end quote. But he also said, quote, I love his passion. End quote. That has always been the balance with Nick Sirianni. The fire that his players love is the same fire that sometimes gets him in trouble. And with a fine now hanging in abeyance, he has every reason to keep that fire pointed at the other team, not at the officials.

Then a reporter asked if anything had changed in the way he deals with officials over the years. And this is where the press conference turned really personal. Sirianni said, quote, you are in constant growth, and I truly believe that I’ve been in constant growth. End quote. He talked about trying to get better every day, as a coach, as a human being, as a Christian, as a father, as a husband and as a son. And he said, quote, there are times where you relapse, and you have a moment that you want back, but I can truly see growth. End quote. And he finished with this, quote, that doesn’t mean we’re perfect. We all fall, and it’s about how you get back up. End quote.

Eagles Nation, I have watched a lot of Nick Sirianni press conferences. That one felt different. No defensiveness. No excuses. Just a man admitting he is not perfect and saying he is trying to be better. You can be frustrated with his decisions on the field, and a lot of people are after London. But you have to respect that answer.

Now, Tanner McKee. Sirianni was asked how much it will help McKee to have a full week of first-team reps and meetings. And he gave McKee a big vote of confidence. Quote, Tanner loves football. He’s highly intelligent. Football IQ. And he’s tough. End quote. And then he said this, quote, those guys, God willing, have a tendency to reach their ceiling. End quote. He said he has no doubt McKee will put in the work, and that because of his intangibles, he will continue to rise. He even compared it to veteran quarterbacks around the league who keep getting better as their minds get sharper, and he finished with, quote, every rep counts and every rep matters. End quote.

That matters, Eagles Nation. Last week, McKee started on short notice. This week, he gets a full week as the starter, with a full game plan built around him. In his first start against Carolina, he went twenty-six of forty-five for two hundred fifteen yards, a touchdown and no interceptions. He protected the ball. Now, with Saquon potentially back and a full week of prep, I expect a much more comfortable McKee on Monday night.

Then came a question about the offense. A reporter asked how the staff balances its core beliefs with adjustments based on the personnel it has. And Sirianni went back to one of his favorite phrases. Quote, it always is players over plays. I mean, it will always be players over plays. End quote. He said you want a system as the bones of your operation, but you also have to attack what the defense gives you with the strengths of your players. And he said, quote, do what you need to do to win each and every game, that we haven’t been able to the last couple weeks, and we need to get back on track. End quote.

To me, that is a message for Sean Mannion and the whole offensive staff. Do not force a system onto a roster that has lost its quarterback, half its running backs and a couple of receivers. Build the game plan around who is actually healthy. Saquon. Dameon Pierce. Elijah Mitchell, who just came back on the practice squad. A tight end group that might be thin if Goedert cannot go. And a quarterback who thrives on timing and getting the ball out quickly.

And then came the last question, and it was the best one. A reporter asked Sirianni what parts of the job he still enjoys, with four straight losses and everything going on with Jalen. And his answer was raw. Quote, hell yeah, I enjoy it. I hate losing. End quote. He said he loves coaching young men to become better football players and better men. And he said, quote, I know I can always control my attitude, my effort, my energy. To me, that’s mental toughness. End quote.

He talked about how hard it is to come home to his kids after a loss, when they want all of his attention. He said, quote, I’m not saying I’m perfect at that, and I imagine if you interviewed my kids, they’d probably say the same thing. End quote. He said he gives himself a little grace time to feel upset, to watch the tape, and then something snaps and he is back into the process. And he compared it to a basketball player dribbling in a chaotic gym, focusing on the dribble so he can make the shot.

Eagles Nation, that is the heart of this press conference. A head coach on a four-game losing streak, with his franchise quarterback out for the year, and a fine hanging over his head, telling you exactly how he climbs out of the hole. Attitude, effort, energy. Focus on the process. And that is the mindset this team needs walking into Monday night.

Now let us talk about the Cowboys. Dallas is two and four, just like the Eagles, and they have lost two straight. Dak Prescott has thrown four interceptions in his last two games. On Thursday’s report, returner KaVontae Turpin did not practice and, according to Kempski, is not expected to play Monday. Starting linebacker Dee Winters did not practice. George Pickens was limited with a shoulder, and left tackle Tyler Guyton and cornerback Cobie Durant were limited too. Linebacker DeMarvion Overshown was a full participant and could be back.
