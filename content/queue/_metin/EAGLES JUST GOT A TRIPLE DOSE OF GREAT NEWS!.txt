Eagles Nation, after a bye week full of bad headlines, trades and releases, I finally have some good news for you. Now, let me be honest about one thing up front. There was no single big announcement from the Eagles this weekend. But when you put together everything that happened over the last few days, there really are three pieces of news that should make Eagles fans feel a little bit better heading into the Steelers game. One, the Steelers just got blown out on Sunday Night Football. Two, the Eagles turned down a team that wanted Dallas Goedert, and he might even stay beyond this season. And three, the Cincinnati Bengals publicly took the blame for the trade that fell apart, which means the Eagles kept a pass rusher they now need more than ever. And on top of all of that, Jordan Davis opened up even more about leaving Philadelphia, and what he said is going to stay with a lot of you.

This is James with Second Floor, and here is what we are covering today. What happened to the Steelers on Sunday night, the 49ers’ interest in Dallas Goedert, the Bengals’ apology, Jordan Davis’s emotional goodbye and why he did not play in his first Jaguars game, the grades for the Davis trade, and what it all means for the Eagles’ defense, including a familiar name some people want back. Before we start, if you are still riding with this team, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor.

Let me start with the first piece of good news, because it happened just hours ago.

The Pittsburgh Steelers went to Cincinnati for Sunday Night Football, and the Bengals beat them thirty-eight to eighteen. That is a twenty-point loss on national television. The Steelers came into that game at six and two, and now they are six and three. And the Bengals improved to seven and two.

Now, I want to be careful here. One loss does not make the Steelers a bad team. They are still one of the better teams in the AFC. But think about the situation for Sunday. According to Ed Kracz of Eagles On SI, the Eagles are coming off a bye week, while the Steelers are coming off that Sunday night game. So the Eagles have had two weeks to rest and prepare. The Steelers are coming off a blowout loss, with a normal week to get ready. And according to Kracz, Nick Sirianni is five and zero coming out of bye weeks in his career, including a thirty-five to thirteen win over the Steelers in 2022.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. That Sunday night result does not guarantee anything for the Eagles. But a team coming off a twenty-point loss can respond in two ways. It can come out angry, or it can come out shaken. And the Eagles, with fresh legs and possibly some key players back, have a real chance to take advantage if Pittsburgh is still shaken.

Now, the second piece of good news. Dallas Goedert.

According to Maliha Akter of ClutchPoints, as published on Yahoo Sports on November fifteenth, citing ESPN’s Ian Rapoport and Adam Schefter, several teams asked about Goedert before the deadline, including the San Francisco 49ers. The 49ers reportedly wanted him to help a pass-catching group that has been hit by injuries. But the Eagles stood firm. According to the report, Goedert, quote, never got close to being traded. End quote. And here is the part I really like. According to that same report, he, quote, could even re-sign with the Eagles for 2027. End quote.

And you can see why the Eagles wanted to keep him. According to ClutchPoints, with DeVonta Smith out with a hamstring injury, Goedert has been one of the Eagles’ top targets. He has thirty-one catches for three hundred eight yards and two touchdowns in seven games. According to the Inquirer, trading him before the deadline would also have roughly doubled his dead cap to eighteen point two million dollars.

And Goedert matters for more than just his catches. Rookie tight end Eli Stowers is still developing. According to Ed Kracz, Stowers played only seven snaps against the Giants, while Goedert played fifty-seven. A lot of people, including me, want Stowers to get more snaps over the final eight games. But having a veteran like Goedert in that room, teaching him every day, is exactly what a young tight end needs. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Stowers even said he watched Goedert during his own move from quarterback to tight end.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I am glad Goedert stayed. A late-round pick was not worth losing a veteran leader in the middle of a season like this. And if the price is right, I would be open to bringing him back in 2027 as a mentor for Stowers. He took a pay cut to stay in 2025 and came back on a one-year deal this year. That tells you how much he wants to be here.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you want Goedert back in 2027. Comment number two if you think it is time to hand the job to Stowers.

Now, the third piece of good news. And it comes with a little bit of an embarrassing story for the Cincinnati Bengals.

You might remember that the Eagles agreed to trade edge rusher Arnold Ebiketie to the Bengals for a 2027 sixth-round pick before the deadline. But according to theScore, the Bengals called off the deal after learning they would also forfeit a third-round compensatory pick if the trade went through. According to the Inquirer, Nick Korte of Over The Cap explained it. Because Ebiketie was a compensatory free agent signing for the Eagles, adding him would have cost Cincinnati that projected third-round comp pick.

And then the Bengals did something you do not see very often. They publicly took the blame. According to theScore, head coach Zac Taylor said director of player personnel Duke Tobin was very explicit about how he wanted it explained. Taylor said, quote, Duke was very explicit with me when he came and spoke to me about how he wanted this portrayed. End quote. He said, quote, as we got into it, obviously it was going to be a little more costly than we initially anticipated. End quote. And he said the Eagles were a, quote, great trade partner, end quote, and that, quote, it was on our end. End quote. According to theScore, Taylor also said Tobin was really apologetic.

And according to theScore, the reason Cincinnati wanted Ebiketie in the first place was to add depth to replace Cashius Howell. So the Bengals had a real need, they made a real offer, and then they had to walk it back because of their own mistake. Taylor even said the failed deal, quote, doesn’t really matter to me, end quote, and the Bengals went out and beat Pittsburgh by twenty anyway.

So why is that good news for the Eagles? Two reasons. First, the Eagles’ reputation as a trade partner is completely intact. Other front offices will see that the Eagles did nothing wrong. And with all the draft picks Howie Roseman is collecting, he is going to be making a lot more trades in the next few years.

And second, the Eagles kept a pass rusher at a time when their defensive line is getting thinner. According to theScore, Ebiketie has twenty-five tackles and two quarterback hits in nine games this season. And with Jordan Davis gone, the Eagles need all the bodies they can get up front. For a sixth-round pick, keeping Ebiketie might actually be the better outcome.

And then, of course, there was the Bengals’ Sunday night win over the Steelers. The same team that backed out of the Ebiketie trade just did the Eagles a big favor by beating their next opponent. I will take it.

Now, let me talk about Jordan Davis, because he said even more this week, and it is worth hearing.

According to ClutchPoints, citing an interview with FOX29 Philly’s Jeff Skversky at the airport, Davis said, quote, and you know, I love those guys, so however I can help the team, even in my departure, I’m absolutely glad to be doing that. End quote.

Think about that. A player who was just traded away is saying he is glad if his departure helps the team that traded him. That is a captain.

And according to the Associated Press, as published by theScore, Davis described the moment he left. He was heading to the Philadelphia airport on Monday when he, quote, had a moment. End quote. He played Frank Sinatra’s My Way on his phone, and he said he tried not to cry.

And he talked about what Philadelphia meant to him. According to the AP, he said, quote, the time that I spent in Philly, I have grown so much from that. End quote. And, quote, I became a better player because of it, became a better man because of it. End quote. He also said, quote, I would never sit there and turn my back on Philly. End quote. And, quote, I don’t have any hate. I don’t have any bad blood. End quote. And then, quote, it’s just the business of it all. It’s just the nature of the beast. End quote.

And then he looked ahead. According to the AP, he said, quote, I’m grateful for the time, but right now, have another mission. I have another city. I have a new city to embrace. End quote.

And earlier in the week, according to PennLive, Davis thanked the fans directly, saying, quote, thank you for everything from the bottom of my heart, end quote, and, quote, I appreciate the cheers and the boos and everything in between. End quote.

And Davis also shared some of the lessons he is taking with him to Jacksonville. According to the AP, he said, quote, it takes a lot to go to the Super Bowl, and it takes a lot to win the Super Bowl. End quote. And he gave his new teammates some advice that a lot of Eagles fans could use right now too. Quote, I know everybody likes to look to the end goal, but you can’t see the end if you don’t see where your feet are right now. End quote. And, in true Jordan Davis fashion, he summed himself up with four words. Quote, big guys get it done. End quote.

Eagles Nation, Jordan Davis left this city the right way. He was a first-round pick in 2022, a Super Bowl champion and a captain, and he walked out the door with nothing but love for Philadelphia. Thank you, Jordan. Philadelphia will always be your home.

Now, here is something you might not know. Davis did not play in his first game with the Jaguars. According to Heavy, citing ESPN’s Cameron Wolfe, head coach Liam Coen gave Davis the week off against the Titans, to give him extra rest and time to learn the playbook, since he never got a bye week this season. And according to CBS Sports, the Jaguars rolled to a forty to twenty-three win over Tennessee anyway. According to the Jaguars’ official website, it was their third straight win, and they now lead the AFC South by four games. According to Yardbarker, Trevor Lawrence threw for three hundred fifty-two yards and three touchdowns, and according to the Jaguars’ website, running back Bhayshul Tuten ran for eighty-eight yards and a touchdown on twenty-six carries. But the Jaguars also gave up twenty-three points, and according to CBS Sports, it was their second-highest total allowed this season with Davis sitting out. So Jacksonville clearly still needs him in the middle, and according to CBS, he is expected to be active when the Jaguars host Tennessee again in two weeks.

And that brings up the one part of this story that is not great news for Eagles fans. The Jaguars are seven and two. That means the 2027 first-round pick the Eagles got from Jacksonville could land late in the first round. But late first-round picks still matter, and you can always package picks to move up. And according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the trade experts still loved the deal for Philadelphia. Daniel Popper of The Athletic gave the Eagles an A plus and the Jaguars a D minus. Garrett Podell of CBS Sports gave the Eagles an A. Kevin Patra of NFL.com gave the Eagles an A. And Seth Walder of ESPN gave the Eagles an A and the Jaguars an F, opening with, quote, this trade is a disaster for Jacksonville. End quote.

And the explanations were just as interesting as the grades. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Popper called it, quote, a significant overpay, end quote, and said Davis, quote, does not offer nearly enough as a pass rusher. End quote. Podell said Roseman, quote, couldn’t have asked for anything more. End quote. And Patra wrote, quote, I’m not sure Howie Roseman wanted to move Davis after just paying him. End quote. That last one is important. Remember, Davis signed a three-year, seventy-eight million dollar extension in March, according to PennLive. The Eagles did not trade him because they wanted to get rid of him. They traded him because the offer was too good to turn down. And according to Heavy, Davis had just one and a half sacks this season, which tied him for thirty-fifth among defensive tackles. The run defense was his strength, not the pass rush, and the Jaguars paid a first-round price anyway.

Now, let me talk about what the Davis trade means for the Eagles’ defense going forward, because there are two big storylines here.

The first is the opportunity for young players. According to Ed Kracz, defensive tackle Ty Robinson has only sixty-one defensive snaps in his second season, but he has held up well in a rotational role. With Davis gone, there should be more snaps for him. According to Larry Rupp of Eagles On SI, Robinson is six foot five and two hundred eighty-eight pounds, with an explosive first step, and he was called a wrecking ball at Nebraska. Kracz also suggested the Eagles elevate practice squad defensive tackle Zion Wilson, who had seven sacks at East Carolina in 2025. And according to PhillyVoice, Moro Ojomo is about to get paid, and he and Jalen Carter are now the future of the interior defensive line.

The second storyline is the one some fans are already dreaming about. A possible reunion with Jim Schwartz.

Let me be very careful here. According to Larry Rupp of Eagles On SI, the Eagles reportedly checked in with Schwartz earlier this year when Vic Fangio considered retiring. Fangio denied it at the time, and he came back. According to Jeff Neiburg of the Inquirer, Fangio may retire after the 2026 season. But nobody has reported that Fangio is leaving, and nobody has reported that the Eagles have offered Schwartz anything. This is a possibility, not a decision.

But here is why the Davis trade fits into that conversation. According to Neiburg, Davis was a perfect fit as a nose tackle in Fangio’s three-four defense, but he could be a poorer fit if the Eagles ever moved to a four-three. And according to Anthony Miller of Inside The Iggles, Davis did not fit Schwartz’s four-three as well as Carter and Ojomo do. Schwartz was the defensive coordinator when the Eagles won Super Bowl fifty-two. According to Inside The Iggles, the Browns ranked in the top four in total defense in two of the last three years under him, and he is currently a free agent.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I do not think the Eagles traded Davis to set up a Schwartz reunion. They traded him because Jacksonville offered a first-round pick. But the trade does give them flexibility. If Fangio stays, great. If he retires, the Eagles have a defensive line built around Carter and Ojomo that could fit a more aggressive, attacking style. And Schwartz would make a lot of sense.

Now, let me give you a quick update on the most banged-up spots on this roster, because that is good news too.

According to Ed Kracz, DeVonta Smith, who has played in just four games because of a hamstring injury, and Dontayvion Wicks, who has missed three straight games with an ankle injury, are both likely to return after the bye. Neither one was placed on injured reserve. At running back, Saquon Barkley is the only back on the active roster after the Eagles waived Jaydon Blue. But according to Kracz, Tank Bigsby and Will Shipley have both missed the required four games on injured reserve, so either one could return for the Steelers game. And with Davis traded and Blue, Hollywood Brown and Fred Johnson released, the Eagles have four open roster spots to fill.

And here is what I will be watching this week. First, whether Smith and Wicks practice fully. Second, whether Bigsby or Shipley is activated. Third, who fills those four open roster spots. Fourth, whether Ty Robinson and Zion Wilson get a bigger role on the defensive line. And fifth, how the Steelers respond after getting blown out in Cincinnati.

According to the Inquirer, the Eagles host the Steelers on Sunday, November twenty-second, at one in the afternoon Eastern on CBS. Then they travel to Dallas for Thanksgiving on November twenty-sixth.
