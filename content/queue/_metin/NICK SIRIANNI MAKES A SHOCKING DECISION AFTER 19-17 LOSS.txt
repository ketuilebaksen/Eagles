Eight seconds. That’s all that stood between the Philadelphia Eagles and the biggest win of their season. A banged-up team, playing in London without Saquon Barkley, without DeVonta Smith, with two undrafted linemen making their first NFL starts, had just taken a 17 to 16 lead on a 48-yard Jake Elliott field goal. Eight seconds left. Jacksonville with no timeouts. And then Nick Sirianni made a decision that shocked the football world. He chose not to kick the ball out of reach. He let the Jaguars return it. Ameer Abdullah took it 53 yards, Cam Little drilled a 57-yard field goal as time expired, and the Eagles lost 19 to 17. And then, standing at the podium, Sirianni made a second decision that might be even more shocking. No excuses. No explanations. Just, quote, “I didn’t make the right decision and I lost that game.” Eagles Nation, today on Second Floor, we’re breaking down both decisions, the one that cost the Eagles the game, and the one that tells you a lot about where this team goes from here.

Welcome in, everybody. This is James, and you’re on Second Floor. I’m not going to lie to you, this one hurts. But we’re going to talk about it honestly, the good, the bad, and the decision everybody in Philadelphia is still screaming about. If you’re with us, hit that like button, subscribe, and turn on notifications. Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with the moment itself, because you need to understand exactly what happened.

Late in the fourth quarter, the Jaguars took a 16 to 14 lead when Trevor Lawrence found Parker Washington for his second touchdown of the day. And here’s a detail that matters. Cam Little missed the extra point. That kept it a two-point game. And the Eagles’ offense, which had struggled for most of the second half, put together just enough. Jake Elliott came out and hit a 48-yarder with eight seconds left. Eagles 17, Jaguars 16. The Eagles sideline exploded. The traveling Eagles fans in London were going crazy. Eight seconds. Jacksonville had no timeouts left.

And now, the decision. The Eagles had options. Kick it deep through the end zone for a touchback. Or even kick it out of bounds. Either way, Jacksonville would have needed to go a long way in eight seconds with no timeouts just to get into field goal range. Instead, the Eagles kept the ball in play and tried to cover the kick. Jake Elliott said afterward that the instruction was relayed to him to keep the ball in play, and he, quote, “just went out and did what I was told.”

Ameer Abdullah fielded the kick, found a seam, got past the coverage, and ran it back 53 yards before Jonathan Jones finally dragged him down at the Eagles’ 39-yard line. One second left on the clock. Cam Little lined up from 57 yards. And he nailed it. Jaguars 19, Eagles 17. Game over.

And think about the irony for Cam Little. A few minutes earlier, he had missed the extra point that would have given Jacksonville a three-point lead. That miss is the only reason Elliott’s field goal put the Eagles ahead instead of just tying it. And then, with the game on the line, Little stepped up and hit from 57. From goat to hero in a matter of minutes.

There was another layer to the final sequence too, and Sirianni explained it. Before the Elliott field goal, the Jaguars had used their timeouts to stop the clock. The Eagles ran the ball, hoping to pick up a first down so they could burn more time and kick with almost nothing left. Sirianni said ideally you’d love to kick the game-winner with about three seconds left. But Jacksonville stopped the run, and the Eagles had to kick with eight seconds on the clock. A reporter asked why the Eagles didn’t just have Hurts hold the ball and drain the clock. Sirianni said that with interior pressure you’d probably lose two, three, four yards doing that, and it was already a long field goal, so it wasn’t part of the plan. He also admitted that there’s gray area in these situations. Quote, “We want in football to be as black and white as we possibly can, but there’s gray in it.”

Eagles Nation, it’s one of the most painful endings you’ll ever see. Because this game was won. The guys on the field had done enough.

And here’s where it gets even more complicated. Just two weeks ago, the whole NFL was talking about another ending. In Rio, the Dallas Cowboys tied the Ravens with seven seconds left and chose to kick a touchback instead of making Baltimore return the kick. Baltimore got the ball at its own 35, Lamar Jackson hit Zay Flowers for 27 yards, and Tyler Loop made a 56-yard field goal to win it. CBS Sports argued that Dallas should have forced a return to burn more time off the clock. So in a strange way, the Eagles may have been trying to avoid the Dallas mistake by keeping the ball in play. And it backfired in the worst possible way.

When a reporter asked Sirianni about that Dallas game, he said, quote, “I watch a ton of those situations.” He said he watched a ton of them after that Dallas game, and after the Eagles’ own late finish in Tennessee. And then, quote, “just didn’t make the right decision in this particular case.”

So that’s decision number one. Now let’s talk about decision number two. Because what Sirianni did at the podium is something you rarely see in the NFL.

He didn’t blame the coverage team. He didn’t blame the kicker. He didn’t talk about injuries or personnel. He put it all on himself. Here’s what he said, quote, “You know, our guys fought their butts off today. Defense had an outstanding game keeping us in that game. Offense did enough at the end to go win us that game. Special teams did a nice job kicking that there. I didn’t make the right decision and I lost that game. And that’s what I said to our guys.”

He went on, quote, “What hurts there is that the guys fought so hard and then I don’t make the decision that I should make at the end of the game, and I cost us that game, and I put that fully on me.”

When reporters pushed him on his thought process, he refused to go into it. Quote, “At the end of the day, I didn’t make the right decision and it cost us the game. There’s no other way around it.” And later, quote, “I didn’t do my job good enough in that situation.”

He even compared it to his playing days. He said it’s like missing a free throw at the end of a game or dropping a pass, and that he’s been in both of those places. Quote, “Those ones stick with you, and that will stick with me for a long time.” And then this, quote, “I’m going to put my chest out. I’m going to stick my head up and I’m going to get better from it. I’m going to own it and move forward.”

And when he was asked if the personnel on the field was different because of injuries, he shut that down immediately. Quote, “No. Everybody that’s on that field, we have total faith in. Should have never got to that, and I take full responsibility for that.”

Eagles Nation, think about that for a second. Head coaches in this league almost never say “I lost us that game” in public. They talk about execution. They talk about looking at the tape. Sirianni didn’t hide behind any of it. He said accountability is, quote, “the key core value to our program,” and that it’s the only way you get better and build trust.

Now, I respect that. I really do. But respect doesn’t erase the result. And that’s the tension every Eagles fan is feeling today.

And here’s the thing. If you only watched the final eight seconds, you missed a game where the Eagles’ depleted roster showed real fight. Let me walk you through it, because there were a lot of positives.

The defense was outstanding for most of the day. Jalen Carter, playing through a hairline fracture in his wrist, was a monster. Two strip-sacks of Trevor Lawrence in the first half and three tackles for loss. The Eagles didn’t recover either fumble, but Carter was wrecking the game. Quinyon Mitchell, who was questionable with a knee earlier in the week, got the first regular-season interception of his career. Zack Baun, back from his concussion, had 11 tackles and three quarterback pressures.

And the goal-line stand right before halftime? That was special. First, Andrew Mukuba made a huge tackle on Brenton Strange on third-and-2, stopping him right at the 1-yard line. Then on fourth-and-goal from the 1, Zack Baun stuffed Bhayshul Tuten to keep the Eagles in front at halftime. Sirianni said there was a ton of energy on that stop. That’s the kind of fight this team showed all day.

And the offense? In the first half, it looked like it might finally be turning the corner. Will Shipley punched in a 2-yard touchdown run. And then Jalen Hurts found Darius Cooper on a 62-yard catch-and-run touchdown. Cooper keeps doing it. That’s his fourth touchdown in three games. Eagles led 14 to 7. And the young offensive line, with Willie Lampkin and Jake Majors making their NFL debuts, held up well enough to give the Eagles a lead at the half. Hurts didn’t turn the ball over all day. After three turnovers in Chicago, that’s real progress. And with a brand-new center snapping him the ball in his first NFL game, a rookie right tackle, and no Saquon behind him, Hurts kept his composure and gave his team a chance. Put Barkley, Smith and Dickerson back around him, and that same quarterback looks very different.

But then the second half happened. Again.

The Eagles gained just 59 yards in the third quarter. They failed on a fourth-and-3 at the Jaguars’ 43. Hurts missed an open Dontayvion Wicks on a play that could have changed the game. Braden Mann punted three times after the break. And Jacksonville controlled the ball for more than 35 minutes, compared to under 25 for the Eagles. Hurts finished 21 of 40 for 220 yards and a touchdown, with no interceptions. Dameon Pierce, who stepped in as the lead back, had just 14 yards on 11 carries. The run game never got going.

Sirianni addressed this too, and it might be the most important thing he said. Quote, “We got to figure out our second-half offense, because we’re putting together some nice first-half offense and have a lot of opportunities to put the game away and we haven’t been able to do that.” And he added, quote, “That’s going to bite us in the butt. Continue to bite us in the butt if we don’t.”

He’s right. Think about the last three games. Against the Bears, the offense disappeared after halftime. Against the Rams, a 20 to 10 lead turned into a 24 to 20 loss with zero fourth-quarter points. And now in London, a 14 to 7 halftime lead turned into a fight for survival. Three straight losses, and the second-half offense has been a huge part of every single one of them. The kickoff decision cost the Eagles this game. But as Sirianni himself said, it never should have come down to that.

And let’s not forget how much adversity this team was dealing with. On the very first play of the game, Jeremiah Trotter Jr. was ejected after a scrum on the opening kickoff. Jacksonville had a kickoff return touchdown called back for holding. During the game, Will Shipley went down with a foot injury, Jalyx Hunt hurt his knee and ankle, and Michael Carter II left with an abdomen injury. All three were ruled out. Tyler Steen left briefly with a shoulder injury but came back. This was a team running on fumes, and it still had the game won with eight seconds left.

And one more thing worth mentioning. Sirianni went out of his way to thank the fans. He said the atmosphere in London was great, everything about the trip was first class, and he was appreciative of all the Eagles fans who traveled. He said it was loud, loud enough that he could see the Jaguars having some issues handling the noise. Quote, “I think the crowd was definitely on our side.” Eagles Nation shows up everywhere. London, Brazil, wherever. That’s something this franchise should never take for granted.

Now let’s talk about what this loss means, because it’s big. The Eagles are 2 and 3. According to CBS Sports, it’s the first time they’ve been under .500 since 2021, and it snapped a franchise-record streak of 76 straight games at .500 or better. Three straight losses. A 20-point loss in Chicago. A blown 10-point lead against the Rams. A last-second loss in London. And CBS Sports is already asking the question out loud. Is Nick Sirianni’s seat getting warmer?

So let’s do this the Second Floor way. Both sides.

Here’s the case for being worried. Sirianni is the head coach. Situational decisions are his job. And this isn’t the first one to sting this season. Two weeks ago against the Rams, he went for it on fourth-and-goal from the 2 instead of kicking a field goal to go up by 13, and the Eagles lost. Now this. When you’re the head coach and late-game decisions keep going the wrong way, people are going to start questioning your judgment. And the second-half offense issue is on the coaching staff too. Sirianni said it himself: “We have to look at ourselves as coaches first.”

Here’s the case for patience. Sirianni took this team to two Super Bowls and won one. He’s got the best road record in the NFL since 2021. His team is missing its best running back, its top receiver, its starting center, a future Hall of Fame right tackle, and its starting left guard. And it still went into London and had the game won against a Jaguars team that’s now 4 and 1. The players clearly haven’t quit on him. In fact, they played their hearts out. And the way he took responsibility, publicly, completely, without throwing anyone under the bus? That’s the kind of thing that keeps a locker room together through tough times.

So here’s my take, and you can fight me in the comments.

The kickoff decision was wrong. Period. With eight seconds left, no timeouts for Jacksonville, and a one-point lead, you take the return game out of it. Make them go the distance with no help. I understand the thinking after the Dallas-Baltimore game, burn as much clock as possible. But the risk of a big return was way bigger than the reward. And on a day when Trotter was ejected and the coverage units were shuffled because of injuries, that risk was even bigger.

But I’ll also say this. I’d much rather have a head coach who stands up and says “I lost us that game” than one who hides behind excuses. That’s not weakness. That’s leadership. That’s what you want your players to see. And the players’ reaction tells you a lot. Arnold Ebiketie said the coverage team could have done a better job tackling the ball carrier. That’s a locker room owning it together, not pointing fingers. When things go bad, teams either split apart or pull closer together. Everything we heard after this game says this team is pulling closer.

The real question is what happens next. Accountability only matters if it leads to change. Sirianni said, “I’m going to own it and move forward.” Now we need to see it. Fix the late-game situations. Fix the second-half offense. Find a way to win a close game, because the Eagles have now lost three games that were very winnable.

And the good news? Help is coming. Landon Dickerson is eligible to come back from injured reserve next week. DeVonta Smith could return. Saquon Barkley’s hamstring isn’t a long-term injury. Dallas Goedert is back. Jalen Carter is playing like a monster even with a broken wrist. And the young guys, Majors, Lampkin, Bell, Cooper, survived their trial by fire in London.

Next up, the Carolina Panthers. And this is now a must-win game. Not because the season is over at 2 and 4. It wouldn’t be. But because this team needs to remember what winning feels like. Here’s what I’m watching. Number one, the late-game decisions. Every situation is going to be under a microscope. Number two, the second-half offense. Can Sean Mannion find answers after halftime? Number three, who’s back? Dickerson, Smith and Barkley would change everything. Number four, the running game. Fourteen yards on eleven carries from Pierce isn’t going to cut it. And number five, the defense. If Carter, Baun and Mitchell keep playing like this, the Eagles are going to win a lot of games.

Let’s zoom out one last time. The Eagles lost a game they should have won. Their head coach made a decision that cost them, and then made a decision to own it completely in front of the whole world. Both of those things happened. And how this team responds over the next few weeks will define its season.
