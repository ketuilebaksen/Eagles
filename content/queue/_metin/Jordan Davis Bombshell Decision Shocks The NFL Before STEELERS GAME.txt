Eagles Nation, Jordan Davis has finally spoken about the trade that sent him from Philadelphia to Jacksonville, and what he said is going to hit a lot of Eagles fans right in the heart. Let me be clear about one thing first. Jordan Davis did not make a bombshell decision. He did not ask to be traded, he did not hold out, and he did not choose to leave. The decision was made by the Eagles. But since the trade, Davis has opened up about how it felt, about what Philadelphia means to him, and about his new team. And at the same time, the trade has sparked a much bigger conversation about the future of the Eagles’ defense, and even about who will be running it next season.

This is James with Second Floor, and here is what we are covering today. Everything Jordan Davis said after the trade, what Nick Sirianni said about losing him, why some people think this trade could be connected to Vic Fangio’s future and a familiar name in Jim Schwartz, what the numbers say about the run defense without Davis, the experts’ grades, and what all of it means heading into the Steelers game. Before we start, if you are still riding with this team, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor.

Let me start with Jordan Davis himself.

According to Johnny McGonigal of PennLive, Davis found out about the trade on a phone call with Howie Roseman. He described that call as, quote, emotional, end quote, with, quote, a lot of feelings involved. End quote. And then he said the words that every Eagles fan has been waiting to hear. Quote, I’m disappointed. But I’m optimistic about the future. End quote.

He did not hide how much this city means to him. Quote, obviously, Philly’s going to have a big piece of my heart. End quote. And about his teammates, he said, quote, I love those guys. End quote.

But Davis also showed that he understands the business. He said, quote, it’s a business. It’s the NFL. It’s the league. End quote. And he said, quote, I have a new opportunity to make an impact in Jacksonville. End quote. According to the Inquirer, he even said the return the Eagles got, quote, obviously helps the team, end quote, during a rebuilding process, while admitting he was sad to leave.

And then he spoke directly to you, the fans. Quote, thank you for everything from the bottom of my heart. End quote. And then, with a little smile you could feel through the words, he said, quote, I appreciate the cheers and the boos and everything in between. End quote.

Eagles Nation, that is class. That is a man who was traded in the middle of a season, just months after signing a three-year, seventy-eight million dollar extension in March, and the first thing he did was thank the people who cheered him, and even the people who booed him. If you ever needed a reminder of why Jordan Davis was a captain, there it is.

Now, Davis is already settling in with the Jaguars. According to ClutchPoints, he gave his first impression of Jaguars head coach Liam Coen. He said, quote, I can tell he really cares about this program. End quote. He said, quote, he cares about the people that’s in this room. End quote. And he said, quote, I’m a culture guy. End quote. He talked about how much it means to him that Coen has an open-door policy, saying it, quote, speaks a lot to a level of coaching. End quote. And he finished with, quote, I’m excited for that. End quote.

According to ClutchPoints, his first game with Jacksonville will be against the Tennessee Titans. And there is a little twist. Because the Eagles are on their bye this week, the trade meant Davis lost a week off. He goes straight from one locker room to another, without a break.

And the Eagles’ head coach made it clear how much Davis will be missed. According to the Inquirer, Nick Sirianni said he will miss Davis, quote, greatly and dearly. End quote. That is a coach who knows what he just lost. Davis was a first-round pick in 2022, a Super Bowl champion, and a team captain.

And to understand why this trade happened, you have to remember where this team is. The Eagles are two and seven. They have lost seven straight games after opening the season with two wins. According to the Inquirer, it is their first two and seven start since 1999. And on Sunday, the Giants beat them nineteen to eleven at Lincoln Financial Field, New York’s first win in Philadelphia since 2013. When a season falls apart like that, a front office has to ask hard questions about every contract on the roster, and that includes the ones belonging to players everybody loves.

Now, let me remind you of the trade itself. According to the team’s official website, the Eagles sent Davis to the Jaguars for a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick. And according to NFL insider Jordan Schultz, as reported by Heavy, the Jaguars first asked about Moro Ojomo and were told the Eagles were not trading him. So they pivoted to Davis.

And the grades are in. According to the Inquirer, ESPN’s Seth Walder gave the Eagles an A and the Jaguars an F. So whatever you think about losing Davis emotionally, the grades say that Howie got a great return.

And here is something else to remember. Davis’s numbers had dropped this season, and the Eagles were paying him like a cornerstone. When a team is two and seven, it has to be honest about which big contracts fit the next version of the roster. That is not a knock on Davis as a person. It is the cold side of the business he talked about himself.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you agree with the A grade for the Eagles. Comment number two if you think the Eagles gave up too much.

And think about what that Jacksonville pick could become. Nobody knows yet where it will land. That depends on how the Jaguars finish this season and next season. But the Eagles also own their own 2027 first-round pick, and with a two and seven record, that pick could end up very high. Two first-round picks in the same draft give Howie Roseman options he did not have a week ago. He can stay put and take the best players available, he can trade up for a star, or he can move around the board to fill several holes. Howie deserves criticism for how this roster looks right now. But he has also put the team in position to recover quickly. Both things can be true.

But draft capital does not tell the whole story. Losing Jordan Davis also means losing someone Jalen Carter trusted. They were teammates at Georgia before they were teammates in Philadelphia, they won a Super Bowl together with the Eagles, and they anchored the middle of that defense together. This is just my opinion, not a report, but I think the leadership part of this trade is underrated. A draft pick cannot replace a captain’s voice in the locker room during a seven-game losing streak. Carter still has enormous talent. But now somebody else has to help keep that defensive line connected and focused when games get rough, and I think that person has to be Moro Ojomo.

Now, here is the conversation that is starting to heat up in Philadelphia. What if the Jordan Davis trade is also a sign of what is coming on the coaching staff?

Here is why people are asking. According to Larry Rupp of Eagles On SI, defensive coordinator Vic Fangio reportedly considered retiring last offseason. He denied it at the time, and he came back. But according to Jeff Neiburg of the Inquirer, Fangio may retire after the 2026 season. And according to Rupp, the Eagles reportedly checked in with former defensive coordinator Jim Schwartz.

Now, let me be very careful here. Nobody has reported that Fangio is leaving. Nobody has reported that the Eagles have offered Schwartz anything. This is a conversation about possibilities, not a report about a decision.

But here is why the Davis trade fits into that conversation. According to Neiburg, Davis was a perfect fit as a run-stopping nose tackle in Fangio’s three-four defense. But if the Eagles were ever to shift to a four-three, he could be a poorer fit. And according to Anthony Miller of Inside The Iggles, Davis does not fit Schwartz’s four-three scheme as well as players like Jalen Carter and Moro Ojomo. That is Miller’s opinion. But it explains why some people think the Eagles chose to keep Ojomo and move Davis.

And remember who Jim Schwartz is. He was the Eagles’ defensive coordinator when they won Super Bowl fifty-two after the 2017 season. According to Inside The Iggles, he went on to coach the Cleveland Browns’ defense starting in 2023, and in two of the last three years, the Browns ranked in the top four in total defense. And according to Miller, Schwartz is currently a free agent, so the Eagles could hire him if Fangio leaves.

And the difference in style matters. Fangio’s defense is built on control up front, with linemen holding their gaps and making the run hard. Schwartz has always wanted his front to attack, to fire off the ball and get after the quarterback. Imagine Jalen Carter and Moro Ojomo with that kind of freedom. That is why some fans are excited about this idea.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I do not think the Eagles traded Jordan Davis because of Jim Schwartz. I think they traded him because Jacksonville offered a first-round pick, his numbers had dropped and his contract was big. But I do think the trade gives the Eagles flexibility for whatever comes next, whether Fangio stays or not. And if Fangio does retire, Schwartz would make a lot of sense.

Now, let us talk about what the Eagles are losing on the field, because the numbers are real.

According to Rupp, the Eagles allowed at least one hundred rushing yards in each of their first eight games this season. That had not happened since 1968. Before the Giants game, they ranked twenty-fourth in rushing yards allowed, at one hundred twenty-six point one yards per game. And then against the Giants, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, they held New York to just eighty rushing yards, the first time all season an opponent was held under one hundred. Davis was part of that. Now the Eagles have to keep it going without him.

So who replaces him? According to the Inquirer, Jalen Carter has played eighty-four point five percent of the defensive snaps, and Moro Ojomo sixty percent. They are now the main interior options. Byron Young and Ty Robinson are behind them, and Gabe Hall and Zion Wilson are on the practice squad.

And this is a big opportunity for Ojomo. According to Rupp, since the start of 2025, Ojomo has sixty-two tackles, six sacks and ten tackles for loss in twenty-six games. And Ty Robinson, according to Rupp, has played about thirteen percent of the defensive snaps over two seasons, with ten tackles. Now he should get more snaps and a real chance to show what he can do.

And then there is the money. The Davis trade leaves dead cap charges on the Eagles’ books. I am not going to give you exact numbers I cannot confirm. But the important point is this. A trade does not make a contract disappear completely. The Eagles will still carry some of Davis’s guaranteed money. What the trade does is give them draft picks and relief from future salary.

And it also explains why one player was not traded. According to the Inquirer, Dallas Goedert stayed because trading him before the deadline would have roughly doubled his dead cap to eighteen point two million dollars. So the Eagles kept a veteran who took a pay cut to stay in 2025 and came back on a one-year deal this year. In a season like this, the locker room needs veterans who keep showing up.

Now, let us look at the draft picks again, because they are the real prize. According to Rupp, the Eagles now hold ten picks in the first two rounds of the next three drafts. According to the team’s official website, that includes two first-round picks in 2027, two first-round picks and two second-round picks in 2028, and multiple second-round picks in 2029. That gives Howie Roseman the flexibility to trade up, acquire a star, or rebuild the aging core.

And think about what those picks could mean for the offense, too. Rookie receiver Makai Lemon has been one of the bright spots this season. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he caught six passes for sixty-one yards against the Giants. Yes, there were drops, and those matter. But he keeps giving the quarterback somewhere to go on a team that is struggling to move the ball. Imagine what this offense could look like with another first-round weapon, or a rebuilt offensive line, next to Lemon and DeVonta Smith.

Now, before we get to the coaches, let me cover the deal that did not happen, because it is connected to all of this. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the Eagles agreed to send edge rusher Arnold Ebiketie to the Cincinnati Bengals for a 2027 sixth-round pick. But the Bengals backed out before it became official. According to the Inquirer, Nick Korte of Over The Cap explained why. Adding Ebiketie could have cost Cincinnati a projected compensatory pick that they are expecting from a player they lost in free agency. In other words, a backup pass rusher was not worth risking a more valuable pick. So Ebiketie stays in Philadelphia. And honestly, with the defensive line getting thinner after the Davis trade, that might not be the worst thing.

And the Eagles did not sell everything at the deadline. Riq Woolen stayed. Dallas Goedert stayed. Landon Dickerson stayed. Tanner McKee stayed. So this was not a full teardown. It was a targeted move, built around one big offer for one big contract.

Now, the trades cannot hide the biggest problem, and that is the offense. Against the Giants, Joe Milton made his first start. According to the box score, he completed eighteen of thirty-four passes for one hundred sixty-nine yards, and he ran for sixty-four more. But he was sacked seven times, and the Eagles scored just eleven points. Milton can move, and he made a few throws that gave fans some hope. But he spent too much of the day running from pressure. You can change the quarterback, but if the protection keeps breaking down, the same problems will show up no matter who is taking the snaps.

And the backfield is thin, too. According to the team, the Eagles waived running back Jaydon Blue on Tuesday. That leaves Saquon Barkley as the only running back on the fifty-three-man roster, with Tank Bigsby and Will Shipley on injured reserve. So expect the Eagles to add depth at that position during the bye week.

This is just my opinion, not a report. If Nick Sirianni makes one serious change during this bye, the offensive line room and the protection plan are the first places I would look. Not every missed block is a coach’s fault. But when the whole line looks confused week after week, the coaching has to be part of the conversation.

Now, let me talk about Nick Sirianni, because he was asked about all the noise this week. According to the Inquirer, Sirianni went on 94 WIP and was asked whether he fears being fired. He said, quote, anything that distracts you from getting the job done is just noise. End quote. And on Monday, according to the Inquirer, he said no staff changes had been made, and the team was going through a self-scout process. He was also asked about a gesture some fans believed they saw him make toward the crowd during the Giants game. His answer was short. Quote, absolutely not. End quote. I understand why he does not want to change people just because the record looks awful. But standing still is also a decision, and the bye week is the last real chance to make an adjustment before the final stretch.

So here is where things stand. The head coach is still in place. The defensive coordinator is still in place. But the defensive line just lost one of its leaders, and the questions about next season are already starting.

Here is what I will be watching before the Steelers game. First, whether the Eagles start extension talks with Moro Ojomo. Second, how Vic Fangio adjusts his defensive front without Davis. Third, whether Ty Robinson and Byron Young get a bigger role. Fourth, any coaching changes that come out of the self-scout. And fifth, Jordan Davis’s first game in Jacksonville against the Titans. I know a lot of Eagles fans will be rooting for him.

And remember, the Steelers game on Sunday, November twenty-second, has been moved to one in the afternoon Eastern on CBS, according to the Inquirer. Then the Eagles go to Dallas on Thanksgiving.

And that Steelers game is going to be a real test. The schedule does not care how many draft picks the Eagles collected. They still have to protect the quarterback, stop the run without Jordan Davis, and play four quarters of disciplined football. If the bye week helped, we will see it right away. If it did not, the questions about the coaching staff will only get louder before Thanksgiving in Dallas.

On the other side, the people who think this was the right move. They will say ESPN gave the Eagles an A. And they will say that if Fangio does retire, the Eagles already have a defensive line that fits a more aggressive style.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I am going to miss Jordan Davis. The way he talked about this city after being traded tells you everything about the person he is. But I still think this was the right trade. The Eagles got real value, they kept the younger and cheaper defensive tackle, and they set themselves up for a big offseason. Now they need to finish the plan by extending Moro Ojomo. And on the coordinator question, I would not rush anything. Let Fangio finish the season, see what he wants, and then make a decision. If he retires, Jim Schwartz is a name worth calling.
