Eagles Nation, the Eagles just made a move that nobody in Philadelphia saw coming, and it is a big blow to this locker room. Let me be clear about what happened, because the headlines are getting it wrong. Nobody was fired. Nick Sirianni is still the head coach, and as of Monday afternoon, no coaches had been let go. But on Monday evening, the Eagles traded one of their captains. Jordan Davis, the big man in the middle of the defense, the former first-round pick, the player who signed a seventy-eight million dollar extension just eight months ago, is now a Jacksonville Jaguar. And the trade deadline is not even over yet. It closes today at four in the afternoon Eastern.

This is James with Second Floor, and today we are breaking it all down. Exactly what the Eagles got for Jordan Davis, why Howie Roseman made this move, the surprising detail about the Jaguars and Moro Ojomo, what Sirianni said on Monday about his coaching staff, the controversy over his walk off the field, how the Giants game slipped away, and what could still happen before the deadline closes today. Before we start, if you are still riding with this team, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor.

Let me start with the trade.

According to Jeff Neiburg of the Inquirer, the Eagles announced on Monday evening that they traded defensive tackle Jordan Davis to the Jacksonville Jaguars for a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick. And according to Reuben Frank of NBC Sports Philadelphia, it is the first time Howie Roseman has traded an Eagles first-round pick since Carson Wentz in 2020. Frank called it a blockbuster. And it is.

Think about what Jordan Davis has meant to this team. According to the Inquirer, the Eagles drafted him thirteenth overall in 2022. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he is twenty-six years old, one of the team’s captains, and he was a key part of the defense that won the Super Bowl. He is the only Eagle to start all sixty regular-season games since 2022. Over seventy-three games with the Eagles, he had nine and a half sacks, seventeen tackles for loss and one hundred ninety-three tackles. And in 2025, he had what Frank called a Pro Bowl-caliber season, with four and a half sacks and nine tackles for loss.

That is not a role player. That is a cornerstone of the defense. And now he is gone.

And remember how he got here. In the 2022 draft, the Eagles traded up with the Houston Texans to take Davis at number thirteen. He came from Georgia, where he won a national championship. Then the Eagles went back to Georgia again and again. Jalen Carter. Nolan Smith. Players who had already won together in college came to Philadelphia to try to win together again. And in February of 2025, they did. Davis was a big part of that. Seeing him leave, while the season is still going, is a strange feeling for a lot of fans.

So why would the Eagles do this? Let me walk you through it.

First, the contract. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, about eight months ago, the Eagles gave Davis a three-year, seventy-eight million dollar extension, making him the tenth-highest-paid interior defensive lineman in the league. He was signed through 2029. And remember, according to Frank, Jalen Carter, Davis’s college teammate and close friend, signed a four-year, one hundred fifty-two million dollar deal in July. That is a lot of money tied up in two defensive tackles.

Second, the production this season. According to Neiburg, citing NFL Next Gen Stats, Davis had fifty-eight run stops and seventy-two total tackles in 2025. Through nine games this season, he has twenty-five run stops and thirty-one total tackles. According to Frank, through eight games he had half a sack and no tackles for loss, before he had a strong game against the Giants on Sunday, including a sack. And according to Neiburg, his snap share this season is about fifty-two percent. So the numbers had dropped.

And third, the price. According to Neiburg, the Eagles will likely end up with a late first-round pick, because Jacksonville is an AFC contender. But a first-round pick is still a first-round pick. And look at what the Eagles’ draft cupboard looks like now. According to Neiburg, the Eagles hold two first-round picks in 2027, their own and Jacksonville’s. They hold two first-round picks in 2028, their own and New England’s from the A.J. Brown trade. And they have extra second-round picks too, including the Ravens’ 2028 second-rounder from the Cam Jurgens deal. According to Frank, the Eagles now hold seven first or second-round picks over the next two drafts.

That is a war chest, Eagles Nation. Very few teams in the league have that kind of draft ammunition right now. Howie Roseman is clearly planning for the future.

And look at the pattern this year. On June first, according to FOX 29, the Eagles traded A.J. Brown to the Patriots for a 2028 first-round pick and a 2027 fifth-round pick. On October seventh, according to NFL dot com, they traded Cam Jurgens to the Ravens for a 2027 fifth-round pick and a 2028 second-round pick. And now Jordan Davis to the Jaguars for a first and a second. Three big names, three trades, and a lot of draft capital. Howie is not tearing everything down, but he is clearly reshaping this roster.

And those picks will feed every conversation this offseason. Inquirer columnist David Murphy has already written that the Eagles are a very strong bet to be drafting in the top five, and he asked whether the Eagles are still committed to Jalen Hurts. Remember, Jalen is out for the season after doctors found blood clots in his lungs, and the team expects a full recovery. With two first-round picks in 2027, the Eagles will have options, whether that means adding protection for Jalen, rebuilding the defensive line, or something bigger. That is a debate for another day. But this trade makes that debate a lot more interesting.

Now, here is the detail that surprised a lot of people. According to Heavy, as reported on Yahoo Sports, NFL insider Jordan Schultz reported that the Jaguars, quote, initially checked on DT Moro Ojomo, but were told Philadelphia was not trading him. End quote. So Jacksonville pivoted to Davis, a player they had assumed was not available.

Think about that. The Eagles had a choice between their two young defensive tackles, and they chose to keep Ojomo. According to Neiburg, NFL on Prime’s James Palmer had also reported that the Eagles told teams they would not trade Ojomo. And Albert Breer of Sports Illustrated had reported the same thing. Ojomo is a former seventh-round pick in the final year of his rookie contract. According to Frank, this trade clears the room to keep him, something that likely would not have been affordable if Davis had stayed.

So the new interior of the defensive line is Jalen Carter, Moro Ojomo, Byron Young and Ty Robinson, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia. Young and Robinson should get more snaps the rest of the way, and that is a chance for the Eagles to see what they have in two young players.

And Davis himself seemed to understand the business before the trade happened. On Sunday, according to the Inquirer, he said, quote, obviously, when somebody does come or leave, it does catch a lot of people by surprise. End quote. He said the players were focused on playing with integrity. He did not know it then, but he was describing his own future.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. This one hurts. Jordan Davis was a captain, a Super Bowl champion and a fan favorite. But I understand the move. When you are two and seven, with a seventy-eight million dollar contract on a player whose numbers have dropped, and a contender offers you a first-round pick, you have to take it seriously. And keeping Ojomo tells you the Eagles believe in his future. If Ojomo keeps developing, and the Eagles use those picks well, this could look like a smart trade in two years.

Now, let us talk about Nick Sirianni, because the trade came less than two hours after he spoke on Monday.

According to Ryan Novozinsky of the Inquirer, when Sirianni was asked if he had made any staff changes after the Giants loss, his answer was simple. Quote, no. End quote. According to ESPN’s Tim McManus, he did not rule out changes, and as of Monday afternoon, none had been made. Sirianni said, quote, right now we’re going through the self-scout process. End quote. And he added, quote, guys are working on studies right now, and we’re determined to figure things out and get on the right track. End quote.

And when he was asked whether the Eagles would sell at the deadline, he did not give a direct answer. According to the Inquirer, he said, quote, what I’m considering is how we get better and how we get out of this funk. End quote.

And after the game on Sunday, Sirianni had already made it clear that the blame starts with him. According to NFL dot com, he said, quote, obviously, none of us offensively have done a good enough job. End quote. He said, quote, it starts with me. End quote. And he said, quote, obviously, I haven’t done a good enough job. End quote.

So there it is. No firing. No staff changes yet. But a self-scout process during the bye, and a door that is still open.

Now, the controversy. After the nineteen to eleven loss to the Giants, a video went viral on social media. According to CBS Sports, the video showed Sirianni walking toward the tunnel and looking at his hand, with one finger raised higher than the others, as fans booed. A lot of people thought he was giving the fans the middle finger.

On Monday, Sirianni shut that down. According to CBS Sports, he said, quote, absolutely not. That was a surprise to me. Absolutely not. End quote. According to the Inquirer, he said Bob Lange, the team’s senior vice president of communications, told him about the video. And according to ESPN, he said, quote, we hear them when they boo. We don’t necessarily like it. I don’t think that’s productive for anybody. End quote. And then, quote, we thrive off the crowd when they cheer for us. That’s all I’ll say. End quote.

Now, Sirianni has had moments with the crowd before. According to CBS Sports, back in 2024, after a win over the Browns, he was booed and cupped his ears at fans, and later apologized, saying, quote, I’m sorry and disappointed on how my energy was directed at the end of the game. End quote. So this is a sensitive topic in Philadelphia. But this time, he says it did not happen, and I am going to take him at his word.

According to CBS Sports, Sirianni also began his postgame press conference more than an hour after the game ended. He said he was talking with players. According to the Inquirer, he said, quote, I want to make sure I talk to every guy, first of all, after the game. End quote. He said a few players stopped by his office to talk it through, and he said, quote, that’s not unusual. End quote.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you believe Sirianni about the video. Comment number two if you think he meant it.

Now, let me take you back to the game, because one drive showed what this offense could be, and the rest of the game showed what it is.

The Eagles’ only touchdown drive came in the first half, and it was the best stretch of Joe Milton’s first start. He hit Dallas Goedert on a crossing route for twelve yards while under pressure. On the next play, he tucked the ball and ran for nineteen yards. A couple of plays later, he went deep for Darius Cooper, and Giants cornerback Deonte Banks never turned around to find the ball. That was a thirty-yard gain into goal-to-go territory. Saquon Barkley finished it with a three-yard touchdown run on third and goal. And according to the Giants’ official website, after a Giants penalty on the extra point, the Eagles moved the ball to the one-yard line and went for two. Milton ran it in. Eight to six Eagles.

That drive showed you Milton’s legs, his arm and his willingness to take a shot downfield. And it gave the home crowd a reason to believe. For a few minutes, it looked like the quarterback change might actually work, and that the Eagles had found a way to steal one against the first-place Giants.

But the offense never built on it. According to the Inquirer, at halftime the Eagles led eight to six, and Braden Mann had just blasted an eighty-six-yard punt, the longest in the NFL since 2001. Then the third quarter came. The Eagles went three and out on all three of their possessions in that quarter. Jameis Winston came in for an injured J.J. McCarthy and led a fourteen-play, seventy-six-yard touchdown drive, finishing with a five-yard pass to Isaiah Likely. The Giants added field goals, and the Eagles’ late push ended with a seventh sack on fourth down.

The final numbers are ugly. According to the Inquirer, Milton went eighteen of thirty-four for one hundred sixty-nine yards with no touchdowns and no interceptions. He led the team with sixty-four rushing yards. But he was sacked seven times. Giants rookie Arvell Reese had three of those sacks, a forced fumble and twelve tackles, according to the Giants’ website. And according to the Inquirer, the Eagles are averaging about two hundred sixty yards and three and a half sacks allowed per game, and the offense Sean Mannion was hired to run is scoring sixteen point four points per game.

And think about the defense now. On Sunday, according to PHLY Sports, the Eagles had six sacks, their most in a game this season, and Davis had one of them. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the defense held the Giants to eighty rushing yards, the first time all season an opponent was held under one hundred. Vic Fangio’s group was the best part of this team on Sunday. Now it has to keep that going without one of its leaders in the middle.

And Zack Baun, who had two sacks before leaving with a back injury, still has no update. No diagnosis has been released, and I am not going to guess. The bye week gives him time.

Now, let us talk about what is next.

The trade deadline closes today, Tuesday, November tenth, at four in the afternoon Eastern. And the Davis trade may not be the last move. According to the Inquirer, Dallas Goedert is among several players on expiring contracts who could be traded, though his salary cap situation makes him less likely to move than others. Landon Dickerson has drawn interest, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia’s Ryan Fowler and ESPN’s Adam Schefter. Riq Woolen, Hollywood Brown and Tanner McKee have all been mentioned. And according to the Inquirer, columnist Marcus Hayes even argued the Eagles should also trade Zack Baun. That is his opinion, not a report. But multiple reports from ESPN and NFL Network said Saquon Barkley will not be traded, according to PhillyVoice.

And look at what Jacksonville is doing. According to the Inquirer, citing ESPN’s Adam Schefter, the Falcons are sending safety Jessie Bates to the Jaguars for a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick. So the Jaguars are going all in. That is good news for them, but it also means the first-round pick they sent to Philadelphia will likely be late in the round.

And there is a schedule change. According to the Inquirer, the Eagles’ Week 11 game against the Pittsburgh Steelers on November twenty-second has been moved to one in the afternoon Eastern on CBS. The NFL flexed it out of its national window. That tells you how the league sees this Eagles team right now. According to the Inquirer, The Athletic puts the Eagles’ playoff odds below one percent. And according to CBS Sports, this is the Eagles’ first two and seven start since 1999.

And let us be honest about what this means for the rest of the season. Trading Davis is a sign that the front office sees 2026 for what it is. You do not trade a twenty-six-year-old captain in the middle of a playoff push. You trade him when you are thinking about the future. That does not mean the Eagles are giving up on the remaining eight games. The players will still fight, and the coaches will still try to win. But the message from the front office is clear. The priority is building the next great Eagles team.

And for the players still in that locker room, this is a reminder that nobody is safe. Davis was a captain. He was beloved. He signed a big extension this year. And he was still traded. That can motivate some players to play harder, and it can shake others. How this locker room responds coming out of the bye will tell you a lot about the leadership that is left.

Here is what I will be watching today and during the bye. First, does Howie Roseman make another trade before four o’clock? Second, does Sirianni make a change on the coaching staff after the self-scout? Third, any update on Zack Baun. And fourth, who starts at quarterback against Pittsburgh?

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the people who hate this trade. They will say Jordan Davis was a captain and a Super Bowl champion, and that trading him sends a terrible message to the locker room. They will say the Eagles just gave him a seventy-eight million dollar extension eight months ago, so this shows the front office does not know what it is doing. They will say a late first-round pick from a contender is not enough for a player like Davis. And they will say that trading captains while keeping the same coaching staff is backwards.

On the other side, the people who love this trade. They will say Davis’s numbers dropped this season, and that his contract was a big commitment for a player playing about half of the snaps. They will say getting a first and a second-round pick for him is a strong return. They will say keeping Moro Ojomo, who is younger and cheaper, is smart planning. They will say the Eagles now have two first-round picks in each of the next two drafts, which gives Howie Roseman enormous flexibility. And they will say this is exactly what a smart team does when the season is slipping away.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I think this is a good trade, even though it hurts. The Eagles sold a big contract at the right time, they kept the younger and cheaper defensive tackle, and they added a first-round pick to an already loaded draft cupboard. That is the kind of move that sets a team up for 2027 and beyond. But here is my concern. Trading players will not fix the offense. The Eagles are scoring sixteen points a game and giving up three and a half sacks a game. If the coaching staff does not find answers during this bye, no amount of draft picks will fix what we are seeing on Sundays.

So let me wrap it up. Nobody was fired, but the Eagles made a huge move. They traded captain Jordan Davis to the Jaguars for a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick. It is the first time Howie Roseman has traded an Eagles first-round pick since Carson Wentz. The Jaguars first asked about Moro Ojomo, and the Eagles said no. The Eagles now have two first-round picks in both 2027 and 2028. Sirianni said he has not made any staff changes yet, and that the team is going through a self-scout process.
