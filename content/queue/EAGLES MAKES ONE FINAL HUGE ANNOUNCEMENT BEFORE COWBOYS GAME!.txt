Eagles Nation, it is official. The Eagles released their final injury report before Monday Night Football against the Dallas Cowboys, and it is a mix of real heartbreak and real hope. Two of the Eagles’ top receivers have been ruled out. A key pass rusher is out. But Saquon Barkley went from limited to a full participant in practice on Saturday, and Dallas Goedert has no injury designation at all. So the picture for Monday night has finally come into focus.

This is James with Second Floor, and today we are going through the final announcement line by line. Who is out, who is questionable, who is good to go, what it means for Tanner McKee in only his second start, and why one name could save Monday night for this team. Before we start, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor, because we are covering this Cowboys game from every angle.

Let me set the stage first. The Eagles are two and four, and they have lost four straight games. Jalen Hurts is out for the rest of the season after being diagnosed with blood clots, and the team says his prognosis is very good and a full recovery is expected. In his first start, Tanner McKee lost to the Carolina Panthers twenty-five to fourteen. He went twenty-six of forty-five for two hundred fifteen yards and a touchdown, and he did not turn the ball over. Now think about this. If it was that hard with most of his weapons available, imagine what it looks like without some of them.

And of course, none of this happens in a vacuum. The Jalen Hurts situation is still hanging over this building. This week, the NFL Players Association called for a full investigation into the medical care Jalen received, while the league has backed the way the Eagles handled it and said there will be no discipline. When Sirianni was asked about the union’s request, he said he was not going to go into any of that, but he also said, quote, I care deeply about Jalen, end quote. Offensive coordinator Sean Mannion said Jalen was around the team last week, and that they want him to keep being a positive presence while he gets healthy. Whatever happens on Monday night, the most important thing is that Jalen makes a full recovery.

So here is the final injury report, according to the Philadelphia Inquirer.

Ruled out for Monday night. Wide receiver DeVonta Smith, with a hamstring injury. This will be the third game he has missed this season. Smith came back against Carolina, caught seven passes for seventy-five yards and a touchdown, and then aggravated the hamstring. Wide receiver Dontayvion Wicks, with an ankle injury, his first missed game of the year. Edge rusher Nolan Smith, who is still in the concussion protocol after the Carolina game and did not practice all week. And defensive back Michael Carter the second, with an abdomen injury, who will miss his second straight game.

Listed as questionable. Saquon Barkley, with the hamstring. Jonathan Greenard, with an ankle injury. Jalyx Hunt, with knee and ankle injuries. And four players who are working their way back from injured reserve. Guard Landon Dickerson, tight ends Eli Stowers and Grant Calcaterra, and cornerback Jakorian Bennett.

And no injury designation at all. Tight end Dallas Goedert, who was a full participant on Saturday after missing practice on Thursday and Friday. And center Drew Kendall, who played against Carolina but did not start.

Let us break all of that down, starting with the hardest part.

Reason number one this could be a long night. The two receivers who have been most productive in this offense are both out. DeVonta Smith is McKee’s most trusted target, and Wicks has been the guy who steps up when Smith is down. Losing both of them at the same time, with a backup quarterback in primetime, against a division rival, is about as tough a combination as it gets. Ask yourself this. On third and long, who is Tanner McKee looking for now?

Reason number two. Who steps in? According to the Philadelphia Inquirer, with Smith and Wicks out, the receiver room is Makai Lemon, Darius Cooper, Hollywood Brown, Elijah Moore and Britain Covey. That is a very young, very unproven group, with a couple of veterans who have barely been involved this season. Hollywood Brown was the only healthy inactive receiver against Carolina. Now, he could be one of McKee’s top options. Brown has speed, and he was a full participant in practice all week. If the Eagles want to take a shot down the field, he might be the guy.

And here is a fun detail about Makai Lemon. Back in April, in the first round of the 2026 draft, the Eagles traded up to the twentieth overall pick to take Lemon out of USC. And the team they made that trade with was the Dallas Cowboys. So on Monday night, Dallas could get an up-close look at the rookie they helped Philadelphia go get. A rookie is a rookie, and primetime against a division rival is a whole different animal. But if Lemon has a big night, Cowboys fans are going to hear about that trade for a long time.

Reason number three. The injury list hits both sides of the ball. On defense, Nolan Smith is out, and Greenard and Hunt are both questionable. Greenard did not practice on Friday or Saturday, and he said himself that he is a game-time decision. Hunt was limited all week. That means the Eagles could be very thin on the edge against Dak Prescott. Vic Fangio is going to have to get creative, with blitzes, disguises and maybe more snaps for guys like Arnold Ebiketie and A.J. Epenesa.

If you are new to Second Floor, welcome. Comment one if you think this season can still be saved. Comment two if you think it is time to admit this year is over. No middle ground. I read the comments, and I want to know where Eagles Nation really stands right now.

Now, the good news. And there is more of it than you might think.

Let us start with Dallas Goedert. Earlier this week, Jeff McLane of the Philadelphia Inquirer said the sense was that Smith and Wicks were going to be down, but that there was optimism Goedert could go. And that optimism turned out to be right. Goedert missed practice on Thursday and Friday, then was a full participant on Saturday, and he has no injury designation. He said the knee issue is the same one that cost him two games earlier this season. But he will be out there on Monday night. For a young quarterback with two top receivers out, having a veteran tight end who knows how to find soft spots in a zone is a huge deal.

Then there is the offensive line. Landon Dickerson was a full participant on Saturday. He is still listed as questionable, and he is still on injured reserve, so the Eagles would need to activate him to the fifty-three-man roster before he can play. But a full practice is a great sign. Dickerson has missed the last five games after playing in the season opener. And Drew Kendall has no designation. If Dickerson and Kendall are both in the lineup, this offensive line is much stronger than the one McKee played behind against Carolina, when he was sacked twice by blitzers who came through unblocked.

And here is something important about those four injured reserve players. According to the Inquirer, Dickerson, Stowers, Calcaterra and Bennett have one more week of practice if they are not activated this week. After that, the Eagles must either activate them to the active roster or place them on season-ending injured reserve. So expect some roster moves before kickoff on Monday.

Now let me take a step back and talk about the pressure on this whole organization. A lot of people are asking quietly whether Nick Sirianni is coaching for his job. Let me be very clear. That is a question, not a report. No credible source has said his job is in danger. Sirianni has a Super Bowl title, and this team won the division in each of the past two seasons. But when a contender drops to two and four, the pressure hits everybody, from the head coach to the front office. And losing to the Cowboys at home in primetime does nobody any favors.

There is also the question of Zach Ertz. When the Eagles waived tight end E.J. Jenkins this week, a spot opened on the active roster. According to Yahoo Sports, Ertz is no longer eligible for practice squad elevations, so the Eagles would have to sign him to the active roster for him to play. But with Goedert now available, and Stowers and Calcaterra working their way back, that move looks less urgent than it did a few days ago. As of now, it has not been announced, so I am not going to sell it to you as anything more than a possibility.

And do not forget the other sideline. The Cowboys are two and four as well, and they have dropped two straight. Dak Prescott has thrown four interceptions in his last two games. Dallas has ruled out linebacker Dee Winters and returner KaVontae Turpin, who might be the best return man in football. But the Cowboys also got good news. According to Yahoo Sports, linebacker DeMarvion Overshown, who has been out since Week 1 with a hamstring, and cornerback Cobie Durant, out since Week 2 with a hamstring, are both set to return on Monday night. Both are defensive starters. Linebacker Marist Liufau and safety P.J. Locke are questionable.

So this game is not decided before kickoff. Not even close. Two banged-up, desperate rivals meeting under the lights at Lincoln Financial Field. Whoever loses sinks even deeper. Whoever wins gets to breathe again. Be honest with me. Would you take a sloppy, ugly win right now? I know I would.

And now, the reason for hope. The name that could save Monday night. Saquon Barkley.

Saquon has missed two straight games, and most of a third, because of that hamstring. The last time he was on the field was early in the Week 4 loss to the Rams. Earlier this week, Mike Garafolo of NFL Network reported that Barkley was expected back at practice, and that barring any setbacks, he was in line to play against the Cowboys. And that is exactly what happened. Saquon was limited on Thursday and Friday, and then, on Saturday, he was a full participant. He is still listed as questionable, but going from limited to full on the final practice day is about as good a sign as you can get.

Think about what that means. Without his top two receivers, McKee needs someone who can take over a game by himself. Someone who takes the pressure off the quarterback. Someone who makes the Dallas defense worry about the run before it ever thinks about the pass. That someone is Saquon Barkley. He is a captain, his teammates voted him one, and Sirianni called him a phenomenal football player and a phenomenal leader this week. Getting a player like that back is not just about yards. It is about energy, belief and attitude in that huddle.

This offense needs him badly. Against Carolina, the Eagles ran for just thirty-five yards as a team. Dameon Pierce has been carrying the load, and the Eagles have been stacking depth behind him, with Elijah Mitchell and Jaydon Blue on the practice squad. Getting Saquon back changes the picture completely. When defenses have to put an extra man in the box to stop Saquon, that is when play action opens up. That is when Goedert gets free over the middle. That is when Hollywood Brown gets one-on-one coverage on the outside. One player can change the math for everybody else.

But there is one more thing to keep in mind. The offense Saquon is returning to looks a lot different from the one he left. Different quarterback. Different receivers. He is walking back into a whole new situation, and he may have to carry more of the load than ever. And yes, there is still that asterisk. Hamstrings are tricky, and Saquon has been out for weeks. The Eagles will be smart with him. He might not get thirty carries. But for the first time in a long time, the Eagles got some real good news out of the training room.

So what should the game plan look like? If I am Sean Mannion, I build it around three things. First, run the football early and often with Saquon and Pierce, even if the first few runs do not go anywhere. Second, use Goedert as McKee’s safety blanket, with quick throws, play action and short crossers. And third, take a couple of shots down the field to Hollywood Brown to keep the Dallas safeties honest. And on defense, keep it simple. Play disciplined, wrap up on tackles, and make Dak Prescott drive the length of the field without any big plays. Do not ask Tanner McKee to throw forty-five passes again. Protect him, keep him ahead of the chains, and let the defense and the crowd do the rest.

And let us talk about Tanner McKee for a moment, because this week is very different for him. Last week, he started on short notice. This week, he got a full week of first-team reps and a game plan built around him. Nick Sirianni said on Thursday that McKee loves football, is highly intelligent and tough, and that guys like him, quote, have a tendency to reach their ceiling, end quote. Sirianni also said every rep counts and every rep matters. McKee protected the football against Carolina, with zero turnovers. If he does that again, with Saquon behind him and Goedert in front of him, the Eagles have a real shot.

There is also a fun chess match on the other side of the ball. The Cowboys’ defensive coordinator, Christian Parker, worked under Vic Fangio in 2024 and 2025. So Parker knows Fangio’s system, and Fangio knows exactly how Parker thinks. Expect a lot of disguised looks from both sides. And with Dak Prescott throwing four interceptions in his last two games, Fangio’s coverages could bait him into another big mistake, even without Nolan Smith on the edge.

And think about what is riding on this game beyond Monday night. According to the Philadelphia Inquirer, the trade deadline is November tenth, and the Eagles play three straight division games before then. Dallas, then Washington, then the Giants. Adam Schefter said on ESPN this week that it is not in Howie Roseman’s DNA to be a seller, and that the Eagles can be buyers and sellers at the same time. But the results of these three games will shape which direction Roseman goes. A win on Monday night keeps the door open. A loss makes the conversation a lot more uncomfortable.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the pessimists. They will say this team is about to play a primetime division game without Jalen Hurts, without DeVonta Smith, without Dontayvion Wicks, without Nolan Smith, and maybe without Jonathan Greenard. They will say a backup quarterback throwing to a rookie and a group of unproven receivers is a recipe for disaster. They will say Saquon coming back from a hamstring injury is not going to be at full speed. And they will say that if the Eagles lose this one at home, the season is effectively over.

On the other side, the optimists. They will say Saquon practiced in full on Saturday, Goedert is good to go, and Dickerson could be back to solidify the offensive line. They will say the Cowboys are two and four for a reason, with a quarterback throwing interceptions and a defense that just got two starters back after weeks on the sideline. They will say a primetime crowd at the Linc can lift a team in a way nothing else can. And they will say nobody in the NFC East is running away with anything, so one win changes everything.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. Losing DeVonta Smith and Wicks hurts a lot. There is no way around it. But I think this game was always going to be decided in the trenches and on the ground, not through the air. If Saquon plays, if Dickerson is activated, and if Goedert gives McKee a reliable target over the middle, the Eagles can win this game ugly. Run the ball. Win the turnover battle. Make Dak beat you with a short field and no Turpin on returns. I am picking the Eagles to snap the losing streak on Monday night. It will not be pretty. But a win is a win.

So let me wrap it up. DeVonta Smith, Dontayvion Wicks, Nolan Smith and Michael Carter the second are out. Saquon Barkley, Jonathan Greenard, Jalyx Hunt, Landon Dickerson, Eli Stowers, Grant Calcaterra and Jakorian Bennett are questionable. Saquon was a full participant on Saturday. Dallas Goedert and Drew Kendall have no designation. The Cowboys are without Dee Winters and KaVontae Turpin, but they get DeMarvion Overshown and Cobie Durant back. And the game kicks off Monday, October twenty-sixth, at eight fifteen Eastern, on ESPN at Lincoln Financial Field.
