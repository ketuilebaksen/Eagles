Let me start with Jalen, because he deserves to come first.

Jalen Hurts is out for the rest of the 2026 season. He was diagnosed with deep vein thrombosis and blood clots in both lungs, and he is on blood thinners. According to Front Office Sports, the Eagles said he is, quote, clinically stable and has no significant functional limitations. End quote. And in a joint statement, the Eagles and his agent Nicole Lynn said, quote, Jalen’s prognosis is very good, and we expect a full recovery. End quote. They also said they anticipate, quote, no long-term effects. End quote.

That is the most important news about Jalen Hurts, and it is good news. The football questions can wait. A young man’s health cannot. And I want to be clear about one more thing. Jalen is under contract. He signed a five-year, two hundred fifty-five million dollar extension in 2023, and according to Front Office Sports, citing Spotrac, twenty-two million dollars of his 2027 pay is already fully guaranteed. So any video telling you Jalen is leaving, or that he made some shocking decision about his future, is not telling you the truth. That is not a report. That is a title.

And let me say this too. Since the diagnosis, there have been a lot of questions about how the situation was handled. The NFL and the players union have been reviewing it, and it has been reported that the Eagles will not be disciplined. I am not going to speculate about medical details, and I am not going to point fingers at anyone. What matters is that Jalen is getting the care he needs, and that when he is ready, he will be back in that building, leading this team again.

So why are we talking about a decision today? Because the season Jalen cannot play in is falling apart around his teammates, and the front office now has a real choice to make.

Sunday night in Landover, the Commanders beat the Eagles twenty-seven to ten. And the worst part is that it did not start that way. The Eagles went up ten to nothing in the first quarter. Saquon Barkley punched in a three-yard touchdown on a direct snap. And then the offense never scored again. Not once. Tanner McKee was sacked four times and hit nine times, according to Pro Football Talk. Saquon and Elijah Mitchell combined for sixty-four yards on twenty-four carries. Jake Elliott missed his first kick of the season. That is six losses in a row. Two and six. The first six-game losing streak for this franchise since 2012, and last place in the NFC East.

Nobody wants to lose like that, Eagles Nation. Nobody. And when a season goes like this, the questions start before the team plane even lands. Who stays? Who goes? And whose name ends up on somebody else’s wish list? On Monday morning, one name did.

Now go to New England, because that is where this starts.

The Patriots beat the Dolphins in Miami on Sunday, and they did it short-handed. But the injury that followed them home came in the first half. Their veteran tight end Hunter Henry hurt his foot. According to WEEI, Henry went to the medical tent, then to the locker room, tried to come back to the sideline, and sat out the rest of the game. According to Heavy, Drake Maye said afterward, quote, he seems fine in there. End quote. But nobody outside that building really knows, and Heavy noted Henry may not be ready for the Patriots’ next game on November eighth.

Then a voice on Boston radio weighed in. Meghan Ottolini of WEEI wrote that the Patriots, quote, cannot continue limping toward the November 10 trade deadline. End quote. She praised Drake Maye for making the most of a thin receiving group, and she argued that the young quarterback needs better talent around him. And then she looked south toward Philadelphia. Here is her line word for word. Quote, would the Patriots call the Eagles back and inquire about Dallas Goedert as a pure rental? End quote. And her answer was simple. They should.

Now, let me be honest with you, because you deserve that. Nobody has reported that the Patriots and the Eagles have talked. Nobody has reported that Goedert is available. This is one columnist’s pitch, and nothing more than that. She wrote the words call the Eagles back, and her column does not spell out exactly what she means by that. There is some history here. Back in March, before free agency, the Boston Herald’s Andrew Callahan listed Goedert first among the tight ends New England could target, according to Eagles On SI. But that was a free agency list, not a trade talk. So I am not going to guess beyond what was written.

Let me pause, because I want to hear from you before we go any deeper. Comment number one if you would trade Dallas Goedert for the right price before the deadline. Comment number two if you would not trade him for anything. I read these, and I want to see how Philadelphia splits on this one.

Now, remember who we are talking about. The Eagles took Dallas Goedert in the second round of the 2018 draft out of South Dakota State. Over his career, according to Heavy, he has caught four hundred thirty-five passes for nearly five thousand yards in one hundred fourteen games, every single one of them in an Eagles uniform. Last season, he caught eleven touchdown passes, a career high. And when the Eagles won the Super Bowl, Goedert was right there in the middle of it. Through that championship run, he was one of the most reliable targets Jalen Hurts had. When Jalen needed a first down on third and six, you knew where the ball was going.

And that is the part that makes this personal. Jalen and Dallas have grown up together in this offense. Jalen became the starter late in the 2020 season, and Goedert has been there for almost every big moment since. The Super Bowl run. The playoff wins. The hard seasons. So when people ask how this connects to Jalen Hurts, that is the answer. If the Eagles trade Goedert, Jalen comes back in 2027 to a different offense, with one less familiar face in the huddle.

That is not a role player, Eagles Nation. That is a championship piece. Think about how rare that is. Tight ends who catch passes in a Super Bowl, who stay with one team for nearly a decade, and who become part of the identity of an offense do not come around often. Zach Ertz was one of them. Dallas Goedert has become another. And when a player like that is on an expiring contract during a losing season, the conversation about his future was always going to come up. It just came up earlier and louder than most of us expected.

So how do we get from there to a Boston columnist asking about him as a rental? The answer is the contract. In March, Goedert signed a one-year deal to come back to Philadelphia. According to Pro Football Rumors, citing ESPN’s Tim McManus, it was worth seven million dollars, with at least four and a quarter million guaranteed as a signing bonus. That deal runs out when this season ends. And that is exactly what pure rental means. A team gets him for the rest of this year, and then he is a free agent.

And here is the line from Heavy that is going to make a lot of Eagles fans angry. Writer Dave Holcomb said that if the Eagles keep sliding, there is, quote, little reason for Philadelphia to keep Goedert other than nostalgic purposes. End quote. That is his opinion, not a report. But hold that thought for a moment, because this is the question that is going to split the fans.

Is it nostalgia to keep a veteran tight end on an expiring deal on a team that just lost six in a row? Or is that exactly what loyalty is supposed to look like? Would you trade away the man who helped bring a Lombardi Trophy to this city just because a columnist in Boston said so? Or would you say that a smart organization sells when the season is going nowhere, and that a Super Bowl memory does not buy a single win this November? Tell me in the comments right now, and be honest, because I do not have a clean answer myself. I am not ready to say goodbye to a guy like this. But I also cannot pretend the question is silly.

Now here is the other side of that. This season has been different for Goedert. He missed time with a knee injury earlier this year. According to Heavy, in six games he has twenty-six catches for two hundred fifty-eight yards and two touchdowns. Both of those touchdowns came in the very first game of the season against Washington. Since then, he has not found the end zone. But he is still getting open, and he is still catching the ball. With Tanner McKee at quarterback, the tight end has become one of the safest throws on the field, and against a defense that keeps getting home on the quarterback, a reliable target matters even more.

And this is where it gets interesting. The Eagles have a rookie tight end named Eli Stowers out of Vanderbilt. On Sunday night, Stowers caught three passes for fifty-three yards, according to Pro Football Talk. That is a young player showing the coaches what he can do. And the question this whole pitch is really asking is simple. Is it time to open the door for the next tight end in Philadelphia? Or is it too early to push the veteran out?

And there is one more thing in this conversation, because it changes the math. Dallas Goedert is not just a pass catcher. He is a blocker too. With Saquon fighting through hamstring and back injuries, and the run game managing only sixty-four yards on Sunday, the Eagles need every good blocker they have. Pulling him out of the lineup in the middle of the season could hurt more than the box score shows.

Now let us look at the Patriots’ side. Without Henry, their tight end room is thin, and that is why this idea is being floated. And even if Henry’s foot turns out to be fine, a veteran like Goedert would be insurance for a team that is trying to protect a young quarterback and win in January. For Boston, a rental tight end would be a relatively cheap way to add a proven playoff performer. For Philadelphia, it would be a quiet exit for a player who is going to be a free agent anyway.

What would the Eagles get back? Nobody knows, because there is no deal, and I am not going to make up a draft pick for you. But here is why a team in Philadelphia might at least listen.

When a team is two and six with the deadline days away, nothing is off the table, and every fan knows it. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler has put the odds of Riq Woolen being traded at thirty-five percent and Landon Dickerson at twenty-five percent. Albert Breer of Sports Illustrated reported the Eagles told teams Moro Ojomo is not available. And Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice wrote that DeVonta Smith isn’t going anywhere. So the picture is clear. The Eagles are listening on some players and protecting others. The question is which list Goedert is on.

And look at the mood around this team. Jalen is out for the year. The offense scored ten points and then disappeared. Nick Sirianni said after the game that the team is in, quote, somewhat uncharted territory. End quote. He said, quote, I got to try a different approach. End quote. When a head coach talks like that, every part of the building is up for evaluation, including the roster.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. The most likely outcome is nothing. No call, no trade. Goedert finishes the year in green, and the Eagles decide what happens in the offseason. The second possibility is that Hunter Henry’s foot turns out to be worse than it looked, the Patriots get more desperate, and somebody picks up a phone. I would not bet on that one, but I would not call it impossible either.

If I am Howie Roseman, I answer the phone. You always answer the phone. But I do not give away a Super Bowl tight end for a late-round pick just to say I made a move. If somebody offers real value, a pick that actually helps you rebuild in 2027, then you have to think about it. If not, you keep him, you let him finish the season, and you give Eli Stowers a bigger role alongside him. That way, the young player still learns, the locker room stays together, and you keep the door open for Goedert’s future.
