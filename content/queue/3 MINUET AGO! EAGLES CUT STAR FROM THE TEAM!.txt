According to Dave Zangaro of NBC Sports Philadelphia, with Blue gone, Saquon Barkley is now the only running back on the Eagles’ fifty-three-man roster. One. And that tells you there is more coming.

Let me start with the news.

According to Zach Berman of The Athletic, the Eagles waived running back Jaydon Blue on Tuesday, November tenth, the same day as the NFL trade deadline. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Blue is twenty-two years old and has been on both the fifty-three-man roster and the practice squad at different points since joining the Eagles from Dallas. And according to Zangaro, the Eagles could sign him back to the practice squad if he clears waivers.

So let me be very clear about what this is. Blue was waived. That means any team in the league can claim him. If nobody does, he becomes a free agent, and the Eagles could bring him back to the practice squad. That is a possibility, not a done deal. Nothing has been announced beyond the waiver.

And if this sounds familiar, that is because it is. According to Rivers McCown of NBC Sports’ Rotoworld, quote, not the first time this season they’ve done this, so we’re expecting a practice squad reunion here. End quote. That is his expectation, not a report. But the history backs it up.

Let me walk you through that history, because it is one of the strangest roster stories of the season.

According to Rotoworld, the Dallas Cowboys waived Blue on August thirty-first. He went unclaimed, and the Eagles signed him to their practice squad. In mid-October, the Eagles promoted him to the active roster. Then, on October twentieth, according to Rotoworld, they waived him, and a couple of days later he was back on the practice squad. Then, on October twenty-eighth, according to Rotoworld, the Eagles signed him back to the active roster to replace Dameon Pierce, who had been placed on injured reserve. And now, on November tenth, he has been waived again.

Think about that. Practice squad. Active roster. Waived. Practice squad. Active roster. Waived. All in about ten weeks. Most players never go through that many moves in an entire career with one team. The Eagles clearly like having Blue around. They just have not found a role for him on Sundays.

Now, who is Jaydon Blue? According to Yardbarker, he was a fifth-round pick by the Dallas Cowboys in 2025 out of Texas. As a rookie in Dallas, he appeared in five games, with thirty-eight carries for one hundred twenty-nine yards and a touchdown. And remember, when he arrived in Philadelphia, Blue told reporters the Eagles had brought him in for a top-thirty visit before the draft, before Dallas took him in the fifth round. He described himself as a complete back. He said a lot of people saw him as just a speed guy, but he takes pride in running between the tackles and in pass protection.

So the Eagles knew who he was. They liked him before the draft. And they grabbed him as soon as Dallas let him go.

But the playing time never came. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Blue played in each of the last two games, against the Commanders and the Giants. But he logged only eight offensive snaps and thirteen special teams snaps. He has also been one of the team’s kick returners. According to Yardbarker, he played in two games for the Eagles with one catch for nine yards and no rushing attempts. And according to Heavy, he did not record a carry in his two games with Philadelphia.

Eight offensive snaps in two weeks, behind Saquon Barkley and Elijah Mitchell. That tells you exactly where he stood on the depth chart.

Now, here is the part that should get every Eagles fan’s attention. With Blue gone, who is left in the backfield?

According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Saquon Barkley is the only running back on the fifty-three-man roster. Will Shipley and Tank Bigsby are on injured reserve. Elijah Mitchell is on the practice squad. And Dameon Pierce, who had been on injured reserve, was released with an injury settlement last week, according to Heavy.

And think about how this running back room got here. Back in late October, Dameon Pierce was placed on injured reserve, and later released with an injury settlement. According to Inside The Iggles, Pierce finished his time in Philadelphia with thirty-seven carries for ninety-six yards and two touchdowns. Shipley and Bigsby went down. Saquon has been banged up. And the Eagles have been patching the position together week after week with practice squad elevations. This has been one of the most injury-hit position groups on the roster all season.

So right now, on the active roster, it is Saquon and nobody else. Obviously, that is not going to stay that way. The Eagles are on their bye, and there is no game until November twenty-second. But it tells you the Eagles have something planned for this backfield, whether that is bringing someone back from injured reserve, promoting Mitchell, or bringing Blue back.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you want Elijah Mitchell signed to the active roster. Comment number two if you want Tank Bigsby or Will Shipley back from injured reserve.

Now, let me talk about Elijah Mitchell, because there is an important rule here. Mitchell has been elevated from the practice squad for game days several times since the Eagles brought him back. Against Dallas, against Washington, and he also played against the Giants. Under NFL rules, a practice squad player can only be elevated to the game-day roster three times in a season. After that, the team has to sign him to the active roster if it wants him to play. That is why Mitchell could be a natural candidate for one of those open spots. And according to Heavy, he is a possible promotion. Against the Giants, according to Athlon Sports, he had three carries for six yards, and he had two late-game moments that hurt, including a short catch where he stayed down short of the marker on third down, and another where he ran upfield instead of stepping out of bounds with no timeouts left.

And Mitchell has quite a story himself. The San Francisco 49ers drafted him in the sixth round in 2021, and as a rookie he ran for nearly a thousand yards in just eleven games. Then the injuries came. In August of 2024, a hamstring injury put him on season-ending injured reserve. The Chiefs signed him in 2025. The Eagles picked him up this past June, then he landed on injured reserve in August and was released with an injury settlement. And on October twentieth, the Eagles brought him back to the practice squad. Since then, he has been the main backup to Saquon on game days. Against Washington, he had eleven carries for thirty-four yards, more than Saquon that night. He is twenty-eight, he knows the offense now, and he has been the most reliable option behind Saquon over the last few weeks.

Now, the injured backs. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, one of Shipley or Bigsby could be nearing a return. But nobody has said which one, and nobody has given a date. And here is how the process works. When a player on injured reserve is eligible to come back, the team can open his practice window. From that point, the team has twenty-one days to activate him or he stays on injured reserve for the rest of the season. With the Eagles on their bye and not practicing until next week, there is no rush to open those windows today. The smart move is to wait until practice starts, so the team gets the full twenty-one days to evaluate.

Let me be careful here. I am not going to guess on anyone’s health. Nobody has reported that Bigsby or Shipley is ready. Nobody has given an injury update. What we know is that the Eagles waived a running back, and that at least one of the backs on injured reserve could be getting close. That is all.

And think about who these two are. Tank Bigsby came over from Jacksonville in a trade back in 2025 and gives the Eagles a bigger, physical runner behind Saquon. Will Shipley, a former fourth-round pick from Clemson, has been a special teams contributor and a change-of-pace option. Getting either one of them back would give this offense more depth than it has had in weeks.

Now, why does this matter so much? Because Saquon Barkley has been playing hurt.

Saquon has been dealing with hamstring and back injuries for weeks. Against the Giants, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he had thirteen carries for twenty-two yards and the Eagles’ only touchdown. He did play more snaps than in the previous couple of weeks, according to Zangaro. But a running back room with only one healthy player on the active roster is not sustainable, especially when that player is banged up. The bye week should help Saquon. But the Eagles need real depth behind him.

And this is my opinion, not a report. I do not want to see Saquon carrying the whole load after the bye. Whether it is Bigsby, Shipley, Mitchell or Blue, the Eagles need to give him help, so he can stay healthy for the rest of this season and for 2027.

And let me remind you how much Saquon wants to be here. Before the deadline, there was plenty of talk about whether the Eagles should trade him. But according to the Inquirer, ESPN reported he would not be traded, and Saquon said, quote, I would love to be here. I plan on being here. End quote. The deadline came and went, and he is still an Eagle. Now the Eagles have to make sure they protect him for the rest of the season.

And there is a quarterback angle to this too. Joe Milton is at his best when the defense has to respect the run. Against the Giants, according to the Inquirer, he led the team with sixty-four rushing yards, but he was also sacked seven times. A healthy, deep running back room would take pressure off the line and off Milton. When the defense has to worry about Saquon, Bigsby or Mitchell, it cannot just pin its ears back and rush the passer. That is why these backfield decisions matter for the whole offense, not just one position.

Now, let us talk about the roster spots, because this is where the Blue move connects to the bigger picture.

On Monday, the Eagles traded captain Jordan Davis to the Jacksonville Jaguars for a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick, according to the team’s official website. Then on Tuesday, they waived Blue. That means the Eagles now have open spots on the fifty-three-man roster. And they have options. They can sign Mitchell. They can activate a back from injured reserve. They can promote a young player from the practice squad, like rookie edge rusher Keyshawn James-Newby. Or they can add someone from outside the building.

And there was one more strange twist on deadline day. According to Dave Zangaro, the Eagles agreed to trade edge rusher Arnold Ebiketie to the Cincinnati Bengals for a 2027 sixth-round pick, but the Bengals backed out. The trade would have canceled a 2027 third-round compensatory pick the Bengals were set to receive after losing Trey Hendrickson. So Ebiketie stays, and that roster spot stays filled.

So here is the full picture after the deadline. Jordan Davis is gone. Jaydon Blue is waived. Ebiketie is still here. And according to Inside The Iggles, Riq Woolen, Hollywood Brown and Dallas Goedert were not traded. Neither were Landon Dickerson or Tanner McKee. And multiple reports said Saquon would not be traded, and he was not.

Now, let me give you a quick reminder of the Davis trade, because it is the biggest move of the season. According to Reuben Frank of NBC Sports Philadelphia, it is the first time Howie Roseman has traded an Eagles first-round pick since Carson Wentz in 2020. According to Jordan Schultz, as reported by Heavy, the Jaguars first asked about Moro Ojomo and were told no. And according to the team’s website, the Eagles now hold two first-round picks in 2027, two first-round picks and two second-round picks in 2028, and multiple second-round picks in 2029. Sports Illustrated gave the Eagles an A for the trade.

And the Davis trade changes another position group too. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the defensive tackle room is now Jalen Carter, Moro Ojomo, Byron Young and Ty Robinson. According to the New York Post, the Eagles plan to prioritize a new deal for Ojomo, who is in the final year of his rookie contract. Nothing has been announced yet. But if the Eagles do extend him, that would confirm the plan behind the trade. And Young and Robinson should see more snaps the rest of the way, which is exactly what a team in this position should want. Find out what your young players can do.

The quarterback room is in a similar spot. Joe Milton started against the Giants. Tanner McKee, who was benched, is still on the roster after the deadline. And rookie Cole Payton is there too. The Eagles have not announced who will start against Pittsburgh, but the bye gives them two weeks to decide.

So the front office is clearly thinking about the future. And moves like the Blue waiver are part of that. Every roster spot matters, and the Eagles are going to use the next two weeks to figure out who deserves those spots.

And what about the coaching staff? After the Giants loss, Nick Sirianni said, according to Eagles Wire, quote, everything can always be on the table, but I don’t think you change just to change because that’s what your record says. End quote. He said, quote, we’ll talk. I’ll look at myself internally first and then go from there. End quote. And he said, quote, none of us offensively have done a good enough job. End quote. On Monday, according to the Inquirer, he said no staff changes had been made yet, and that the team was going through a self-scout process.

So the running back room is changing. The defensive line has changed. And the coaching staff is being evaluated. This bye week could look very different from any bye week in the Sirianni era.

Let me remind you why. The Eagles are two and seven. They have lost seven straight games. According to CBS Sports, it is their first two and seven start since 1999. They lost to the Giants nineteen to eleven, and Joe Milton was sacked seven times in his first start. Jalen Hurts is out for the season after doctors found blood clots in his lungs, and the team expects a full recovery. This is a season where every move counts, even the small ones.

And here is the schedule. According to the Inquirer, the Steelers game on Sunday, November twenty-second, has been moved to one in the afternoon Eastern on CBS. Then the Eagles go to Dallas on Thanksgiving. And think about that. If the Eagles bring Blue back, his first game back could be the week before he faces his old team, the Cowboys, on Thanksgiving. That would be quite a storyline.

And let me say something about the headlines you are going to see on this story. Some are going to call Blue a star. He is not, at least not yet. He is a young player who has barely touched the ball in Philadelphia. But that does not mean this move is meaningless. When a team gets down to one running back on its active roster, it is a sign that bigger decisions are coming. And for a young player like Blue, every one of these moves is personal. He has been told he is in, then out, then in, then out again, all in his second NFL season. That is not easy for anyone, and it says a lot about him that he keeps showing up ready to work.
