Let me start with what Sirianni said after the game, because this is the part that matters most.

According to the Eagles’ official website, Sirianni took responsibility right away. He said the team will not be where it needs to be, quote, until we coach better, until we are better with our detail as coaches. End quote. And then he said this, quote, with all of the detail that’s not happening right now, I got to try a different approach. End quote. He called the situation, quote, somewhat uncharted territory. End quote. And he added, quote, we need to get better as a whole. End quote.

That is not the kind of thing you usually hear from a head coach in the middle of a season. Coaches love to say we just have to execute better. Sirianni went further than that. He pointed the finger at himself and his staff first.

Eagles Nation, think about that. Nick Sirianni has been the head coach for six seasons. He has made the playoffs every full season. He has been to two Super Bowls and won one. He has never been through a six-game losing streak as the head coach of this team. So when he says uncharted territory, he means it. And when he says he has to try a different approach, that is a coach admitting that what he has been doing is not working.

And Sirianni made it clear that he has not lost faith in the people inside that building. He said he still believes in his locker room, and he said the team will continue to fight. That is the message he wants his players to hear this week. The coaches have to be better, the details have to be better, and the fight cannot stop.

And Tanner McKee echoed that message. According to the team, McKee called the offense’s performance frustrating and said it came down to a failure in details and execution. Quote, we need to be better. End quote. And then, quote, we have the urgency. We have the fight. End quote.

Now, let me tell you about the man who called his shot. On Thursday, Commanders cornerback Amik Robertson was asked about the Eagles game. The Eagles jumped out to a ten to nothing lead in the first quarter. Saquon Barkley scored on a three-yard direct snap touchdown, according to the team. And then the Eagles did not score again. Washington scored the next twenty-seven points. According to Pro Football Talk, Jake Elliott missed a field goal that hit the upright, his first miss of the season. And two Eagles drives ended in turnovers. When you are already struggling to move the ball, you cannot give away possessions and leave points on the field.

Now, let us talk about the culture debate, because this week, it came from one of the most beloved players in Eagles history. Nick Foles.

On a podcast on Tuesday, after the loss to Dallas, Foles did not hold back. According to the Philadelphia Inquirer, he said, quote, there is dysfunction in Philly. End quote. He said what bothered him most were, quote, culture things. End quote. And he said, quote, something is changing. End quote. Foles said he knew something bad was going to happen when the Eagles parted ways with longtime offensive line coach Jeff Stoutland after last season. And he said the Eagles are, in his words, an offensive scheme away. He also said something that hurt to hear. Quote, you’re in no position to succeed this year, right now. End quote.

And Foles knows what a turnaround looks like. In 2018, the year after the Super Bowl fifty-two win, the Eagles were in deep trouble late in the season. Sirianni did not like it. According to the Inquirer, he said the team has a lot to fix, but he made it clear he wants his players to ignore, as he put it, that crap, and he pointed out that Foles is not in the building.

And then another Eagles legend weighed in. Jason Kelce. According to EssentiallySports, Kelce said, quote, honestly, I don’t even like the word culture, because it’s meaningless. End quote. His point was simple. A word does not win games. What matters is what is actually happening inside the building, every single day.

Eagles Nation, here is my take on that debate. Both of these men won a Super Bowl in this city, and both of them care deeply about this team. Foles is right that something is off. Six straight losses do not happen by accident. But Kelce is right too. Culture is not a slogan. It is accountability, every day, from the head coach down to the last player on the practice squad. And that is exactly what Sirianni was talking about when he said the details are not happening.
