Eagles Nation, we are just hours away from kickoff in London, and Howie Roseman wasn’t done. Right before the team took the field against the Jacksonville Jaguars, Philadelphia made one more surprise move. The Eagles released veteran quarterback Andy Dalton. At the same time, they promoted a center who has never played a regular-season snap and a power back who hurdled two Patriots in the preseason. So is this just a necessary roster shuffle? Or is Howie seeing something the rest of us aren’t? Today on Second Floor, we’re breaking down every move, what it says about the quarterback room behind Jalen Hurts, the final injury report, and exactly what the Eagles need to do to walk out of London with a win.

Welcome in, everybody. This is James, and you’re on Second Floor. If you want Eagles talk that’s honest, verified and ready for game day, you’re in the right place. Hit that like button, subscribe, and turn on notifications, because the moment the final whistle blows in London, we’ll be breaking it all down for you. Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with the quarterback room, because this one caught a lot of people off guard. On Friday, October 9th, the Eagles released Andy Dalton.

Now, here’s the backstory. The Eagles acquired Dalton from Carolina back in March for a 2027 seventh-round pick. He’s 38 years old, he has 169 career starts, and he came in to compete for the backup job behind Jalen Hurts. He spent the whole summer battling Tanner McKee for that spot. And according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Dalton started the season as the backup but eventually lost that role.

And there was one moment that really hurt him. In the Bears game on Monday Night Football, Jalen Hurts left briefly in the second quarter to be evaluated for a concussion. Dalton came in, and his one and only pass for the Eagles was an interception at the goal line. In a game the Eagles ended up losing 27 to 7, those were points they badly needed. After that game, Nick Sirianni said, quote, “I still have faith in Tanner. I still have faith in Andy.” But he also said, quote, “we can’t turn the ball over, and we did in a situation and lost points from it.”

So why release him now? Simple. Roster math. The Eagles were carrying four quarterbacks on the 53-man roster. Four. With the injury list growing every day, that just wasn’t sustainable anymore. They needed the roster spots for players who can help on Sunday. And according to NFL Network’s Mike Garafolo, Dalton could even come back to the practice squad. So this isn’t necessarily goodbye.

And let’s give Dalton some credit, because he deserves it. He’s a true pro. Drafted back in 2011, a three-time Pro Bowler, and a guy who has started 169 games in this league. That’s a career most quarterbacks would dream of. He came to Philadelphia to help, he competed every day, and he handled a tough situation with class. Sometimes the numbers just don’t work out. That’s football.

So what does the quarterback room look like now? It’s Jalen Hurts, then Tanner McKee, then young Cole Payton. And honestly, I think that’s a cleaner setup. McKee is 26, and according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he has a better arm and more upside than Dalton at this stage of his career. He’s been in this system for years. He knows the guys. If something happens to Hurts, McKee is the guy, and now everybody in the building knows it. No more splitting reps, no more guessing.

Now, I’ll be fair and give you the other side, because some fans have a point. The Eagles spent all of training camp splitting opportunities instead of clearly naming who was ready to step in. And when that moment came in Chicago, it didn’t go well. Some people also wonder whether Cole Payton should have gotten more meaningful work in the preseason. He showed some mobility and playmaking flashes, but he didn’t get much room to develop compared to the other quarterbacks. Those are fair questions. But the decision now is clear, and clarity is a good thing.

And let’s talk about the most important quarterback, because there was a little scare this week. Jalen Hurts showed up on Friday’s injury report with a calf injury. Don’t panic. He was a full participant in practice, he has no game status, and according to ProFootballTalk, he’ll play. Hurts is good to go. And that’s the best news of the week.

Now, the two players who took those roster spots. Move number two and move number three.

First, Jake Majors. The Eagles signed him from the practice squad to the active roster on Friday. And with Drew Kendall officially ruled out with a knee injury, Majors is expected to start at center against the Jaguars. Here’s his story. He’s a former Texas Longhorn who played 58 games in college. He went undrafted in 2025, signed with Tampa Bay, and after the Bucs waived him with an injury designation, the Eagles grabbed him for their practice squad in September of 2025. He played in all three Eagles preseason games this summer. And now, he gets his first NFL regular-season snaps, as a starter, in London.

I like what I’ve seen from Majors. He’s not the biggest center. But awareness, leverage, communication and positioning matter just as much as size at that position. He’s going to have to identify pressure fast and keep everyone on the same page. And remember, Jason Kelce worked with him this summer and told him to be confident in who he is. That’s good advice for Sunday. Don’t try to do too much. Just do your job. And think about the story. Two undrafted guys, Majors and Lampkin, side by side in the middle of the Eagles’ offensive line, in London. Philadelphia loves an underdog. Today, the underdogs get their chance.

Second, Carson Steele. And Eagles fans are going to love this guy. Steele is a third-year power back out of UCLA. He went undrafted in 2024 and signed with the Chiefs, then the Eagles signed him to a reserve/future contract in January. And in the preseason, he was a highlight machine. Two receiving touchdowns. He led the Eagles in receiving yards in two different preseason games. And he hurdled not one, but two Patriots defenders. Two! This is a physical, decisive runner who can catch the ball out of the backfield.

And with Saquon Barkley ruled out with his hamstring and Tank Bigsby on injured reserve, the running back rotation looks completely different. Will Shipley gets a bigger opportunity. Dameon Pierce brings the veteran experience, after running for 1,674 yards in three seasons with Houston. And now Carson Steele adds some thunder. Nobody should expect one guy to replace everything Saquon brings. But a three-man committee of Shipley’s quickness, Pierce’s experience and Steele’s power? That’s something Sean Mannion can work with.

And there was one more move. Defensive tackle Uar Bernard was placed on the practice squad injured list.

Now, let’s look at the final injury report, because there’s some real good news mixed in with the bad.

Ruled out for London: Saquon Barkley with the hamstring. DeVonta Smith with the hamstring. Hollywood Brown with the ankle. Marcus Epps with a groin injury. And Drew Kendall with the knee. Jonathan Greenard is questionable with an ankle injury after not practicing all week.

But here’s the good news, and there’s a lot of it. Dallas Goedert is back. He was a full participant on Friday and is off the injury report. Zack Baun is back from his concussion, also a full participant. Quinyon Mitchell, who hurt his knee against the Rams, is off the report and set to play. Jihaad Campbell, A.J. Epenesa, Fred Johnson, and Dontayvion Wicks, who had popped up with a hamstring on Thursday, are all off the report and set to go. That’s a big chunk of the defense back at full strength, and Hurts gets his tight end back.

And don’t underestimate what those returns mean. Goedert has 13 receiving touchdowns since the start of 2025, tied with Trey McBride for the most by any tight end in the league. He’s the safety valve Hurts has been missing, and he’s an extra blocker for a young line. Baun has four forced incompletions this season, the most among NFL linebackers, and he’s the communicator of this defense. Mitchell is allowing just 7.9 yards per catch, one of the best marks among cornerbacks. Getting all three back on the same day is like making three signings at once. And Wicks being cleared is huge too. He’s averaging 16.4 yards per catch, the best by an Eagles receiver through four games since Kevin Curtis in 2007. With Smith and Brown out, Hurts needed him.

One more thing to watch on defense. Jalen Carter has been playing through a hairline fracture in his wrist, and Vic Fangio admitted this week that it’s limiting him, and that the team might reduce his snaps. The Eagles have only four sacks through four games. So with Greenard questionable, guys like Nolan Smith, Jalyx Hunt and A.J. Epenesa have to step up and win their one-on-ones. If the Eagles can get Trevor Lawrence off his spot a few times, this game changes.

So let’s talk about the offensive line, because that’s where these moves matter most. With Cam Jurgens traded to Baltimore, Lane Johnson retired, Landon Dickerson still on injured reserve and Kendall out, the expected line in London looks like this. Jordan Mailata at left tackle. Willie Lampkin at left guard. Jake Majors at center. Tyler Steen at right guard. And Markel Bell at right tackle. That’s a lot to ask of a group with so little time together, especially against a Jacksonville defense that can disrupt the pocket and force turnovers. It’s the first career start for Lampkin, the first for Majors, and only the second for Bell.

And there’s a coaching angle here too. Chris Kuper is the Eagles’ offensive line coach this season. He came over from the Vikings in February to replace Jeff Stoutland, the legendary line coach who stepped away this offseason. And a lot of fans haven’t been happy with how the line has looked. Kuper himself said this summer he isn’t trying to replace Stoutland. He’d rather be himself. That’s fair. But it’s also fair to say that this is a huge test for him. Injuries, new combinations, a brand-new center. That would make it hard for any coach to build consistency. Still, Philadelphia needs to see improvement now. The line can’t be treated as a long-term development project while the offense keeps wasting possessions.

And that brings us back to the Jurgens trade. Philadelphia sent Jurgens and a 2027 seventh-round pick to Baltimore for a 2027 fifth-rounder and a 2028 second-rounder. That’s serious compensation for a player whose recent play had raised real concerns, especially in pass protection. And I still like it as a long-term move. But future picks don’t block a defensive tackle on Sunday, and they don’t protect Hurts when the pocket collapses. If Majors and the young linemen hold their own today, that trade looks brilliant. If the offense spends another afternoon under constant pressure, the critics will get louder. The short-term cost has to be managed.

Now, let’s be honest about the offense as a whole, because it isn’t all on the line. Hurts has missed some reads and held the ball too long on certain plays. The protection has broken down. The running game hasn’t consistently helped. And the play calling hasn’t always given the offense easy answers. All of those things can be true at the same time. Blaming everything on the quarterback ignores what’s happening around him. Blaming everything on the line ignores the plays the quarterback can still make.

So here’s what I want to see from Sean Mannion today. Creativity. If the line is struggling to handle pressure, don’t ask it to win every straight-up blocking matchup. Use screens. Use running back wheel routes. Make the Jaguars pay for blitzing. Carson Steele catching a screen with blockers in front of him? That’s a big play waiting to happen. Goedert on the seam when the linebackers fly up? That’s another. Quick, decisive throws that actually punish the defense, not just quick throws for the sake of it.

And here’s one player I want to see more of. Makai Lemon. The first-round rookie has shown he can get open, especially on third down. But he and Hurts haven’t quite found their timing yet, and that makes sense. Lemon got hurt in the spring and again heading into camp, so he missed valuable practice time with his quarterback. But with Smith and Brown out, now’s the time. Don’t just use him on predictable motions and quick throws every time he’s on the field. Let him run real routes downfield and give Hurts chances to find him when the coverage opens up. Philadelphia needs to make its young playmakers part of the solution.

Now let’s talk about the Jaguars, because they’re a serious opponent. They’re 3 and 1. Since Week 11 of last season, they’re 11 and 1. Their defense is physical, well coached, and they’re the only team this season that hasn’t allowed more than 20 points in a single game. They can force turnovers. And on the other side, Trevor Lawrence has weapons, including Brian Thomas Jr. and Jakobi Meyers. The Eagles’ defense has to make Jacksonville earn every yard, instead of letting quick completions turn into long drives.

But there are reasons to feel good, too. The Eagles have won five straight games against the Jaguars. They’re 2 and 0 in international regular-season games. Nick Sirianni has the best road record in the NFL since 2021, 31 and 14, and he’s 6 and 0 against the AFC South. And with Hurts at quarterback, the Eagles are 15 and 5 against top-five scoring defenses. This team knows how to win games like this.

Third downs and the red zone could decide everything. The Eagles can’t keep putting their defense in tough spots by failing to finish drives. Remember the last two games. Zero for twelve on third down against the Rams. Two goal-line plays that came up empty. That can’t happen again.

And here’s a little something from around the division. The Dallas Cowboys lost on Thursday night to Tampa Bay, 24 to 16. Bucky Irving ran all over them for 165 yards, and Dak Prescott threw two interceptions, one of them off a deflection. That dropped Dallas to 2 and 3. Now, the Eagles shouldn’t assume their rivals are going to fall apart. And they definitely can’t use Dallas’ struggles as an excuse for their own. But the NFC East is not running away from anybody. A win today, and the Eagles are 3 and 2 and right back in the thick of it.

So here’s my take, and you can fight me in the comments.

I like these moves. Releasing Dalton was the right call. Four quarterbacks on a roster this banged up was a luxury the Eagles couldn’t afford. McKee is the clear backup now, and clarity helps everybody. Promoting Majors gives the Eagles a real center for today. Adding Steele gives the backfield some much-needed power. And getting Goedert, Baun and Mitchell back is a huge boost.

But I’ll also say this. Good roster management means two things. Finding future value, which Howie has done with the Jurgens trade. And giving the coaching staff enough healthy, capable players to compete right now. The second part is what we’ll learn about today. Philadelphia has to show that these moves are part of a coherent plan, not just a series of reactions to problems that have already piled up.

For me, the biggest thing to watch in London is simple. Can the Eagles finally give this offense some balance? Protect Hurts. Establish enough of a running threat with Shipley, Pierce and Steele. And use quick throws that actually punish Jacksonville for blitzing. If they do those things, they’ll give themselves a real chance, injuries and all.

Here’s my checklist for today. Number one, Jake Majors’ first snap and his first protection calls. Number two, Markel Bell versus the Jaguars’ edge rushers. Number three, the Goedert effect. How quickly does Hurts find his favorite target? Number four, Carson Steele. I want to see him get a few touches in space. And number five, the defense. With Mitchell and Baun back, can this group get a turnover or two and flip the field?

A win in London could provide the boost this team badly needs and show that it can adjust when key players are missing. A loss would turn the heat way up heading into next week against Carolina. But either way, the response matters just as much as the result. I want to see the offense adjust. I want to see the young guys handle bigger roles. And I want to see Jalen Hurts lead this team the way he talked about all week. Next man up.
