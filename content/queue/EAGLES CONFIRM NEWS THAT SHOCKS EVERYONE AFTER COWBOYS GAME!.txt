Eagles Nation, I am not going to sugarcoat this one. The Dallas Cowboys came into Lincoln Financial Field on Monday Night Football and beat the Eagles thirty-eight to twenty-one. That is five straight losses. The Eagles are now two and five. And after the game, the news got even tougher. Saquon Barkley left with a back injury that he said came from compensating for his hamstring, and his status for Sunday is now a real question. On top of that, several other players got banged up, and the defense gave up more rushing yards to one running back than any Vic Fangio defense in Philadelphia ever has.

This is James with Second Floor, and today we are going through everything. What happened in the game, what went right, what went very wrong, the injury news coming out of it, what Nick Sirianni and the players said afterward, and what comes next with a Sunday night trip to Washington. Before we start, if you are still riding with this team through the hard times, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor. This is exactly when real fans show up.

Let me start with how the game unfolded, because for a while, it looked like the Eagles had a real chance.

Dallas got on the board first with a fifty-five-yard field goal from Brandon Aubrey. But then the Eagles answered, and it was Saquon Barkley who did it. On the opening drive, Saquon caught a pass for twelve yards and broke off a twenty-nine-yard run. And then he finished the drive with an eight-yard touchdown run. Seven to three, Eagles. The Linc was rocking.

But Dallas answered right back. Dak Prescott hit CeeDee Lamb for a nine-yard touchdown, and the Cowboys led ten to seven. In the second quarter, Dameon Pierce punched in a five-yard touchdown run, and the Eagles were back in front, fourteen to ten. Then Dallas put together a fourteen-play, eighty-one-yard drive that ended with a twenty-one-yard touchdown from Prescott to George Pickens. And Aubrey hit a twenty-seven-yard field goal as time expired in the first half. Twenty to fourteen, Dallas, at the break.

And then came the moment that gave Eagles fans hope. To open the third quarter, Tanner McKee led a touchdown drive and found rookie Makai Lemon for a four-yard touchdown. It was the first touchdown of Lemon’s NFL career, and according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, it was the first time all season the Eagles opened the second half with a touchdown drive. Twenty-one to twenty, Eagles. Remember, Lemon is the rookie the Eagles traded up with Dallas to draft. And there he was, scoring against the Cowboys on Monday Night Football.

But that was the last time the Eagles scored. Dallas scored eighteen unanswered points. Prescott found Lamb again for a sixteen-yard touchdown and then ran in the two-point conversion himself. Twenty-eight to twenty-one. In the fourth quarter, Aubrey added a twenty-four-yard field goal, McKee threw an interception to DeMarvion Overshown, who had just returned from injury, and Javonte Williams finished it off with a two-yard touchdown run. Late in the game, the Eagles turned it over on downs on a fourth-down throw to Hollywood Brown. Final score, thirty-eight to twenty-one.

Now, let us talk about the biggest problem, because it was not the offense. It was the run defense.

Javonte Williams ran for one hundred fifty-five yards and a touchdown. According to the Philadelphia Inquirer, that is the most rushing yards any running back has gained against a Vic Fangio-coached Eagles defense. Dallas ran for two hundred four yards as a team, and gained four hundred sixteen total yards. And here is the stat that tells you everything. According to the Eagles’ own game recap, the Eagles had zero sacks, zero tackles for loss and, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, zero quarterback hits. Dak Prescott went fifteen of twenty-seven for two hundred twelve yards and three touchdowns, and he was never sacked and never hit.

Jordan Davis said it plainly after the game. The Eagles did not stop the run well enough, and that, in his words, opened up their whole playbook. And Nick Sirianni took responsibility too. Quote, we got to do a better job coaching. End quote.

Eagles Nation, that is not the defense we have known under Fangio. This is a unit that has carried the Eagles through tough stretches before. Yes, Nolan Smith was out with a concussion. Yes, Jonathan Greenard and Jalyx Hunt were both limited by injuries and played limited snaps. But the run defense was the problem, and that is about tackling, gap discipline and effort at the point of attack. Dallas converted eight of thirteen third downs. When you cannot get off the field on third down and you cannot stop the run, you are not going to win, no matter what the offense does.

NBC Sports Philadelphia gave the defensive line an F. The linebackers got a D. The secondary got a D, and Riq Woolen gave up a career-high one hundred eleven yards, according to their grades. And coaching got a D, with the article saying the defense was atrocious and that Fangio needed to find a way to generate pressure.

And give Dallas credit. Dak Prescott came into this game with four interceptions in his previous two games, and he played clean, mistake-free football. CeeDee Lamb caught five passes for seventy-four yards and two touchdowns. George Pickens made the big catch on that long second-quarter drive. And the Cowboys’ defense, with Overshown and Cobie Durant back in the lineup, made the plays they needed in the second half. Dallas played like a team that knew its season was on the line. The Eagles have to find that same urgency.

Now, let us talk about the offense, because there were some real positives. Tanner McKee went twenty-three of thirty-five for two hundred eleven yards, a touchdown and an interception. It was his first interception of the season, and after the game, he said he wished he had it back and that he had to move on. NBC Sports Philadelphia gave him a C minus. Not great, but remember, he was playing without DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks, and he lost Britain Covey to a knee injury in the first quarter.

The star of the night for the Eagles was Makai Lemon. Seven catches, sixty-seven yards and his first NFL touchdown. And after the game, he kept it simple. Quote, appreciate my teammates. End quote. Eagles Nation, that is the future. Lemon looked comfortable, he ran good routes, and McKee clearly trusts him. In a season like this, watching a rookie first-round pick step up in primetime against your biggest rival matters. Dallas Goedert added five catches for forty-eight yards, playing through the knee issue that kept him out of practice early in the week. And Hollywood Brown, after all the talk this week, had two catches on five targets for thirty yards.

And the offensive line got some good news. Landon Dickerson was activated from injured reserve and returned to the lineup after missing five games. NBC Sports Philadelphia said he was moving better than he did in Week 1, but that he was extremely hard on himself about two late plays. And after the game, Dickerson said something every fan needs to hear. Quote, nobody’s quitting, and that’s what you want to see. End quote.

Now, the injury news, and this is the part that really hurts.

Saquon Barkley played, but he was not himself for long. According to Heavy, Sirianni said Saquon was on a pitch count, and that the staff was in constant communication with him to see how he was feeling. On that first drive, Saquon pulled up a bit after his twenty-nine-yard run. According to CBS Sports, citing EJ Smith of the Philadelphia Inquirer, Barkley said the back injury was compensatory, meaning it came from trying to play through the hamstring strain that he had re-aggravated early in the game. He was listed as questionable to return early in the fourth quarter with the back injury and did not return. He finished with seven carries for sixty-two yards and a touchdown, plus two catches for nine yards.

And after the game, Saquon said, quote, I just got to find a way to get healthy. End quote. According to the Inquirer, Saquon also addressed the team, which he said was unusual for him, and told his teammates they have to keep fighting. Sirianni praised Saquon’s toughness and said player health comes first, and that the medical staff and trainers had cleared him to play. CBS Sports wrote that the back issue casts considerable doubt on whether Saquon plays against Washington on Sunday.

And Saquon was not the only one. Britain Covey left in the first quarter with a knee injury and was ruled out. Dameon Pierce got banged up late in the game. Moro Ojomo went down late in the fourth quarter and did not return. And the Eagles’ own recap listed Quinyon Mitchell as being injured in the second quarter. We do not have official updates on any of them yet, so I am not going to guess. But if Saquon and Pierce both miss time, the Eagles’ backfield could be down to Elijah Mitchell and Jaydon Blue from the practice squad. Remember, Elijah Mitchell also muffed two kickoffs in this game, according to the team recap.

Now, what did Sirianni say about the season? According to the Associated Press, he made it clear the Eagles are not giving up on the playoffs. Quote, we’ve got ten more games, and then we’ll see after that. End quote.

Eagles Nation, I respect that. But the reality is that the hole is getting deeper. According to the Associated Press, this was Dallas’s first win in Philadelphia since 2021. The Cowboys are now three and four and ahead of the Eagles in the NFC East. And the New York Giants, with J.J. McCarthy now starting, are leading the division at four, two and one.

Now, let me connect this to something we talked about before the game. A lot of analysts said the Eagles’ offense needed to change. More shotgun. Quick throws early. Manageable third downs. And honestly, the offense did look better in some stretches. The opening drive was crisp. The touchdown drive to start the third quarter was the best opening drive of a second half all year. McKee spread the ball around, and Lemon was a real weapon. But the Eagles still went just five of twelve on third down, and once Saquon went out, the running game had nothing left to lean on. Sean Mannion’s offense showed progress. It just was not nearly enough to keep up with a Dallas offense that the defense could not slow down.

And the trade deadline conversation is about to get louder. According to the Philadelphia Inquirer, the deadline is November tenth, and the Eagles have two more division games before it, at Washington this Sunday and then against the Giants. Before this game, Adam Schefter said it was not in Howie Roseman’s DNA to be a seller. And on ESPN’s Bill Barnwell Show, Conor Orr said the Eagles should unquestionably trade Saquon Barkley. Now, with Saquon dealing with both a hamstring and a back injury, any trade conversation around him becomes even more complicated. Teams do not trade for injured running backs. So I think Saquon is very likely staying in Philadelphia. But other veterans on expiring deals could be on the move if the next two games go badly.

So what has to change before Sunday night? Let me give you my four keys.

Number one, fix the run defense. That starts up front. Jalen Carter and Jordan Davis have to win their matchups, the linebackers have to fill their gaps, and everybody has to tackle. Washington will have watched this tape, and the Commanders will try to do exactly what Dallas did. Run the ball, stay ahead of the chains, and keep the Eagles’ offense on the sideline.

Number two, find a pass rush. Zero sacks and zero quarterback hits is not acceptable for any defense, let alone one coached by Vic Fangio. If Nolan Smith is still out and Greenard and Hunt are still limited, Fangio has to manufacture pressure with blitzes and simulated pressures. A quarterback who never feels the rush will pick any secondary apart.

Number three, keep building the offense around what worked. The quick passing game and the connection between McKee and Lemon were real positives. The touchdown drive to open the third quarter showed what this offense can look like when the plan fits the players. Build on that. Do not go away from it.

And number four, protect the football and win on special teams. McKee’s interception came at the worst possible time, and the two muffed kickoffs put the Eagles in tough spots. The one bright spot on special teams was punter Braden Mann, who pinned Dallas inside the twenty three times, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia. But kick coverage struggled too. In close games, those little details decide everything.

And through all of this, let us not forget the bigger picture. Jalen Hurts is out for the season while he recovers from blood clots, and the team says his prognosis is very good. DeVonta Smith is still fighting that hamstring. This team has been hit as hard by injuries as any team in the league. That does not excuse a performance like Monday night’s on defense. But it does explain why the margin for error is so thin right now.

Now let us look ahead. The Eagles play the Washington Commanders on Sunday, November first, at eight twenty Eastern, on Sunday Night Football, in Landover, Maryland. Washington comes in at one, four and one, coming off a loss in San Francisco and a bye week. And there is a little extra on the line, because Washington is also fighting to stay alive in this division. Two desperate teams, under the lights, on a Sunday night. This is a game the Eagles have to win. Not should. Have to. Lose this one, and the season is hanging by a thread before the deadline.
