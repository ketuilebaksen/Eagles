Eagles Nation, another injury has hit the Eagles’ backfield, and this time it forced Howie Roseman to make a roster move right in the middle of a brutal stretch. The Eagles placed running back Dameon Pierce on injured reserve, and they signed Jaydon Blue to the active roster. According to the team, Pierce is now the fourth Eagles running back to land on injured reserve this season. Four. And on top of that, the Eagles added three more players to the practice squad, including a familiar face who is getting yet another chance in Philadelphia.

But the biggest news came on Friday afternoon. Saquon Barkley returned to practice, and he does not have an injury designation for Sunday night. He is on track to play against the Washington Commanders. At the same time, DeVonta Smith and four other players were ruled out.

Let me be clear about one thing first. Jaydon Blue is not a brand new face. He has been with the Eagles for most of the season, mostly on the practice squad. But this move puts him back on the fifty-three-man roster at a time when the Eagles desperately need healthy running backs. And that is the real story here. Because when four running backs have landed on injured reserve before November even starts, you know something has gone very wrong with health in that room.

Let me start with Dameon Pierce, because his story this season is a great example of how wild this running back room has been.

Pierce joined the Eagles in free agency and was later signed to the practice squad. Then the injuries started. Tank Bigsby got hurt. Will Shipley got hurt. Saquon Barkley hurt his hamstring in Week 4 against the Rams. And suddenly, Pierce went from a practice squad back to the main ball carrier. He stepped in against Jacksonville in London, started against Carolina, and was the lead back against Dallas on Monday night. According to the team, over those three games, he ran for ninety-six yards and two touchdowns on thirty-seven carries. Against the Cowboys, he ran fourteen times for forty-seven yards and a touchdown before getting banged up in the fourth quarter.

And Pierce has real NFL history. He played at Florida, and as a rookie with the Houston Texans in 2022, he ran for nine hundred thirty-nine yards and four touchdowns in just thirteen games. In 2024, he had a career-high one hundred seventy-six yards in a Week 18 game. He was the kind of veteran this team needed when everyone else went down. Now, because he is on injured reserve, he will miss at least four games. That means the earliest he could return is after the bye week, and by then the trade deadline will have come and gone.

That is a tough break for a guy who earned his chance. But the bigger issue is how many times this backfield has had to change. Bigsby and Shipley are on injured reserve. Carson Steele ended up on injured reserve after he was waived with an injury designation. And now Pierce. That is four running backs on injured reserve in one season, according to the team.

And when you keep changing running backs, you are not just changing who carries the football. You are changing who picks up the blitz. You are changing who catches passes out of the backfield. You are changing what kinds of runs fit the players you have. The Eagles want a physical, dependable run game. Right now, the hardest part is just keeping a running back healthy long enough to build that.

So, what does Jaydon Blue bring to the table?

Blue was a fifth-round pick by the Dallas Cowboys in the 2025 draft out of Texas. His biggest strength is obvious. Speed. He ran a four point three eight forty-yard dash at the combine. In his final college season, he ran for seven hundred thirty yards and eight touchdowns at five point four yards per carry, and he also caught six touchdown passes. That is a really interesting combination for an offense that badly needs more ways to create space.

But let us keep expectations realistic. As a rookie in Dallas, Blue played in five games and ran for one hundred twenty-nine yards and a touchdown on thirty-four carries. The Cowboys waived him in final cuts, and the Eagles grabbed him for their practice squad right away. They promoted him before the Carolina game in Week 6, waived him on October twentieth, brought him right back to the practice squad two days later, and now he is on the active roster for the second time this season. According to the team, this is his second active roster stint.

Here is what I like about Blue. Get him the ball in space, on the outside or out of the backfield, and his acceleration could turn an ordinary play into a big gain. His college receiving numbers give the coaches something real to work with, especially with a young quarterback in Tanner McKee who needs quick, easy throws.

But here is my question. Can Blue earn the coaches’ trust on the plays where he does not touch the ball? A running back in this league has to recognize pressure, pick up the blitz, protect the quarterback, hold on to the football and know his assignment on every snap. One explosive run is exciting. Reliable execution for a whole game is what earns playing time.

And then there is Elijah Mitchell. The Eagles brought him back to the practice squad earlier this month, and he was elevated for the Dallas game. Mitchell has had real success in this league, including a big rookie season with the San Francisco 49ers. He could be an important piece this week too. I would rather see the Eagles use Blue and Mitchell smartly, in roles that fit their strengths, than ask either of them to be an instant star.

Now, the Eagles did not stop at running back. They also signed three players to the practice squad.

First, guard Evan Beerntsen. He is a twenty-six-year-old rookie from Appleton, Wisconsin, and he had a seven-year college career. He spent six seasons at South Dakota State, where he started on the offensive line in two FCS national championship games, in 2022 and 2023. Then he transferred to Northwestern for the 2025 season. And according to the Eagles, he did not allow a single sack or quarterback hit while protecting Northwestern quarterback Preston Stone. The Baltimore Ravens drafted him in the seventh round of the 2026 draft. He was waived after the preseason, joined Seattle’s practice squad, and was released on October twenty-first. Now he gets a chance to develop in Philadelphia. And I am all for adding offensive line depth. The Eagles’ run game depends on blocking just as much as it depends on the backs. And with Lane Johnson retired, Cam Jurgens traded and Landon Dickerson’s name on trade lists, the Eagles need as many young linemen in the pipeline as they can get.

Second, wide receiver Quentin Skinner. He went undrafted out of Kansas in 2025 and signed with the New York Jets. He spent most of his rookie season on their practice squad before getting a late-season chance. According to the Eagles, in Week 18 against Buffalo, he caught a ten-yard pass and made a diving catch on a two-point conversion. At Kansas, he finished with eighty catches for one thousand five hundred eighty-four yards and eleven touchdowns. And he is six-foot-four. That size gives the Eagles an interesting developmental option for contested catches and red zone situations. With DeVonta Smith, Dontayvion Wicks and Britain Covey all hurt, adding a big receiver to the practice squad makes sense.

And third, a familiar face. Cornerback Shaun Wade is back. Wade was originally a fifth-round pick by Baltimore out of Ohio State, and he was traded to New England, where he spent three seasons and played in twenty games. He signed with the Eagles in June, and in the preseason, he had eighteen tackles, three passes defended and three forced fumbles. According to the team, those three forced fumbles led the entire NFL in the preseason. The Eagles waived him in final cuts, signed him back to the practice squad in September, released him on October thirteenth, and now he is back again. The coaching staff knows exactly what he can do, and with Jonathan Jones hurt, cornerback depth matters.

Put those three together, and the plan is clear. Beerntsen adds developmental depth on the offensive line. Skinner adds size to a banged-up receiver room. And Wade brings familiarity and a history of attacking the football. None of them guarantees an immediate impact. But depth matters, especially in a season where the Eagles have already lost four running backs to injured reserve.

Now, let us get to the most important news of the week. The final injury report for Sunday night.

According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Saquon Barkley missed practice on Wednesday and Thursday, then returned on Friday afternoon at the Jefferson Health Training Complex. He was limited, and he has no game status designation, which means he is on track to play against Washington. That is a huge relief. A week ago, after Monday night, there were real doubts about whether he would be able to play at all this week. Now he looks ready to go, even if he is not at one hundred percent. On Friday morning, before practice, Nick Sirianni was asked about Saquon and said, quote, we’ll see at the end of this. End quote. And about the running back room, Sirianni said, quote, obviously, we’ve been beat up in that room with injuries. But I feel good about the depth and the guys that we have. End quote.

Remember, Saquon played through a hamstring and a back injury on Monday night against Dallas. He still ran for sixty-two yards and a touchdown on seven carries, including a twenty-nine-yard run on the first drive. When he is on the field, the Eagles’ run game comes alive. The question is how much he can handle on Sunday night. I would expect the Eagles to be careful, with Blue and Mitchell sharing some of the load.

Now, the bad news. The Eagles ruled out five players. Wide receiver DeVonta Smith with his hamstring, his second straight missed game. Wide receiver Dontayvion Wicks with his ankle. Wide receiver Britain Covey with his knee. Linebacker Jihaad Campbell with hamstring and quad injuries. And cornerback Jonathan Jones with his hamstring. Cornerback Jakorian Bennett is questionable, and he would need to be activated from injured reserve to play.

There is some good news on defense, though. According to Yahoo Sports, Nolan Smith, who has been working through a concussion, practiced fully on Friday. So did Jonathan Greenard, Marcus Epps and Michael Carter the second. Jalyx Hunt and Moro Ojomo were limited but have no game designation. Getting Nolan Smith and Greenard back would be a big boost to a pass rush that did not record a single sack against Dallas.

At receiver, NBC Sports Philadelphia expects the group to be Makai Lemon, Darius Cooper, Elijah Moore and Hollywood Brown. And Lemon looks likely to take over punt return duties with Covey out. Lemon was the bright spot on Monday night, with seven catches for sixty-seven yards and his first NFL touchdown. He will be a big part of the plan again.

Now, Washington. The Eagles play the Commanders on Sunday, November first, at eight twenty Eastern, on Sunday Night Football. Washington is one, four and one and coming off a bye. Quarterback Jayden Daniels and receiver Stefon Diggs were full participants on Thursday. The Eagles are two and five after five straight losses, and after a thirty-eight to twenty-one loss to Dallas, this team needs a much better response.

And here is what has to happen. Injuries are real, but they cannot become an excuse for sloppy football. The Eagles need productive early downs, sound blocking, ball security and a game plan that fits the players who are actually available. Blue does not need to run for a hundred yards to make a difference. If he gives the Eagles a few useful carries, catches a couple of passes and handles his responsibilities in pass protection, that is real value. And on defense, after Javonte Williams ran for one hundred fifty-five yards on Monday night, the Eagles have to stop the run and get after Jayden Daniels.

So what should the game plan look like on Sunday night? If I am Sean Mannion, I start with a simple idea. Protect Saquon and protect Tanner McKee. Use Saquon early, while he is fresh, and mix in Blue and Mitchell so nobody gets worn down. Give Blue a few outside runs and screens to take advantage of his speed. Let Mitchell handle some of the tougher inside runs and pass protection. Get the ball to Makai Lemon early to build confidence. Use Dallas Goedert as McKee’s safety blanket over the middle. And take a shot or two down the field to Hollywood Brown to keep Washington’s safeties honest. Nick Sirianni said this week that everything is on the table. This is the week to prove it.

And on defense, the plan is just as clear. Stop the run first. Washington will try to copy exactly what Dallas did. Then make Jayden Daniels uncomfortable. If Nolan Smith and Jonathan Greenard are close to full strength, the Eagles finally have the pass rushers to do that. And tackle. After Monday night, nothing matters more than wrapping up and finishing plays.

And remember the bigger picture. The trade deadline is November tenth. Riq Woolen and Landon Dickerson are on ESPN’s list of top trade candidates. Jalen Hurts is out for the season while he recovers from blood clots, with a full recovery expected. And a win on Sunday night would snap the losing streak and change the conversation going into the deadline.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides on these moves.

On one side, the skeptics. They will say this is more roster shuffling while the same problems remain. They will say the Eagles have now lost four running backs to injured reserve, and that promoting a practice squad back who could not crack the lineup in Dallas is not a real solution. They will say three practice squad signings will not fix an offense that ranks near the bottom of the league, or a defense that just gave up two hundred four rushing yards. And they will say Saquon playing through hamstring and back injuries is a risk that could make things worse.

On the other side, the believers. They will say Saquon is back, and when he plays, this offense looks different. They will say Blue’s speed is exactly what this offense needs, and that the Eagles have seen enough from him in practice to keep bringing him back. They will say the practice squad additions are smart, low-cost moves that add depth at positions hit by injuries. And they will say Washington is one, four and one, Nolan Smith and Greenard are getting healthier, and this is a real chance to snap the losing streak.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. Promoting Blue was the right call with Pierce on injured reserve. His speed gives the Eagles something different, but he still has to prove he can handle the details. The practice squad moves are sensible depth moves, nothing more, nothing less. The real headline is Saquon. If he plays and the offensive line gives him room, the Eagles can run the ball, protect McKee and win this game. I am cautiously picking the Eagles to snap the losing streak on Sunday night. They need it more than anyone in this league right now. And Eagles Nation needs it too.
