Let me start with the timeline, because I want you to have it right. This comes from reporting by the Associated Press and from the Philadelphia Inquirer.

On October fourth, in the game against the Los Angeles Rams, Jalen reportedly took a cleat to the calf. On October eighth, three days before the London game, he told the medical staff about a bruise. On October tenth, in London, specialists diagnosed a superficial blood clot in his left calf. On October eleventh, Jalen played against the Jacksonville Jaguars in London. He did not carry an injury designation. After the team flew back to Philadelphia, a follow-up ultrasound found that the clot had moved. According to the Inquirer, that was on October fourteenth. On October seventeenth, Jalen went on injured reserve. And on October nineteenth, the Eagles and his agent, Nicole Lynn, announced that he had deep vein thrombosis and blood clots in both lungs, and that he would likely miss the rest of the season.

Here is the most important part of that statement, and I want to repeat it every time we talk about this. Jalen is, quote, clinically stable and has no significant functional limitations, end quote. And, quote, Jalen’s prognosis is very good, and we expect a full recovery, end quote. He is being treated with blood thinners, and he cannot take part in football activities until he is off those medications.

Eagles Nation, that is the news that matters most. A full recovery is expected. Everything else in this video is about how this was handled. But never lose sight of the fact that a young man’s health comes first. Blood clots in the lungs are serious, and the fact that the team and his agent are expressing confidence in a full recovery is the best news we could ask for. Football can wait. Jalen’s long-term health cannot.

Now, let us talk about the joint review.

On Sunday, October twenty-fifth, the NFL and the NFL Players Association released a joint statement. Quote, the NFL and NFL Players Association are working together to review the matter. End quote. That came after the union had called for a full investigation earlier in the week. In the same report, the league said that chief medical officer Dr. Allen Sills arranges for medical resources with leading providers in every international market, and that, quote, medical care for players is provided by club medical staff in coordination with independent doctors. End quote.

So what does this review mean? First, it is a review, not a punishment. According to the Associated Press, a source said the Eagles would not be disciplined for how they handled the situation. And Dr. Sills had already said earlier in the week that the Eagles’ handling was very conservative, very patient-focused and appropriately guarded in every step. So the league’s medical leadership has publicly backed the care Jalen received.

But the questions are not going away. Mike Florio of Pro Football Talk wrote that the union has not yet used its separate right under the collective bargaining agreement to launch its own investigation, but he said some investigation is, in his word, inevitable. And he listed the questions that people keep asking. How was Jalen allowed to fly to London, play in the game, and fly back, all with a blood clot in his leg? Those are questions, not accusations. But they are fair questions, and the review is where they should be answered.

Now, here is the part that has people talking in Philadelphia. On Sunday, Inquirer columnist Marcus Hayes wrote that, according to several unnamed NFL sources, the league is, quote, furious, end quote, with the Eagles. And I want to be very precise here, because this matters. According to the column, the league’s problem is not with the medical treatment or the timeline. It is with communication. The column says the Eagles released incomplete statements when Jalen went on injured reserve and when his season ended. It says no medical professional was made available to explain the situation. And it says questions about the exams, the testing, the travel and the return to play went unanswered.

Hayes also wrote that, according to an unnamed union source, the NFLPA could demand a fully independent investigation if it believes the cooperative review is being obstructed or misled. And he pointed out that Nick Sirianni is the only team representative who has spoken about it, and that Howie Roseman and the team’s medical leadership have not directly addressed it.

Eagles Nation, let me be fair to everyone here. That column is opinion, built on anonymous sources. The Eagles have cited patient privacy, and that is a real thing. Medical information belongs to Jalen. But I also understand why people want more transparency. When your franchise quarterback goes from playing in a game to being out for the season with blood clots in both lungs in about a week, fans deserve to understand what happened, as much as Jalen is comfortable sharing. And I think the best thing for everyone, especially Jalen, is for this review to be thorough, honest and finished as soon as possible. Because the longer the questions hang over this team, the harder it is for anyone in that building to focus on football.

And what has Sirianni said? Last week he said, quote, I care deeply about Jalen, end quote, and that Jalen’s wellbeing is first and foremost. He also said, quote, I miss Jalen, miss being around him on a daily basis, end quote. And offensive coordinator Sean Mannion said Jalen was around the team and that they want him to keep being a positive presence while he gets healthy.

Now, let us talk about Monday night, because this is what the Eagles look like without Jalen.

The Cowboys beat the Eagles thirty-eight to twenty-one at Lincoln Financial Field. Tanner McKee went twenty-three of thirty-five for two hundred eleven yards, a touchdown and an interception. Rookie Makai Lemon caught seven passes for sixty-seven yards and scored his first NFL touchdown. The Eagles actually led twenty-one to twenty in the third quarter. But Dallas scored the final eighteen points.

And the defense had its worst night of the season. Javonte Williams ran for one hundred fifty-five yards on twenty-five carries, and according to the Inquirer, that is the most rushing yards a running back has ever gained against a Vic Fangio-coached Eagles defense. Dallas ran for two hundred four yards as a team. Dak Prescott threw three touchdowns, and the Eagles did not sack him or hit him once. Sirianni said afterward, quote, we got to do a better job coaching, end quote. And according to the Associated Press, it was Dallas’s first win in Philadelphia since 2021.

There were positives, and I do not want to ignore them. The touchdown drive to open the third quarter was the first time all season the Eagles opened the second half with a touchdown, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia. McKee and Lemon clearly have chemistry. Landon Dickerson returned from injured reserve and played for the first time since Week 1. And after the game, Dickerson said, quote, nobody’s quitting, and that’s what you want to see. End quote. According to the Inquirer, Saquon also addressed the team after the game, which he said was unusual for him, and told everyone they have to keep fighting. That tells you the locker room has not given up, even if the results have been painful. And in a season like this, that matters more than people think. Teams that quit in October do not come back. Teams that keep fighting give themselves a chance.

And then the injuries. Saquon Barkley scored an eight-yard touchdown on the opening drive and had sixty-two yards on seven carries. But according to CBS Sports, citing the Inquirer’s EJ Smith, Saquon said he strained his hamstring early and developed a back injury from compensating for it.
