There is no press release and no new depth chart. But the people who cover this team every day are pushing hard for one change after the bye week, and the Steelers game is the perfect place to start. And the answer is not as simple as it sounds, because this could turn out to be either a smart, brave move or one more risky bet in a season full of them.

Let me start with a quick reminder of who Eli Stowers is.

According to PhillyVoice, the Eagles drafted him fifty-fourth overall, in the second round, out of Vanderbilt. According to Dave Zangaro of NBC Sports Philadelphia, he was a high school quarterback who played quarterback at Texas A&M until a torn labrum ended that career, then switched to tight end at New Mexico State. According to the team, he won the John Mackey Award as the best tight end in college football, and he finished his college career with one hundred eleven catches for one thousand four hundred seven yards and eleven touchdowns. According to PhillyVoice, he ran a four point five one forty and set records for tight ends with a forty-five and a half inch vertical jump. The Eagles took a second-round swing because they believed he could become a real weapon.

But here is the part a lot of fans do not remember. His rookie summer did not go well.

According to Sportsnaut, citing Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice in late August, Kempski wrote that, quote, throughout camp Stowers hasn’t shown much to get excited about as a receiver or as a blocker. End quote. And then he went even further. Quote, for now, Stowers is unplayable, and likely to be a gameday inactive. End quote. According to that report, Kempski said Stowers sometimes looked tentative as a blocker, had not caught many passes more than about ten yards downfield, and may have been mentally overwhelmed while learning the offense and adjusting to NFL speed. He also noted that veteran free agent Johnny Mundt appeared to be ahead of Stowers on the depth chart.

And according to that same report, the Eagles were even asked whether they could redshirt Stowers by placing him on injured reserve for the season. But the belief was that the team had invested too much draft capital in him to keep him off the field, and that he should get chances to improve during the season. That is an important detail. Even when he was struggling, the Eagles did not want to give up on getting him real reps this year.

And then came the injuries. According to Jeff Neiburg of the Inquirer, Stowers had a slow start to training camp and was not a big factor even with the backups. Then he suffered a hamstring injury late in camp. He said he thought he was going to play in Week 1, but instead he was placed on injured reserve before the game. According to Neiburg, his recovery from hamstring and quad injuries took about seven weeks, and his debut finally came in Week 7.

And here is a stat that shows how unusual that was. According to Reuben Frank of NBC Sports Philadelphia, Stowers is the first Eagles second-round pick not to play in the first six weeks of a season since guard Bobbie Williams in 2001. That is twenty-five years.

But according to Frank, the Eagles did not rush him back, and Stowers said his body was feeling better and moving better. He said, quote, I just attacked it every single day of getting it back right and be prepared for when I get back. End quote. And according to Neiburg, while he was out, Stowers worked on everything he could without running. Catching, hand placement, hand combat, playbook study and mental preparation. He said, quote, sometimes injuries like that, you can’t do anything about it. End quote.

Now, let me walk you through what he has actually shown since coming back.

In his debut against Dallas on Monday Night Football, according to Ed Kracz of Eagles On SI, he was targeted once and did not make a catch.

Then came Washington, and that is when people started paying attention. According to Reuben Frank, Stowers caught three passes for fifty-three yards in the second half, the first three catches of his career. The first was a seven-yarder for a first down. According to Zangaro, it came on second and six in the third quarter. The second was a twelve-yarder that set up a fourth and seven. And the third was a thirty-four-yard catch that took the ball all the way to the Washington eight-yard line as time expired. He nearly scored on the final play of the game. According to Neiburg, on one short catch, Darius Cooper blocked for him and it took three defenders to bring Stowers down. Stowers said, quote, I was hoping I could drag them in there. End quote.

After the game, according to Frank, Stowers said, quote, it’s always good to get the first one out of the way and to be able to feel that in a game. End quote. And according to Ed Kracz, offensive coordinator Sean Mannion said Stowers had been, quote, working his tail off to get healthy, end quote, and that he is, quote, mentally really, really sharp. End quote. When your coordinator uses words like that about a rookie, it tells you the coaches believe in him.

Now, let me be fair. According to Neiburg, two of those three catches came in garbage time in a lopsided loss. But Stowers said they still helped his confidence, and he called the game, quote, a good first step. End quote. And according to Frank, he showed the soft hands, the athleticism and the speed the Eagles liked when they drafted him.

And the snap counts tell you how small his role still was. According to Zangaro, in that Washington game, Dallas Goedert played fifty-five snaps, eighty percent. Stowers played thirteen. Johnny Mundt played thirteen. And Grant Calcaterra played twelve. And Zangaro wrote, quote, it’s time to see more of him. End quote. He also pointed out that Mundt was signed as a blocking tight end, and in his view, that has not worked out.

And look at what else happened in that Washington game, because it shows why the offense needs new answers. According to Zangaro, Tanner McKee was sacked five times. Makai Lemon caught six of nine targets for fifty-seven yards. And Saquon Barkley played just twenty-two snaps, thirty-two percent, while playing through hamstring and back injuries. When your offense is that banged up, a rookie who can catch everything thrown his way is exactly the kind of player you should be trying to get on the field. And according to Zangaro, it was the Eagles’ sixth straight loss, twenty-seven to ten, on Sunday Night Football. The offense needed a spark, and one of the few sparks it got came from a rookie playing just thirteen snaps. Imagine what he could do with twice that.

Then the Giants game. According to Kracz, Stowers played just seven snaps, while Goedert played fifty-seven. But Eagles analyst Fran Duffy noticed something. He posted, quote, second week in a row we are seeing Eli Stowers, number eighty-seven, give this kind of effort blocking a CB. End quote. And according to Larry Rupp of Eagles On SI, the caption was, quote, baby steps. End quote.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you think Stowers deserves at least twenty snaps against the Steelers. Comment number two if you think thirteen snaps is about right for now.

Now, here is a part of this story I really love, and it happens off the field.

According to Neiburg and Frank, Stowers’ locker sits right next to Zach Ertz. According to Neiburg, that was intentional, and the two even share an agency. Ertz, who is now on the practice squad, regularly checks in and offers help. According to Frank, Ertz has eight hundred twenty-eight career catches, the fifth-most by a tight end in NFL history, and he told Stowers to ask him any questions. Stowers said the veteran tight ends have, quote, poured into, end quote, him.

Think about that. One of the greatest tight ends in Eagles history, a Super Bowl champion, sitting next to the rookie who might be the future of the position, and helping him every day. And Dallas Goedert is in that room too. According to ClutchPoints, citing ESPN’s Ian Rapoport and Adam Schefter, the 49ers were among several teams that asked about Goedert before the deadline, but he never got close to being traded, and he could even re-sign for 2027. So Stowers has two veterans teaching him the position. That is a huge advantage for a young player.

And Stowers is honest about how much he still has to learn. According to Neiburg, he said there is some mental carryover from playing quarterback, but tight end requires a lot more, quote, nuances and technique. End quote. He is still working on hand placement, blocking technique and route running at the NFL level. And he said he wants to, quote, put my best foot forward, end quote, and attack whatever his job is each week.

And there is one more storyline in that room. According to Kracz, Ertz has five hundred eighty-two catches with the Eagles, just six short of Harold Carmichael’s franchise record of five hundred eighty-nine. But because Ertz is on the practice squad, he would need to be elevated or signed to the active roster to chase that record. So the Eagles have three tight ends with three different goals. Goedert, who according to ClutchPoints has thirty-one catches for three hundred eight yards and two touchdowns in seven games, is still the starter. Ertz is a legend chasing history. And Stowers is the rookie trying to prove he belongs. Balancing all three over the final eight games is going to be one of the more interesting jobs for this coaching staff.

Now, here is the big question. Should the Eagles speed this up?

Let me give you the case for doing it first.

According to Ed Kracz, one of the five necessary changes the Eagles must make after the bye is to build a role for Stowers. He argued that Goedert’s workload should shift to the rookie, because Goedert is unlikely to come back on another one-year deal. He also questioned why Mundt and Calcaterra are still on the roster. According to Larry Rupp, Goedert is thirty-one and on an expiring contract, and a bigger role would let the Eagles evaluate Stowers as a possible starter. And according to Glenn Erby of Eagles Wire, Stowers can line up in the slot, off the line, or in two-tight-end sets with Goedert, so he does not have to take Goedert’s job to get on the field.

And here is the logic. The Eagles are two and seven. The playoffs are basically gone. These final eight games are a laboratory. If you hold Stowers back now, you go into the offseason not knowing what you have. And that matters for Howie Roseman. If Stowers shows he can be a starter, the Eagles can use their ten picks in the first two rounds of the next three drafts on other needs. If he does not, they may have to spend money or a pick on the position again. Either way, they need the answer.

Now, the case against rushing it.

Stowers did not go through a normal training camp. He missed weeks of practice with hamstring and quad injuries. I am not going to speculate about his health beyond what has been reported, but a player who missed that much time has not built up the same foundation as a healthy rookie. Pushing his snaps up too fast could be a risk. And there is the pressure. On a two and seven team, every dropped pass and every missed block from a second-round pick is going to be picked apart. For a young player still finding his confidence, that can be tough.

And there is the quarterback factor. The tight end position depends on trust. The quarterback has to know exactly where the tight end is going to be. Joe Milton made his first start against the Giants, completing eighteen of thirty-four passes for one hundred sixty-nine yards, and he was sacked seven times. Building chemistry between a young quarterback and a young tight end takes repetition. That is not something that happens in one week.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I would give Stowers a real role, starting against the Steelers. Not every snap. Maybe twenty to twenty-five snaps, with Goedert still leading the room. Use him in two-tight-end sets. Move him into the slot against linebackers. Let him run the routes where his speed and his hands can win. And keep developing his blocking, because the effort Fran Duffy noticed is exactly where it starts.

Now, let me tell you exactly what I think the Eagles should be looking for over these final eight games. Because the goal should not be for a rookie to save the season. The goal should be steady growth.

First, separation. Can he get open against NFL linebackers and safeties, not just in garbage time, but when the game is close?

Second, blocking. Can he hold up against defensive backs and linebackers in the run game, and can he chip an edge rusher on a pass play without getting beat?

Third, reading coverage. This is where his quarterback background should help. Does he find the soft spots in a zone and sit down in them?

Fourth, connection with the quarterback. Whether it is Joe Milton, Tanner McKee or even Cole Payton later in the season, does the quarterback trust him on third down?

And fifth, consistency. Does he get better from week to week when he gets consecutive opportunities?

If he shows growth in those five areas, the Eagles will have a real answer at tight end for 2027. And if he struggles, they will know that too, before they have to make big decisions in free agency and the draft.

And Nick Sirianni has opened the door to changes. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, during the bye week, Sirianni said, quote, this week gives us a great opportunity to spend extra time. End quote. He said, quote, you’re always working self-scout, you’re always trying to figure out what you do well, what you don’t do well. End quote. And he added, quote, obviously, we have new pieces here that have not been here. End quote. He did not mention Stowers by name. But a rookie tight end who has barely played is exactly the kind of piece a self-scout should be looking at.

Now, let me bring this back to the Steelers. According to the Inquirer, the Eagles host Pittsburgh on Sunday, November twenty-second, at one in the afternoon Eastern on CBS. And the Steelers are coming off a rough night. The Bengals beat them thirty-eight to eighteen on Sunday Night Football, dropping Pittsburgh to six and three. According to Kracz, Sirianni is five and zero coming out of bye weeks in his career, including a thirty-five to thirteen win over the Steelers in 2022.

And the offense could look very different. According to Kracz, DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks are both likely to return after the bye. Tank Bigsby and Will Shipley have both missed the required four games on injured reserve, so either one could return. And with more weapons around him, Stowers would not have to be the focus of the defense. That might be the perfect time to give him more snaps.

Here is what I will be watching on Sunday. First, how many snaps Stowers gets, and whether he lines up next to Goedert. Second, whether he is targeted when the game is still close. Third, how he holds up as a blocker. Fourth, whether Smith and Wicks are back to take pressure off the young players. And fifth, whether the self-scout produces a smarter, more creative offense.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the people who think the Eagles should be patient with Stowers. They will say he was called unplayable in training camp, and that he is still learning the basics of blocking and route running. They will say he missed about seven weeks with hamstring and quad injuries, and that pushing him too hard now could set him back. They will say two of his three catches came in garbage time. They will say Goedert is still the better all-around tight end today. And they will say a rookie should not carry the weight of a two and seven season.

On the other side, the people who think it is time to unleash Stowers. They will say the Eagles used the fifty-fourth pick on him for a reason, and he is a Mackey Award winner with record-breaking athleticism. They will say he caught all three of his targets for fifty-three yards on just thirteen snaps against Washington. They will say his blocking effort has improved two weeks in a row. They will say he has two great teachers in Goedert and Ertz. And they will say a lost season is exactly the time to find out whether he can be the starter in 2027.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I think the Eagles should make Eli Stowers a real part of the plan, starting Sunday. Not as an emergency fix for a broken offense, and not as the guy who has to save the season. Just as a young player who deserves consistent snaps so the Eagles can learn what they have. Three months ago, he was called unplayable. Now he is making plays, giving effort as a blocker and learning from two of the best tight ends this franchise has ever had. That is real progress, and it happened in just a few weeks of actual game action. Give him the snaps, be patient with the mistakes, and watch for steady growth. Stowers does not need to save the Eagles’ season. He needs to show he belongs in the next chapter.
