Eagles Nation, you may have seen a headline calling this a Mike Garafolo bombshell decision that shocked the NFL. Let me be honest with you. Mike Garafolo did not make a decision, because Mike Garafolo is a reporter, not a member of the Eagles’ front office. What he did was share what he is hearing about Nick Sirianni’s future, and what he said surprised a lot of fans. According to Garafolo, the head coach should be safe. And now, with the first practices after the bye week in the books, there is a lot more real news. Sirianni has named his starting quarterback for the Steelers game. DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks are back on the practice field. And one of the Eagles’ most important defenders was not. So let me walk you through all of it.

This is James with Second Floor, and here is what we are covering today. Exactly what Garafolo said and what it does and does not mean, the case for keeping Sirianni and the case against it, the coaching move the Eagles already made, who will start at quarterback on Sunday, the injury news from Wednesday’s practice, the young players who need to show something over the final eight games, and what all of it means for the Steelers. Before we start, if you are still riding with this team, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor.

Let me start with Garafolo.

According to Heavy and The Spun, NFL Network insider Mike Garafolo went on The Anthony Gargano Show and said, quote, everything I’m hearing, and you’re hearing, is that he should be safe. End quote. According to Heavy, that was published on November fourteenth.

And here is why that surprised so many people. The Eagles are two and seven. They have lost seven straight games. Fans have been calling for changes for weeks. And still, the feeling around the organization, at least according to what Garafolo is hearing, seems very different from the feeling on social media.

But let me be very clear about what this is. Garafolo is not saying a decision has been made. He is saying what he is hearing. Nothing has been finalized, and there are still eight games left. According to The Spun, a finish of four and thirteen or worse could increase the pressure on Sirianni. So this is a strong signal, not a final answer.

And Garafolo made another point that a lot of fans missed. According to The Spun, he said, quote, some of these hotter offensive minds right now are not play-callers. End quote. Think about what that means. Fans keep throwing around the names of young offensive coordinators as replacements. But if those coaches have never called plays in the NFL, hiring one of them is a big gamble.

And Garafolo is not the only respected voice who sees value in Sirianni. Jason Kelce, who played for him and won a Super Bowl ring before Sirianni arrived, said on 94 WIP, according to The Spun, quote, okay, this guy can clearly get it done. End quote. But Kelce also said, according to Heavy, quote, I think it’s less about wins and losses and more about improvement. End quote. And that is really the key for the final eight games. The record is not going to save Sirianni or sink him by itself. What matters is whether this team looks like it is getting better.

Now, let me give you the case for keeping Sirianni, because the Eagles clearly have their reasons.

According to Heavy, Sirianni has a winning percentage of sixty-four point nine percent, two NFC Championship wins and a Super Bowl title within five seasons. According to ESPN, he made the playoffs in each of his first five seasons. And this year has been brutal. Jalen Hurts is out for the season. Lane Johnson retired. Cam Jurgens and Jordan Davis were traded. Other key players have missed weeks at a time. That would be a tough situation for any coach.

And there is the money. According to ESPN’s Tim McManus, Jeffrey Lurie signed Sirianni to a multi-year extension in May 2025, believed to be worth around fifteen million dollars per year. Moving on from him would mean paying a coach who is no longer coaching the team.

And Sirianni has been here before. According to ESPN, the 2023 Eagles started ten and one, then lost six of their last seven games and lost thirty-two to nine to Tampa Bay in the wild-card round. A lot of fans wanted him gone then too. Lurie kept him, and two seasons later, the Eagles won the Super Bowl. According to ESPN, Sirianni has said, quote, I’ve always felt nothing but complete support from Mr. Lurie in everything. End quote. And Saquon Barkley, talking about his head coach, said, quote, Nick is that to a T. End quote. That history is a big reason why this organization might be more patient than the fan base.

And then there is the hardest question of all. Who replaces him? According to PhillyVoice, Jimmy Kempski listed five hypothetical candidates earlier this month. Grant Udinski of the Jaguars, Declan Doyle of the Ravens, Klay Kubiak of the 49ers, Kliff Kingsbury of the Rams and Nate Scheelhaase of the Rams. Kempski made it clear that the list was hypothetical and, quote, not necessarily endorsements. End quote. And according to PhillyVoice, the main concern with Udinski is that Liam Coen calls the plays in Jacksonville. Declan Doyle shares play-calling duties in Baltimore. So Garafolo’s point about play-callers is a real one.

Now, the case against keeping Sirianni.

According to Heavy, since last year’s bye week, including the one playoff game, the Eagles are seven and twelve. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the offense ranks thirty-second in yards per game and thirtieth in points per game. According to Heavy, the Eagles changed offensive coordinators, and the system still has not improved. And Jason Kelce said on 94 WIP, according to Heavy, quote, this season, though, is not going well. End quote. And, quote, they haven’t made these corrections. End quote.

And there is a pattern that worries me. When the Eagles had Shane Steichen, the offense worked. When they had Kellen Moore, the offense worked. And then the coordinator leaves, the system changes, and fans are right back to asking why the offense cannot stay consistent. This year, Sean Mannion’s unit has struggled badly. And this is just my opinion, not a report, but the head coach cannot completely separate himself from that. When the team starts flat, makes bad decisions or fails to adjust, some of that belongs to him.

And Kempski went further. According to PhillyVoice, in an article published on November seventeenth, Kempski said he sees a stronger case for moving on from Sirianni, and that Lurie and Howie Roseman must decide which coaches stay for 2027. That is his opinion. And it is the opposite of what Garafolo is hearing.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I respect everything Sirianni has accomplished. Two Super Bowl trips and a ring are not luck. But if the Eagles bring him back, I need to know what will actually be different. Who runs the offense? Who develops the young players? How does this team get its identity back? If those answers are convincing, keep him. If they are not, maybe a new voice really is necessary.

Now, the Eagles have already made one coaching move, and I like it.

According to ESPN’s Adam Schefter, the Eagles brought back TJ Paganetti as a consultant. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he spent ten seasons with the Eagles, worked on both Super Bowl staffs, and was the run game specialist and assistant offensive line coach in 2024, when, according to Athlon Sports, the Eagles set a franchise record with three thousand eight hundred sixty-six rushing yards. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he will advise Roseman and Sirianni, but his exact role has not been made public. And Jason Kelce said, quote, he brought a ton of value for us in Philadelphia. End quote.

And this is important. According to Athlon Sports, the running game ranks in the bottom five in the NFL, with seven hundred ninety-seven yards and three point seven yards per carry. The offensive line is coached by Chris Kuper now, and he has faced criticism for the line’s struggles. But I do not think one position coach can explain everything that has gone wrong. Bringing in Paganetti gives the staff another experienced voice who knows exactly what a strong Eagles running game is supposed to look like.

And some fans are already wondering whether this could open the door for longtime offensive line coach Jeff Stoutland to come back someday. I would love that, because his work with this line mattered for years. But there is no report of any plan to bring him back, so I am not going to pretend there is.

Now, the biggest news from Wednesday. The quarterback.

According to Pro Football Rumors, citing ESPN’s Adam Schefter, Nick Sirianni announced that Joe Milton the third will start on Sunday against the Steelers. According to Yahoo Sports, it will be Milton’s second straight start, and Sirianni is not going back to Tanner McKee, who went zero and three as the starter.

Let me remind you how Milton’s first start went. According to Yahoo Sports, against the Giants, he completed eighteen of thirty-four passes for one hundred sixty-nine yards, with no touchdowns and no interceptions. He ran nine times for sixty-four yards, and he scored a two-point conversion on a tush push. But he also lost a fumble, and he was sacked seven times in the nineteen to eleven loss.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I like this decision. His accuracy was not where it needed to be against the Giants, and he missed some throws he should have made. But he also showed flashes with his legs and that big arm. And one start, behind a line that gave up seven sacks, with his top two receivers out, is not a fair evaluation. I am not saying he is the franchise quarterback. I just want to see whether his decision-making improves when he gets more chances. And this time, he might have real weapons.

Because here is the other big news. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, both DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks practiced on Wednesday. Smith has been dealing with a hamstring injury, and Wicks has been dealing with an ankle injury. According to BVM Sports, both were limited.

According to Olivia Reiner of the Inquirer, Smith said he feels, quote, one hundred percent. End quote. But he also said he felt just as good before the Carolina game, when he re-aggravated the hamstring. So he is being careful. According to the Inquirer, Smith also said about the offense’s struggles, quote, majority of the time, it’s us. End quote. And about the season, he said, quote, at the end of the day, it’s not over. End quote. And according to the Inquirer, since Smith left the lineup, the offense has topped three hundred total yards just once and two hundred passing yards just once in four weeks.

And there is a reason to be careful. According to the Inquirer, when Smith tried to come back against Carolina, he played a season-low forty-two snaps, sixty-five point six percent, before the hamstring flared up again. Nobody wants to see that happen twice. If he plays on Sunday, I would expect the Eagles to manage his snaps, at least early.

And Sirianni is staying careful too. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he said, quote, we’ll see how that plan unfolds as it goes. End quote. According to Athlon Sports, he said he feels good about both players possibly returning, but he did not guarantee anything.

And there was more good news at practice. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, linebacker Jihaad Campbell, defensive back Michael Carter the second, returner Britain Covey and cornerback Jonathan Jones all returned to practice. And running back Will Shipley, whose twenty-one-day practice window was opened earlier this week, also returned to practice. Shipley has been on injured reserve since October sixteenth with a foot injury. That does not mean he will play on Sunday, and there is no timeline. But it is real progress, and for a backfield that had only one healthy running back on the active roster just last week, it matters. According to the Inquirer, Tank Bigsby’s practice window has not been opened yet.

But here is the one piece of news that could worry Eagles fans. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, linebacker Zack Baun did not practice on Wednesday. He was a spectator, and the reason was not stated. I am not going to speculate about it. But Baun is one of the leaders of this defense, so his status will be one of the big things to watch on Thursday and Friday.

Now, let me talk about the roster moves and the future, because they all connect to the Sirianni question.

According to ClutchPoints, the Eagles signed Zach Ertz from the practice squad to the active roster on Monday. According to the Inquirer, he has five hundred eighty-two catches as an Eagle. Harold Carmichael holds the franchise record with five hundred eighty-nine. So Ertz needs seven catches to tie it and eight to break it, with eight games left. I know people want the young tight ends playing too, and I agree. But giving Ertz this chance does not mean you stop developing everybody else.

The Eagles also promoted offensive tackle John Ojukwu, according to the Inquirer. Running back Jaydon Blue was promoted back to the active roster on Tuesday, according to the team, giving Saquon Barkley a backup. And defensive tackle Leki Fotu signed to the practice squad, according to the team, to help replace Jordan Davis in the middle.

And Howie Roseman deserves some credit for the future. According to Boston.com, the A.J. Brown trade brought back a 2028 first-round pick and a 2027 fifth-round pick. According to ESPN’s Adam Schefter, as reported by Newsweek, the Cam Jurgens trade brought back a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 fifth-round pick, with a 2027 seventh going to Baltimore. And according to the team, the Jordan Davis trade brought back a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick. According to Larry Rupp of Eagles On SI, that is ten picks in the first two rounds over the next three drafts. Roseman still deserves criticism for where this team stands today. But he has created real flexibility.

And the final eight games should be about finding out which young players can be part of the future. Eli Stowers needs more snaps, because the Eagles have to know whether he can take over after Dallas Goedert. According to PhillyVoice, he has played just thirty-two snaps in the last three games. Makai Lemon, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, has thirty-seven catches for three hundred twelve yards, and when the veterans come back, I do not want him disappearing from the game plan. Rookie right tackle Markel Bell needs every snap he can get. And on defense, with Jordan Davis gone, Moro Ojomo has a huge chance to prove he deserves a long-term deal, while Jeremiah Trotter Junior and Jihaad Campbell keep pushing for snaps at linebacker.

Now, the schedule. According to the Inquirer, the Eagles host the Steelers on Sunday, November twenty-second, at one in the afternoon Eastern on CBS. Then they go to Dallas for Thanksgiving. According to Ed Kracz of Eagles On SI, they also have games at Arizona on December sixth, at home against Indianapolis on December thirteenth, and they finish with San Francisco on January third and the Giants on January tenth. Are there winnable games on that list? Sure. But after seven straight losses, it is hard to confidently circle any of them. And if the Eagles lost all eight, that would be fifteen straight losses to end the season. I do not see how any organization could ignore that, no matter what anyone thinks about the coach today.

But there is hope for Sunday. The Steelers just lost thirty-eight to eighteen to the Bengals on Sunday Night Football, dropping to six and three. According to Kracz, Sirianni is five and zero coming out of bye weeks in his career, including a thirty-five to thirteen win over Pittsburgh in 2022. And the Steelers have not won in Philadelphia since 1965.

Here is what I will be watching on Sunday. First, how Joe Milton plays in his second start. Second, whether Smith and Wicks are active, and how big their roles are. Third, whether Zack Baun can play. Fourth, whether the running game looks different with Paganetti in the building. And fifth, whether this team shows the kind of improvement that makes Garafolo’s report look right.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the people who think Sirianni should be gone no matter what Garafolo is hearing. They will say the Eagles are seven and twelve since last year’s bye. They will say the offense has been near the bottom of the league all season, and that the problems started before Hurts got hurt. They will say the Eagles keep depending on finding the perfect coordinator instead of fixing the system. They will say Jimmy Kempski sees a stronger case for moving on. And they will say if the Eagles lose all eight remaining games, there is no way to justify keeping him.

On the other side, the people who think Garafolo’s report makes sense. They will say Sirianni has a sixty-four point nine percent winning percentage and a Super Bowl ring. They will say this season has been wrecked by injuries, a retirement and trades. They will say the hot offensive names are not play-callers, so there is no obvious upgrade. They will say the Eagles would have to pay him around fifteen million dollars a year to leave. And they will say with Smith and Wicks back and Paganetti on staff, the offense finally has a chance to improve.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I think Garafolo is probably right, and the Eagles are more patient than a lot of fans expected. But patience has to come with a plan. I am not asking for a dramatic firing just because we are frustrated. I am asking for clear answers about what changes on offense, who develops the young players, and how this team gets its identity back. Sunday against the Steelers is the first chance to see those answers on the field. If Milton looks better with Smith and Wicks back, and the running game shows some life, Sirianni will have taken the first step toward proving Garafolo right.
