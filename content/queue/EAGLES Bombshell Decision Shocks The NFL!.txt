Let me start with the decision itself, because the details are what make it so interesting.

On Friday, October sixteenth, the Eagles signed Joe Milton off the Dallas Cowboys practice squad and put him directly on their fifty-three-man roster. And that last part matters. Under NFL rules, when a team signs a player off another team’s practice squad, it has to put him on its active roster. The original team cannot simply block the move. The only way Dallas could have kept him was by promoting him to its own fifty-three-man roster, and the Cowboys did not do that. So Philadelphia offered him a real roster spot, and Milton walked across the division.

And remember how he ended up on that practice squad in the first place. According to Pro Football Talk, Dallas waived Milton when he did not make its initial fifty-three-man roster, and then brought him back to the practice squad. That kept him in the building, but it also left him exposed to every other team in the league. And the team that came calling was the Eagles.

Now here is why people are calling this a bombshell decision. It is not just who the Eagles signed. It is who they did not sign.

A lot of people expected the Eagles to bring back Andy Dalton. And there is some history here. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Dalton actually won the backup job over Tanner McKee in training camp. But he threw an interception in Week 3 against the Bears, he was demoted, and then the Eagles released him earlier this month. There were reports he might come back on the practice squad, but that never happened. And when Hurts went down, instead of bringing back the thirty-eight-year-old veteran who already knew the system, Roseman went for upside. He went for a twenty-six-year-old with elite physical tools.

Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice made a really sharp point about that. He said choosing a developmental option like Milton over a veteran like Dalton could be a sign of how the team views this season. In other words, Roseman might be thinking not just about the next four weeks, but about the next few years at the backup quarterback spot. I want to be careful here, because that is analysis, not a report. Nobody from the Eagles has said that. But it is a fair way to read the move.

And to be fair to Andy Dalton, this is not a knock on his career. He was a multiple-time Pro Bowler in Cincinnati, he led the Bengals to the playoffs year after year, and he has started well over a hundred NFL games. That experience is real. But the Eagles clearly decided that at this point, a veteran who already lost the backup job once this year was not the answer. They wanted a different kind of insurance. And that is a choice you can agree or disagree with, but it is a bold one.

So who exactly is Joe Milton? Let me tell you his story, because it is a wild one.

He started his college career at Michigan, where expectations were sky-high. He was a backup in 2018 and 2019, and then he started the first three games of the shortened 2020 season. He threw for one thousand seventy-seven yards, four touchdowns and four interceptions, but he lost the job to Cade McNamara, and Michigan went two and four. So he transferred to Tennessee.

At Tennessee, he waited again. He was the backup behind Hendon Hooker. But when he finally got his moment, he made it count. In the Orange Bowl to finish the 2022 season, Milton threw for two hundred fifty-one yards and three touchdowns in a thirty-one to fourteen win over Clemson, and he was named the game’s MVP. Then in 2023, he finally got the full keys to the offense. He completed sixty-four point five percent of his passes for nearly twenty-eight hundred yards, with twenty touchdowns and just five interceptions, plus six rushing touchdowns, and he went eight and four as the starter. In the SEC, that is real production.

The New England Patriots drafted him in the sixth round of the 2024 draft, one hundred ninety-third overall. And here is a fun piece of trivia for you. That draft pick came from the trade that sent Mac Jones to Jacksonville.

Milton got his big moment with the Patriots in the 2024 season finale against Buffalo. He came in after Drake Maye left early, completed twenty-two of twenty-nine passes for two hundred forty-one yards, and accounted for two touchdowns, one through the air and one on the ground, in a twenty-three to sixteen win. It was the kind of performance that made a lot of people sit up and say, wait, who is this guy? Remember, that was a game where the Bills were resting a lot of their regulars, so you have to keep it in context. But you do not complete twenty-two of twenty-nine passes in an NFL game by accident.

Then in April of 2025, the Patriots traded him to Dallas, along with a seventh-round pick, for a fifth-round pick. Last season with the Cowboys, as the backup to Dak Prescott, he appeared in four games, completing fifteen of twenty-four passes for one hundred eighty-three yards, one touchdown and two interceptions.

Put it all together, and here is his NFL career line, according to Pro Football Talk. Five games, zero starts. Thirty-seven of fifty-three passing, which is almost seventy percent, for four hundred twenty-four yards, two touchdowns and two interceptions. Plus sixty-six rushing yards and a rushing touchdown.

Now, let me be honest with you about the scouting report, because I do not want to oversell anybody on this channel. NFL.com’s Lance Zierlein described Milton as a, quote, rare physical specimen, end quote, with a huge arm. In other words, the arm has never been the problem. The question has always been whether he can make the right throw, on time, again and again.

And fifty-three NFL pass attempts is a tiny sample. You cannot crown anyone off that. So I am not going to call Joe Milton a future star. I am going to call him a talented quarterback with real tools, who has shown flashes, and who still has a lot to prove.

But think about what this coaching staff could do with those tools. A quarterback who can throw the ball sixty yards without stepping into it. Who is big enough to see over the line and strong enough to shrug off a free rusher. Who can escape the pocket and pick up yards with his legs when a play breaks down. Those are things you cannot teach. Timing, footwork, reading coverages, those are things you can coach. That is the bet Philadelphia is making. That this building, with this staff, can sand down the rough edges and find out what Joe Milton really is.

And think about what that means for Cole Payton too. A fifth-round rookie is one snap away from playing in an NFL game this afternoon. That is the reality of where this quarterback room is right now. Hurts on injured reserve, McKee making the biggest start of his career, a rookie as the backup, and a brand new arm who arrived two days ago. If you ever wondered why Roseman felt he needed to add another quarterback this week, there is your answer.

And that brings me to Tanner McKee, because today is his day. McKee has been in this offense for four seasons. In his career through last season, he completed fifty-four of eighty-eight passes for five hundred ninety-seven yards, five touchdowns and one interception. He won his first start, against the Giants in Week 18 of the 2024 season, with two hundred sixty-nine yards and two touchdowns. But both of his starts came in Week 18 finales. Today is the first time he starts a game in the middle of a season, with a three-game losing streak on the line. At home, in front of a crowd that wants a win badly. That is a completely different level of pressure.

And here is a detail that makes the Milton signing even more interesting. Tanner McKee is in the final year of his rookie contract. So depending on how this stretch goes, the Eagles will have a decision to make about their backup quarterback spot after the season. If they can develop Milton into a reliable option, that gives them flexibility. That is the long game Roseman might be playing.

And there is a bigger lesson in all of this, one this franchise knows better than most. Quarterback depth is never a luxury. It is a necessity. Injuries happen in this league every single week, and the teams that survive them are the ones that planned ahead. The Eagles have lived that lesson before. They know how quickly a season can depend on the guy who was supposed to be standing on the sideline with a headset on.
