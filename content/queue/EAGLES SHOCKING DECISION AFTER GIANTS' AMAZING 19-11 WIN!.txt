Eagles Nation, the Giants walked into Lincoln Financial Field and walked out with a nineteen to eleven win and first place in the NFC East. And now the Eagles have some huge decisions to make. Let me be honest with you right from the start. As of this morning, the Eagles have not announced a shocking decision. Nobody has been fired, nobody has been traded, and no new quarterback has been named. But the decisions that are coming in the next few days could be the biggest this franchise has faced in years. The coaching staff. The quarterback. The trade deadline on Tuesday. Saquon Barkley’s health. And, maybe, the future of Nick Sirianni himself. So today, we are going to go through every one of them.

This is James with Second Floor, and here is the plan. First, I will take you through exactly how this game slipped away, because the details matter. Then we will talk about the injury that may hurt more than the score. And then we will go through the five decisions that are now staring this organization in the face. Before we start, if you are still riding with this team, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor.

Let me start with how this one slipped away.

The Eagles’ defense set the tone early. According to the Inquirer, on the Giants’ first drive, Zack Baun sacked J.J. McCarthy, and the Giants settled for a fifty-three-yard field goal. Then Jonathan Greenard got his first sack as an Eagle, and the Giants had to kick another field goal, this time from forty-nine yards. Six to nothing Giants, but the Eagles’ defense was winning.

Then came the Eagles’ only touchdown. According to the Inquirer, Saquon Barkley scored on a three-yard run out of a jumbo set. And according to the Giants’ official website, after a Giants penalty for too many men on the field, Joe Milton ran in the two-point conversion. Eight to six Eagles.

And then came the moment a lot of fans will remember from this game. At the end of the first half, according to PHLY Sports, punter Braden Mann blasted an eighty-six-yard punt. That was the longest punt in the NFL since 2001, and the second-longest in Eagles history, behind Randall Cunningham’s ninety-one-yarder in 1989. It pinned the Giants deep, they took a knee, and the Eagles went into halftime leading eight to six.

Think about that, Eagles Nation. At halftime, the Eagles were winning. After seven weeks of bad news, this team had a lead going into the locker room against the first-place Giants, with a quarterback making his first start. The defense had already piled up sacks. The crowd was alive. And then the second half happened.

In the third quarter, according to the Inquirer, McCarthy took a hard hit and hurt his hip. Veteran Jameis Winston came in, and he led a fourteen-play, seventy-six-yard drive that ended with a five-yard touchdown pass to tight end Isaiah Likely. According to the Giants’ website, Winston completed six of eight passes for seventy-one yards while he was in the game. Thirteen to eight Giants. And McCarthy came back later in the third quarter.

From there, the Giants just kept kicking. According to the Giants’ website, Dominic Zvada made all four of his field goal attempts, from fifty-three, forty-six, thirty-eight and forty-nine yards. It was nineteen to eight with about four and a half minutes left. Jake Elliott, playing through an ankle injury, hit a forty-five-yard field goal to make it nineteen to eleven. And on the final drive, according to the Inquirer, the Eagles reached their own forty-three, but Milton was sacked for a seventh time on fourth and two with six seconds left.

Seven sacks. And according to the Giants’ website, it was a season high for New York. Rookie linebacker Arvell Reese had three sacks, a forced fumble and twelve tackles. He strip-sacked Milton on third and five, and the Giants recovered at midfield. According to Athlon Sports, the Giants had only nine sacks in seven games coming into this one.

There was also a key third down that summed up the day. According to the Inquirer, on third and seven, Milton’s pass was thrown behind receiver Darius Cooper. Incomplete. Another drive over.

And the numbers for the Eagles’ offense are brutal. According to Athlon Sports, they gained just two hundred twenty yards. It was the first time since 2019 that the Eagles scored eleven or fewer points in back-to-back games. According to PHLY Sports, they scored one touchdown in thirteen drives and went three and out seven times, and TruMedia data ranked it two hundred forty-fifth out of two hundred sixty offensive performances in the entire NFL this season.

And this was not just any loss. According to the Inquirer, it was the Eagles’ third straight loss to an NFC East opponent, after Dallas and Washington. And according to Athlon Sports, the Eagles have now allowed twelve sacks in the last two games and thirty through nine games, with three different quarterbacks under center. That number tells you this problem is not about one quarterback.

Now, let me give credit where it is due, because the defense deserved a better result.

According to PHLY Sports, the Eagles had six sacks, their most in a game this season. Baun had two, and Jalen Carter, Jordan Davis, Nolan Smith and Greenard had one each. According to the Inquirer, the defense had only ten sacks through eight games before this one. According to the Giants’ website, the Giants ran thirty-one times for just eighty yards, and Devin Singletary, starting in place of the injured Cam Skattebo, had twenty-one carries for thirty-one yards. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, it was the first time all season the Eagles held an opponent under one hundred rushing yards. Jeremiah Trotter Junior had ten tackles, three tackles for loss and a forced fumble. And Quinyon Mitchell held Malik Nabers to two catches for seven yards.

Jordan Davis explained what changed. According to the Inquirer, he said the defense looked at what needed fixing, and that stopping the run would give them a chance to rush the passer. That is exactly what happened. Vic Fangio’s group showed up. The offense did not.

And now, the injury that may hurt more than the score.

According to the Inquirer, Zack Baun suffered a back injury in the third quarter after a hard hit on McCarthy, and he did not return. Before he left, he had two sacks and seven tackles. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he left the game with a back injury after that two-sack day. And with Jihaad Campbell already out, the Eagles’ linebacker room got even thinner.

Let me be very clear. No diagnosis has been released. No timetable has been announced. I am not going to guess how serious it is, and nobody else should either. The good news is that the bye week gives him extra time. And the Eagles do not play again until November twenty-second against the Pittsburgh Steelers.

Now, let us talk about the five decisions the Eagles are facing.

Decision number one. The coaching staff.

After the game, Sirianni was asked if staff changes are on the table during the bye. According to Crossing Broad, he said, quote, we’ll talk. End quote. And according to Zach Berman of The Athletic, as cited by ClutchPoints, Sirianni said, quote, everything can always be on the table, but I don’t think you just change just to change just because your record says. End quote. He also said, quote, you have got to look at what’s really real and not react to anything else. End quote. And he said that if he has a strong reason and he is convicted about it, that is how the team will proceed.

So the door is open. The biggest question is the offensive coordinator, Sean Mannion. According to Athlon Sports, pass game coordinator Josh Grizzard, a former Buccaneers play caller, is the in-house option if the Eagles make a change. And according to PHLY Sports, Mannion did call more quarterback runs, rollouts and deep shots in this game. But the result was still the worst offensive performance of the season by expected points per drive. Then there is offensive line coach Chris Kuper. Sirianni has publicly backed him, saying, quote, Chris is coaching his butt off. End quote. But after seven sacks, that support is going to be tested.

And here is why the offensive line is at the center of all of this. According to NFL dot com, Lane Johnson announced his retirement on October sixth, and the next day the Eagles traded Cam Jurgens to Baltimore. Rookie Markel Bell has been starting at right tackle. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he has struggled, and the whole line earned an F grade after this game. And according to Dave Zangaro, the Eagles had already allowed a free rusher on eighteen point two percent of pass plays before the Giants game, the most in the league. Against the Giants, Arvell Reese came off the edge unblocked on the strip sack. When defenders are getting to your quarterback without being touched, that is a coaching and communication problem, not just a talent problem.

Decision number two. The quarterback.

Joe Milton made his first career start twenty-three days after the Eagles signed him off the Dallas Cowboys’ practice squad. According to the Inquirer, he went eighteen of thirty-four for one hundred sixty-nine yards, with no touchdowns and no interceptions. He led the team in rushing with nine carries for sixty-four yards, including scrambles of nineteen and twenty-four yards, according to Athlon Sports. And according to Reuben Frank of NBC Sports Philadelphia, his receivers dropped four passes.

After the game, Milton said, quote, for my first game here, I feel like I did all right. End quote. He said, quote, there are a lot of things that I can clean up as far as protection. End quote. And about the strip sack, he said, quote, cost us a turnover. Just got to fix it and slide the protection over there. End quote. Sirianni called it a mixed bag, saying Milton, quote, had some really good plays, and then he’s going to have plays that he’s going to want back. End quote.

So does Milton stay the starter after the bye? Or do the Eagles go back to Tanner McKee? And remember, according to Albert Breer of Sports Illustrated, McKee is among the players other teams perceive to be available before the deadline. Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I keep Milton. He gave this offense something it did not have, his legs. With two weeks to practice as the starter, if the coaches stick with him, he should be better against Pittsburgh.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you want Joe Milton to keep starting after the bye. Comment number two if you want Tanner McKee back.

Decision number three. The trade deadline.

It is Tuesday, November tenth, at four in the afternoon Eastern. That is the next big moment for this franchise. According to Athlon Sports, the Eagles are slotted at number five in Tankathon’s 2027 draft order heading into Week 10, and Athlon argues the Eagles should sell. Guard Landon Dickerson has drawn interest from several teams, according to Ryan Fowler of NBC Sports Philadelphia and ESPN’s Adam Schefter. Cornerback Riq Woolen and defensive tackle Moro Ojomo are the other names that keep coming up. According to Larry Rupp of Eagles On SI, receiver Hollywood Brown and defensive tackle Byron Young are also candidates. And Tanner McKee could be moved too.

But there are limits. According to PhillyVoice, multiple reports from the ESPN and NFL Network media family said Saquon Barkley will not be traded. Albert Breer reported that the Eagles told teams Ojomo is not available. And ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler said his sense is that the Eagles, quote, could be trading some players but likely won’t engage in a rebuild. End quote.

And remember, Howie Roseman already made one big move this season. According to NFL dot com, on October seventh, the Eagles traded center Cam Jurgens to the Baltimore Ravens for a 2027 fifth-round pick and a 2028 second-round pick. So he has shown he is willing to make tough calls.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion. I would sell veterans on expiring deals who are not part of the future, like Hollywood Brown and Byron Young. But I would keep Ojomo, I would keep Dickerson unless someone offers real value, and I would absolutely keep Saquon.

Decision number four. Saquon Barkley’s health.

Saquon has been playing through hamstring and back injuries for weeks. Against the Giants, according to Athlon Sports, he had thirteen carries for twenty-two yards, and Elijah Mitchell had three carries for six yards. He did score the Eagles’ only touchdown. And with the bye week here, he finally gets some time to rest. I am not going to speculate about medical details, and I am not going to blame anyone in that building. But this is my opinion, not a report. The Eagles need to use these two weeks wisely. If Saquon is not close to healthy by the Steelers game, they should think hard about how much they ask of him. The goal has to be the long term.

And speaking of Mitchell, there were two late mistakes that hurt. According to Athlon Sports, with about two and a half minutes left, he caught a low pass on third down, went to the ground without being touched and stayed down short of the marker, forcing the field goal. And with thirty-one seconds left and no timeouts, he caught a short pass and kept running upfield instead of stepping out of bounds. Those are the details Sirianni keeps talking about.

And remember the player who is not on the field. Jalen Hurts is out for the season after doctors found blood clots in his lungs, and the Eagles and his agent have said they expect a full recovery. Last week, according to ESPN’s Tim McManus, Jalen visited the team facility and sat in on meetings, and his presence, quote, did provide a morale boost for some. End quote. Sirianni said, quote, I miss him like crazy. End quote. Every decision the Eagles make this week, from the quarterback room to the trade deadline, will also shape the team Jalen comes back to.

Decision number five. The biggest one of all. What does Jeffrey Lurie do?

Last week, Sirianni said, according to Dave Zangaro of NBC Sports Philadelphia, quote, I’ve always felt nothing but complete support from Mr. Lurie in everything. End quote. But Zangaro also wrote that nobody, including Lurie, knows yet whether Sirianni will keep his job. And according to Zangaro, The Athletic gave the Eagles just a one percent chance to make the playoffs.

Here is the history. The last time the Eagles had a losing streak like this was 2012. That year, they lost eight straight, and Lurie moved on from Andy Reid at the end of the season. Lurie usually waits until the season is over. The one big exception was Chip Kelly in 2015, who was released with one game left, after the Eagles were eliminated, according to Athlon Sports. And Athlon argued that Lurie should not fire Sirianni during the bye, because handing eight games to an interim coach would accomplish nothing a January decision could not.

But there is another side. Sirianni has made the playoffs in every full season as head coach. He has been to two Super Bowls and won one. And after the Eagles lost six of their last seven games in 2023, Lurie kept him, and the Eagles won Super Bowl fifty-nine the very next season.

So I want to be clear. Nobody has reported that Lurie is about to fire Sirianni. Nobody has reported a decision. But this is the question that will hang over this team for the rest of the season.

Now, let me talk about the Giants for a second, because their story tells you how fast things change in this league. According to the Giants’ website, they are now five, two and one in John Harbaugh’s first season, and they lead the NFC East. They won without their starting quarterback for part of the game and without their top running back. Isaiah Likely set a personal single-season reception record. And according to Athlon Sports, it was the Giants’ first win in Philadelphia since 2013. Two seasons ago, the Eagles were Super Bowl champions. Now the Giants are on top of the division, and the Eagles are at the bottom. That is a hard thing to accept.

And there was one moment that reminded everyone what this franchise means. Merrill Reese is in his fiftieth season as the radio voice of the Philadelphia Eagles. Fifty years of calling the good days and the bad days. That is something to celebrate, even on a day like this.

Here is what I will be watching this week. First, Tuesday at four o’clock. Does Howie sell, hold, or surprise everybody? Second, any update on Zack Baun’s back. Third, whether Sirianni makes a change on the coaching staff during the bye. And fourth, whether the Eagles announce who will start at quarterback against Pittsburgh.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the people who say it is time for big changes. They will say the Eagles are two and seven with seven straight losses, and that the offense has not scored more than eleven points in two straight games. They will say seven sacks against a team that had nine all season is unacceptable, and that the coaching staff has to answer for it. They will say Howie Roseman should sell veterans before Tuesday and build for 2027 with a top-five pick. And they will say that if nothing changes during the bye, Lurie has to consider a change at head coach.

On the other side, the people who say stay patient. They will say the defense just had six sacks and held the Giants to eighty rushing yards, and that the team is still fighting. They will say the Eagles were leading at halftime with a quarterback who had been on the roster for three weeks. They will say no team can win with its franchise quarterback, its top two receivers and its right tackle all out, and with a star running back playing hurt. They will say Lurie stuck with Sirianni in 2023, and it led to a championship. And they will say the bye week is a chance to get healthy and reset.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. The defense is not the problem. The problem is an offense that cannot protect its quarterback and cannot finish drives. So the first decision has to be about the offense. Whether that means a new play caller, a new approach on the offensive line, or a simpler game plan, something has to change during this bye. On Tuesday, I would sell the players who are not part of the future, but keep the young core together. And on Sirianni, I think Lurie waits until January. But the second half of this season is now a full evaluation, and Sirianni knows it.
