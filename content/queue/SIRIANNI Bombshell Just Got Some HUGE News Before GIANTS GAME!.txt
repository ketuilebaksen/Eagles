Eagles Nation, on Monday, Nick Sirianni stood at the podium and made a promise. He said, quote, Tanner’s our guy. We’re not making any changes there. End quote. Forty-eight hours later, that promise was gone. On Wednesday, ESPN’s Adam Schefter and NFL Network’s Mike Garafolo reported that the Eagles are benching Tanner McKee, and that Joe Milton the third will make the first start of his NFL career on Sunday against the New York Giants. That is the bombshell. And it raises a big question about the head coach. Is this a bold move from a coach who finally decided to shake things up? Or is it the clearest sign yet that this team is running out of answers?

This is James with Second Floor, and today we are going inside Nick Sirianni’s decision. What he said on Monday and why it changed so fast, who Joe Milton really is, what the scouting reports say about him, why one columnist says the Eagles are throwing in the towel, what his new teammates are saying, and what it all means for Sirianni heading into the Giants game. Before we start, if you are still riding with this team, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor.

Let me start with Monday, because the timeline matters.

The Eagles had just lost to the Washington Commanders, twenty-seven to ten. That was loss number six in a row. Reporters asked Sirianni about the quarterback spot, and he gave a clear answer. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he said, quote, Tanner’s our guy. End quote. He said he was not making any changes. But he also said, quote, everything’s on the table for us in that aspect. End quote. And he talked about finding solutions in the scheme, in the schedule and in the practice plays.

Two days later, the Eagles made the change. Tanner McKee went from the guy on Monday to the bench on Wednesday.

Now, let me be fair to Sirianni here. When he said everything is on the table, he left himself some room. And coaches change their minds all the time when they see something new in practice or in the film room. But for a lot of fans, the problem is not the decision itself. It is the message. If you say a player is your guy, and then you bench him two days later, people are going to ask whether you had a plan in the first place.

Now, let me set the scene, because the numbers are ugly. The Eagles are two and six. They have lost six straight. They are in last place in the NFC East. And after the Giants game, they finally get their bye week. So this Sunday is the last chance to make a statement before the break.

And the quarterback room has been a mess all year. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, over the summer, the Eagles rotated Andy Dalton and Tanner McKee and gave Dalton the backup job. Then in Week 3 against the Bears, Dalton threw one pass in relief of Jalen Hurts, and it was intercepted. The Eagles demoted him and later released him. Then Jalen was diagnosed with blood clots in his lungs, and he is out for the season. The team expects a full recovery, and he has not played since October eleventh in Jacksonville. And here is the part that still stings. According to Newsweek, after Jalen went on injured reserve, the Eagles offered Dalton a chance to come back. He turned them down and signed with the Atlanta Falcons instead.

So think about this. Jalen is out. Dalton is gone. McKee is benched. And now the starting quarterback of the Philadelphia Eagles is a man who was on the Dallas Cowboys’ practice squad three weeks ago.

So who is Joe Milton?

He is twenty-six years old. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he is listed at six foot five and two hundred thirty-six pounds. He played at Michigan and Tennessee, and the Patriots drafted him in the sixth round in 2024. In Week 18 of his rookie season, he played most of the game and completed twenty-two of twenty-nine passes for two hundred forty-one yards and a touchdown, and he ran for another score. Then he went to Dallas, where he backed up Dak Prescott last season. He did not make the Cowboys’ fifty-three-man roster this summer and landed on their practice squad. And the Eagles signed him off that practice squad on October sixteenth.

According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he has played in five NFL games without a start. In those games, he has completed thirty-seven of fifty-three passes, almost seventy percent, for four hundred twenty-four yards, with two touchdowns and two interceptions. That is a very small sample. And it has been up and down. According to NFL dot com, in four games with Dallas last season, he completed fifteen of twenty-four passes for one hundred eighty-three yards, with a touchdown and two interceptions, and he ran five times for fifty yards. So you have one strong day in New England, a few rough moments in Dallas, and a lot of questions in between. And within about a week of arriving in Philadelphia, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he moved ahead of rookie Cole Payton as the backup.

Now, here is what everyone agrees on. Joe Milton has a big arm. NBC Sports Philadelphia described him as a player with a big arm and considerable talent, who was seen as a developmental prospect when he was drafted. According to NJ.com, he has an NFL starting-caliber arm. But NJ.com also said his accuracy and decision-making are shaky. And that is the big question. Can a quarterback who has always had the physical tools finally put it all together in his first NFL start?

And here is why the Eagles are willing to find out. Mobility. According to Olivia Reiner of the Inquirer, the Eagles lead the league in unblocked pressure, with a free rusher on eighteen point two percent of pass plays through November first. And they are among the worst teams in the league in quarterback pressure rate allowed. When defenders are getting to your quarterback without anybody touching them, you need a quarterback who can escape. That is what Milton can do. And it is not just about running away from pressure. A quarterback who can move forces the defense to think twice about blitzing, because one missed tackle can turn into a big gain. Tanner McKee is a pocket passer. Milton can roll out, extend plays and make throws on the run.

Now, listen to what Sirianni said about Milton on Wednesday. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he said, quote, I think he’s worked really hard. End quote. He said Milton and quarterbacks coach Parks Frazier, quote, have spent a ton of time together, getting him caught up to speed. End quote. And he said, quote, I like his progression. End quote. According to the Inquirer, he also said, quote, he works extremely hard. End quote.

That is a coach who believes in the work. But is it real belief, or is it a coach trying to sound positive because he has no better option? That is the debate.

And Milton’s new teammates sound excited. According to the Inquirer, Dallas Goedert said Milton, quote, throws it hard, end quote, but it is a, quote, really catchable ball. End quote. And Goedert said, quote, if he gets pressure, he can roll out and keep the play alive. End quote. Goedert also made sure to defend Tanner McKee, saying McKee did not play terrible. And Jordan Mailata said the focus this week has been on urgency and communication at the line of scrimmage. That matters, because pre-snap mistakes cost this team dearly in Washington, when a false start and an illegal formation wiped out a scoring chance.

But here is the challenge for Sean Mannion. According to John McMullen of Eagles On SI, this offense may simply be too complicated for the current roster. It is a system built on pre-snap motion, different formations and play action, influenced by Kyle Shanahan and Sean McVay. When Mannion was asked about the fourth down false start in Washington, he said, quote, I think any quarterback’s going to want to gather some degree of information at the line of scrimmage. End quote. That is a lot to ask of a quarterback who has been in the building for three weeks. McMullen even suggested that starting Milton might be a way for Sirianni to push his coordinator to simplify things. If that is the plan, I am all for it.

And Milton himself sounds ready. On the team’s official website, he said, quote, we all know that you have to be ready to be on the field at all times. So, I’m going to be ready on Sunday. End quote. He said, quote, I’m going to play my game and stay within the offense, and that’s the job. End quote. And he said, quote, it’s football, and I’m looking forward to the opportunity to lead this team and play the game I love. End quote.

And I have to say it again. Tanner McKee handled this like a pro. He said, quote, I’m here to help us win, no matter what that means. End quote. And he said, quote, Joe is doing a great job, and I think he’s going to go out there and play a good game on Sunday. End quote. According to NJ.com, McKee had a thirty point six QBR and a seventy-six point seven passer rating in his three starts. Not good enough. But he stepped into a terrible situation, and he never complained.

Now, here is the part that will make some Eagles fans angry. Not everybody thinks this is a bold move. Cayden Steele of NJ.com wrote, quote, by pulling the plug on McKee, the Eagles are throwing in the towel. Their season is over. End quote. That is his opinion, not a report. But it is a strong one. His argument is that Milton is not a clear upgrade, and that the Eagles are simply trying something new because nothing else has worked.

So which is it? A spark or a surrender? Honestly, it could be a little bit of both.

Now, let me talk about what this means for Nick Sirianni himself.

A few weeks ago, nobody would have imagined Sirianni would be making a decision like this. He has made the playoffs every full season as head coach. He has been to two Super Bowls and won one. But this season has been different. After the Washington loss, he said the struggles are, in his words, one hundred percent on him. He said, quote, I got to try a different approach. End quote. And he said, quote, to keep doing the same thing over and over again and expect a different result is crazy. End quote.

So in a way, this move is Sirianni living up to his own words. He said he had to do something different. He kept his offensive coordinator, Sean Mannion. He kept his offensive line coach, Chris Kuper, saying, quote, Chris is coaching his butt off. End quote. But at quarterback, he made the change. And now, the results on Sunday will say a lot about whether that was the right call.

Think about the position he is in. His starting quarterback is out for the year with a serious health issue. His veteran backup turned down a return. His third quarterback just lost three straight starts. His offensive line has been reshuffled, with a rookie at right tackle and Landon Dickerson playing only three of eight games, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia. And his top two receivers are banged up. That is not an excuse. Sirianni would be the first to say that. But it is the reality of what he is working with, and it is why this quarterback decision carries so much weight.

And let us be honest. Sirianni needs this one. A seventh straight loss going into the bye would bring a lot of noise about his future. A win, especially with a quarterback making his first start, would give him something to build on during the break.

There was also some good news around Sirianni this week. Jalen Hurts made an appearance at the team’s facility on Wednesday and sat in on meetings, according to ESPN’s Tim McManus, and his presence, quote, did provide a morale boost for some. End quote. Sirianni said, quote, I miss him like crazy. End quote. And Saquon Barkley was upgraded to limited in practice on Thursday, according to Eagles Wire, after missing Wednesday with hamstring and back injuries. On the other hand, DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks still did not practice. So Milton may not have his top two receivers.

And the Giants are no pushovers. They come to Lincoln Financial Field on Sunday at one in the afternoon Eastern, on FOX. They are four, two and one, and according to Sports Illustrated, they are three-and-a-half-point favorites on the road. They are without their starting quarterback, Jaxson Dart, and pass rusher Brian Burns, both out for the season. But they will be rested after their bye. And Giants coach John Harbaugh said, according to the New York Post, quote, we’ll try to anticipate what exactly they’re gonna run with him, but it’s still gonna be their offense. End quote.

And remember, this all happens with the trade deadline just two days after the Giants game. It is Tuesday, November tenth, at four in the afternoon Eastern. According to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, his sense is that the Eagles, quote, could be trading some players but likely won’t engage in a rebuild. End quote. Multiple reports say Saquon will not be traded. The Eagles are inclined to keep Moro Ojomo, even with the Jaguars interested. And Hollywood Brown, who has been a healthy scratch, is a name teams believe could be moved. So Sunday is not just a test for Joe Milton. It is a test for the whole roster, and for the head coach who made the call.

Here is what I will be watching on Sunday. First, the first quarter. Can Milton get the offense moving early, before the Giants adjust? Second, the pre-snap penalties. If the Eagles are still jumping offsides and lining up wrong, that is on the coaching staff, not the quarterback. Third, the deep ball. Milton’s arm is his biggest weapon, and I want to see Mannion give him a few shots down the field. And fourth, the scrambles. If Milton can turn those free rushers into big plays with his legs, the whole game could change.

On one side, the people who say this is a sign of a team in trouble. They will say Sirianni said Tanner’s our guy, and then benched him two days later, and that makes the coaching staff look like it has no plan. They will say Joe Milton has never started an NFL game and has always had accuracy questions. They will say NJ.com is right, and that the Eagles are throwing in the towel. And they will say the real problem is the offense and the offensive line, and that changing quarterbacks will not fix that.

On the other side, the people who say this is a bold and smart move. They will say Sirianni promised a different approach, and this is exactly what that looks like. They will say Milton’s legs can help against all those free rushers. They will say Goedert loves the way he throws the ball, and that the offense needs a spark. They will say there is nothing to lose with a two and six record, so you might as well see what you have in a young quarterback with a big arm. And they will say Sirianni deserves credit for not standing still.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I wish Sirianni had not said Tanner’s our guy on Monday. That part was a mistake, because it made the change look messy. But the decision itself? I like it. McKee was not the answer, and Milton gives this offense something different. He can run. He can throw it a mile. And he has nothing to lose. If Sean Mannion simplifies the offense and lets Milton play fast, this could be a fun Sunday at the Linc. And if it does not work, at least the Eagles tried something. That is more than you can say for standing still. And for a fan base that has watched six straight losses, that matters.
