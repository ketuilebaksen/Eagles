Let me start with the hard part, because you deserve the truth.

The Eagles opened this season with two wins, and everything felt possible. Now they are two and seven, with seven straight losses. According to the Associated Press, that is their longest losing streak since an eight-game skid in 2012. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the offense ranks thirty-second in the league in yards per game, thirtieth in points per game and twenty-ninth in passing yards per game. And the defense, which a lot of us expected to be one of the best in the league, has not been able to save them.

And the standings make it even harder to look at. The Giants are leading the NFC East at five, three and one. The Cowboys are four and five. The Commanders are three, five and one, and that one is the tie they had with the Giants earlier in the season. And the Eagles are sitting at the bottom of the division at two and seven.

According to the Associated Press, the Eagles’ playoff odds have dropped to zero point two percent. So let me be real with you. I still want this team to compete every single week. But a playoff run is not the main thing on my mind anymore. At some point, you have to ask what comes after this season. And that is exactly why I am paying close attention to Howie Roseman.

Before we get to the big picture, there are two roster moves from this week you need to know about.

First, Hollywood Brown is gone. According to ESPN sources, as reported by Yardbarker, the Eagles released Brown at his own request. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he played in four of nine games, catching five passes for sixty-three yards, after signing a one-year, five million dollar deal in March. And according to Yardbarker, the Eagles pointed to the expected returns of DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks, and the improved play of Darius Cooper and Makai Lemon. And according to Yardbarker, all five of Brown’s catches this season moved the chains. Back in October, he talked about how he was handling the limited playing time, and he said, quote, when I was younger, I might’ve reacted differently. End quote. That is a veteran handling a tough situation the right way, and I wish him the best wherever he lands next.

Second, according to Ian Rapoport of NFL Media, as reported by Pro Football Talk, the Eagles are also releasing veteran offensive tackle Fred Johnson. According to PFT, Johnson played in seven games this season, with twenty-four offensive snaps and twenty-two special teams snaps. He first joined the Eagles in 2023 and has played fifty-eight games for the team with fourteen starts. He re-signed on a one-year deal this offseason. And according to PFT, it is another move in what has been a complete overhaul of the offensive line this season. Remember, Lane Johnson retired in October, and Cam Jurgens was traded to Baltimore. So the line that helped this team win a Super Bowl looks very different now, and the Eagles are clearly making room for younger players to show what they can do.

Now, why do those two moves matter? Because they tell you what this front office is doing right now. They are clearing out veterans on one-year deals who are not part of the future, and they are giving those snaps and roster spots to players who might be. And with Jaydon Blue waived earlier in the week, Saquon Barkley is still the only running back on the active roster, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia. So now the Eagles have room to add help where they really need it.

Now, let me get to the good news. And it starts with three trades.

The first one is the most recent. The Eagles sent Jordan Davis to Jacksonville. I know a lot of fans hated seeing that. He was a first-round pick, a captain, and a Super Bowl champion. You do not erase that just because a player gets traded. But according to the team, Jacksonville gave up a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick. For a player the Eagles drafted in the first round, getting a first and another premium pick back is a serious return. And according to the Inquirer, ESPN’s Seth Walder graded the trade an A for Philadelphia.

And here is the interesting part. According to NFL insider Jordan Schultz, as reported by Heavy, the Jaguars first asked about Moro Ojomo and the Eagles said no. So Jacksonville wanted a defensive tackle badly enough to pay a price Philadelphia could not ignore. Maybe that helps the Jaguars right now. Maybe the Eagles will miss Davis inside. Both things can be true. But when I look at that return, I see two more chances to build the next great Eagles team.

The second trade was Cam Jurgens. He was a second-round pick who became a two-time Pro Bowl center and part of a championship offensive line. But according to reporting around the deal, the Eagles decided to move on. According to ESPN’s Adam Schefter, as reported by Newsweek, Baltimore sent a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 fifth-round pick, with a 2027 seventh-round pick also part of the deal.

And Jurgens has been playing well in Baltimore. According to the Ravens’ official website, he has played well as their starter over the past two games. The Ravens also traded for Atlanta center Ryan Neuzil at the deadline, which made some fans wonder what that meant for Jurgens. But according to the team, Neuzil was added as well-rounded depth on the offensive line. For the Eagles, the bigger point is simple. Roseman got a high pick for a situation that was not working the way everyone hoped.

And the third trade was A.J. Brown. Let me be fair to Brown, because he was one of the best receivers this franchise has ever had. The Eagles gave Tennessee a first and a third for him, and he delivered huge plays, thousand-yard seasons and a championship. Losing him was not easy. But according to Boston.com, the Patriots acquired Brown in June, sending the Eagles a 2028 first-round pick and a 2027 fifth-round pick.

And his time in New England has been difficult so far. According to the Associated Press, Brown left his Patriots debut with a high ankle sprain and was placed on injured reserve. And according to Boston.com, he later missed a game because of a knee issue. According to Boston.com, he has appeared in just three of the Patriots’ nine games. Patriots executive Eliot Wolf said the team knew about a, quote, degenerative knee condition, end quote, before the trade. And ESPN’s Adam Schefter said on Get Up, quote, it’s a knee that I think raises some concerns as to how long he’s gonna keep playing here in New England. End quote.

Now, I want to be careful here. I am not going to speculate about A.J. Brown’s health. I wish him nothing but the best, and nobody could have known exactly how things would play out. But from a pure team-building perspective, that 2028 first-round pick looks very valuable for Philadelphia right now.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you think the A.J. Brown trade was the best of the three. Comment number two if you think it was the Jordan Davis trade.

Now, when you put those three trades together, the whole picture starts to make sense. Roseman has not just been clearing names off the roster. He has been getting real value back. And look, I know draft picks do not get anybody excited on a Sunday afternoon. But they are how you replace players, fix weak spots, and keep a team from staying down for too long.

So let me break down exactly what the Eagles have.

In 2027, the Eagles have their own first-round pick, Jacksonville’s first-round pick, and their own second-round pick. That is three picks in the first two rounds of one draft.

In 2028, the Eagles have their own first and second, New England’s first from the A.J. Brown trade, and Baltimore’s second from the Cam Jurgens trade. That is four more.

And in 2029, the Eagles have their own first and second, plus Jacksonville’s second from the Davis trade. That is another three.

Add it up. Five first-round picks, five second-round picks, and ten premium selections over three drafts. According to Larry Rupp of Eagles On SI, that is ten picks in the first two rounds of the next three drafts. And according to the Inquirer, the Eagles are projected to have twenty-seven total picks in that span.

Eagles Nation, that is a lot of chances to get better.

And the Eagles do not have to use every pick exactly where it lands. Jacksonville is six and two right now, so that 2027 first-round pick could fall late in the round. But late first-round picks still matter. You can find a very good player there. You can package picks to move up. And you can spread those picks across several positions instead of expecting one rookie to solve everything. That is the freedom Howie has given himself.

And here is the other reason those picks matter so much. According to the Associated Press, Tankathon currently projects the Eagles with the number five pick in the 2027 draft. Nothing is locked in, and I am not rooting for losses. But if this season keeps going the way it has, Philadelphia could be picking very early. And picking near the top also helps in the second round, the third round and beyond. That can turn one terrible season into several chances to add players who can really help.

And this is just my opinion, not a report, but the draft itself is changing too. With college players now able to earn money through name, image and likeness deals, some players who might have entered the NFL as mid-round or late-round picks can choose to stay in school longer. That could make some draft classes a little thinner than we are used to. And it is one more reason I love having strong picks near the top.

Now, here is where Howie Roseman’s track record becomes so important. Because we have seen this movie before.

Remember what happened after Chip Kelly was fired at the end of 2015? Roseman got control of personnel back in 2016, and Philadelphia started building something special. In that 2016 draft, the Eagles picked second overall. And the very next season, they won Super Bowl fifty-two under Doug Pederson.

Then in 2021, the Eagles drafted in the top ten again. And one season later, in 2022, they reached another Super Bowl, this time under Nick Sirianni.

And after taking Jalen Carter in the top ten in 2023, the Eagles went on to win Super Bowl fifty-nine after the 2024 season.

Now, early picks do not guarantee a Super Bowl. But those examples show why one terrible year does not have to define this franchise for long. Pederson won a Super Bowl in his second season. Sirianni reached the Super Bowl in his second season and won it in his fourth. We have seen Philadelphia recover quickly when the coaching, the drafting and the roster decisions all come together.

And look at who came out of those drafts.

In 2016, Carson Wentz was the second overall pick. He tore his ACL late in the 2017 season, before the playoffs, but his play helped put the Eagles in position to win it all. Isaac Seumalo, a third-round pick, became an important guard for years. Halapoulivaati Vaitai, a fifth-round pick, stepped in at left tackle when Jason Peters went down in 2017. Wendell Smallwood gave the team running back depth. And Jalen Mills, a seventh-round pick, was a starter in that championship secondary. They were not all stars. But they were players with jobs to do, and together, they mattered.

The 2021 class tells a similar story. DeVonta Smith came in as a top-ten pick and became a major part of this offense. Landon Dickerson, a second-round pick, became one of the best guards in the league. Milton Williams, a third-round pick, became a big piece of the defensive front and made huge plays against Kansas City in Super Bowl fifty-nine. And Kenneth Gainwell, a fifth-round pick, gave the Eagles reliable work behind the starters.

That is what I want from these future drafts. Not just one exciting name on the first night, but a group of players who can make the whole roster better.

And then there is 2023. Jalen Carter went ninth overall, and Nolan Smith came off the board at number thirty. Smith became part of the pass rush that got after Patrick Mahomes in Super Bowl fifty-nine. Tyler Steen, a third-round pick, developed into a starting guard. Sydney Brown, a third-round pick, and Kelee Ringo, a fourth-round pick, both contributed. And then there is the seventh-round pick from that class, Moro Ojomo, who has grown into a serious presence inside.

And that is exactly why you do not have to draft in the top ten to find somebody who can really help your team. Look at the defensive line right now. With Jordan Davis gone, the Eagles kept Ojomo, a former seventh-round pick. According to Eagles On SI, since the start of 2025, Ojomo has sixty-two tackles, six sacks and ten tackles for loss in twenty-six games. And according to PhillyVoice, he is about to get paid. That is what happens when you draft well across several rounds. You have options when a starter leaves, when somebody struggles, or when a trade offer becomes too good to pass up.
