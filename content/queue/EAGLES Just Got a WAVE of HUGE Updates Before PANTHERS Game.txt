Update number one, and the biggest one. Jalen Hurts is on injured reserve.

On Saturday, the Eagles officially placed Hurts on IR because of the superficial blood clot in his calf. That means he has to miss at least the next four games. According to the Philadelphia Inquirer, the earliest he can return is November twenty-second, against the Pittsburgh Steelers. And I want to be very careful here, because the team has not set a firm timeline. Earliest possible return does not mean expected return. It just means that is the first date the rules allow.

Let me quickly remind you how we got here, using only what the team has said. Hurts was cleated in the calf during the loss to the Rams on October fourth. He asked for treatment on October eighth. In London, out of an abundance of caution, an ultrasound found a superficial blood clot, and the medical staff concluded he could safely play. He played the whole game against Jacksonville. Then a follow-up ultrasound on Wednesday night showed movement in that small clot. On Thursday, he was ruled out for today. And on Saturday, he went on injured reserve.

Now, I have seen a lot of numbers flying around the internet this week. Forty-five days. Three months. The whole season. So let me be straight with you. Those numbers are not from the Eagles, and they are not from anyone treating Jalen Hurts. Some doctors have talked in general terms about how these situations can work, and the big question they keep coming back to is the treatment plan, and whether blood thinners are needed. The Eagles have not said what the treatment plan is. As of Saturday, according to the Inquirer, the team was still determining the appropriate course of treatment. So nobody outside that building knows how long this will take. And on this channel, I am not going to pretend I do.

What we do know is what Nick Sirianni said on Friday. Quote, Jalen’s health is of the utmost importance to all of us in this building, we care deeply about Jalen. End quote. And that is exactly where the focus should be. Football comes second. Every Eagles fan should be wishing Jalen a full recovery today, no matter what you thought about the offense two weeks ago.

And I want to make one more point about the IR move, because some people are reading it as bad news. I actually see it as the team being smart and honest. Rapoport had already reported that Tanner McKee would start until Hurts’ medical situation is solved. Putting him on IR now just makes it official, opens up a roster spot, and takes the week-to-week pressure off Hurts. He can focus on his health without everybody asking every Wednesday if he will play on Sunday.

Update number two. The Eagles signed a new quarterback, and he came from the Cowboys.

On Friday evening, the Eagles signed Joe Milton off the Dallas Cowboys practice squad and put him on the fifty-three-man roster. And yes, Eagles Nation, you heard that right. The Cowboys. And guess who the Eagles play next week, according to Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice? The Dallas Cowboys. More on that in a second.

So who is Joe Milton? He is twenty-six years old, listed at six foot five and two hundred thirty-six pounds, and he has one of the strongest arms you will ever see. He spent six seasons in college at Michigan and Tennessee. The New England Patriots drafted him in the sixth round in 2024. He played one game for them, the Week 18 finale against Buffalo, and he was impressive. Twenty-two of twenty-nine for two hundred forty-one yards, a passing touchdown, no interceptions, and a rushing touchdown. A few months later, the Patriots traded him to Dallas along with a seventh-round pick for a fifth-round pick. Last season with the Cowboys, he played in four games, completing fifteen of twenty-four for one hundred eighty-three yards, one touchdown and two interceptions, plus five carries for fifty yards.

Now, let me give you Kempski’s honest take, because I think it is important. He said Milton has elite physical traits, but his game is still raw, with scouting reports pointing to inconsistent accuracy and timing. And he made a really interesting point. The Eagles chose this developmental option over bringing back Andy Dalton, the veteran who spent the offseason with the team. Kempski read that as a possible sign about how the team views this season. And he also mentioned a smaller factor. Milton just came from Dallas, and the Eagles play Dallas next week. So he might be able to share a little bit of information about how the Cowboys do things. I will let you decide how much that matters.

So the quarterback room today looks like this. Tanner McKee starts. Rookie Cole Payton, a fifth-round pick, is the backup. And Joe Milton is the new arm in the room, getting up to speed as fast as he can.

And let me remind you why I feel good about McKee today. In Week 18 of the 2024 season, his first career start, he went into a game against the Giants and threw for two hundred sixty-nine yards and two touchdowns with no turnovers in a twenty to thirteen win. In his career, through last season, he has five touchdown passes and just one interception. He is six foot six, he has been in this offense for four seasons, and he has a Super Bowl ring from the Fifty-Nine team. This week he told Eagles.com, quote, At any moment, you have to be ready to play on a Sunday. End quote. Today is that Sunday. The difference is that this is not a Week 18 finale. This is a meaningful October game with a losing streak on the line. That is the biggest test of his career.

Update number three. The running back room got another reshuffle.

On Saturday, the Eagles promoted running back Jaydon Blue from the practice squad to the active roster, and he is expected to make his Eagles debut today. That move also served as the corresponding move for Hurts going on IR. This week, Will Shipley was also placed on injured reserve with his foot injury. And Ian Wheeler, the MVP of the 2026 UFL United Bowl, was signed to the practice squad on Thursday.

So for today, with Saquon Barkley officially out with his hamstring, the backfield should be Dameon Pierce, Carson Steele and Jaydon Blue. Three young backs who need to give Tanner McKee a running game, because the backs ran sixteen times for twenty yards in London. That cannot happen again today. Not with a backup quarterback. A good running game is a backup quarterback’s best friend. It keeps him in manageable third downs, it sets up play-action, and it keeps that Carolina pass rush honest.

The Eagles elevated outside linebacker Keyshawn James-Newby and safety Cole Wisniewski from the practice squad. And now, the good news I promised you. Update number six. DeVonta Smith is playing.

Smith came off the injury report on Friday and will play today after missing the last two games with his hamstring. Eagles Nation, that is huge. When you have a backup quarterback making the biggest start of his career, the best gift you can give him is a receiver who runs perfect routes and catches everything thrown his way. DeVonta Smith is that guy. I expect McKee to look for him early and often.

And do not underestimate what Smith’s return means for everyone else on this offense.
