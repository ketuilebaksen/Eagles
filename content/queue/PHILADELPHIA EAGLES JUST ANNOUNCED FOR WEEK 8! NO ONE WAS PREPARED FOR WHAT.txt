Eagles Nation, the Week 8 announcements are in, and after a twenty-seven to ten loss to the Washington Commanders, nobody in Philadelphia was ready for what Nick Sirianni had to say. Let me be clear about what this is. Nobody was fired, nobody was traded, and there was no surprise signing. What happened is that the head coach of the Philadelphia Eagles stood at that podium on Sunday night and said things we have never heard him say in six seasons. He said the struggles are, in his words, one hundred percent on him. He said, I got to try a different approach. He said everything is on the table. And then on Monday, he made two announcements that a lot of fans did not want to hear. He is not changing his offensive coordinator, and he is not changing his quarterback.

This is James with Second Floor, and today we are going inside that press conference, line by line. The fourth down decision that went sideways, the question about Sean Mannion’s job, what a different approach really means, the truth about Saquon Barkley’s snap count, and the Monday announcements that set the tone for the Giants game and the trade deadline. Before we start, if you are still riding with this team, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor.

Let me set the scene first. The Eagles went up ten to nothing in the first quarter. Saquon Barkley scored on a direct snap. And then the offense never scored again. Washington scored the next twenty-seven points. Jake Elliott missed his first kick of the season. Two drives ended in turnovers. That is six losses in a row, two and six on the season, and last place in the NFC East. The first six-game losing streak for this franchise since 2012.

And the very first question Sirianni got was about one sequence that summed up the whole night.

The Eagles were trailing sixteen to ten and driving. It was fourth and four, and Sirianni wanted to go for it. Here is what he said. He liked going for it on fourth and four, because, in his words, we were moving the ball well on that drive, and he thought it might be their best chance to get a touchdown, because, quote, we were going to need a touchdown. End quote. Then a false start pushed them back to fourth and nine. And Sirianni said, quote, as soon as it went to fourth and nine, obviously my mindset changed. End quote. He also mentioned an illegal formation on the drive, and in the end, the drive produced no points.

Think about that, Eagles Nation. A team that is fighting for its season, down six, in a perfect spot to take the lead, and it falls apart because of a pre-snap penalty. That is not bad luck. That is detail. And that is exactly the word Sirianni kept coming back to all night.

Here is the part of the press conference that everyone is talking about. Sirianni said, quote, until we coach better, until we have better, you know, with our detail as coaches, until our players play with better detail, then we’re going to see the same results. End quote. And then he said, quote, and that falls on me. End quote. He went on. Quote, with all the detail that’s not happening right now, I got to try a different approach. End quote. And then he said it was, in his words, one hundred percent on him.

I have watched a lot of Nick Sirianni press conferences. He takes responsibility a lot. But this felt different. This was not a coach giving the usual line. This was a coach who sounded like he was hurting.

And he kept going. When a reporter brought up the false starts, Sirianni said, quote, I take everything as my responsibility that happens out in that field, everything. End quote. And then, quote, obviously I’m not doing a good enough job right now in that case, and I got to be better. End quote.

If you are new to Second Floor, welcome. Drop a comment right now. Do you believe Nick Sirianni can turn this around, yes or no? I want to see where this fan base is today.

Now, let us get to the question that made the room go quiet.

A reporter pointed out that the Eagles scored ten points against a team that had been giving up around twenty-eight points a game. He pointed out that there is a new offensive coordinator and a new system, and that it does not seem to be working. And then he asked directly. Will Sean Mannion keep his job as offensive coordinator, and will he keep calling plays?

Sirianni did not answer the question. He said, quote, I’m not thinking about any of that stuff right now. End quote. He said, quote, I got a bunch of guys in that locker room that are hurting, every player, every coach. End quote. And he finished with, quote, I got a lot of faith in everybody in that locker room. I got a lot of faith in the coaches in that locker room. I’ll keep it at that. End quote.

And to understand why that question was asked, you need to know who Sean Mannion is. He is in his first year calling plays for the Eagles. Before coaching, he was a quarterback in the NFL. According to the Inquirer, Mannion played in fourteen NFL games and started three. He has been around the game his whole life, and Sirianni clearly trusts him. But this is his first time running an offense at this level, and he lost his starting quarterback to a season-ending health issue in October. That is a brutal situation for any coordinator, let alone one in his first season.

That answer made a lot of noise. NBC Sports Philadelphia reported that Sirianni would not commit to Mannion as the play caller on Sunday night. And by Monday morning, Inquirer columnist Marcus Hayes had written that it was time to fire Mannion. Hayes wrote, quote, Mannion cannot be allowed to continue in this role. End quote. He criticized the fourth down play call, saying it left out both Dallas Goedert and Saquon Barkley, and he called it, quote, coaching malpractice. End quote. That is his opinion, and it is a strong one. But it tells you how loud the pressure has become.

And then came Monday. And here is the first announcement. According to Dave Zangaro of NBC Sports Philadelphia, Sirianni shut the door on an offensive coordinator change. He said, quote, I’m not making any changes there. End quote. He said, quote, it is on all of us to figure out how to get things going in the right direction. End quote. And he said, quote, that brunt falls on myself, that falls on Sean. End quote.

And the second announcement. Tanner McKee stays at quarterback. Sirianni said, quote, Tanner’s our guy. We’re not making any changes there. End quote. He said, quote, how do we find solutions? That could be within scheme, that could be within what our schedule is, practice plays. End quote. And then, quote, everything is on the table for us in that aspect. End quote.

So that is what the Eagles announced after Week 8. Same coordinator. Same quarterback. Different approach. And the question every fan is asking is simple. If the people stay the same, what exactly is going to change?

Sirianni tried to answer that on Sunday night. When a reporter asked what a different approach could mean, he said, quote, sometimes it’s just your delivery. End quote. And then he said, quote, if my delivery needs to be even harsher, then it needs to be harsher. End quote. He said, quote, I’m open to try anything to help us. End quote. And he said, in his words, everything’s on the table there.

Eagles Nation, I want you to think about what that means. Nick Sirianni has always been an energy coach. He is emotional, he is fiery, and his players have always responded to that. But he is now saying that maybe the message needs to be delivered in a harder way. Maybe practices need to be tougher. Maybe the accountability for false starts and illegal formations needs to be stronger. That is a coach admitting that the old way is not getting through right now.

And Sirianni explained why he still trusts his staff. When a reporter asked where his faith in his coaches comes from, especially with Mannion in his first year calling plays, he said, quote, we spend a lot more time together than what you guys get to see on a weekly basis. End quote. He said, quote, I’ve been around coaches my entire life. I know what good coaches look like. And I know I got good coaches on that staff. End quote. And he said it is, in his words, a daily evaluation.

That last part matters. Daily evaluation. He is not saying nothing will ever change. He is saying he is watching every day.

Now, let us talk about Saquon Barkley, because one of the most important answers of the night was about him.

A reporter said it looked like Saquon was on a snap count. Saquon missed practice on Wednesday and Thursday with hamstring and back injuries, and he only returned on Friday. He finished the game with thirty yards on thirteen carries, according to Pro Football Talk. Sirianni did not deny a plan to manage him. He said, quote, we’re seeing where the numbers are. End quote. He said they have, quote, plays in for him that are specifically Saquon plays on the game plan, end quote, to make sure he gets touches and affects the game. And he said, quote, I thought he ran hard and ran well and was productive. End quote. But he admitted, quote, we didn’t get the explosive one that we’re so accustomed to seeing with him. End quote.

And there was one more detail. At the end of the first half, the Eagles handed the ball to Saquon. A lot of fans thought they were just running out the clock. Sirianni said no. He said they were trying to see if Saquon could, in his words, bust one there. They had two timeouts. If he broke a long run, they could try a long field goal or throw a jump ball. He said, quote, he’s the guy that can do that. End quote.

Here is my take on that, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I understand why the Eagles want Saquon on the field. He is still the most dangerous player on this offense. But a planned snap count tells you he is not one hundred percent. And with the bye week coming after the Giants game, I hope the Eagles think very carefully about how much they ask of him. He has given this city everything. Protecting him is part of protecting the future.

Now, here is the moment that I think every Eagles fan should hear.

A reporter asked if opponents are now enjoying the chance to beat the Eagles. Remember, Washington went for a two-point conversion late in the game to go up three scores. Sirianni said that was the right decision on their part, and he even said the Eagles have done the same thing before. And then he said this. Quote, I know nobody feels sorry for us. End quote. He said, quote, everyone’s going to give us their best shot. Everyone’s always given us their best shot. End quote. And he said, quote, it’s our job to go and do everything we can do to fix it. End quote.

And then he described how it feels inside that building. He said there are a lot of great competitors in that locker room, coaches and players. He called it, quote, somewhat uncharted territory for us. End quote. And he said, in his words, it sucks, and they are angry. And he finished with, quote, all we need to do is continue to fight. End quote.

That is the most honest Sirianni has ever sounded in a press conference, Eagles Nation. No spin. No excuses.

And his quarterback said the same thing. Tanner McKee called the offense’s performance frustrating and said it came down to details and execution. Quote, we need to be better. End quote. And then, quote, we have the urgency. We have the fight. End quote. When the head coach and the quarterback are saying the same thing, that tells you the message is getting through inside the building. Now it has to get through on the field.

And remember, this all comes in a week when the culture of this team was already being questioned. Nick Foles said on a podcast that there is, in his words, dysfunction in Philly. Sirianni pushed back on that, telling his players to ignore what he called that crap and pointing out that Foles is not in the building. And Jason Kelce said he does not even like the word culture, because it’s meaningless. So when Sirianni stood at that podium and said I have to try a different approach, he was answering more than one loss. He was answering a whole week of noise.

And the last answer might be the one that sticks with me the most. A reporter asked about the end of the first half, the second straight week that the Eagles had an empty possession before halftime and then gave up points. Sirianni said, quote, I don’t think we’ve been real sharp at any sequence right at this particular time. End quote. And then he said, quote, it’s not good enough and it falls on my shoulders. End quote. He listed everything. Offense, defense, special teams, fundamentals, situational football. A player making a mistake. A coach making a mistake. And he said, in his words, that is my job. And then he said, quote, I’m pissed at myself, and I know I’ll fight like hell to do everything I can do to get it fixed. End quote.

Sirianni also said he reminded the team after the game of who they are and what they have accomplished. Quote, but you know, we got to go out now and go do it. End quote. And then, quote, I believe in those guys, and I believe that we can get it turned around. End quote.

So now, let us talk about what comes next.

The Eagles face the Giants this Sunday. The Giants are four, two and one and coming off their bye. After that, the Eagles finally get their own bye week. And the trade deadline is Tuesday, November tenth, at four in the afternoon Eastern. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler has put the odds of Riq Woolen being traded at thirty-five percent and Landon Dickerson at twenty-five percent. Albert Breer of Sports Illustrated reported that the Eagles told teams Moro Ojomo is not available. And Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice wrote that DeVonta Smith isn’t going anywhere. So the Giants game is not only a chance to snap the streak. It is also the last look Howie Roseman gets before he decides whether this team buys, holds, or sells.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the people who say this is not enough. They will say Sirianni has been saying the same things for weeks, and that words do not fix false starts. They will say keeping Sean Mannion as the play caller after scoring ten points against a defense that had been giving up almost twenty-nine points a game is a mistake. They will say Marcus Hayes is right, and that Sirianni should take over the play calling himself. They will say that if the coordinator stays and the quarterback stays, nothing will really change. And they will say that six straight losses means the time for patience is over.

On the other side, the people who say stay the course. They will say firing a coordinator in the middle of the season rarely fixes anything, and that it could make the locker room even more unstable. They will say Tanner McKee is a backup quarterback doing his best without Jalen Hurts, and that changing quarterbacks again would not help. They will say Sirianni taking the blame and promising a different approach is exactly what a leader should do. And they will say this coaching staff has won a Super Bowl together, and that it deserves the chance to fix this before anyone gets fired.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I respect everything Sirianni said. He owned it. He did not blame the players. He did not blame Jalen’s injury. He put it on himself. That is what you want from your head coach. But I also think the Eagles need to show something different against the Giants, not just say something different. If Sirianni is not changing the coordinator, then the changes have to show up in the game plan, in the discipline, in the way this team handles the details. Fewer penalties. Smarter situational football. More creative ways to help Tanner McKee. If that does not happen on Sunday, then the pressure is going to grow over the bye week, and I think the conversation about play calling will come right back.

So let me wrap it up. After Week 8, the Eagles announced no firing, no trade and no new quarterback. What they announced was a new mindset. Nick Sirianni said the struggles are on him, he said he has to try a different approach, and he said everything is on the table. On Monday, he said Sean Mannion stays, and that Tanner McKee is still his guy. Saquon is being managed with a plan. The Giants are next, then the bye, and the trade deadline is November tenth. Nobody feels sorry for the Eagles, and Sirianni knows it.
