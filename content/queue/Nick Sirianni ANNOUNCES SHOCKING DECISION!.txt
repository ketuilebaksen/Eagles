A few days ago, we did a full breakdown on the Tyreek Hill rumor, his career, his injury, and the "cold weather" post that started it all. If you missed it, go check it out. Today is different. Since that video, a lot has changed. For Hill, and for the Eagles. So let's start with where the Tyreek Hill sweepstakes stands right now.

On Monday, October 5th, Hill's agent, Drew Rosenhaus, told ESPN's Adam Schefter, quote, "I would anticipate Tyreek potentially signing with a team over the next few days." He also said he'd begin negotiating with multiple teams right away. That's not a guy testing the waters anymore. That's a guy ready to pick a jersey.

And the team at the front of the line? The Kansas City Chiefs. Where it all started. According to The Athletic's Mike Silver and ESPN's Nate Taylor, the Chiefs have reached out to Rosenhaus and stayed in contact. And their need suddenly became urgent. In their Week 4 comeback win over the Raiders, receiver Tyquan Thornton suffered a dislocated ankle and is out indefinitely, according to NFL Network's Ian Rapoport, and Rashee Rice injured his hamstring. And here's the scary part for the rest of the league. Those Chiefs are 4 and 0.

Then came the post. On Sunday, right after Thornton went down, Tyreek Hill went on X and posted two letters. Quote, "hm." That's it. Just "hm." And the internet lost its mind. Combine that with "I think I'm ready for some cold weather" back on September 30th, and fans in Kansas City are already printing the jerseys.

And Patrick Mahomes was asked about it. Talking to Mike Silver, Mahomes said, quote, "I have no idea if that would happen, but it'd be pretty cool." He also called Hill, quote, "a one-of-a-kind player." And according to Silver, Chiefs players are all for bringing him back. When the best quarterback in football says it'd be pretty cool, that's not nothing.

And there's a little history there too. After the Chiefs traded Hill to Miami back in 2022, Hill made some comments that rubbed people in Kansas City the wrong way. Mahomes addressed that as well, saying, quote, "I'm surprised a little just because I felt like we love Tyreek here." So yes, there's some old baggage. But four years later, with both sides needing each other, it sounds like the door is wide open for a reunion.

And remember why this guy is worth all the noise, even now. Fifth-round pick out of West Alabama in 2016. Six seasons in Kansas City, six Pro Bowls. Traded to Miami and kept dominating. Eight Pro Bowls overall. Five first-team All-Pro selections. A spot on the NFL's All-Decade Team of the 2010s. And in 2023, he led the entire league with 1,799 receiving yards and 13 touchdowns. When he hit free agency, he had 819 catches, 11,363 yards and 83 touchdowns. That's a Hall of Fame resume. The only question is how much of that player is still left.

Other teams are floating around too. The Green Bay Packers lost Jayden Reed for the season to a neck injury, and Heavy argued they could step in if the Kansas City talks fall apart. The Buffalo Bills have been mentioned as a fit as well. But there are no confirmed reports of interest from those teams. The only team with a confirmed line of communication is Kansas City.

Now, let's remember where Hill is physically, because that's the whole story. His last NFL game was September 29th, 2025, with Miami. That's when he dislocated his left knee and tore multiple ligaments, including his ACL. He's said he nearly lost his left leg. He's been posting videos of himself running routes and catching passes, and he's targeting a return sometime in November. So whoever signs him is signing a guy who probably won't play for at least another month.

And what would a deal even look like? Honestly, nobody knows yet. There's no public number. Back in August, Rosenhaus said on The Pat McAfee Show that he and Hill hadn't even talked about money, and that the priority wasn't when Hill came back, but how. Remember, the Dolphins released him this offseason rather than carry a cap hit of around 51 million dollars, and the move freed up about 22.8 million in space, according to Adam Schefter. My guess, and this is just my opinion, not a report, is that whoever signs him will want a deal loaded with incentives. Pay him if he plays, pay him more if he produces. That protects the team if the knee doesn't hold up, and it gives Hill the chance to cash in if he's still Tyreek. That kind of structure is exactly what makes a contender like the Chiefs comfortable taking the risk.

So let's map out the scenarios. Scenario one, Hill goes back to Kansas City. A 4 and 0 team with Mahomes adds a former All-Pro for the stretch run. That's the most likely outcome based on everything we know, and it's the one that should worry the rest of the NFC the most if the Chiefs make another Super Bowl run. Scenario two, a cold-weather team like Green Bay jumps in. The Packers are 2 and 2, lost a key receiver, and fit the "cold weather" hint. Scenario three, a surprise. A team nobody's talking about makes a huge offer. And scenario four, the one Eagles fans keep refreshing their phones for. Philadelphia. Based on everything that's been reported, that one is the longest shot of all.

So that's Hill's side. Now let's look at the Eagles' side, because this is where it gets really interesting.

In our first Hill video, the Eagles' situation was simple. Two straight losses, a banged-up receiver room, and a lot of questions. Since then? This team has gone through an earthquake. On Tuesday, Lane Johnson retired. On Wednesday, Howie Roseman traded Cam Jurgens to Baltimore for a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 fifth-rounder. And with that trade, two things changed that matter for this conversation.

Number one, money. Roseman said there was money coming back in the Jurgens deal, and he talked about it directly. Quote, "being able to create some flexibility is a good situation for us." Jurgens was on a four-year, 68 million dollar contract. Moving that deal gives the Eagles room to work with.

Number two, draft capital. According to Dave Spadaro, the Eagles now hold 16 combined picks in the 2027 and 2028 drafts, not counting compensatory picks. Two first-rounders and two second-rounders in 2028 alone. And here's the beautiful part about Tyreek Hill. He's a free agent. He wouldn't cost a single one of those picks. Just money and a roster spot.

And then there's the line Howie said on Wednesday that every Eagles fan should remember. Quote, "We're trying to win this year."

So put it all together. You have a GM who says he's trying to win now. You have new cap flexibility. You have a war chest of picks you don't even need to touch. And your number one receiver, DeVonta Smith, is out with a hamstring injury and expected to miss multiple games. Hollywood Brown didn't practice Wednesday with an ankle injury. On paper, this is the perfect setup for the Eagles to jump into the Tyreek Hill race.

And yet, nothing. So why?

Let's walk through the reasons, because I think they tell us a lot about how this front office is thinking right now.

Reason number one. The real crisis isn't at receiver. It's up front. Think about what this offensive line looks like heading into London. Lane Johnson is gone. Jurgens is gone. Landon Dickerson is on injured reserve. Rookie Markel Bell is at right tackle after allowing six pressures and three sacks in his first career start against the Rams. Willie Lampkin, an undrafted guard who has never started an NFL game, is likely at left guard. And Drew Kendall, the new starting center, didn't practice on Wednesday with a knee injury. If you're Howie Roseman and you have money to spend, where does it go first? A 32-year-old receiver coming off a knee reconstruction? Or a veteran offensive lineman who can keep Jalen Hurts alive? I think we all know the answer.

Reason number two. The timeline doesn't fit the emergency. Hill is targeting November. The Eagles' receiver emergency is right now. Smith's hamstring will heal. Dallas Goedert was back at practice in a limited role on Wednesday for the first time since his MCL sprain. By the time Hill would actually be ready to play, the Eagles could have Smith, Goedert and Hollywood Brown all back. At that point, Hill wouldn't be a savior. He'd be an expensive fourth option.

Reason number three. The young receivers are actually showing something. Dontayvion Wicks, who came over in a trade from Green Bay, had 9 catches for 179 yards and a touchdown through the first three weeks. Darius Cooper has scored three touchdowns in his last two games, including the game-winner in Tennessee and two more against the Rams. Makai Lemon, the first-round rookie, is getting more and more snaps, and Landon Dickerson said he's doing a tremendous job. The Eagles invested a lot in these guys. A future Hall of Famer walking into the room might shrink their roles right when they're starting to grow.

Reason number four. Fit and culture. We talked about this in the first video. Hill has had a series of off-field incidents throughout his career, and back in 2022, when the Chiefs traded him, Inside the Birds reported that the Eagles didn't show interest then either, with those concerns being part of the reason. Nick Sirianni and Howie Roseman have built a very specific culture in that building. And right now, after a week like this one, the locker room needs stability, not a circus.

And reason number five. The Chiefs. Honestly, if Hill wants to go back to Kansas City, to Mahomes, to a 4 and 0 team, to the place where he won a Super Bowl, there's not much the Eagles could do to change that. Why get into a bidding war you're probably going to lose, just to drive up the price for a rival contender? Howie Roseman is famous for picking his spots. He jumps when the value is there, like he did with the Jurgens trade. He doesn't chase headlines just because the fans are loud.

Now, let me flip it. Because there's a real argument that the Eagles are making a mistake by sitting this one out. And I want to give it its strongest version.

Look at the passing game. Two weeks ago, Jalen Hurts threw for 153 yards in Chicago. Then he threw for 93 against the Rams. Ninety-three. The Eagles went zero for twelve on third down. Two for twenty-one over two games. There's nobody on this roster right now who scares a defense deep. And Tyreek Hill, even at 80 percent, changes how defenses line up. Safeties back up. Linebackers get nervous. Space opens up underneath. That's exactly what this offense is missing.

And think about the long game. If the Eagles get healthy and make the playoffs, January football is decided by explosive plays. Hill has 28 career touchdowns of 50 yards or more, which according to CBS Sports ranks fourth in NFL history. Imagine that kind of weapon next to DeVonta Smith and Saquon Barkley in a playoff game. That's a scary thought for the rest of the NFC.

And here's the part that stings the most for some fans. If the Chiefs get him, they get stronger. And if the Eagles end up meeting Kansas City in the Super Bowl again, Eagles Nation might be watching Tyreek Hill burn their secondary while thinking, "we could have made that call."

So, who's right? Let me tell you where I land, and you can fight me in the comments.

I still say the Eagles are right to stay out of the Tyreek Hill race. And honestly, this week made me even more sure of it. A week ago, you could at least make the case that receiver was this team's biggest problem. After Lane Johnson's retirement and the Jurgens trade, that's just not true anymore. The offensive line is the emergency. Every dollar and every bit of energy Howie has should go toward protecting Jalen Hurts first.

But here's the twist, and this is the part I really want you to hear. The Jurgens trade didn't just create flexibility. It created expectations. Howie said, "We're trying to win this year." He now has 16 picks over two drafts and fresh cap room. So if he's passing on Tyreek Hill, fine. I get it. But that means the pressure to make a different move just went way up. The November 10th trade deadline is now the biggest date on the Eagles' calendar. If Howie walks out of that deadline without adding a real veteran offensive lineman and maybe another weapon for Hurts, then all of this flexibility was just talk.

So what should that move look like? Here's my wish list, and again, this is opinion, not a report. First, a veteran tackle with real starting experience. Not necessarily a superstar. Just a steady, proven guy who can either take over at right tackle or give Markel Bell a safety net while he develops. Second, interior depth. This week proved how fast things can fall apart. Lane retires, Jurgens gets traded, Kendall's knee acts up, and suddenly you're one injury away from a practice squad guy snapping the ball. You can never have too many linemen who've actually played on Sundays. And third, only if the price is right, a receiver who can win deep. Not a 32-year-old coming off a knee reconstruction who won't play until November, but a guy who can help now. If Howie checks those boxes by November 10th, nobody in Philadelphia will care that Tyreek Hill went somewhere else.

So passing on Tyreek Hill isn't a decision to do nothing. At least, it better not be. It's a decision to do something smarter. And we're going to hold Howie to that.
