Eagles Nation, you have probably seen the headlines saying the Eagles are trying to push Jalen Hurts out through the back door. So let me be clear right from the start. There is no report that the Eagles are trying to get rid of Jalen Hurts. Nobody credible has reported that Howie Roseman is shopping him, that a replacement has been picked, or that the team wants him gone. But there is a real conversation happening in Philadelphia right now about Jalen’s future. With the Eagles at two and six and possibly picking near the top of the 2027 draft, columnists are asking the big question. Are the Eagles still committed to Jalen Hurts? So today, we are going to separate what is real from what is noise.

This is James with Second Floor, and here is what we are covering. Where this conversation is coming from, what Howie Roseman and Jeffrey Lurie have actually said about Jalen, what his contract really looks like, the history that makes fans nervous, the draft picks the Eagles could use, and why Jalen’s health has to stay separate from all of this. Before we start, if you are still riding with this team, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor.

Let me start with Jalen himself, because his health comes first.

Jalen is out for the rest of the season after doctors found blood clots in his lungs. In a joint statement, the Eagles and his agent, Nicole Lynn, said, quote, Jalen’s prognosis is very good, and we expect a full recovery. End quote. They also said they anticipate, quote, no long-term effects. End quote. And this week, according to ESPN’s Tim McManus, Jalen visited the team facility and sat in on meetings, and his presence, quote, did provide a morale boost for some. End quote. Nick Sirianni said, quote, I miss him like crazy. End quote. And Donovan McNabb said he had talked to Jalen, and that, quote, he’s doing well. He’s in good spirits. End quote.

And I want to say this clearly. Jalen’s health situation cannot become a shortcut in this conversation. The expectation is a full recovery. That does not give anybody permission to announce the end of his time in Philadelphia. And if people have questions about how the situation was handled, the NFL and the players union have been reviewing it. Those questions deserve answers on their own. But they are not proof that Howie Roseman is trying to engineer an exit.

So where is this conversation coming from?

On November third, Inquirer columnist David Murphy wrote that the Eagles have a realistic shot at the number one overall pick in the 2027 draft, and that they are, quote, a very strong bet to be drafting in the top five. End quote. And then he asked the question that started this whole debate. Quote, are the Eagles committed to Jalen Hurts as their quarterback next season, and for the foreseeable future? End quote. He wrote, quote, it all starts with Jalen Hurts. He is the key variable. End quote. Murphy mentioned college quarterbacks like Arch Manning, CJ Carr and Drew Mestemaker. And he argued that the Eagles must make that call now instead of avoiding it.

That is a columnist asking a question. It is not a report that the Eagles have made a decision. But it is a question a lot of people in Philadelphia are asking.

And this conversation did not start this week. Back in April, according to Inside The Iggles, the Inquirer’s Jeff McLane reported that the Eagles had no plans to extend Jalen this offseason, per NFL sources. And an Eagles source told McLane, quote, he knows this is the last year of his guaranteed money. End quote. That line got a lot of attention. But remember, that same month, Howie Roseman stood at the podium and defended his quarterback.

And there is one more quarterback story this week. According to Gridiron Heroics, citing Albert Breer of Sports Illustrated, Tanner McKee is among the players other teams perceive to be available before the trade deadline. With Joe Milton now starting, the Eagles’ quarterback room could look very different by Tuesday afternoon.

Now, let me show you what Howie Roseman has actually said, because his words belong in this conversation.

Back in April, after an ESPN story criticized Jalen’s role in the offense, Roseman spoke at his pre-draft press conference. According to Pro Football Talk, he said, quote, if we have any issue with any of our players, we talk to them directly. End quote. He said it was, quote, unfair to have these articles written, end quote, though he understood that they, quote, sell. End quote. And he called the situation, quote, unfortunate. End quote. He even admitted that one of the team’s faults in the past had been not being direct enough with players, and he said that from his perspective, Nick’s perspective and Jeffrey’s perspective, the Eagles are not hiding behind anything.

And then in September, before the season, Roseman went on the WIP Morning Show. According to Athlon Sports, he was asked whether 2026 was make or break for Jalen. He said, quote, no, I don’t agree with that. End quote. He said the focus is, quote, winning football games, end quote, and then he added, quote, and all the guy does is win football games. End quote. He called Jalen, quote, another guy with a tremendous track record. End quote.

And Jeffrey Lurie? At the league meetings this spring, he was asked about Jalen’s future. He said the team never discusses contract negotiations. But then he said this. He said there is no bigger fan of Jalen than him. He talked about Jalen’s clutch gene, and he said Jalen was the MVP of the Super Bowl and should have been the MVP of the Super Bowl before that, against Kansas City the first time. He called Jalen exceptional and dedicated, and he said, in his words, I love everything about him.

Now, does that guarantee Jalen stays forever? No. Ownership can believe in a player and still evaluate what comes next. But we cannot decide that every supportive answer is secretly a lie. If criticism is proof they want him out, and praise is proof they are covering it up, then nothing anyone says will ever matter.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you trust what Howie and Lurie have said about Jalen. Comment number two if you think actions will tell a different story.

Now, here is why some fans take this possibility seriously. Howie Roseman’s history.

In 2020, the Eagles drafted Jalen Hurts in the second round, fifty-third overall, while Carson Wentz was their franchise quarterback on a long-term contract. At the time, the team called it an investment in the quarterback room. That investment became the starter. So Jalen knows better than anybody that having the job and having a contract does not stop this organization from adding somebody behind you.

And Howie has shown this year that he will move big names. On June first, according to FOX 29, the Eagles traded A.J. Brown to the New England Patriots for a 2028 first-round pick and a 2027 fifth-round pick. And on October seventh, they traded center Cam Jurgens to the Ravens for a 2027 fifth-round pick and a 2028 second-round pick. So yes, Howie is willing to make tough decisions. That history is real.

But that history does not tell us when he will make another change, or whether the next quarterback would actually be better. The Eagles’ willingness to take a risk is established. The wisdom of the next risk still has to be proven.

Now, let us talk about the money, because this is where the conversation gets serious.

Jalen signed a five-year, two hundred fifty-five million dollar extension in 2023. According to Front Office Sports, citing Spotrac, twenty-two million dollars of his 2027 pay is already fully guaranteed. According to Eagles On SI, his 2027 cap hit is about forty-two million dollars. And according to Inside The Iggles, a trade in 2027 would carry a dead cap hit of about sixty-seven million dollars. The real decision point is 2028, when he has no guaranteed money left. And Jalen has a no-trade clause, which means the Eagles cannot simply pick a destination and send him there without his agreement.

So before anybody tells you a rookie quarterback would save the Eagles money, ask what the whole transition would cost. The old quarterback’s dead money. The new quarterback’s contract. The picks you might spend moving up in the draft. And the players you cannot add because those resources went somewhere else. Cheaper at quarterback and better as a team are not the same thing.

And remember, the Eagles could already lead the NFL in dead cap money in 2027, according to the Inquirer. Adding a huge quarterback dead money hit on top of that would make building a winner even harder.

And there is a lesson in Eagles history here too. According to Eagles On SI, a sixty-seven million dollar dead cap hit would be about double what the team absorbed when it moved on from Carson Wentz in 2021. The Eagles came out of that one, but it took a lot of work and a lot of patience. Doing it again, with an even bigger number, is not something any general manager takes lightly.

And Nick Faria of Eagles On SI made a strong point back in October. He wrote that, quote, there’s no reason to move on from Hurts just yet. End quote. He said Jalen, quote, doesn’t turn the ball over, and he finds ways to win games. End quote. And he argued that trading for a veteran or drafting a rookie would not guarantee better results. That is his opinion. But it is a reminder that not every voice in Philadelphia is calling for a change.

Now, the draft picks. The Eagles have their own 2027 first-round pick, which could be in the top five. They also have the Patriots’ 2028 first-round pick from the A.J. Brown trade, and the Ravens’ 2028 second-round pick from the Jurgens trade. Those assets give Howie options. Could he use them to move up for a quarterback? Absolutely. But a college quarterback is still a projection. You would be comparing someone you hope can handle NFL pressure with someone you have already watched win the biggest game on the biggest stage.

And remember what Jalen did on that stage. In Super Bowl fifty-nine against Kansas City, he threw for two hundred twenty-one yards and two touchdowns, ran for seventy-two yards and another touchdown, and was named Super Bowl MVP. Before this season, according to Athlon Sports, he had a fifty-seven and twenty-five record as a starter. In 2025, he threw for three thousand two hundred twenty-four yards, twenty-five touchdowns and six interceptions, and ran for eight more touchdowns.

Yes, he had help. That was the point of building that team, and that is exactly how championships are won. If an offense can win a championship by leaning on its quarterback, its running back and its offensive line, why is that evidence you need a different quarterback? That sounds like evidence you found something worth building around.

Now, the fair criticism. Jalen has been the one constant through the coaching changes. That means his part needs a hard evaluation. Back in the spring, an ESPN story raised questions about his willingness to adapt the offense. And in June, Jalen answered that publicly. He said he is always open to growth and always open to improving. He talked about how the team spends its time and what it reps, and said he was looking forward to building on the foundation they had started to lay. Saying you are willing to grow does not prove you fixed everything. The film has to show it. But he deserves to be judged on that film, not on headlines.

And think about this season. With Jalen out, the Eagles have started Tanner McKee and now Joe Milton. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, through eight games, the Eagles rank thirtieth in points and thirty-second in total yards, and they have allowed a free rusher on eighteen point two percent of pass plays, the most in the league. Lane Johnson retired. Cam Jurgens was traded. The offensive line has been rebuilt. Those problems do not go away by changing the quarterback. In fact, a young quarterback walking into that situation could struggle even more.

And today’s game matters in this conversation too. Joe Milton makes the first start of his NFL career against the Giants this afternoon at Lincoln Financial Field. According to the Inquirer, the Eagles are home underdogs for the first time since 2009. If Milton plays well, some people will say the Eagles can find answers at quarterback without Jalen. If he struggles, others will say this season proves how much the team misses its leader. Either way, the quarterback position is going to be the biggest storyline in Philadelphia for the next several months.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the people who think the Eagles should consider moving on. They will say the Eagles did not extend Jalen this past offseason, and that 2028 has no guaranteed money, which gives the team a way out. They will say the Eagles could pick in the top five, and that a young quarterback on a rookie deal would give Howie more money to rebuild the roster. They will say Howie has already traded A.J. Brown and Cam Jurgens, so nobody is untouchable. And they will say the passing game has not been good enough, even before Jalen got hurt.

On the other side, the people who say Jalen is still the guy. They will say Howie and Lurie have both defended him publicly. They will say he is a Super Bowl MVP with a winning record and a reputation for protecting the football. They will say his dead money in 2027 would be enormous and his no-trade clause limits the options. They will say the problems this season are bigger than any one player, from the offensive line to the play-calling. And they will say his health comes first, and that nobody should be writing him off while he is recovering.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I do not think a Super Bowl ring makes Jalen untouchable in Philadelphia. Howie Roseman has shown that much. But I am not convinced that Howie has already decided to move on. And I believe that replacing Jalen should require a lot more than frustration with a bad season. If the Eagles want to replace their quarterback, they need to prove that changing him would fix problems that changing the coaching, the protection or the play design would not. Otherwise, they could spend major resources replacing the most visible player, and find themselves arguing about the same offense a year later.

What would change my mind? An actual pursuit of a new starting quarterback. A contract decision that clearly changes the path. Or a real explanation of why another quarterback gives this team a better chance. Until then, this is a debate, not a back door.

And here is what I will be watching over the next few months. First, the trade deadline on Tuesday. If the Eagles move Tanner McKee, it tells you they are already thinking about how the quarterback room will look next year. Second, where the Eagles end up in the draft order. A top-five pick will make the quarterback conversation louder, no matter what anyone says. Third, Jalen’s recovery. I am not going to put a timeline on it, and nobody outside that building should. But the team expects a full recovery, and every positive update, like his visit to the facility this week, is a reminder that he is still very much a part of this team. And fourth, the coaching staff. If the Eagles change the offensive coordinator or the scheme after the season, that will tell you they believe the problem was around the quarterback, not the quarterback himself.

So let me wrap it up. There is no report that the Eagles are trying to get rid of Jalen Hurts. Jalen is recovering, in good spirits, and expected to make a full recovery. David Murphy of the Inquirer asked whether the Eagles are still committed to him, with the team possibly picking in the top five. Howie Roseman has called criticism of Jalen unfair and said, all the guy does is win football games. Jeffrey Lurie said, I love everything about him. Jalen’s 2027 cap hit is about forty-two million dollars, a 2027 trade would mean around sixty-seven million in dead money, and he has a no-trade clause. The Eagles have extra picks from the A.J. Brown and Cam Jurgens trades. And Tanner McKee could be on the trade market before Tuesday’s deadline.
