Eagles Nation, after seven straight losses, a lot of people are asking the same question this week. Did the Eagles just lose their head coach? So let me answer it right away. No. Nick Sirianni is still the head coach of the Philadelphia Eagles. He has not been fired, he has not resigned, and as of Monday afternoon, he had not even made a change to his coaching staff. But the Eagles did lose someone big on Monday. One of their captains, Jordan Davis, was traded to the Jacksonville Jaguars. And with the trade deadline closing today at four in the afternoon Eastern, more players could be on the way out. So today, we are going to separate the truth from the noise.

This is James with Second Floor, and here is what we are covering. Where Sirianni’s job really stands, what he said on Monday about his staff, the pressure on offensive coordinator Sean Mannion, the Jordan Davis trade, the three Eagles who might not survive the deadline, what Riq Woolen, Dallas Goedert and Saquon Barkley said about their futures, and the former Eagles star who thinks the team should sit its starters and chase a superstar in the draft. Before we start, if you are still riding with this team, hit that like button and subscribe to Second Floor.

Let me start with the head coach.

After the nineteen to eleven loss to the Giants on Sunday, the Eagles fell to two and seven. According to CBS Sports, it is their first two and seven start since 1999, and the worst start of the Sirianni era. They have lost seven straight games, and they are in last place in the NFC East. According to the Inquirer, The Athletic now puts their playoff odds below one percent.

So yes, the pressure is real. But here is what actually happened on Monday. According to Ryan Novozinsky of the Inquirer, Sirianni was asked if he had made any staff changes after the Giants loss. His answer was one word. Quote, no. End quote. According to ESPN’s Tim McManus, Sirianni said, quote, right now we’re going through the self-scout process. End quote. And he said, quote, guys are working on studies right now, and we’re determined to figure things out and get on the right track. End quote.

After the game on Sunday, Sirianni left the door open. According to NFL dot com, he said, quote, we’ll talk. End quote. He said, quote, everything can always be on the table. End quote. But he also said, quote, I don’t think you change just to change because that’s what your record says. End quote. And he took the blame himself. Quote, obviously, none of us offensively have done a good enough job. End quote. Quote, it starts with me. End quote. Quote, obviously, I haven’t done a good enough job. End quote.

So let me be clear. The Eagles did not lose their head coach. Nobody has reported that Jeffrey Lurie is about to fire him. But the questions about his future are louder than ever.

And there was one more story about Sirianni on Monday. After the Giants loss, a video went viral that seemed to show him giving booing fans the middle finger as he walked off the field. According to CBS Sports, Sirianni said, quote, absolutely not. That was a surprise to me. Absolutely not. End quote. According to ESPN, he also said, quote, we hear them when they boo. We don’t necessarily like it. I don’t think that’s productive for anybody. End quote. And then, quote, we thrive off the crowd when they cheer for us. That’s all I’ll say. End quote.

If you are new to Second Floor, welcome. Drop a comment right now. Do you think Nick Sirianni will still be the Eagles’ head coach next season, yes or no?

Now, the man who has been defending Sirianni might surprise you. Nick Foles. Two weeks ago, Foles said on a podcast that there was dysfunction in Philly. But this week, according to Ariel Simpson of the Inquirer, Foles said on The SZN podcast, quote, I think it would be a major mistake to fire him at any point, after this season or during the season. End quote. He pointed to Sirianni’s six years of success in Philadelphia. But Foles also said, quote, there’s got to be some sort of shift. End quote. He said he expects changes during the bye, but he does not like seeing coaches fired in the middle of a season, because it hurts game planning. And Foles predicted the Eagles will finish five and twelve.

Think about that. The same man who said there was dysfunction in Philly is now saying firing Sirianni would be a major mistake. That tells you how complicated this situation is. Foles knows this building. He knows what Sirianni has done here. And he still thinks the answer is change around the head coach, not change of the head coach.

And history is on Sirianni’s side, at least for now. Jeffrey Lurie has almost always waited until the end of the season to make a coaching change. Rich Kotite in 1994, Ray Rhodes in 1998 and Andy Reid in 2012 all finished their seasons. The one exception was Chip Kelly in 2015, who was released with one game left, according to Athlon Sports, after the Eagles were eliminated. And Athlon argued that firing Sirianni during the bye would hand eight games to an interim coach and accomplish nothing that a January decision could not.

But there is another side of Lurie’s history too. In January 2021, he moved on from Doug Pederson, the coach who had won the franchise’s first Super Bowl, after a four and eleven and one season. Lurie said at the time that the team was at a transition point and needed to get younger and add more draft picks. So a Super Bowl ring does not protect anybody forever. And on the other hand, after the 2023 Eagles lost six of their last seven games, including the playoff loss, Lurie kept Sirianni. The Eagles changed coordinators, and the very next season, they won Super Bowl fifty-nine. That second chance is a big reason Sirianni still has Lurie’s trust. Last week, according to Dave Zangaro of NBC Sports Philadelphia, Sirianni said, quote, I’ve always felt nothing but complete support from Mr. Lurie in everything. End quote.

Now, the bigger question in the building right now is not the head coach. It is the offensive coordinator.

Sean Mannion is in his first season calling plays. According to the Inquirer, the offense he was hired to run is scoring sixteen point four points per game. That ranks thirtieth in the NFL. According to the Inquirer, the Eagles are averaging about two hundred sixty yards and allowing three and a half sacks per game. Against the Giants, according to Athlon Sports, they gained just two hundred twenty yards, and it was the first time since 2019 that they scored eleven or fewer points in back-to-back games.

And remember the timeline. After the loss in Washington, Sirianni said he was not making changes at offensive coordinator or quarterback. He said, quote, Tanner’s our guy. End quote. Two days later, Joe Milton was named the starter. So Sirianni has already shown he is willing to change his mind when the situation changes.

Milton’s first start was a mixed bag. According to the Inquirer, he went eighteen of thirty-four for one hundred sixty-nine yards, with no touchdowns and no interceptions, and he led the team with sixty-four rushing yards. But he was sacked seven times. The Eagles took an eight to six lead in the first half on a three-yard Saquon Barkley touchdown and a Milton two-point run. And then the offense disappeared until a forty-five-yard Jake Elliott field goal late in the fourth quarter.

And injuries have played a huge role. Jalen Hurts is out for the season after doctors found blood clots in his lungs, and the team expects a full recovery. Lane Johnson retired in October. DeVonta Smith has missed five games this season. But Sirianni has made it clear he is not using any of that as an excuse.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you would fire Sean Mannion during the bye. Comment number two if you would give him the rest of the season.

Now, the player the Eagles did lose. Jordan Davis.

According to Jeff Neiburg of the Inquirer, the Eagles traded Davis to the Jaguars on Monday evening for a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick. According to Reuben Frank of NBC Sports Philadelphia, Davis is twenty-six, a captain, a Super Bowl champion, and the only Eagle to start all sixty regular-season games since 2022. He signed a three-year, seventy-eight million dollar extension just eight months ago. And according to Frank, it is the first time Howie Roseman has traded an Eagles first-round pick since Carson Wentz in 2020.

And here is the twist. According to Jordan Schultz, as reported by Heavy, the Jaguars first asked about Moro Ojomo, and the Eagles told them they were not trading him. So Jacksonville pivoted to Davis. The Eagles chose to keep the younger, cheaper defensive tackle.

And look at the Eagles’ draft picks now. According to Neiburg, they have two first-round picks in 2027, their own and Jacksonville’s. They have two first-round picks in 2028, their own and New England’s from the A.J. Brown trade. And according to Frank, they hold seven first or second-round picks over the next two drafts.

And Moro Ojomo, the player the Eagles chose to keep, had already made his feelings clear before the deadline. According to Eagles On SI, he said, quote, Howie and Lurie mean so much to me for giving me this opportunity to play for this team. End quote. And Davis himself said on Sunday, before the trade, according to the Inquirer, quote, obviously, when somebody does come or leave, it does catch a lot of people by surprise. End quote. He did not know it then, but he was talking about himself.

So the Eagles did not lose their head coach. But they did lose a captain. And that tells you which direction the front office is leaning.

Now, let us talk about the three Eagles who might be next.

According to Michael Kaskey-Blomain of Eagles On SI, three players likely will not survive the trade deadline after the loss to the Giants.

The first is quarterback Tanner McKee. He was benched for Joe Milton, and according to Kaskey-Blomain, the Eagles appear ready to go with Milton. McKee is in the final year of his contract and is unlikely to return in 2027, so the Eagles should try to get something for him now. And according to Albert Breer of Sports Illustrated, McKee is among the players other teams perceive to be available. Rookie Cole Payton is also in the quarterback room behind Milton.

The second is cornerback Riq Woolen. He is on a one-year deal worth twelve million dollars, according to Johnny McGonigal of PennLive. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler had put his odds of being traded at thirty-five percent. And according to PennLive, a contender with secondary issues, like the Bills or the Lions, could trade a fourth or fifth-round pick for him.

But Woolen made his feelings very clear after the Giants game. According to PennLive, he said, quote, my heart is here. End quote. And then, quote, my heart is with this team and the guys I’ve been working with. End quote. He said, quote, it sucks as a human when you don’t know the unknown, but you want to know the unknown. End quote. And he said, quote, whatever lies ahead lies ahead. End quote. When asked about his future, he said, quote, I don’t know. I’ll just leave it to them. End quote. And he finished with, quote, I would love to be here. End quote.

That is a player who wants to stay and fight. But he also knows it is not up to him. And according to PennLive, Woolen is twenty-seven, six foot four and two hundred ten pounds, with good recovery speed, though he has had some penalties and missed assignments this season. He signed in March as a one-year option opposite Quinyon Mitchell, and he is hoping to earn a multi-year deal. If he stays past today, the rest of this season becomes his audition.

The third is receiver Hollywood Brown. According to Kaskey-Blomain, Brown had one catch on one target against the Giants, and he has been a non-factor this season. That is a tough look, especially with DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks out. This was supposed to be Brown’s chance to step up. The Eagles are not expected to bring him back in free agency, and the projected return is a Day 3 draft pick.

And there are others. Landon Dickerson has drawn interest from several teams, according to NBC Sports Philadelphia’s Ryan Fowler and ESPN’s Adam Schefter. According to the Inquirer, Jeff Neiburg named Dickerson, Woolen, Ojomo and Dallas Goedert among the most likely players to be moved before the Davis deal. And according to the Inquirer, Jeff McLane reported that Roseman had been shopping for a starting-caliber offensive lineman before Quenton Nelson was traded to Seattle, and ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported the Eagles were among the teams that checked on Nelson.

Now, listen to what Dallas Goedert said. According to SB Nation, Goedert said he has not dwelled on a possible trade, but he accepts that it could happen. He said he had a brief talk with Howie Roseman about his future, and that it went well. And then he said, quote, I love this city. I always dreamt of retiring here, so we’ll see what happens. End quote.

Goedert is on a one-year deal, so this could be his last season in Philadelphia either way. But it is clear he wants it to end with him retiring in green.

And Saquon Barkley? According to the Inquirer, ESPN reported he would not be traded before the deadline. And Saquon said, quote, I would love to be here. I plan on being here. End quote. Not everyone agrees. Pete Prisco of CBS Sports asked, quote, what about Barkley? If somebody is interested in Barkley, do you make that move? End quote. And Tyler Sullivan of CBS Sports wrote, quote, after Philadelphia dropped to two and seven with a loss to the Giants, everything should be on the table. End quote. But the reports say Saquon is staying.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you would trade Riq Woolen. Comment number two if you would keep him and try to sign him long term.

Now, here is the idea that is going to divide this fan base. LeSean McCoy.

According to the Inquirer, the former Eagles running back said, quote, the blessing in losing is we can go out there and get us that player from Ohio State, Jeremiah Smith. End quote. McCoy suggested the Eagles should sit their starters to keep them healthy, and he argued that Howie Roseman could land Smith and bring this team right back. In his words, quote, we’ll be back to Super Bowl contenders. End quote.

Jeremiah Smith is the star wide receiver at Ohio State, and he is widely seen as one of the most talented players in college football. Imagine him with a healthy Jalen Hurts and Saquon Barkley. That is the dream McCoy is selling.

But there are some big problems with that idea. First, you never know where you will land in the draft. According to Athlon Sports, the Eagles are slotted at number five in Tankathon’s draft order heading into Week 10. Other bad teams are fighting for those top spots too. Second, the Eagles still have eight games left, and some of them are against good teams. After the bye, they host the Pittsburgh Steelers on November twenty-second, then they go to Dallas on Thanksgiving. Later, they play at Arizona, then host the Colts, the Seahawks and the Texans, before finishing at San Francisco and at the New York Giants. Third, players and coaches do not play to lose. The locker room will fight every Sunday, no matter what the draft order says. And fourth, with two first-round picks in 2027, the Eagles might not need to tank at all. They already have the ammunition to move up if they want a player badly enough.

Here is my take, and this is just my opinion, not a report. I understand where McCoy is coming from, and I agree with part of it. If a player is not healthy, sit him. Protect Saquon. Protect anyone who is banged up. But I do not want this team to give up. Young players like Makai Lemon, Eli Stowers, Markel Bell and Jeremiah Trotter Junior need to play and grow. Joe Milton needs to show what he can do. And the coaches need to prove they can fix this offense. You learn a lot more from trying to win than from trying to lose.

Here is what I will be watching over the next two weeks. First, today at four o’clock. Does Howie Roseman make another trade after Jordan Davis? Second, the self-scout. If Sirianni changes the play caller or the offensive line coach, that tells you he is serious about a new approach. Third, Zack Baun. He left the Giants game with a back injury and has no diagnosis yet, and I am not going to guess. And fourth, the quarterback. Does Joe Milton keep the job against Pittsburgh? Every one of those answers will tell us more about where this franchise is going than any headline.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the people who say it is time for a complete reset. They will say the Eagles are two and seven, their worst start since 1999, and that Sirianni has lost control of the season. They will say the offense ranks thirtieth in scoring and that Sean Mannion has to go. They will say the Jordan Davis trade proves the front office is already looking to 2027, so they should sell McKee, Woolen, Brown and anyone else they can before four o’clock today. And some will say McCoy is right, and that the Eagles should rest starters and chase a top pick.

On the other side, the people who say stay patient. They will say Sirianni has won a Super Bowl and made the playoffs every full season, and that even Nick Foles says firing him would be a major mistake. They will say this team has been destroyed by injuries, from Jalen Hurts to Lane Johnson to DeVonta Smith. They will say the defense just had six sacks against the Giants, which proves the players are still fighting. They will say players like Woolen, Goedert and Saquon want to be here, and that you cannot build a culture by trading everyone who loves this city. And they will say the Eagles already have the draft picks to rebuild without tanking.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. The Eagles did not lose their head coach, and I do not think they will during the bye. Lurie’s history says he waits until the season is over. But I do think Sirianni has to make real changes during this bye, especially on offense. On the trade front, I would move Hollywood Brown and Tanner McKee if anyone offers a pick, because they are not part of the future. With Woolen, I would only trade him for a real return, because he wants to be here and he is still young. And I keep Saquon and Goedert. As for tanking, no. Protect the injured players, play the young guys, and let the draft order take care of itself. With two first-round picks in 2027 already, this team does not need to lose on purpose to rebuild.

So let me wrap it up. No, the Eagles did not lose their head coach. Nick Sirianni is still in charge, and as of Monday, he had not made any staff changes. He said the team is going through a self-scout process, and he denied flipping off fans after the Giants loss. Nick Foles said firing him would be a major mistake. Sean Mannion’s offense ranks thirtieth in scoring. The Eagles did lose captain Jordan Davis, who was traded to Jacksonville for a first and a second-round pick. Tanner McKee, Riq Woolen and Hollywood Brown are the names most likely to go next, according to Eagles On SI. Woolen said, my heart is here. Goedert said, I always dreamt of retiring here.
