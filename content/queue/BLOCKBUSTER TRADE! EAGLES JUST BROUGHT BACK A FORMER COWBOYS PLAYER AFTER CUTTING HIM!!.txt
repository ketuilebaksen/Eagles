Eagles Nation, with just days left before the Dallas Cowboys come to Lincoln Financial Field for Monday Night Football, Howie Roseman made a move that has a whole lot of people talking. The Eagles brought back a running back who was drafted by the Cowboys, cut by the Cowboys, picked up by the Eagles, promoted, waived, and then signed again, all in less than two months. And of all the weeks to bring him back, it happens during Cowboys week.

Now, before we go any further, let me be straight with you, because that is how we do things on this channel. This was not a blockbuster trade. No draft picks changed hands, and no player went to Dallas. What actually happened is a practice squad signing. But it is a really interesting one, and the story behind it says a lot about where this Eagles team is right now and what they are preparing for on Monday night.

This is James with Second Floor, and today I am going to tell you exactly who this player is, why the Eagles keep bringing him back, the other moves they made on the same day, and what all of it means for the running game against Dallas. If you are a diehard Eagles fan, hit that like button and subscribe right now, because we are covering every roster move this Cowboys week the moment it happens.

Let me set the scene first, because you need to understand why this move happened.

The Eagles are two and four, and they have dropped four straight games. The latest one was that twenty-five to fourteen loss to the Carolina Panthers on October eighteenth. Jalen Hurts is out for the rest of the season after being diagnosed with deep vein thrombosis and blood clots in both lungs. The team and his agent have said his prognosis is very good and that a full recovery is expected, but he will not play again this year. Tanner McKee is now the starting quarterback.

And the running back room has looked more like an emergency room. Saquon Barkley hurt his hamstring early in Week 4 against the Rams and has missed time since. Other backs have gone down too, and the roster has been shuffled again and again. Against Carolina, the Eagles ran for just thirty-five yards as a team. With a backup quarterback under center, that is a recipe for disaster. This team needs fresh, healthy legs. Guys who can carry the football, protect the football, and keep McKee out of third and long.

And that is when Howie Roseman went back to a familiar face.

Let me give you the clues. This running back is in his second year in the NFL. He was taken in the fifth round of the 2025 draft, one hundred forty-ninth overall, by the Dallas Cowboys. In college, he played for the University of Texas. And he ran a four point three eight forty-yard dash at the combine. Do you know who I am talking about yet?

His name is Jaydon Blue.

On Thursday, October twenty-second, the Eagles officially announced that they signed Jaydon Blue to the practice squad. That was just two days after they waived him from the active roster. And here is the full timeline, because it is a wild one. On August thirtieth, the Cowboys waived Blue in their final roster cuts. The very next day, the Eagles signed him to their practice squad. Before Week 6, the Eagles promoted him to the active roster. He was inactive for that game against Carolina. On Tuesday, October twentieth, when the Eagles signed Elijah Mitchell to the practice squad, they waived Blue and Carson Steele from the active roster. And on Thursday, Blue was right back in the building.

Think about that for a second. Cut by Dallas. Scooped up by Philadelphia. Promoted. Waived. Back again. A lot of young players who get bounced around like that start to wonder if anybody in this league really believes in them. But here is a team that keeps picking up the phone and calling his number.

Now, let us go back in time, because this young man’s story has been a real roller coaster.

When Dallas drafted Blue in 2025, there was real excitement. He was a speed back with home run ability, and a lot of people thought he could carve out a role. But it never really clicked in Dallas. According to Yahoo Sports, he dressed for only five games as a rookie, and he was inactive for eight straight games before the season finale. His rookie numbers were one hundred twenty-nine rushing yards and one touchdown, plus one catch for five yards. He also returned three kickoffs for seventy-two yards. Not a lot of opportunity, and not a lot of production.

But in college, it was a different story. At Texas, Blue ran for one thousand one hundred sixty-one yards and eleven touchdowns over thirty-eight games, and he added seven receiving touchdowns. His best season was his last one. In 2024, he ran for seven hundred thirty yards and eight touchdowns at five point four yards per carry, and he caught six touchdown passes. That is a running back who can hurt you on the ground and through the air. And before that, he was a four-star recruit out of Klein Cain High School in Texas, where he once ran for more than two thousand yards and thirty touchdowns in a single season.

So the talent is there. What has been missing is opportunity, and maybe consistency. And that brings me to the question I want to ask you. Is Howie Roseman seeing something that Dallas did not see?

Because here is what we know. The Eagles grabbed him the day after Dallas let him go. They kept him on the practice squad for weeks. They promoted him when the backfield got thin. And even after they had to waive him to make room, they brought him right back as soon as they could. He still has not played a single snap for the Eagles. And yet the team keeps finding a spot for him. To me, that tells you the coaching staff likes what it sees on the practice field.

Now, let me be careful here. Some people are saying the Eagles signed Blue this week specifically to get inside information on the Cowboys. That is speculation. No report says that. Blue spent one season in Dallas, and teams change their game plans every week. But I will say this. It is impossible not to notice the timing. And if Blue ever gets onto the field on Monday night, against the team that drafted him and then cut him, you know he is going to play with something extra. If that is not a revenge game, I do not know what is.

Now, the Eagles did not stop with Jaydon Blue. On that same Thursday, they made three more moves.

First, they signed guard Evan Neal to the practice squad. And you know that name. Neal was the seventh overall pick in the 2022 draft by the New York Giants. According to Yahoo Sports, he started twenty-seven games over three seasons with the Giants. He was a consensus All-American and a national champion at Alabama. He moved from tackle to guard in the 2025 offseason. This year, the Giants released him in final cuts, and he landed on the Raiders’ practice squad before being let go earlier this week. Then he had a tryout with the Eagles, and now he is in Philadelphia. A former top ten pick from a division rival, now on the Eagles practice squad. Who would have thought?

And here is why that matters. The Eagles have a long history of helping big, talented offensive linemen get the most out of their ability. If anybody can help Neal get his career back on track, it is this organization. And with injuries all along the Eagles’ offensive line this season, depth is never a bad thing.

Second, they signed linebacker Anfernee Orji to the practice squad. Orji went undrafted out of Vanderbilt in 2023 and signed with the Saints. In 2024, he played sixteen games for New Orleans and posted thirty tackles. He tore his ACL during a joint practice with the Titans in 2025, spent time with the Giants this summer, and actually had a brief stint on the Eagles’ practice squad earlier this month before being released five days later. Now he is back too.

And third, to make room, the Eagles waived tight end E.J. Jenkins from the active roster. Jenkins had appeared in all six games this season. According to Yahoo Sports, he did not have a single target, and he played sixty-seven snaps on offense and one hundred twenty-six on special teams. He was also part of the Eagles’ Super Bowl season in 2024.

So with Jenkins gone, a spot opens up on the active roster. Who could fill it? A lot of people are pointing at a very familiar name. Zach Ertz. According to Yahoo Sports, Ertz is no longer eligible for practice squad elevations, which means if the Eagles want him on the field Monday night, they would have to sign him to the active roster. And with Dallas Goedert missing practice on Thursday and Friday with a knee injury, that move suddenly makes a lot of sense. But I want to be very clear. As of right now, that has not been announced. It is a possibility, not a fact.

And remember, Elijah Mitchell is part of this story too. The Eagles signed Mitchell to the practice squad on Tuesday, October twentieth, the same day they waived Blue and Carson Steele. Mitchell is a veteran who has had real success in this league when healthy, including a big rookie season with the 49ers. So in the span of one week, the Eagles added a proven veteran and brought back a young speed back. One brings experience, the other brings upside. That is not panic. That is a front office covering every possible outcome before a must-win game.

Now let us step back and look at the running back picture for Monday night.

The best news is that Saquon Barkley practiced on Thursday and Friday. He was limited both days, but NBC Sports Philadelphia wrote that he appears to be on track to play against Dallas, and PhillyVoice listed him as anticipated to play. If Saquon is back, that changes everything. He forces defenses to respect the run, he opens up play action for McKee, and he can turn a small hole into a huge gain.

Behind him, Dameon Pierce has been the lead back during Saquon’s absence, and he scored the Eagles’ rushing touchdown against Carolina. Elijah Mitchell was signed to the practice squad on October twentieth. And now Jaydon Blue is back. The message is loud and clear. Philadelphia is stacking up as many healthy legs as it can, so it does not get caught short again. After everything this backfield has been through this season, you cannot blame them. Depth at running back is not glamorous, but when your starting quarterback is a backup and your star back is coming off a hamstring injury, depth is survival.

So what is plan B if Saquon cannot go? Dameon Pierce should stay in the starting role. And because Mitchell and Blue are on the practice squad, they would need to be elevated or signed to the active roster to suit up. Nick Sirianni has not said exactly how he plans to divide the work, so we will have to wait and see. Remember, because this is a Monday night game, the final injury report with official game statuses comes out on Saturday.

And here is the bigger point. With Tanner McKee running the offense, a strong ground game is not a luxury. It is a must. McKee went twenty-six of forty-five for two hundred fifteen yards, a touchdown and no interceptions against Carolina. He protected the ball. But forty-five pass attempts is way too many for a backup quarterback. The Eagles need to run the football, stay ahead of the chains, and take some of the pressure off his shoulders. Whether it is Saquon, Pierce, Mitchell or Blue, somebody has to carry the load.

So how would Jaydon Blue actually fit in this offense if he gets the call? Think about what he does well. He is a smaller, faster back, listed at five foot nine, with real burst. At Texas, he was at his best when he got the ball in space, on outside runs, screens and passes out of the backfield. Remember, he caught forty-two passes in his final college season. That is a skill set that could help Tanner McKee. When a young quarterback is under pressure, a running back who can catch a quick dump-off and turn it into a first down is a lifeline. Dameon Pierce is more of a downhill, between-the-tackles runner. Blue is the change of pace. That is why having both of them, plus Elijah Mitchell, gives Sean Mannion options.

And speaking of the offensive line, there was good news on Friday that matters for every one of these running backs. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, Drew Kendall and Landon Dickerson practiced and lined up next to each other with the first-team offensive line. Kendall has missed the last couple of games with a knee injury, and Dickerson has missed the last five games. One important detail, though. Dickerson is still on injured reserve with his practice window open, so the Eagles would have to activate him to the fifty-three-man roster before he can play. Keep an eye on that move this weekend. If Kendall and Dickerson are both back, this running game has a much better chance, no matter who is carrying the ball.

Now, quickly, let us look at the other sideline. The Cowboys are also two and four, and they have lost two straight. Dak Prescott has thrown four interceptions in his last two games. Returner KaVontae Turpin did not practice on Thursday with a ribs injury, and Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice wrote that he is not expected to play. Starting linebacker Dee Winters also did not practice on Thursday, and George Pickens, left tackle Tyler Guyton and cornerback Cobie Durant were limited. So Dallas is banged up too. And if Winters cannot go, that is one less starting linebacker trying to stop the Eagles’ running game.

And there is one more layer to this rivalry. The Cowboys’ defensive coordinator, Christian Parker, used to work under Vic Fangio. So Fangio knows exactly how Parker thinks, and Parker knows Fangio’s system. Add in a running back the Cowboys drafted and cut, sitting on the Eagles’ practice squad, and this Monday night game has storylines everywhere you look.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides on this move.

On one side, the skeptics. They will say this is just practice squad shuffling, not a real solution. They will say Jaydon Blue could not crack the lineup in Dallas, has not played a snap in Philadelphia, and is not going to save a running game that has been broken for weeks. They will say signing Evan Neal and Anfernee Orji to the practice squad is just filling holes, not building a contender. And they will say the Eagles need real answers on the offensive line and in the passing game, not more depth pieces.

On the other side, the believers. They will say this is exactly what good front offices do. They find young, cheap, talented players and keep them in the building. They will say Blue’s college production shows real talent, and that his speed is something this offense does not have much of right now. They will say Evan Neal is a former top ten pick who could become a useful player in the Eagles’ system. And they will say none of these moves cost the Eagles anything except a practice squad spot, which means there is no downside at all.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I like the Jaydon Blue move. Not because I think he is going to be the hero on Monday night. Honestly, he might not even see the field. But because it shows the Eagles believe in him, and because a back with four point three speed and real receiving ability is worth developing, especially in a season where the running back room has been crushed by injuries. I like the Evan Neal signing even more as a long-term flyer. Now, for Monday night, it all comes down to Saquon. If he plays and the offensive line holds up, the Eagles can run the football and give McKee a real chance. If he cannot go, Pierce has to carry the load, and the depth behind him suddenly matters a lot.

So let me wrap it up. This was not a trade. On Thursday, October twenty-second, the Eagles signed running back Jaydon Blue, a former Cowboys fifth-round pick, to the practice squad, just two days after waiving him. They also signed former Giants first-round pick Evan Neal and linebacker Anfernee Orji to the practice squad, and they waived tight end E.J. Jenkins, which opens a roster spot that could go to Zach Ertz, though nothing has been announced. Saquon Barkley was limited on Thursday and Friday and appears to be on track to play.
