Let me start with good news number one. The streak is over.

According to Field Level Media, the Eagles' win on Sunday was their first since September twentieth. That is two full months without a win. Seven straight losses. And it ended against a Steelers team that came in at six and three and was favored by four and a half points, according to CBS Sports.

The Eagles came out on fire. According to the official game data, they scored thirteen points in the first quarter. Jake Elliott kicked a forty-one-yard field goal and a twenty-nine-yard field goal, and Saquon Barkley ran thirty-eight yards for a touchdown with forty-three seconds left in the quarter. Thirteen to nothing.

Then Pittsburgh chipped away. Chris Boswell kicked a twenty-five-yarder before halftime. In the third quarter, after Jaydon Blue fumbled the second-half kickoff, Boswell kicked a forty-yarder. Then Elliott answered with a fifty-three-yard field goal in the third quarter, and Boswell hit one from fifty-two. In the fourth quarter, Elliott drilled a fifty-five-yarder with about four and a half minutes left to make it nineteen to nine. And according to the official game data, Elliott went four for four on field goals. Two of them came from more than fifty yards.

The Steelers scored a late touchdown, a twenty-yard pass from Aaron Rodgers to rookie Max Hurleman, to make it nineteen to sixteen with a minute and twenty-one seconds left. But on the Eagles' final drive, Nick Sirianni did not play it safe. On third and six, Joe Milton threw a seven-yard out to DeVonta Smith for the first down, and the Eagles ran out the clock.

After the game, Sirianni explained the call. He said, quote, you just want to be aggressive in four-minute situations. End quote. He said that if the Eagles had punted, the Steelers would have had about a minute left, and in his words, quote, that's plenty of time to go get a field goal. End quote. And then he said, quote, we wanted to end it with the ball in our hands. End quote.

And when Sirianni was asked how this win felt after seven straight losses, he kept it simple. He said it just felt good to get back in the win column. He said the feeling in the locker room was basically, man, we missed this.

Now, let me get to good news number two. And this is the one that should excite every Eagles fan about the future.

The Eagles' defense played this game without linebacker Zack Baun, who was ruled out with a back injury. They played without linebacker Smael Mondon Junior. And according to Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice, they played without Jordan Davis, who was traded to Jacksonville at the deadline. That is the middle of the defense, and a big part of the run defense, gone.

And yet, according to the official game data, the Eagles' defense sacked Aaron Rodgers five times, hit him six times, forced four three-and-outs and made a fourth-down stop. The Steelers finished with just two hundred fifty-five total yards. According to Kempski, seventy-two of those yards came on a late garbage-time drive, and Pittsburgh had fewer than one hundred yards in the first half.

And look at the Steelers' running game. According to the official game data, Pittsburgh ran the ball twenty times for just seventy-three yards, and their longest run of the day was twelve yards. That is against a defense with no Zack Baun and no Jordan Davis in the middle.
