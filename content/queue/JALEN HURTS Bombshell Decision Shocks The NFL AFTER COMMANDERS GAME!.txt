Eagles Nation, there is a question hanging over this franchise that nobody wanted to ask a few months ago. Is Jalen Hurts’ time in Philadelphia coming to an end? With the Eagles at two and five, Jalen out for the season, the trade deadline approaching and national insiders now openly talking about his contract, the conversation about the future of the most important player on this team has officially started.

So let me be completely honest with you, the way I always am. Jalen Hurts has not asked to leave. The Eagles have not said they want to move on. There is no report of a trade, a release or any decision. And Jalen cannot be traded right now anyway, because his season is over while he recovers from blood clots. What is real is that respected insiders are saying this will be one of the biggest stories of the offseason, and that a decision on his contract may be coming. That is what we are going to break down today, along with everything else happening before Sunday night in Washington.

This is James with Second Floor, and before we start, hit that like button and subscribe, because this is one of the most important conversations about this franchise in years, and you deserve the facts.

And all of this comes after another painful week on the field. On Monday night, the Dallas Cowboys beat the Eagles thirty-eight to twenty-one at Lincoln Financial Field. It was the Eagles’ fifth straight loss. Javonte Williams ran for one hundred fifty-five yards, the most a running back has ever gained against a Vic Fangio defense in Philadelphia, according to the Inquirer. And Dak Prescott threw three touchdowns without being sacked. When a team is struggling like this, every big question about the future gets louder. That includes questions about the head coach, too. Kempski wrote this week that Nick Sirianni’s offense has struggled for two seasons, but that an in-season firing feels unlikely.

Let me start with the contract, because that is where this conversation comes from.

On October fourteenth, NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport talked about Jalen’s future on the This is Football podcast with Kevin Clark. And he said something that got everyone’s attention. Quote, I think everyone knows that his guaranteed money is mostly up after this year. End quote. He said, quote, if you’re the Eagles, it’s either extend him or not. End quote. And he added, quote, contractually, something probably has to happen. End quote.

But Rapoport also said this, and it is just as important. Quote, generally, teams don’t move on from franchise quarterbacks who’ve won Super Bowls. End quote.

So think about what that means. According to Rapoport, after this season, the Eagles reach a point in Jalen’s contract where they have a real choice. They can extend him and commit to him for the long term. Or they can go in a different direction. That is not a report that they are moving on. It is a description of the decision they will face.

And Rapoport is not the only one. According to a Roundtable Sports story on Yahoo Sports, ESPN’s Dan Graziano and Jeremy Fowler described Jalen’s situation as one of the more curious ones for any quarterback heading into the 2027 offseason. That same story said only about twenty-two million dollars of Jalen’s contract is guaranteed for 2027 and beyond. And on Wednesday, Jimmy Kempski of PhillyVoice wrote that Jalen is not a trade option right now, because his season is over, but that a trade could be considered in the offseason.

And there is one more thing worth remembering about Jalen’s health. According to the joint statement from the team and his agent, he is clinically stable, he has no significant functional limitations, and they expect a full recovery. He will be treated with blood thinners and cannot take part in football activities until he is off them. I am not a doctor, and I am not going to guess at timelines. But a full recovery is the best news anyone could ask for, and it matters when you think about his future.

Now, let me be very clear about what that means and what it does not mean. Kempski did not report that the Eagles want to trade Jalen. He was explaining why Jalen was not on his deadline list. He was saying the offseason is when that kind of conversation could happen, if it happens at all.

So why is this even a conversation? Let me give you the context.

First, the results. The Eagles started two and three with Jalen this season before his injury. According to that same Yahoo story, in his first five games, he completed fifty-seven point eight percent of his passes for nine hundred thirty-three yards, eight touchdowns and three interceptions. Those are not the numbers we are used to from Jalen, and the offense as a whole has struggled for almost two seasons.

Second, the health situation. Jalen was diagnosed with deep vein thrombosis and blood clots in both lungs, and he is out for the season. The team and his agent, Nicole Lynn, have said his prognosis is very good and that a full recovery is expected. That is the most important thing. But it also means the Eagles will not see him on the field again until 2027. And the NFL and the players union are reviewing how his situation was handled.

Third, the history. This franchise has made tough quarterback decisions before. They moved on from Donovan McNabb. They traded Carson Wentz a few years after he was an MVP candidate, and they drafted Jalen Hurts while Wentz was still their starter. Howie Roseman has never been afraid of a big decision at the most important position.

And let us remember what Jalen has meant to this franchise. He took over as the starter late in the 2020 season, when the Eagles were in a tough spot. In 2021, he led a team that started two and five into the playoffs. In 2022, he was a finalist for MVP and led the Eagles to the Super Bowl, where he put up one of the greatest individual performances in Super Bowl history, even in a loss. And in the 2024 season, he led the Eagles to a Super Bowl title and was named Super Bowl MVP. He has three Pro Bowl selections and was an All-Pro in 2022. That is not a quarterback you just let go because of a bad stretch.

And think about what the alternative would look like. Tanner McKee has battled, and he has shown real toughness in his first two starts. Joe Milton has a big arm and is still developing. But neither of them has proven they can lead a team to a Super Bowl. Jalen has done it. Replacing a quarterback like that through the draft or free agency is one of the hardest things in football. Plenty of teams spend a decade looking for what the Eagles already have.

Eagles Nation, here is my honest take. I do not believe Jalen Hurts is leaving Philadelphia. He is a Super Bowl MVP. He led this team to two Super Bowls. He is twenty-eight years old. And as Rapoport said, teams generally do not move on from quarterbacks like that. But I also understand why the question is being asked. When a contract reaches a decision point, when the team is struggling and when the quarterback is recovering from a serious health issue, the conversation is going to happen. The real answer will come in the offseason, not before Sunday night. And until then, anyone telling you Jalen is already gone is selling you a headline, not a fact.

Now, let us talk about Sunday night, because the injury news this week has been brutal, especially at running back.

On Wednesday, the Eagles placed Dameon Pierce on injured reserve. According to the team, Pierce led the backfield for three games with ninety-six rushing yards and two touchdowns on thirty-seven carries. That makes three Eagles running backs on injured reserve, along with Tank Bigsby and Will Shipley. And on that same day, the Eagles signed Jaydon Blue from the practice squad to the active roster. Remember, Blue is the former Cowboys fifth-round pick that the Eagles have now added, waived and brought back multiple times this season. This is his second stint on the active roster. The Eagles also signed guard Evan Beerntsen, receiver Quentin Skinner and cornerback Shaun Wade to the practice squad.

And then there is Saquon Barkley. He did not practice on Wednesday or Thursday because of his hamstring and back. Saquon said earlier this week that he expects to play on Sunday, but missing two straight days is not a great sign. If Saquon cannot go, the backfield could come down to Jaydon Blue and Elijah Mitchell, who would likely need to be elevated from the practice squad.

And here is what makes it so frustrating. When Saquon has been on the field, he has looked like himself. On Monday night against Dallas, he broke off a twenty-nine-yard run on the first drive and scored an eight-yard touchdown, even though he strained his hamstring early and then developed a back injury from compensating for it. When Saquon and the run game are rolling, it unlocks everything else in this offense. He just cannot stay healthy.

At receiver, DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks also missed practice on Wednesday and Thursday. Smith is still dealing with his hamstring, and this is his third hamstring absence since training camp. Britain Covey, Jihaad Campbell and Jonathan Jones also did not practice. On defense, Marcus Epps was limited with a jaw injury and a concussion evaluation, and Nolan Smith, Jonathan Greenard, Jalyx Hunt, Moro Ojomo and Michael Carter the second were all limited.

The one bright spot in all of this is rookie Makai Lemon. On Monday night, he led the team in catches and yards, with seven catches for sixty-seven yards and his first NFL touchdown. And he is earning respect around the league for the way he plays. Former Eagles receiver Jason Avant said on WIP that the thing he loves about Lemon is that the kid is tough, that he wants to block defensive ends and linebackers, and that he wants to be involved. Avant said you have to respect the way Lemon plays the game.

Now, let us talk about two Eagles who made headlines this week for what they said.

First, Jalen Carter. After the Cowboys game, Carter skipped the media for the third straight game. On Wednesday, he finally spoke, and according to the Philadelphia Inquirer, he did not hold back. When asked to sum up his 2026 season, he said one word. Quote, trash. End quote. He said his play has not been, quote, to my expectations and, well, I guess not to what the fans want. End quote. And then he said, quote, don’t really matter what they want. End quote. As for skipping interviews, he said, quote, I don’t think I’m a public spokesperson, end quote, and, quote, I think y’all just want to talk to me because we’re losing. End quote.

Eagles Nation, I understand why a lot of fans did not like that. Philadelphia fans give everything to this team, and hearing that what they want does not really matter stings. But I also see a player who is honest about his own play. He called his season trash. That is accountability. What I want from Jalen Carter is not perfect press conferences. I want him wrecking the Commanders’ offensive line on Sunday night. According to the Inquirer, he had zero solo tackles and zero quarterback pressures against Dallas. That has to change. The Eagles’ run defense was gashed on Monday night, and the best way to fix that starts with their best defensive lineman dominating up front.

Second, Riq Woolen. His name has been all over the trade rumors, and on Wednesday he addressed them. According to the Inquirer, Woolen said the rumors are just that, and that only the general manager and the coach know the truth. Quote, you hear things, but nobody knows the truth. End quote. He said he is focused on his job, and that he is here every day working with his teammates. He said he went through the same thing in Seattle and kept showing up because his teammates needed him. That is the right attitude. Whether he stays or goes, that is the kind of professionalism you want from a veteran in a tough season.

And Woolen is one of several Eagles who keep coming up as possible trade candidates. According to the Inquirer’s deadline tracker, the most likely Eagles to be moved include Landon Dickerson, Dallas Goedert, Moro Ojomo, Nolan Smith, Tyler Steen and Riq Woolen, with Hollywood Brown also mentioned on expanded lists. Jeff McLane also reported that Howie Roseman has been shopping for a starting-caliber offensive lineman. And Kempski wrote that DeVonta Smith isn’t going anywhere. The deadline is Tuesday, November tenth, at four in the afternoon Eastern.

There are a few young players giving fans hope, too. Rookie right tackle Markel Bell keeps growing, and Drew Kendall has looked solid at center. Darius Cooper has made some nice plays. Kempski listed Lemon, Kendall, Bell, Campbell, Jeremiah Trotter and Andrew Mukuba as young developing starters. If this season is going to be a lost one, at least the young core is getting real experience.

Now, Sunday night in Washington. The Commanders are one, four and one, and they are coming off a bye. On their Thursday injury report, offensive lineman Nick Allegretti, tight end John Bates, receiver Treylon Burks and defensive tackle Javon Kinlaw did not practice. Quarterback Jayden Daniels was a full participant with a left elbow issue, and Stefon Diggs was full as well. This is a winnable game on paper. But at two and five, with this many injuries, the Eagles cannot take anything for granted. A win snaps the five-game losing streak and keeps the season breathing. A loss pushes the Eagles closer to selling at the deadline.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides on the Jalen Hurts question.

On one side, the people who think the Eagles should move on. They will say Jalen’s numbers before his injury were not good enough, and the offense has struggled for two seasons. They will say his guaranteed money is mostly done after this year, which gives the Eagles a natural exit point. They will say he is coming off a serious health issue, and the team has to think about the long term. They will say the Eagles have a ton of draft capital, including two first-round picks in 2028, and could look for their next quarterback. And they will point out that this franchise has moved on from big-name quarterbacks before, from McNabb to Wentz.

On the other side, the people who want Jalen to stay. They will say he is a Super Bowl MVP who led this team to two Super Bowls, and you do not let a player like that walk. They will say the struggles of the last two seasons have more to do with the offensive scheme and the coordinator changes than with Jalen. They will say the team and his agent expect a full recovery, and that he is only twenty-eight. They will say finding a franchise quarterback is the hardest thing in sports, and the Eagles already have one. And they will say Rapoport is right, teams generally do not move on from quarterbacks who have won a Super Bowl.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I want Jalen Hurts back. When he is healthy, with a real offensive plan around him, he is a championship quarterback. We have seen it. I do not think the Eagles’ problems start with him, and I think the smartest move this offseason is to fix the offense around him, not to start over at quarterback. But I also understand that the Eagles will have a real decision to make on his contract, and that the conversation is going to be loud. For now, the most important thing is that Jalen gets fully healthy. Everything else can wait until the offseason. Philadelphia has his back.
