Let me start with the problem, because you cannot understand this move without understanding just how bad the running back situation got.

Saquon Barkley hurt his hamstring against the Los Angeles Rams back in Week 4, and he has missed the last two games. Tank Bigsby went on injured reserve with a core muscle injury. Will Shipley got hurt in London against Jacksonville and he is on injured reserve too. And on Sunday against Carolina, Carson Steele hurt his shoulder. So by the end of the Panthers game, the Eagles had lost four running backs in a matter of weeks. Four.

And you could see it on the field. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the Eagles rank twenty-eighth in the NFL in rushing yards per game, at eighty-two, and twenty-fifth in yards per carry, at three point seven. Against Carolina, the whole team ran for just thirty-five yards. With a backup quarterback starting, that is simply not going to work.

So on Tuesday afternoon, October twentieth, the Eagles made their moves. And there were a lot of them. They signed running back Elijah Mitchell to the practice squad. They released center James Brockermeyer and running back Ian Wheeler from the practice squad, and remember, Wheeler had just signed the Thursday before. They waived running back Jaydon Blue, who had been promoted to the active roster for the Panthers game. And they waived Carson Steele with an injury designation because of that shoulder.

According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, that leaves Dameon Pierce and Elijah Mitchell as the healthy running backs. Two names. Three days before practice for a Monday night game against your biggest rival.

And here is a little twist I love. Jaydon Blue, the back the Eagles just waived, was a fifth-round draft pick of the Dallas Cowboys. The Eagles had brought him to the practice squad back in September. And now, less than a week before Dallas comes to town, he is gone. Meanwhile, Joe Milton, the quarterback the Eagles took off the Cowboys practice squad last week, is still here. This rivalry never takes a week off. And I promise you, somebody in that Cowboys building noticed both moves.

So who is Elijah Mitchell? Let me tell you his story, because it is a good one, and a frustrating one.

He played his college ball at Louisiana, where he was a first-team All-Sun Belt running back. The San Francisco 49ers took him in the sixth round of the 2021 draft. And his rookie year was special. He ran for nine hundred sixty-three yards and five touchdowns, averaging around four point seven yards a carry, and he added nineteen catches for one hundred thirty-seven yards and another touchdown. Those nine hundred sixty-three rushing yards were the second most of any rookie in the league that season, behind only Najee Harris of the Steelers. A sixth-round pick, out-rushing almost every first and second-round back in his class.

But then the injuries started. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, since 2022 he has just one hundred twenty carries for five hundred sixty yards and four touchdowns, in parts of thirteen games. He spent the entire 2024 season on injured reserve. In total with San Francisco, according to the Eagles, he ran for one thousand five hundred twenty-three yards and nine touchdowns in twenty-seven games. Last season, he played in one game for the Kansas City Chiefs, and then he signed with the New England Patriots practice squad.

And his history with the Eagles is not new. Philadelphia signed him on June second, after he took part in rookie minicamp as a tryout player. He was in training camp, but he got hurt, he was placed on injured reserve in August, and then he was released with an injury settlement. He never played a preseason game for the Eagles, and he has been out of football ever since. Until now. And think about what this moment means for him personally. He fought his way back from injury after injury, got another shot in Philadelphia, got hurt again in camp, and was let go. Most players would have been done. Instead, he kept himself ready, and the phone rang. That is the kind of story you root for, and the kind of player who will not take a single snap for granted.

So why him? Why bring back a guy you released a couple of months ago?

Two reasons, and they are both pretty interesting.

Number one, the scheme. According to Heavy, the Eagles originally brought Mitchell in because he fit the new offense. First-year offensive coordinator Sean Mannion wants more snaps under center and a heavy dose of outside zone runs, the kind of running game Kyle Shanahan made famous. And where did Elijah Mitchell have his best football? Exactly. With Kyle Shanahan in San Francisco. He knows the timing of those runs. The patience. One cut, and go. He speaks that language.

Number two, the head coach was already thinking about him. At his press conference on October twelfth, more than a week before the signing, Nick Sirianni was asked about running back depth, and he brought up Mitchell on his own. He said, quote, I was really pleased with how he was looking as well. End quote. Reuben Frank of NBC Sports Philadelphia pointed out that Sirianni does not mention former players for no reason. So this reunion may have been in the works for a while.

Now, let me be honest about what this move is and what it is not, because the title of this video says incredible trade, and I want to be straight with you. This is not a trade. It is a practice squad signing. Nobody gave up a draft pick. And Elijah Mitchell is not walking in as a savior. He has a long injury history, and he has not played a real football game in a long time. Nobody should expect the twenty twenty-one rookie who ran for almost a thousand yards. But for a team that is down to two healthy backs and needs somebody who already knows the offense, he could be exactly the right piece at exactly the right time. A practice squad player can be elevated for game day, so if the Eagles need him against Dallas, he can be in uniform.

And there is one more area where he could help. According to Bleeding Green Nation’s film review, Dameon Pierce was asked to stay in and pass protect nineteen times against Carolina, and he struggled at times. With Tanner McKee at quarterback, keeping him upright is a matter of survival. If Mitchell can take some of that load, protect on third down, and give Pierce a breather, that matters.

Now, the second weapon. The bigger one. Saquon Barkley.

Here is what we know. Last week, Ian Rapoport and Adam Schefter reported that the Eagles believe there is a realistic chance Barkley returns for the Monday night game against Dallas. Reuben Frank wrote that there is a chance he plays Monday, but that it remains to be seen. And Heavy pointed out that the fact the Eagles let go of so many running backs this week could be a sign that Barkley is close.

Eagles Nation, think about what that would mean. Saquon back, with Pierce and Mitchell behind him. A backup quarterback suddenly has the best running back in the building in the backfield next to him. The play-action game comes alive. The defense has to put an extra man in the box. And DeVonta Smith, who came back against Carolina with seven catches for seventy-five yards and a touchdown, gets more one-on-one looks. That is how you take pressure off Tanner McKee.

And McKee needs that help. In his first start against Carolina, he went twenty-six of forty-five for two hundred fifteen yards, a touchdown and no interceptions. He protected the football, and he made a beautiful over-the-shoulder throw to DeVonta for the touchdown. But forty-five pass attempts with a backup quarterback is not a recipe for winning. The Eagles had five three-and-outs that day. When the run game gives you nothing, every third down becomes third and long, and every third and long becomes a blitz. A real running game turns those third and eights into third and threes. That is the difference between a stalled drive and a scoring drive.

But I want to be careful. Saquon has missed two straight games, and hamstrings are tricky. The Eagles will practice later this week, because it is a Monday night game, and that is where we will get our first real answer. If Saquon is out there practicing, that is a huge sign. If he is not, Pierce is the lead back and Mitchell is the likely game-day elevation. That is my projection, not something the team has confirmed.

So here is what I will be watching for at practice this week. Number one, is Saquon on the field, and is he a full participant or limited? A limited practice on the first day and a full practice by the end of the week would be the best sign you could ask for. Number two, does Elijah Mitchell take reps with the second team, or even the first team? That will tell you how quickly the coaches trust him. And number three, does Landon Dickerson get a full practice? Because the thinking around the team has been that he needs a full practice before he returns.

Even the Cowboys’ own preview of this game mentioned that Dickerson is hoping to return. Getting a Pro Bowl guard back on that offensive line, with Saquon behind it, would change the entire feel of this offense.

Now, let us talk about the Cowboys, because this matchup looks a lot different than people expected.

Dallas is two and four. They have lost two straight, most recently to Green Bay, where their offense scored just thirteen points. According to the Cowboys’ own website, after averaging more than thirty points a game through the first four weeks, Dallas has scored just twenty-nine points in its last two games combined. Dak Prescott has five interceptions this season, and four of them came in those last two games. And their defense has given up at least twenty points in all six games. Green Bay converted eleven of eighteen third downs against them.

And there is a fun coaching storyline too. The Cowboys’ defensive coordinator, Christian Parker, worked under Vic Fangio from 2024 to 2025. So the student is going up against the teacher. Fangio knows exactly how Parker thinks.

And look at what that means on the field. Remember those four interceptions in two games? Vic Fangio’s defenses are built on disguise, making a quarterback think he sees one coverage and then rotating into another after the snap. If Dak is pressing, and the Cowboys offense is sputtering, that is exactly the kind of quarterback Fangio loves to face. This Eagles defense has kept the team in games all season, even through the losing streak. On Monday night, it might need a takeaway or two to win it.

The Cowboys also have some injuries of their own. According to their website, linebacker DeMarvion Overshown, who has been out since Week 1 with a hamstring, could return, and so could cornerback Cobie Durant. But linebacker Dee Winters is likely out for a few weeks with an Achilles strain, and returner KaVontae Turpin left last week’s game early with a rib injury. So both teams are walking into this one banged up.

And here is the most important part. The Cowboys’ site pointed out that all four NFC East teams are bunched together. The Eagles are two and four, the Cowboys are two and four, and nobody is running away with this division. So this is not a meaningless game between two struggling teams. The winner walks out of Monday night with momentum and a share of the conversation for first place. This is a game that could reshape the entire division race.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let us talk about the word trade, because I know that is what a lot of you want to hear about.

There is no trade right now. But the deadline is November tenth at four in the afternoon Eastern time, and there is a lot of talk out there about what the Eagles might do. And here is the uncomfortable part. Some of that talk is about Saquon Barkley himself. With the team at two and four and Jalen Hurts out for the season, Barkley’s name has come up in trade speculation. Zach Berman of The Athletic, according to reports, believes trading Barkley would be a tougher debate, because he could be part of the plans for 2027. Let me be very clear. That is speculation, not a report of anything happening. And I do not think the Eagles should do it.

So let me give you both sides on where this team should go before the deadline.

On one side, the people who say add. Go get a real running back or another weapon. They will say a practice squad signing is not enough when your backfield has been wiped out. They will point out that the running backs keep getting hurt, and that relying on a back who spent all of twenty twenty-four on injured reserve is a risky plan. They will say the NFC East is wide open, the Cowboys are two and four too, and one or two moves could put the Eagles right back in the race. And they will say the defense is good enough to win games if the offense can just stay on the field.

On the other side, the people who say be careful. They will say Jalen Hurts is out for the season, and you do not trade future picks to chase a playoff spot with a backup quarterback. They will say the smart move is to stay patient, get Saquon healthy, see what Tanner McKee can do, and protect the long-term future of the franchise. And some will even say that if the losing continues, the Eagles should consider selling.

And here is where I land. Again, this is just my opinion, not a report. I like the Mitchell move. It is cheap, it is smart, and it brings in a back who knows this offense. But I do not want the Eagles to panic in either direction. Do not sell Saquon. He is the heartbeat of this offense, and you need him for next year when Jalen comes back. And do not throw away future picks for a quick fix either. Win the games in front of you. Dallas, Washington, the Giants, all division games before the bye. If you are four and five or better going into that bye, you buy. If not, you hold, and you build for twenty twenty-seven.

And honestly, the biggest weapon the Eagles could add before Monday night is not a trade at all. It is a healthy Saquon Barkley. If he is back, this offense changes overnight. Defenses would have to respect the run again, and that one change would make everything else easier for everybody wearing midnight green.

Let me also say something about what Monday night means emotionally. This will be the first game since the news that Jalen Hurts is out for the season. The whole city will be watching. The whole team knows what Jalen went through in London. And this is a chance to play for him. A primetime win over Dallas, at the Linc, would be the perfect way to say we are still here.

So let me wrap it up. No, the Eagles did not make an incredible trade. What they did was sign Elijah Mitchell to the practice squad, a back who was the second-best rookie rusher in the NFL in twenty twenty-one, who knows this offense, and who Nick Sirianni was already praising more than a week ago. They cleared out the backfield, waiving Jaydon Blue and Carson Steele and releasing Ian Wheeler, leaving Pierce and Mitchell as the healthy backs. And the real weapon might be Saquon Barkley, who has a realistic chance to return against the Cowboys on Monday night, according to Rapoport and Schefter.
