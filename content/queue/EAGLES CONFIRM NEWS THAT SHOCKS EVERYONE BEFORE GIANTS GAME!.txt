Eagles Nation, the Eagles have made it official, and the final report before the Giants game is not the one this city wanted to see. Six players are ruled out for Sunday, including DeVonta Smith. Dameon Pierce is gone. And with the trade deadline on Tuesday, a three-time Pro Bowler on this offensive line is drawing interest from other teams. Let me be clear right from the start. Nobody has been traded, and no deal has been reported as close. But the news coming out of Philadelphia this week tells you just how different this season has become.

The final injury report the Eagles confirmed on Friday, the running back who is no longer with the team, the Landon Dickerson trade rumors and what Dickerson said himself, how Nick Sirianni explained the quarterback switch, and why the Eagles are home underdogs against the Giants for the first time since 2009. Let me start with the injury report, because it shapes everything about Sunday.

According to NBC10 Philadelphia, the Eagles ruled out six players for the Giants game. Wide receiver DeVonta Smith with a hamstring injury. Wide receiver Dontayvion Wicks with an ankle injury. Linebacker Jihaad Campbell with hamstring and quad injuries. Wide receiver Britain Covey with a knee injury. Defensive back Michael Carter the second with an abdomen injury. And cornerback Jonathan Jones with a hamstring injury. Tanner McKee is questionable with a rib injury.

Think about that for a second. Joe Milton is making the first start of his NFL career, and he will do it without his top two receivers. According to Inside The Iggles, this is the fifth game DeVonta Smith has missed this season. That puts a lot of pressure on Dallas Goedert, rookie Makai Lemon and rookie tight end Eli Stowers. Lemon has led the Eagles in catches and receiving yards in each of the last two games, according to the Delaware News Journal. Now he may have to be the number one receiver for a quarterback making his first start. That is a lot to ask of a rookie, but he has earned the chance.

But there is good news too. Saquon Barkley does not have a game status, which means he is expected to play. According to NBC10, he missed Wednesday’s practice, then practiced on a limited basis on Thursday and Friday with his hamstring and back injuries. And according to Inside The Iggles, he is expected to be on a snap count again and split carries with Elijah Mitchell.

And here is an exit that a lot of fans missed this week. According to Inside The Iggles, citing Dave Zangaro of NBC Sports Philadelphia and the NFL transaction wire, the Eagles reached an injury settlement with running back Dameon Pierce. That takes him off injured reserve and makes him a free agent. Pierce finished his time in Philadelphia with thirty-seven carries for ninety-six yards and two touchdowns, plus seven catches for eleven yards. He was one of the backs the Eagles leaned on during a season full of injuries in the backfield, and now he is gone.

Now, let me talk about the trade story that has everyone talking. Landon Dickerson.

To understand why this one matters, you have to remember what this offensive line has already been through. According to NFL dot com, Lane Johnson announced a surprise retirement on October sixth, after missing Week 4. The very next day, Howie Roseman traded Cam Jurgens to the Baltimore Ravens. According to NFL dot com, the Eagles sent Jurgens and a 2027 seventh-round pick to Baltimore for a 2027 fifth-round pick and a 2028 second-round pick. And Jurgens had signed a four-year, sixty-eight million dollar extension in 2025.

So in two days, the Eagles lost their right tackle to retirement and traded their center. At the time, according to Newsweek, Adam Schefter said the Jurgens deal was not a white flag in Philadelphia. He said the Eagles had moved Drew Kendall to center, that they, quote, like him an awful lot, end quote, and that Jurgens did not fit well at guard. So the Eagles had a plan. But a month later, with the line struggling, a lot of fans are wondering whether that plan is working. And now, the name being linked to another exit is the left guard.

On Wednesday, Ryan Fowler of NBC Sports Philadelphia wrote, quote, Landon Dickerson continues to draw interest from teams looking for help up front, per source. End quote. He added that, quote, conversations over the last few days suggest multiple teams have expressed interest in acquiring the veteran guard. End quote. According to Heavy, Fowler noted that much of the attention had been on Riq Woolen and Moro Ojomo, but that Dickerson’s name was now coming up too.

And it is not just one reporter. According to ClutchPoints, Adam Schefter said on ESPN’s The Aftermath, quote, I know they have some veterans on expiring contracts that teams would consider. End quote. And then, quote, Landon Dickerson, I know some teams have been interested in. End quote. And ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler had already put Dickerson’s odds of being traded at twenty-five percent.

Now, notice the words. Interest. Interested. Nobody has said a deal is close. Nobody has named a team that is negotiating. Nobody has said the Eagles are shopping him. This is interest, not a trade.

Here is who Landon Dickerson is. He is twenty-eight years old. He is a three-time Pro Bowler. He was a second-round pick in 2021, and he was part of the team that won Super Bowl fifty-nine. But according to ClutchPoints, he has spent much of the last two years dealing with injuries. And according to NBC Sports Philadelphia, he has played in only three of the Eagles’ eight games this season.

And then there is the contract. According to ClutchPoints, Dickerson is on a long-term deal that runs through 2028, with void years through 2032. That makes a trade complicated. And according to the Inquirer, the Eagles could already lead the NFL in dead cap money in 2027, and a fire sale could make that even worse.

And think about what Dickerson means to this group right now. With Johnson retired and Jurgens in Baltimore, he and Jordan Mailata are the last of the veteran core that helped this team win a Super Bowl. Rookie Markel Bell is at right tackle. Drew Kendall moved to center. Tyler Steen has been the starting right guard, and his name has come up in trade talk too, with The Athletic’s Daniel Popper naming Denver as the best fit. According to NBC Sports Philadelphia, the Eagles have allowed a free rusher on eighteen point two percent of pass plays this season, the most in the league. So trading Dickerson would not just remove a good player. It would remove one of the last steady voices in that room.

Now, here is the part every Eagles fan should hear. Dickerson was asked about all of this, and he did not hide. According to Heavy, he said on 94 WIP, quote, I love this city, it’s my home. End quote. And then, quote, I really don’t want to play for another team. End quote.

And when he talked to reporters at the team facility, according to Ed Kracz of Eagles On SI, he said, quote, it’s really not up to me. End quote. And then he said, quote, really, all I care about is winning a championship. End quote.

That is a player who loves Philadelphia, but who also understands the business. He knows he does not get to make that call. And he knows the phone could ring at any moment.

Let me pause here. Comment number one if you think the Eagles should listen to offers for Dickerson. Comment number two if you think they should keep him and build around him.

And Dickerson was not the only player asked about the rumors. According to Eagles On SI, Moro Ojomo, the former seventh-round pick who is in a contract year, said, quote, Howie and Lurie mean so much to me for giving me this opportunity to play for this team. End quote. Remember, Albert Breer reported that the Eagles told teams Ojomo is not available, and according to PhillyVoice, an ESPN report said the Eagles are inclined to keep and potentially extend him. And Dallas Goedert stayed focused on the field. He said, quote, I’m here right now, trying to go through practice, go through meetings, and prepare for this game. End quote. According to the Inquirer, Goedert’s situation is less likely to change because of the salary cap.

And there was one more interesting moment. According to Eagles On SI, defensive coordinator Vic Fangio was asked about the trade rumors. He said he would only address a rumor if it had no truth to it. And when he was asked if that was the case now, his answer was one word. No.

And Sirianni? He said, quote, you’re always in constant communication with your guys. End quote.

Now, one more thing on Dickerson. According to the Inquirer, he was also fined twenty-two thousand four hundred fifty-six dollars for unnecessary roughness on a pull block. That is a guy who plays with an edge, the kind of edge this offensive line badly needs.

Now, let me talk about how Sirianni explained the quarterback switch, because he finally addressed it on Friday.

You remember the timeline. On Monday, Sirianni said Tanner’s our guy. Two days later, reports came out that Joe Milton would start against the Giants. On Friday, according to Inside The Iggles, citing Zach Berman of The Athletic, Sirianni explained that the team wanted to keep the switch private for as long as possible. He said, quote, every second that goes by in a situation like that, that you can hold onto information as long as you possibly can. End quote. And he said, quote, my job is to win football games. End quote.

So according to Sirianni, it was about not giving the Giants extra time to prepare. Some fans will accept that. Others will say it made McKee look bad. According to the Inquirer, columnist Marcus Hayes accused Sirianni of shifting blame onto McKee. That is his opinion. But it shows you how heated this has become.

And Milton? According to Inside The Iggles, citing Berman, he said he stayed ready while he was on the practice squad, and that his focus is on the next play. Quote, just be where my feet is. End quote. According to the Inquirer, his teammates have been praising his deep ball in practice, including Zack Baun and Eli Stowers. And Giants defensive coordinator Dennard Wilson said Milton can run, is elusive in the pocket and can throw deep, bringing what he called a different dynamic.

And the columnists are not holding back. According to the Inquirer’s newsletter, Mike Sielski wrote that the Eagles put their plan ahead of their people, and that they are near the bottom for the first time in years. David Murphy wrote that if Milton fails, the team only buys itself a few more weeks before the next change. And offensive coordinator Sean Mannion, who is under heavy criticism, said he is blocking out the noise. Those are opinions, and some of them are harsh. But they show you how much pressure is on this building heading into Sunday.

Now, the shock that sums up this season. According to the Inquirer, the Eagles are home underdogs for the first time since 2009. The Giants are favored by three, and the over under is under forty points. And every Inquirer writer picked the Giants. Jeff McLane picked Giants twenty-three, Eagles twenty. Jeff Neiburg and Olivia Reiner both picked Giants twenty-seven, Eagles twenty. And Ryan Novozinsky picked Giants thirty-one, Eagles fourteen.

And here is another number that tells you how bad it has been. According to the Inquirer, the Eagles’ point differential through eight games is minus sixty-five or worse, something this franchise has not seen since 1998. Only the winless Chargers are worse.

But the Giants have their own problems. Jaxson Dart and Brian Burns are out for the season. J.J. McCarthy is their quarterback. And according to the Inquirer, running back Cam Skattebo is doubtful with a hamstring injury, and receiver Charlie Jones is questionable with a hand injury.

And let me remind you of the bigger trade picture, because Dickerson is not alone. According to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, his sense is that the Eagles, quote, could be trading some players but likely won’t engage in a rebuild. End quote. Fowler has put the odds of Riq Woolen being traded at thirty-five percent. Hollywood Brown, who has been a healthy scratch, is a name teams believe could be moved, according to Jordan Schultz. And multiple reports say Saquon Barkley will not be traded. According to the Inquirer, Woolen, Ojomo, Dickerson and Goedert could all be on the market. And here is a fun one. The Inquirer ran a Save or Trade poll with its readers, and fans voted to trade nearly every player they were asked about. Only three were saved. That tells you how frustrated this fan base is right now.

And in the middle of all of this, there was one piece of news that made everyone smile. According to ESPN’s Tim McManus, Jalen Hurts visited the team facility on Wednesday and sat in on meetings, and his presence, quote, did provide a morale boost for some. End quote. Sirianni said, quote, I miss him like crazy. End quote. Jalen is out for the season, and the team expects a full recovery. But seeing him in that building is a reminder of what this team can be again when he is back.

Alright, Eagles Nation, let me give you both sides.

On one side, the people who say sell. They will say the Eagles are two and six and home underdogs for the first time since 2009. They will say Lane Johnson is retired, Jurgens is in Baltimore, and the line is already being rebuilt, so why not move Dickerson too while teams are interested? They will say his injuries over the last two years make him a risk, and that getting a good pick now is better than waiting. And they will say Howie Roseman has already shown he is willing to make tough moves, like the Jurgens trade.

On the other side, the people who say keep him. They will say Dickerson is only twenty-eight, a three-time Pro Bowler and a Super Bowl champion. They will say he loves this city and wants to stay. They will say his long-term contract and the dead cap money make a trade hard to justify. They will say this line has already lost too much, and that trading Dickerson would leave the Eagles starting from scratch in 2027. And they will say that when Jalen Hurts comes back, he needs a strong offensive line in front of him.

And here is where I land. This is just my opinion, not a report. I keep Landon Dickerson. And here is why. When you trade a player like Jurgens, you get a second-round pick and you have a younger player ready to take over. That was the plan with Drew Kendall. But who is ready to take over for Dickerson? There is no obvious answer on this roster. And a guard who has dealt with injuries is not going to bring back the kind of pick that changes your future. On top of that, the Eagles have already lost Lane Johnson and Cam Jurgens. If you trade Dickerson too, you are not reshaping the line, you are tearing it down. And with his contract and the dead money involved, I do not think the return would be worth it unless a team offers something really big. He said it himself. He loves this city and he wants to win a championship here. That is exactly the kind of player you build around.

As for Sunday, I want to see Joe Milton play fast and free. Without DeVonta and Wicks, the Eagles need Goedert, Lemon and Stowers to step up. And they need Saquon, even on a snap count, to give this offense some balance.
